Wishes and Regular Forms

The subjunctive (el subjuntivo) is not a tense but a mood. The indicative presents things as facts; the subjunctive presents them as wishes, feelings, possibilities or things not (yet) real. It appears mostly in a second clause introduced by que, after a trigger in the main clause.

Forming the present subjunctive

  1. Take the yo form of the present indicative.
  2. Drop the -o.
  3. Add the "opposite" ending: -ar verbs take -e endings; -er/-ir verbs take -a endings.
Person hablar (habl-o) comer (com-o) vivir (viv-o)
yo hable coma viva
tú hables comas vivas
él / ella / usted hable coma viva
nosotros hablemos comamos vivamos
vosotros habléis comáis viváis
ellos / ustedes hablen coman vivan

Because you start from the yo form, irregular yo stems carry over: tengo → tenga, hago → haga, salgo → salga, conozco → conozca, digo → diga, pongo → ponga, vengo → venga, veo → vea.

Wishes and desires

When the main clause expresses what someone wants another person to do or to happen, the second verb is subjunctive — provided the two clauses have different subjects.

  • Quiero que vengas a la fiesta. — I want you to come to the party.
  • Mis padres desean que estudie medicina. — My parents want me to study medicine.
  • Preferimos que nos llaméis antes. — We'd rather you called us first.

Same subject? Use the infinitive: Quiero venir (I want to come), quiero que yo venga.

Want + someone + to

English I want you to… has no que, but Spanish always does: Quiero que tú… + subjunctive.

Emotions and Irregular Verbs

Emotions

After expressions of feeling about someone else's action — joy, fear, sadness, surprise, anger — the subjunctive follows.

  • Me alegra que estés aquí. — I'm glad you're here.
  • Tengo miedo de que se pierdan. — I'm afraid they'll get lost.
  • Nos sorprende que no lo sepas. — It surprises us that you don't know.
  • Siento que no puedas venir. — I'm sorry you can't come.
  • Le molesta que hablemos alto. — It bothers him that we talk loudly.

Common triggers: alegrarse de que, encantar que, gustar que, molestar que, sentir que, temer que, tener miedo de que, sorprender que, estar contento/triste de que.

Six irregular verbs

These don't follow the yo-form rule. A handy memory aid is DISHES: dar, ir, ser, haber, estar, saber.

dar ir ser haber estar saber
yo dé vaya sea haya esté sepa
tú des vayas seas hayas estés sepas
él dé vaya sea haya esté sepa
nosotros demos vayamos seamos hayamos estemos sepamos
vosotros deis vayáis seáis hayáis estéis sepáis
ellos den vayan sean hayan estén sepan

Accents matter

Dé (give) carries an accent to distinguish it from the preposition de. Esté, estés, estén need accents to keep the stress on the last syllable (este without accent means "this").

Expressions and Stem Changes

Impersonal expressions

Many expressions with es + adjective + que trigger the subjunctive when they express opinion, necessity, possibility or judgement.

  • Es importante que descanses. — It's important for you to rest.
  • Es necesario que lleguemos a tiempo. — We need to arrive on time.
  • Es posible que llueva. — It may rain.
  • Es una pena que se vayan. — It's a shame they're leaving.
  • Es mejor que no digas nada. — It's better you don't say anything.

But expressions of certainty take the indicative: Es verdad que, es cierto que, es obvio que, está claro que. (Es verdad que llueve.)

Stem changes

-ar and -er verbs keep their usual stem change (e → ie, o → ue) in every form except nosotros and vosotros:

querer poder
quiera, quieras, quiera, queramos, queráis, quieran pueda, puedas, pueda, podamos, podáis, puedan

-ir verbs with e → ie or o → ue also change in nosotros/vosotros, but to i and u:

sentir dormir
sienta, sientas, sienta, sintamos, sintáis, sientan duerma, duermas, duerma, durmamos, durmáis, duerman

-ir verbs with e → i keep i in every form:

pedir
pida, pidas, pida, pidamos, pidáis, pidan

Nosotros in -ir verbs

Durmamos is right, dormamos is wrong. And the subjunctive of sentir is sintamos, not sentamos (which belongs to sentar, to sit).

Recommendations and Spelling Changes

Recommendations and requests

Verbs of advising, asking, ordering, allowing and forbidding take the subjunctive when directed at someone else.

  • Te recomiendo que pruebes el gazpacho. — I recommend you try the gazpacho.
  • El médico me aconseja que haga ejercicio. — The doctor advises me to exercise.
  • Les pido que no fumen. — I ask you not to smoke.
  • Sugiero que salgamos temprano. — I suggest we leave early.
  • No permiten que entremos. — They don't let us go in.

Common triggers: aconsejar, recomendar, sugerir, pedir, rogar, exigir, insistir en, mandar, ordenar, permitir, prohibir.

Spelling changes

To keep the sound of the stem's last consonant, some verbs change their spelling before e or a:

Ending Change Example
-car c → qu sacar → saque, saques…
-gar g → gu llegar → llegue, llegues…
-zar z → c empezar → empiece, empieces…
-ger / -gir g → j escoger → escoja, dirigir → dirija
-guir gu → g seguir → siga
  • Espero que lleguen pronto. — I hope they arrive soon.
  • Es mejor que empieces ya. — You'd better start now.
  • Quiere que escoja yo. — She wants me to choose.

Same as the preterite yo form

The -car/-gar/-zar changes are the ones you already know from the preterite: saqué, llegué, empecé.

Trigger Type

Os ruego que… request

Practice

  1. Es fundamental que (tú, beber) ___ agua.
  2. Te aconsejo que (buscar) ___ otro trabajo.
  3. Es raro que Ana no (contestar) ___.
  4. Os ruego que (ser) ___ puntuales.
  5. Conviene que (nosotros, reservar) ___ mesa.

Answers

  1. bebas 2. busques 3. conteste 4. seáis 5. reservemos

Indirect object + que

Recommendation verbs usually carry an indirect object pronoun that refers to the same person as the subjunctive subject: Te pido que vengas.

Doubt and Ojalá

Doubt and denial

When you doubt or deny that something is true, the subjunctive follows.

  • Dudo que tengan razón. — I doubt they're right.
  • No creo que venga. — I don't think he'll come.
  • Niega que sea culpable. — She denies she's guilty.
  • No es cierto que haya problemas. — It isn't true that there are problems.

The positive versions express belief and take the indicative: Creo que viene. Pienso que tienen razón. No dudo que es verdad.

Creer flips

Creo que + indicative; No creo que + subjunctive. In questions, both are possible: ¿Crees que viene / venga?

Ojalá

Ojalá (from Arabic, roughly "if God wills") means "I hope" / "let's hope". It is always followed by the subjunctive; que is optional.

  • ¡Ojalá haga sol mañana! — I hope it's sunny tomorrow!
  • Ojalá que todo salga bien. — I hope everything goes well.
  • Ojalá no se enteren. — Let's hope they don't find out.

Other hope expressions: Espero que…, Que… on its own for wishes: ¡Que te mejores! (Get well soon!) ¡Que tengas buen viaje! (Have a good trip!)

Conditional Outcomes

Certain conjunctions introduce an action that depends on a condition, a purpose or something that hasn't happened yet. These always take the subjunctive:

Conjunction Meaning
para que so that
a menos que unless
con tal de que provided that
en caso de que in case
sin que without
antes de que before
a no ser que unless
  • Te lo explico para que lo entiendas. — I'll explain it so that you understand.
  • Iremos a la playa a menos que llueva. — We'll go to the beach unless it rains.
  • Lleva paraguas en caso de que llueva. — Take an umbrella in case it rains.
  • Salió sin que nadie lo viera. — He left without anyone seeing him. (past: imperfect subjunctive)

Time conjunctions: future = subjunctive

Cuando, en cuanto, tan pronto como, hasta que, después de que, mientras take the subjunctive when the action is still in the future, but the indicative for habits or past facts.

  • Cuando llegues, llámame. — When you arrive, call me. (future → subjunctive)
  • Cuando llego, siempre llamo. — When I arrive, I always call. (habit → indicative)

No future after cuando

Cuando llegarás → Cuando llegues. Spanish never uses the future tense after cuando in a time clause.

Relative Clauses 1

The subjunctive also appears in relative clauses (after que, donde, etc.) when the antecedent is unknown, hypothetical or not yet found.

  • Busco un piso que tenga terraza. — I'm looking for a flat that has a terrace. (any flat — I don't know if one exists)
  • Busco el piso que tiene terraza. — I'm looking for the flat that has a terrace. (a specific, known flat → indicative)
  • Necesitamos a alguien que hable japonés. — We need someone who speaks Japanese.
  • Quiero un coche que no gaste mucho. — I want a car that doesn't use much fuel.

Known or unknown?

Ask yourself whether the speaker knows that this particular person or thing exists. Known → indicative. Unknown or wished for → subjunctive.

Note the personal a: it is often dropped when the person is not specific (Busco un médico que…), but kept with alguien and nadie.

Relative Clauses 2

Non-existence and negation

When the antecedent doesn't exist (with nadie, nada, ningún/ninguna), the relative clause takes the subjunctive.

  • No hay nadie que me entienda. — There's nobody who understands me.
  • No conozco ningún restaurante que abra los lunes. — I don't know any restaurant that opens on Mondays.
  • No hay nada que podamos hacer. — There's nothing we can do.

Questions about existence

  • ¿Hay alguien aquí que sepa cocinar? — Is there anyone here who can cook?
  • ¿Conoces a alguien que viva en Lima? — Do you know anyone who lives in Lima?

Indefinite expressions

"-ever" expressions take the subjunctive for open, undetermined reference:

  • Haz lo que quieras. — Do whatever you like.
  • Iré donde tú vayas. — I'll go wherever you go.
  • Quien llegue primero, gana. — Whoever arrives first wins.

Contrast

Hay alguien que sabe cocinar (someone exists — indicative) vs. No hay nadie que sepa cocinar (no one exists — subjunctive).

Expressions and Recommendations 2

More triggers to add to your list:

Expression Example
Es preciso que Es preciso que firmes aquí. — You must sign here.
Es urgente que Es urgente que llamemos al médico.
Más vale que Más vale que te des prisa. — You'd better hurry.
Hace falta que Hace falta que alguien lo arregle. — Someone needs to fix it.
Exigir que El jefe exige que lleguemos a las ocho.
Insistir en que Insisten en que nos quedemos a cenar.
Decir que (command) Dile que venga. — Tell him to come.

Decir and insistir: two meanings

As information, they take the indicative: Dice que viene (he says he's coming). As a command, they take the subjunctive: Dice que vengas (he says you should come).

  2. Les digo que (traer) ___ comida.
  3. Hace falta que (yo, hacer) ___ la compra.

Answers

  1. estudiéis 2. traigan 3. haga

Extra Practice 3

Correct the mistakes.

  1. Quiero que vienes. → Quiero que vengas.
  2. Espero llegues pronto. → Espero que llegues pronto.
  3. Ojalá tienes suerte. → Ojalá tengas suerte.
  4. Cuando tendrás tiempo, llámame. → Cuando tengas tiempo, llámame.
  5. No creo que es buena idea. → No creo que sea buena idea.
  6. Quiero que yo vaya. → Quiero ir.

The big picture

Facts and beliefs → indicative. Wishes, emotions, judgements, advice, doubt, unreal or future conditions and unknown antecedents → subjunctive. Same subject in both clauses → usually infinitive.

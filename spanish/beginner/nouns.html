Masculine Nouns

Every Spanish noun has a grammatical gender, even nouns for objects and ideas. For people and animals the gender usually follows biology; for everything else it is a matter of form and habit. The ending of a noun is your best clue.

Nouns are usually masculine when they end in:

Ending Examples
-o el libro — book, el vaso — glass
-ma (many of Greek origin) el problema, el sistema, el idioma — language
a stressed vowel el sofá, el café, el menú
a consonant other than -d or -z el papel — paper, el amor — love, el reloj — watch
-aje, -or el viaje — trip, el color
  • El clima de aquí es suave. — The climate here is mild.
  • Tengo un problema con el coche. — I have a problem with the car.

Masculine by meaning

Some groups are masculine regardless of their ending: days of the week (el martes), months (un julio muy caluroso — a very hot July), languages (el español), colours used as nouns (el rojo), numbers (el siete), and most rivers, seas and mountains (el Amazonas, el Mediterráneo).

Masculine words in -a

el día (day), el mapa (map), el planeta (planet) and el sofá end in -a but are masculine. Learn them as a little group.

Feminine Nouns

Nouns are usually feminine when they end in:

Ending Examples
-a la casa — house, la playa — beach
-ción, -sión la canción — song, la decisión
-dad, -tad, -tud la ciudad — city, la libertad, la salud — health
-z la luz — light, la paz — peace, la vez — time (occurrence)
-umbre, -ie la costumbre — custom, la serie
-is (many) la crisis, la tesis
  • La ciudad tiene una catedral muy antigua. — The city has a very old cathedral.
  • Esa canción me pone contenta. — That song makes me happy.

Feminine words that don't end in -a

la mano (hand), la foto (photo, short for fotografía), la moto (motorbike, short for motocicleta), la radio and la flor (flower) are all feminine.

Feminine nouns with el

As you saw in Articles, feminine nouns that start with a stressed a- or ha- take el and un in the singular: el agua, el hambre (hunger), el arma (weapon). They are still feminine — el agua está fría, mucha hambre — and the plural uses las: las armas.

Endings beat guessing

If a noun ends in -ción, -dad or -tud, it's feminine practically every time. That covers thousands of words that are almost the same as English (nation → la nación, university → la universidad).

Professions

Words for jobs often have a masculine and a feminine form, depending on who does the job.

-o → -a

  • el abogado / la abogada — lawyer
  • el médico / la médica — doctor
  • el camarero / la camarera — waiter / waitress

Consonant → add -a

  • el profesor / la profesora — teacher
  • el doctor / la doctora — doctor
  • el bailarín / la bailarina — dancer (note the accent disappears)

One form for both

Nouns ending in -ista, -ante, -ente and many in -e don't change. Only the article shows the gender:

  • el / la periodista — journalist
  • el / la estudiante — student
  • el / la cantante — singer
  • el / la policía — police officer

A few special pairs

  • el actor / la actriz — actor / actress
  • el rey / la reina — king / queen

Remember that after ser, a bare profession takes no article: Mi hermano es enfermero. — My brother is a nurse.

Feminine forms are spreading

Older usage kept some job titles masculine for everyone (la médico, la juez). Today la médica, la jueza and la presidenta are standard in most countries.

Family Terms

Family words follow the same -o / -a pattern, and the masculine plural covers mixed groups.

Masculine Feminine Mixed plural
el padre — father la madre — mother los padres — parents
el hijo — son la hija — daughter los hijos — children
el hermano — brother la hermana — sister los hermanos — siblings
el abuelo — grandfather la abuela — grandmother los abuelos — grandparents
el tío — uncle la tía — aunt los tíos — aunt and uncle
el primo — cousin la prima — cousin los primos — cousins
el sobrino — nephew la sobrina — niece los sobrinos
el nieto — grandson la nieta — granddaughter los nietos

In-laws and step-relations

  • el suegro / la suegra — father-in-law / mother-in-law
  • el yerno / la nuera — son-in-law / daughter-in-law
  • el cuñado / la cuñada — brother-in-law / sister-in-law
  • el padrastro / la madrastra — stepfather / stepmother
  • Mis padres viven en Sevilla. — My parents live in Seville.
  • ¿Cuántos hermanos tienes? — How many brothers and sisters do you have?

Los padres

los padres nearly always means "parents", not "fathers". To say "the children" in a family, use los hijos; los niños means children in general.

Invariable Nouns

Some nouns don't change at all, or change in only one way.

Same form for both genders

Many nouns for people have one form, and the article alone shows whether you mean a man or a woman: el / la turista, el / la joven (young person), el / la testigo (witness).

Same form in singular and plural

Nouns that end in an unstressed vowel + -s (or -x) keep the same form in the plural. Only the article changes.

  • el lunes → los lunes — Monday → Mondays
  • la crisis → las crisis — crisis → crises
  • el paraguas → los paraguas — umbrella → umbrellas
  • el tórax → los tórax — chest → chests

Compound nouns made of verb + plural noun work the same way: el abrelatas (can opener), el cumpleaños (birthday), el sacacorchos (corkscrew).

One gender for both sexes

Some animal and people words have a fixed gender that covers both sexes: la persona (a man is also una persona), la víctima, el bebé, la jirafa. To be specific about animals, add macho or hembra: la jirafa macho.

  • Mi hermano es una persona muy tranquila. — My brother is a very calm person.

Plural Nouns

Making a noun plural depends on how it ends.

Singular ending Rule Example
unstressed vowel add -s la mesa → las mesas
consonant (not -s) add -es el árbol → los árboles, la ciudad → las ciudades
-z change to -ces el lápiz → los lápices, la vez → las veces
stressed -á, -é, -ó add -s el sofá → los sofás, el café → los cafés
stressed -í, -ú add -es or -s el rubí → los rubíes, el menú → los menús
unstressed vowel + -s no change el martes → los martes

Watch the written accent

Adding a syllable can move a word into a different stress pattern, so accents can appear or disappear:

  • la canción → las canciones — accent dropped
  • el joven → los jóvenes — accent added
  • el examen → los exámenes — accent added

Words with no singular

A few nouns are normally plural: las gafas (glasses), las vacaciones (holiday), las tijeras (scissors), los pantalones (trousers, also el pantalón).

Say it, then spell it

The stress never moves when you make a noun plural: jo-ven → jó-ve-nes. Keep the stress where it was and let the accent rules decide whether you need a tilde.

Capitalization

Spanish uses capital letters far less than English. Capitals are used for the first word of a sentence, names of people and places, and names of institutions — but many words English capitalizes are lowercase in Spanish.

Lowercase in Spanish

Category Spanish English
days of the week el lunes Monday
months en marzo in March
languages hablo inglés I speak English
nationalities es mexicana she's Mexican
religions el budismo Buddhism
titles (in general) el señor López, la reina Sofía Mr López, Queen Sofía
the pronoun I yo I
  • Mi amigo francés llega el viernes 3 de mayo. — My French friend arrives on Friday 3 May.

Titles of works

In titles of books, films and songs, only the first word (and any proper names) gets a capital: Cien años de soledad, El laberinto del fauno.

Abbreviated titles

Abbreviations of titles are capitalized: Sr. (señor), Sra. (señora), Dr. (doctor), Ud. (usted).

Days and languages

El Lunes estudio Inglés. → El lunes estudio inglés. — On Monday I study English.

Noun-Adjective Agreement

Adjectives in Spanish agree with the noun they describe in gender and number. Most descriptive adjectives come after the noun.

Adjectives ending in -o (four forms)

Singular Plural
Masculine el gato negro los gatos negros
Feminine la gata negra las gatas negras

Adjectives ending in -e or a consonant (two forms)

These change only for number: un chico inteligente / una chica inteligente; un examen fácil / unos exámenes fáciles.

Adjectives of nationality

Nationality adjectives ending in a consonant do add -a for the feminine: un chico español / una chica española, un vino francés / una película francesa.

Mixed groups

When an adjective describes nouns of both genders, use the masculine plural:

  • El pan y la leche están frescos. — The bread and the milk are fresh.

Gender of the noun, not the person

The adjective agrees with the grammatical gender of the noun: Mi hermano es una persona simpática — the adjective is feminine because persona is feminine.

Before the noun

A few common adjectives can go before the noun and some shorten in the masculine singular: un buen libro (bueno), un mal día (malo), el primer piso (primero), una gran ciudad (grande shortens before any singular noun).

Tricky Nouns

Some nouns break the patterns or change meaning with gender.

Same word, different gender, different meaning

Masculine Feminine
el capital — money, capital la capital — capital city
el cometa — comet la cometa — kite
el corte — cut la corte — royal court
el orden — order, tidiness la orden — command, religious order
el frente — front (military) la frente — forehead
el cura — priest la cura — cure
  • Madrid es la capital de España. — Madrid is the capital of Spain.
  • No tenemos el capital para abrir una tienda. — We don't have the capital to open a shop.

Masculine and feminine forms that look unrelated

Some animal pairs use different words: el caballo / la yegua (horse / mare), el toro / la vaca (bull / cow), el gallo / la gallina (rooster / hen).

Gender changes meaning in pairs

  • el manzano / la manzana — apple tree / apple
  • el bolso / la bolsa — handbag / bag
  • el puerto / la puerta — port / door

Arte and mar

el arte is masculine in the singular but often feminine in the plural (las bellas artes), and el mar is masculine in everyday speech but feminine in sailors' and poets' language (la mar).

Usually masculine Usually feminine
-o, -ma, stressed vowel, -aje, -or, most consonants -a, -ción/-sión, -dad/-tad/-tud, -z, -umbre

Practice: masculine or feminine?

  • ___ verdad → la verdad — the truth (-dad)
  • ___ tema → el tema — the topic (-ma)
  • ___ mano → la mano — the hand (exception)
  • ___ garaje → el garaje — the garage (-aje)
  • ___ nariz → la nariz — the nose (-z)
  • ___ día → el día — the day (exception)

Comprehensive Review 3

This review covers capitalization, agreement and tricky nouns.

Spot the mistake

  • Las casas blancos → Las casas blancas — the white houses
  • Viajo en Julio. → Viajo en julio. — I'm travelling in July.
  • una chica francés → una chica francesa — a French girl
  • Mi padre y mi madre son altas. → Mi padre y mi madre son altos. — My father and mother are tall.
  • Los niños vuelan el cometa. → Los niños vuelan la cometa. — The children are flying the kite.

One sentence, three checks

Before you say a noun phrase, check three things in one go: the article, the noun's plural, and the adjective ending. Las ciudades grandes, los problemas difíciles, las manos frías.

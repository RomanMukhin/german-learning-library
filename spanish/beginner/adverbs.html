Adverbs of Place

Adverbs describe a verb, an adjective or another adverb, answering questions like where?, when?, how? and how much?. Unlike adjectives, they never change for gender or number.

Adverbs of place answer where?.

Spanish English
aquí / acá here
ahí there (near you)
allí / allá over there (far from both)
cerca near
lejos far
dentro / adentro inside
fuera / afuera outside
arriba up, upstairs
abajo down, downstairs
delante / adelante in front, ahead
detrás / atrás behind, back
enfrente opposite
  • Ven aquí. — Come here.
  • El supermercado está muy cerca. — The supermarket is very close.
  • Los niños juegan fuera. — The children are playing outside.

Adverb vs preposition

Many of these become prepositions with de: cerca de la playa (near the beach), dentro de la caja (inside the box), delante de mí (in front of me).

Acá and allá

acá and allá are preferred in much of Latin America, and they also suggest movement or a vaguer area: Ven para acá. — Come over here.

Adverbs of Time

Adverbs of time answer when?, how long? and how often?.

When

Spanish English
hoy today
ayer yesterday
mañana tomorrow
ahora now
antes before
después / luego after, later
pronto soon
ya already, now
todavía / aún still, yet
entonces then

How often

Spanish English
siempre always
a menudo often
a veces sometimes
casi nunca hardly ever
nunca never
  • Ahora no puedo, te llamo luego. — I can't now, I'll call you later.
  • ¿Ya has comido? — Have you already eaten?
  • Todavía estoy en la oficina. — I'm still at the office.
  • A veces voy en bici. — Sometimes I go by bike.

Mañana: tomorrow or morning

mañana alone is "tomorrow"; la mañana is "the morning". mañana por la mañana — tomorrow morning.

Adverbs of Quantity and Degree

These answer how much? or to what extent?.

Spanish English
muy very
mucho a lot, much
poco little, not much
bastante quite, enough
demasiado too, too much
más more
menos less
casi almost
tan so
nada not at all

Muy vs mucho

muy goes before adjectives and adverbs; mucho goes with verbs (or alone).

  • Es muy guapo. — He's very handsome.
  • Hablas muy rápido. — You speak very fast.
  • Te quiero mucho. — I love you a lot.
  • Estoy mucho cansado. → Estoy muy cansado. — I'm very tired.

The exceptions: mejor, peor, mayor, menor, más and menos take mucho: mucho mejor — much better.

Adverb or adjective?

mucho, poco and demasiado are adverbs (invariable) when they modify a verb, but adjectives (agreeing) before a noun:

  • Comen mucho. — They eat a lot. (adverb)
  • Comen muchas verduras. — They eat lots of vegetables. (adjective)

Adverbs of Manner

Adverbs of manner answer how? or in what way?.

Spanish English
bien well
mal badly
así like this, so
despacio slowly
deprisa quickly
mejor better
peor worse
regular so-so
  • Cantas muy bien. — You sing very well.
  • Me siento mal. — I feel bad / ill.
  • Hazlo así. — Do it like this.
  • Habla más despacio, por favor. — Speak more slowly, please.

Bien / bueno, mal / malo

bien and mal are adverbs (they describe actions); bueno and malo are adjectives (they describe nouns):

  • Es un buen cocinero; cocina bien. — He's a good cook; he cooks well.

Adjectives used as adverbs

Some short adjectives work as adverbs in the masculine singular: hablar claro (speak clearly), trabajar duro (work hard), ir rápido (go fast). They don't agree: Ellas hablan claro.

Turning Adjectives into Adverbs

Many adverbs of manner are made by adding -mente (like English -ly) to the feminine singular form of an adjective.

Adjective Feminine Adverb
lento lenta lentamente — slowly
rápido rápida rápidamente — quickly
claro clara claramente — clearly
feliz feliz felizmente — happily
fácil fácil fácilmente — easily
general general generalmente — generally

Adjectives that don't have a separate feminine form (ending in -e or a consonant) just add -mente.

Accents stay

If the adjective has a written accent, the adverb keeps it: rápido → rápidamente, fácil → fácilmente.

Two in a row

When two -mente adverbs are joined by y, o, pero, only the last keeps -mente:

  • Habló clara y lentamente. — She spoke clearly and slowly.
  • Normalmente desayuno en casa. — I normally have breakfast at home.
  • Afortunadamente, nadie se hizo daño. — Fortunately, nobody was hurt.

Bueno and malo don't take -mente

buenamente, malamente for "well/badly" → use bien and mal.

Adverb Placement

With verbs: usually after

An adverb modifying a verb normally comes right after it:

  • Mi hermana conduce bien. — My sister drives well.
  • Llegamos tarde. — We arrived late.

Spanish never places an adverb between haber and the participle: He ya comido. → Ya he comido. / He comido ya.

With adjectives and adverbs: before

  • Es bastante caro. — It's quite expensive.
  • Corre muy rápido. — He runs very fast.

At the start: for emphasis or the whole sentence

Adverbs that comment on the whole sentence, and time adverbs, often go first:

  • Sinceramente, no me gusta. — Honestly, I don't like it.
  • Mañana vamos a la playa. — Tomorrow we're going to the beach.

Negatives

nunca, tampoco and nada can go before the verb (without no) or after it (with no). See Negation.

Flexible, but not between haber and the participle

Spanish word order is freer than English: Hablo muy bien español and Hablo español muy bien are both correct. The one fixed rule is that nothing goes between haber and the participle.

Adverbial Expressions

Spanish often prefers a phrase over a single -mente adverb. Useful patterns:

con + noun

  • con cuidado — carefully
  • con frecuencia — frequently
  • con calma — calmly

de manera / de forma / de modo + adjective

  • de manera clara — clearly
  • de forma rápida — quickly

Fixed expressions

Spanish English
a veces sometimes
de repente suddenly
de nuevo / otra vez again
por fin finally, at last
a menudo often
sin duda undoubtedly
a propósito on purpose / by the way
de vez en cuando from time to time
por supuesto of course
a la derecha / a la izquierda on the right / left
  • De repente se fue la luz. — Suddenly the power went off.
  • ¡Por fin es viernes! — At last it's Friday!
  • Conduce con cuidado. — Drive carefully.

Comprehensive Review 1

Choose the right adverb.

  • Vivo ___ del trabajo, tardo una hora. → lejos — I live far from work.
  • Hablas ___ bien. → muy — You speak very well.
  • Me gusta ___ esta canción. → mucho — I really like this song.
  • ___ como en casa; los domingos salimos. → Normalmente — Normally I eat at home.
  • Llegué ___ y perdí el tren. → tarde — I arrived late and missed the train.

Comprehensive Review 2

Form the adverb and use it.

  • tranquilo → tranquilamente · Duerme tranquilamente. — He sleeps peacefully.
  • perfecto → perfectamente · Lo entiendo perfectamente. — I understand it perfectly.
  • amable → amablemente · Nos atendió amablemente. — She served us kindly.
  • rápido + fácil → rápida y fácilmente — quickly and easily

Spot the mistake

  • Es mucho interesante. → Es muy interesante. — It's very interesting.
  • Cocina buenamente. → Cocina bien. — He cooks well.
  • lentomente → lentamente — slowly

Direct Object Pronouns

Me, te, lo, la, nos, os, los and las — what direct objects are, how the pronouns replace them, and where they go in the sentence.

Me, Te

The direct object is the person or thing that directly receives the action of the verb: in I see Ana, Ana is the direct object. A direct object pronoun replaces it: I see her.

Singular Plural
1st person me — me nos — us
2nd person te — you (informal) os — you all (informal, Spain)
3rd person lo — him, it (m.), you (formal m.) / la — her, it (f.), you (formal f.) los — them (m.), you all / las — them (f.), you all (f.)

Me and te are the easiest: they refer to the speaker and to the person you talk to informally.

  • Mi madre me llama todos los días. — My mother calls me every day.
  • Te quiero. — I love you.
  • ¿Me ves? — Can you see me?
  • Te invito a cenar. — I'm inviting you to dinner (my treat).

Position: before the conjugated verb

Direct object pronouns go in front of a conjugated verb, and after no in negative sentences:

  • Te escucho. — I'm listening to you.
  • No te entiendo. — I don't understand you.

With an infinitive or a present participle, the pronoun may also be attached to the end:

  • Quiero verte. = Te quiero ver. — I want to see you.
  • Está llamándome. = Me está llamando. — He's calling me.

Not after the conjugated verb

Veo te. → Te veo. — I see you. Only infinitives, participles and affirmative commands take pronouns on the end.

Lo, La

For third-person singular objects, choose by gender: lo for masculine, la for feminine. They can mean him/her, it, or formal you.

  • —¿Dónde está el libro? —Lo tengo yo. — Where's the book? — I have it.
  • —¿Conoces a Pablo? —Sí, lo conozco. — Do you know Pablo? — Yes, I know him.
  • Compré una chaqueta y la llevo hoy. — I bought a jacket and I'm wearing it today.
  • Señora, la llamo mañana. — Madam, I'll call you tomorrow.

Personal a disappears

When a direct object is a specific person, Spanish puts a in front of it (the personal a). When you replace it with a pronoun, the a goes too:

  • Veo a Lucía. → La veo. — I see Lucía. → I see her.

Lo for ideas

Lo also replaces a whole idea or clause, like English it or so:

  • —¿Sabes que Juan se casa? —Sí, lo sé. — Do you know Juan is getting married? — Yes, I know.
  • No lo creo. — I don't think so.

Le for "him" in Spain

In much of Spain you'll hear le instead of lo for a male person: Le veo (leísmo). It is accepted for men, but lo is used everywhere and is always correct.

Nos, Os

Nos means us. Os means you all (informal) and is only used in Spain, matching vosotros.

  • El profesor nos ayuda mucho. — The teacher helps us a lot.
  • ¿Nos llevas a la estación? — Will you take us to the station?
  • Os espero en la puerta. — I'll wait for you guys at the door. (Spain)
  • ¿Quién os invitó? — Who invited you? (Spain)

In Latin America, and in formal speech in Spain, "you all" as an object is los or las (matching ustedes):

  • Los espero en la puerta. — I'll wait for you all at the door. (Latin America)

Attached forms

  • Van a visitarnos. = Nos van a visitar. — They're going to visit us.
  • ¡Ayúdanos! — Help us!

Nos with verbs ending in -mos

When nos attaches to a nosotros command, the final -s drops: sentémosnos → sentémonos — let's sit down.

Los, Las

For third-person plural objects, use los (masculine or mixed) and las (feminine only). They mean them or formal you all.

  • —¿Tienes las entradas? —Sí, las tengo. — Do you have the tickets? — Yes, I have them.
  • Estos zapatos me gustan; los compro. — I like these shoes; I'll buy them.
  • Invité a Carlos y a Marta; los veo el sábado. — I invited Carlos and Marta; I'll see them on Saturday. (mixed → los)
  • Señoras, las acompaño. — Ladies, I'll go with you.

Finding the right pronoun

Ask: who or what receives the action? Then check gender and number.

  • Leo los periódicos. → Los leo. — I read them.
  • Lavo las tazas. → Las lavo. — I wash them.
  • Llamamos a los abuelos. → Los llamamos. — We call them.

Same as the article

lo, la, los, las look like the articles el, la, los, las. If you know the noun's article, you nearly know its pronoun — only el becomes lo.

Comprehensive Review 1

Replace the direct object with a pronoun.

  • Compro el pan. → Lo compro. — I buy it.
  • Escribimos la carta. → La escribimos. — We write it.
  • Ves a tus primos. → Los ves. — You see them.
  • Necesito las gafas. → Las necesito. — I need them.
  • Conozco a tu madre. → La conozco. — I know her.
  • ¿Ayudas a mí y a mi hermano? → ¿Nos ayudas? — Will you help us?

Comprehensive Review 2

Focus: placement with infinitives, participles and negatives.

  • Voy a comprar el regalo. → Lo voy a comprar. / Voy a comprarlo. — I'm going to buy it.
  • Estoy preparando la comida. → La estoy preparando. / Estoy preparándola. — I'm making it.
  • No veo a los niños. → No los veo. — I can't see them.
  • Tengo que llamar (a ti). → Tengo que llamarte. / Te tengo que llamar. — I have to call you.

Spot the mistake

  • Lo veo a la chica. → La veo. — I see her.
  • No sé lo. → No lo sé. — I don't know.
  • Quiero lo. → Lo quiero. / Quiero tenerlo. — I want it.

Ser and Estar

Both mean "to be". The short version:

ser estar
what something is: identity, characteristics, origin, profession how or where something is: location, condition, state
time, dates, events progressive (estar + gerund)
Es simpático. — He's nice. Está cansado. — He's tired.
  • Soy de Perú pero estoy en Canadá. — I'm from Peru but I'm in Canada.
  • La boda es en la playa. — The wedding is on the beach (event → ser).
  • El hotel está en la playa. — The hotel is on the beach (location → estar).
  • Es aburrido. — He's boring. / Está aburrido. — He's bored.

Event test

If you can replace "is" with "takes place", use ser: El concierto es a las ocho.

For a full treatment, see More Ser vs. Estar.

Tener and Ser

English uses "to be" in many expressions where Spanish uses tener. Don't translate "be" with ser in these:

English Spanish Wrong
to be 20 years old tener 20 años ser 20 años
to be hungry / thirsty tener hambre / sed ser hambriento
to be cold / hot (person) tener frío / calor ser frío
to be afraid tener miedo
to be right tener razón ser correcto
to be lucky tener suerte ser suertudo (colloquial)
to be in a hurry tener prisa
  • Tengo frío. — I'm cold. / Soy frío. — I'm a cold (unfriendly) person.
  • Mi abuela tiene 90 años. — My grandmother is 90.
  • Tienes razón. — You're right.

When ser is right

For characteristics, ser is correct: Es joven (he's young), Es una persona miedosa (she's a fearful person).

Muy vs. mucho

With tener expressions, use mucho/a: Tengo mucho frío. With ser + adjective, use muy: Es muy frío.

See Tener Expressions for more.

Saber and Conocer

Both mean "to know".

saber conocer
facts, information, answers people (with personal a)
how to do something (+ infinitive) places, works, being familiar
  • Sé dónde vive. — I know where she lives.
  • Conozco a su hermana. — I know her sister.
  • ¿Sabes conducir? — Can you drive?
  • ¿Conoces Roma? — Have you been to Rome?

In the preterite: supe = I found out; conocí = I met.

Clause after?

If "know" is followed by que, dónde, cuándo, si, por qué or an infinitive, use saber.

See Saber vs. Conocer for more.

Traer and Llevar

Both can translate as "to bring" or "to take". The choice depends on the direction relative to the speaker.

traer llevar
bring here, towards where the speaker is take there, away from the speaker
  • Trae tu guitarra a mi casa. — Bring your guitar to my house (I'm speaking from my house, or thinking of it as "here").
  • Voy a llevar vino a la fiesta. — I'm going to take wine to the party.
  • ¿Me traes un vaso de agua? — Will you bring me a glass of water?
  • Lleva a los niños al colegio. — She takes the children to school.

Other meanings of llevar

Llevar also means "to wear" (Lleva gafas — He wears glasses), "to carry" and, with time, "to have been": Llevo dos años aquí. — I've been here two years.

Conjugation reminder

traer is irregular: traigo (present yo), traje, trajo, trajeron (preterite).

Bring vs. take don't map exactly

English often says "bring" where Spanish needs llevar: "I'll bring a cake to your party" → Llevaré un pastel a tu fiesta (unless you're already at the party place).

Ir and Irse

Ir means "to go" somewhere; irse means "to leave", "to go away", focusing on departure.

ir irse
to go (to a destination) to leave, go away
Voy al supermercado. Me voy. — I'm off.
  • Vamos a la playa. — We're going to the beach.
  • ¿Ya te vas? — Are you leaving already?
  • Se fueron sin despedirse. — They left without saying goodbye.
  • Me voy a Chile. — I'm going off to Chile (leaving here).

With destinations

Irse a + place is possible, but it stresses leaving the current place: Se fue a vivir a México. — She went off to live in Mexico.

Commands

  • Ve a tu habitación. — Go to your room.
  • ¡Vete! — Go away!
  • ¡Vámonos! — Let's go (let's leave)!
  • No te vayas. — Don't go (don't leave).

Other -se pairs

The pattern repeats: dormir (to sleep) / dormirse (to fall asleep), quedar / quedarse (to stay), llevar / llevarse (to take away).

Pedir and Preguntar

Both mean "to ask", but:

pedir preguntar
to ask for something, to request, order to ask a question, ask for information
  • Pedí un café. — I ordered a coffee.
  • Le pido ayuda a mi hermano. — I ask my brother for help.
  • Te quiero pedir un favor. — I want to ask you a favour.
  • Le pregunté la hora. — I asked him the time.
  • Pregúntale dónde vive. — Ask her where she lives.
  • Me preguntó si tenía hambre. — He asked me if I was hungry.

Useful structures

  • preguntar por + person = to ask after someone: Tu madre preguntó por ti.
  • hacer una pregunta = to ask a question: ¿Puedo hacerte una pregunta?
  • pedir is stem-changing (e → i): pido, pides, pide; preterite pidió, pidieron.

Don't add "for"

Pedir already contains "for": pedir por un café → pedir un café.

Jugar and Tocar

Both translate "to play":

jugar tocar
games and sports musical instruments, music
  • Juego al fútbol los sábados. — I play football on Saturdays.
  • Los niños juegan en el parque. — The children are playing in the park.
  • ¿Jugamos a las cartas? — Shall we play cards?
  • Mi hermana toca el violín. — My sister plays the violin.
  • La banda tocó tres canciones. — The band played three songs.

Grammar notes

  • Jugar changes u → ue: juego, juegas, juega, jugamos, jugáis, juegan.
  • With sports and games, jugar a + article is standard in Spain (jugar al tenis); in much of Latin America jugar tenis without a is also common.
  • Tocar has other meanings: "to touch" (No toques eso) and "to be someone's turn" (Te toca a ti).

Other "play" words

Poner a song or film: Pon música. — Put some music on. Hacer de / interpretar a role: Hace de malo en la película. — He plays the villain in the film.

Instrument = tocar

If it makes music, tocar. If it has rules and a winner, jugar.

Trabajar and Funcionar

Both translate "to work":

trabajar funcionar
people doing a job or making an effort machines, devices, plans that operate
  • Trabajo en un hospital. — I work in a hospital.
  • Trabajamos mucho este año. — We worked hard this year.
  • Mi móvil no funciona. — My phone doesn't work.
  • El ascensor funciona otra vez. — The lift is working again.
  • Tu idea funcionó. — Your idea worked.

Other related words

  • estar roto / estropeado / descompuesto — to be broken (not working)
  • servir — to be useful, do the job: Este cuchillo no sirve. — This knife is useless.
  • salir bien — to work out: Todo salió bien. — Everything worked out.

Machines never trabajan

El coche no trabaja → El coche no funciona. Machines never trabajan.

Intentar, Probar, and Tratar De

All three can mean "to try", but with different nuances.

Verb Meaning Followed by
intentar to try, attempt (to do something) infinitive or noun
tratar de to try (to do something); a little more effortful infinitive
probar to try out, taste, try on, test noun
probarse to try on (clothes) noun
  • Intenté abrir la puerta. — I tried to open the door.
  • Trata de llegar a tiempo. — Try to arrive on time.
  • ¿Has probado la paella? — Have you tried paella?
  • Prueba esta crema. — Try this cream.
  • Me probé los zapatos. — I tried on the shoes.

Probar a + infinitive

In Spain, probar a + infinitive means "to try doing" (experiment): Prueba a reiniciar el ordenador. — Try restarting the computer.

Tratar without de

Tratar alone means "to treat" or "to deal with": Me trata bien. El libro trata de la guerra means "The book is about the war".

Food and clothes = probar

Intenté la sopa → Probé la sopa.

Faltar, Fallar, and Extrañar

These three are often confused because they can all relate to "missing" something.

Verb Meaning Pattern
faltar to be missing, lacking; to miss (an event, class) like gustar or with a
fallar to fail, let down; to miss (a target) normal verb
extrañar / echar de menos to miss (someone or something you love) normal verb
  • Me falta un botón. — I'm missing a button.
  • Faltan dos semanas para Navidad. — It's two weeks till Christmas.
  • Faltó a clase ayer. — He missed class yesterday.
  • Falló el penalti. — He missed the penalty.
  • Me fallaste. — You let me down.
  • Te extraño. / Te echo de menos. — I miss you.

Echar de menos is more common in Spain; extrañar in Latin America.

Missing a person

Me falta mi madre can sound like she's missing or has died. For emotional missing, say Extraño a mi madre or Echo de menos a mi madre.

Mover, Mudarse, and Trasladar

All relate to "moving", but:

Verb Meaning
mover to move an object or a part of the body
moverse to move oneself, get moving
mudarse to move house, move to a new home
trasladar / trasladarse to transfer, relocate (often for work)
  • ¿Me ayudas a mover el sofá? — Will you help me move the sofa?
  • No te muevas. — Don't move.
  • Nos mudamos a Valencia el mes pasado. — We moved to Valencia last month.
  • La empresa me trasladó a Bogotá. — The company transferred me to Bogotá.
  • Se trasladaron a la nueva oficina. — They relocated to the new office.

Conjugation

Mover changes o → ue (muevo, mueves). Mudarse is regular (me mudo, te mudas).

House = mudarse

Whenever "move" means changing where you live, it's mudarse: Me moví a Madrid → Me mudé a Madrid.

Comprehensive Review 1

Choose the right verb:

  • Mi padre ___ (es / está) médico. → es
  • Hoy ___ (soy / tengo) mucho sueño. → tengo
  • ¿___ (sabes / conoces) a mi novio? → Conoces
  • ¿Me ___ (traes / llevas) el periódico? → traes
  • Bueno, ___ (voy / me voy). Hasta mañana. → me voy
  • Le ___ (pedí / pregunté) dinero a mi madre. → pedí
  • ¿___ (juegas / tocas) el piano? → Tocas

Comprehensive Review 2

Choose the right verb:

  • El microondas no ___ (trabaja / funciona). → funciona
  • ___ (intenté / probé) el pastel y estaba rico. → Probé
  • Los niños ___ (juegan / tocan) al escondite. → juegan
  • ___ (extraño / me falta) a mis amigos. → Extraño
  • El jugador ___ (faltó / falló) el gol. → falló
  • Vamos a ___ (mover / mudarnos) a un piso más grande. → mudarnos
  • ¿Puedo ___ (preguntarte / pedirte) algo? ¿Dónde naciste? → preguntarte

Think about the meaning, not the English

Every pair here shares one English word. Ask what kind of thing follows (person, object, question, place) and the choice usually becomes obvious.

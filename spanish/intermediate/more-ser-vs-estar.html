More Ser vs. Estar

Conjugation Review

Both verbs mean "to be", and both are irregular. Here they are in the tenses you know so far.

Subject ser present estar present ser preterite estar preterite ser imperfect estar imperfect
yo soy estoy fui estuve era estaba
tú eres estás fuiste estuviste eras estabas
él / ella / usted es está fue estuvo era estaba
nosotros somos estamos fuimos estuvimos éramos estábamos
vosotros sois estáis fuisteis estuvisteis erais estabais
ellos / ustedes son están fueron estuvieron eran estaban

Future and conditional are regular: seré, sería; estaré, estaría.

A quick rule of thumb

Ser says what something is (identity, essence). Estar says how or where something is (state, location). The lessons below break this into specific uses.

Notice the accents in estar: estás, está, están are stressed on the last syllable. Without the accent, esta means "this".

  • Somos hermanos. — We're siblings.
  • Estamos en casa. — We're at home.
  • Fue un viaje increíble. — It was an incredible trip.
  • Estuvimos dos semanas en Cuba. — We were in Cuba for two weeks.

Remember that fui, fue, fueron are shared by ser and ir: Fue a la tienda (he went) vs. Fue difícil (it was).

Using Ser with Descriptions

Use ser for characteristics that define a person or thing: personality, physical appearance, nationality, profession, material, colour, shape.

  • Mi hermano es alto y moreno. — My brother is tall and dark-haired.
  • Eres muy paciente. — You are very patient.
  • La mesa es de madera. — The table is made of wood.
  • Son médicos. — They're doctors.
  • El coche es rojo. — The car is red.

Identity and definitions

Ser links two nouns or pronouns that refer to the same thing:

  • Madrid es la capital de España. — Madrid is the capital of Spain.
  • Esto es un regalo para ti. — This is a present for you.
  • Soy yo. — It's me.

Possession and relationships

  • El libro es de Carlos. — The book is Carlos's.
  • Ella es mi novia. — She's my girlfriend.

Description vs. condition

Es aburrido — He's boring (his nature). Está aburrido — He's bored (right now). The adjective is the same; the verb changes the meaning.

Using Ser with Origins

Ser de tells you where someone or something comes from, or what it is made of.

  • Soy de Colombia. — I'm from Colombia.
  • ¿De dónde eres? — Where are you from?
  • Este café es de Etiopía. — This coffee is from Ethiopia.
  • La camisa es de algodón. — The shirt is made of cotton.

Nationality adjectives also use ser: Somos mexicanos.

Origin vs. location

Origin doesn't change even when you move, so it takes ser. Where you are right now takes estar:

  • Soy de Chile, pero estoy en Canadá. — I'm from Chile, but I'm in Canada.
  • Mis padres son de Galicia y ahora están en Madrid. — My parents are from Galicia and now they're in Madrid.

Ser de for possession too

De after ser can also show ownership: La casa es de mis abuelos. Context makes clear whether it's origin, material or owner.

Using Ser with Time

Times, dates, days and seasons all take ser.

Clock time

Use es for one o'clock and son for the other hours. In the past, use the imperfect era / eran.

  • Es la una y media. — It's half past one.
  • Son las cuatro menos cuarto. — It's quarter to four.
  • Eran las diez cuando llegamos. — It was ten when we arrived.
  • ¿Qué hora es? — What time is it?

Dates, days and seasons

  • Hoy es martes. — Today is Tuesday.
  • Es el 5 de mayo. — It's the fifth of May.
  • Era invierno. — It was winter.

Where and when an event takes place

An event "is" somewhere or at some time with ser, not estar:

  • La fiesta es en mi casa. — The party is at my house.
  • El concierto es a las nueve. — The concert is at nine.

Estar with dates

Estamos a + date is a common alternative for today's date: Estamos a 3 de marzo. Estamos en + season or month also works: Estamos en verano.

Using Ser for Permanence

Use ser for qualities seen as lasting or essential, estar for states seen as temporary. This is about how the speaker sees the quality, not about real permanence.

  • Mi abuela es muy activa. — My grandmother is very active (that's her nature).
  • La nieve es blanca. — Snow is white.
  • Es muy inteligente. — She's very intelligent.

Exceptions to "permanent"

Some lasting states still use estar:

  • Mi abuelo está muerto. — My grandfather is dead.
  • Están casados. — They're married. (Son casados also exists in some regions.)

And some changeable things use ser:

  • Es joven. — He's young.
  • Es rico. — She's rich (a characteristic).

Adjectives that change meaning

Adjective with ser with estar
listo clever ready
rico rich tasty
malo bad (person) ill
verde green unripe
vivo lively, sharp alive
orgulloso arrogant proud (of something)
  • La fruta es verde. — The fruit is green. La fruta está verde. — The fruit is unripe.

Using Ser in the Passive

The passive voice uses ser + past participle (+ por + agent). The participle agrees with the subject.

  • La novela fue escrita por García Márquez. — The novel was written by García Márquez.
  • Los ladrones fueron detenidos. — The thieves were arrested.
  • El puente será construido el año que viene. — The bridge will be built next year.

The participle ends in -o, -a, -os, -as: La casa fue vendida, Los coches fueron reparados.

Ser vs. estar with participles

Ser + participle describes the action; estar + participle describes the resulting state.

  • La puerta fue cerrada a las diez. — The door was closed (by someone) at ten.
  • La puerta estaba cerrada. — The door was closed (it was in a closed state).
  • El restaurante está abierto. — The restaurant is open.

The passive is formal

In everyday Spanish the ser passive is less common than in English. Speakers prefer the se construction (Se vendió la casa) or an active sentence (Vendieron la casa).

Using Estar with Locations

Estar tells you where a person, object or place is.

  • ¿Dónde están mis gafas? — Where are my glasses?
  • El museo está en el centro. — The museum is in the centre.
  • Estamos cerca de la playa. — We're near the beach.
  • Lima está en Perú. — Lima is in Peru.

Even permanent locations use estar: cities and buildings don't move, but location is still a matter of estar.

The exception: events

When the subject is an event (party, meeting, concert, wedding, class), use ser for where it takes place:

  • La reunión es en la sala 3. — The meeting is in room 3.
  • La boda fue en una iglesia antigua. — The wedding was in an old church.

Hay vs. estar

Hay introduces something new (often with un, una or no article); estar locates something already known (with el, la):

  • Hay un banco en la esquina. — There's a bank on the corner.
  • El banco está en la esquina. — The bank is on the corner.

Parties are not places

La fiesta está en casa de Ana → La fiesta es en casa de Ana. Events "take place" with ser.

Using Estar with Conditions

Use estar for physical and emotional conditions: health, mood, how something looks, feels or tastes at a given moment.

  • Estoy cansado. — I'm tired.
  • ¿Estás bien? — Are you OK?
  • Mi madre está enferma. — My mother is ill.
  • Estamos muy contentos. — We're very happy.
  • La sopa está fría. — The soup is cold.

Food and how things seem

Estar + adjective often means "tastes" or "looks" right now:

  • ¡Esta paella está buenísima! — This paella is (tastes) delicious!
  • Estás muy guapa hoy. — You look very pretty today.

Compare: La paella es un plato típico (what it is) vs. La paella está rica (how it tastes).

Fixed expressions with estar

estar de acuerdo (to agree), estar de vacaciones (to be on holiday), estar de pie (to be standing), estar de buen / mal humor (to be in a good / bad mood), estar a punto de (to be about to).

Feliz vs. contento

Feliz is usually used with ser (Es una persona feliz), though estar feliz is common in Latin America. Contento always takes estar.

Using Estar for Transience

Estar expresses a state that is the result of a change or that may change again. Often it means "has become" or "is currently".

  • Has crecido mucho, estás altísimo. — You've grown a lot, you're so tall now.
  • Está muy delgada. — She's (become) very thin.
  • El cielo está nublado. — The sky is cloudy.
  • La tienda está cerrada. — The shop is closed.

Same adjective, different message

  • Es nervioso. — He's a nervous person. / Está nervioso. — He's nervous (right now).
  • Es callado. — He's a quiet person. / Está callado. — He's being quiet.
  • Eres guapo. — You're handsome. / Estás guapo. — You look handsome today.

Jobs held temporarily

Estar de + job describes a temporary role: Está de camarero este verano. — He's working as a waiter this summer. Compare Es camarero — He's a waiter (his profession).

Speaker's point of view

The same fact can take either verb, depending on whether you see it as a defining trait or a current state. That is why native speakers sometimes disagree.

Using Estar in the Progressive

The progressive (continuous) tenses are formed with estar + gerund (-ando / -iendo). They describe actions in progress.

Infinitive Gerund
hablar hablando
comer comiendo
escribir escribiendo
leer leyendo
dormir durmiendo
pedir pidiendo
  • Estoy estudiando. — I'm studying.
  • ¿Qué estás haciendo? — What are you doing?
  • Estaban durmiendo cuando sonó el teléfono. — They were sleeping when the phone rang.
  • Estaremos viajando todo el mes. — We'll be travelling all month.

Not like English

Spanish uses the progressive only for actions actually in progress. For the future or for general facts, use the simple present:

  • Estoy saliendo mañana. → Salgo mañana. — I'm leaving tomorrow.
  • Vivo en Madrid. — I live / I'm living in Madrid.

Never ser + gerund

Es lloviendo → Está lloviendo. The progressive always uses estar.

description and identity location of people and things

  • Mi padre ___ ingeniero. → es
  • El examen ___ mañana a las diez. → es
  • Hoy ___ muy cansados. → estamos
  • ¿De dónde ___ tus abuelos? → son
  • El supermercado ___ al lado del banco. → está
  • Los niños ___ jugando en el jardín. → están
  • ___ las seis de la tarde. → Son

Comprehensive Review 2

Choose the verb and explain the meaning:

  • El pescado ___ malo. → está (has gone off) / es (is bad for you, bad quality)
  • Mi hermana ___ lista. → es (clever) / está (ready)
  • La carta ___ escrita por Ana. → fue (passive action)
  • La carta ___ escrita en inglés. → está (resulting state)
  • El concierto ___ en el estadio. → es (event)
  • Mis llaves ___ en el coche. → están (location)

Translate:

  • The coffee is from Colombia and it's very hot. → El café es de Colombia y está muy caliente.
  • We were in Peru when it was summer. → Estábamos en Perú cuando era verano.
  • She's working as a guide this year. → Está de guía este año.

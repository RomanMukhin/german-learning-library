Reflexive Verbs and Pronouns 1

A reflexive verb describes an action that the subject does to itself: Me lavo — I wash (myself). In dictionaries, reflexive verbs end in -se: lavarse, levantarse, ducharse.

The pronoun must match the subject:

Subject Pronoun lavarse
yo me me lavo
tú te te lavas
él / ella / usted se se lava
nosotros nos nos lavamos
vosotros os os laváis
ellos / ustedes se se lavan

English often leaves out "myself" or uses "get": Me visto — I get dressed.

Daily routine

  • Me despierto a las siete. — I wake up at seven.
  • Me levanto, me ducho y me visto. — I get up, have a shower and get dressed.
  • Mi hermano se afeita. — My brother shaves.
  • Nos acostamos tarde. — We go to bed late.
  • ¿A qué hora te duermes? — What time do you fall asleep?

Reflexive vs. non-reflexive

The same verb without the pronoun means the action is done to someone else:

  • Lavo el coche. — I wash the car.
  • Me lavo. — I wash myself.
  • Acuesto a los niños. — I put the children to bed.
  • Me acuesto. — I go to bed.

Body parts take the article

Me lavo las manos (I wash my hands), Se cepilla los dientes (She brushes her teeth). The reflexive pronoun already shows whose hands they are.

Reflexive Verbs and Pronouns 2

Placement

Reflexive pronouns follow the same rules as other object pronouns:

  1. Before a conjugated verb: Me ducho. No se levantó.
  2. Attached to an infinitive or before the conjugated verb that comes first: Voy a ducharme = Me voy a duchar.
  3. Attached to a gerund or before estar: Estoy vistiéndome = Me estoy vistiendo.
  4. Attached to affirmative commands: ¡Siéntate!, ¡Levántense!
  5. Before negative commands: ¡No te sientes!

When you attach a pronoun to a gerund or command, add an accent to keep the stress: vistiéndome, siéntate.

Reflexive with other tenses

  • Ayer me levanté a las seis. — Yesterday I got up at six.
  • De niño, me acostaba muy pronto. — As a child I went to bed very early.
  • Mañana nos quedaremos en casa. — Tomorrow we'll stay at home.

Other common reflexive verbs

sentarse (to sit down), quedarse (to stay), llamarse (to be called), ponerse (to put on), quitarse (to take off), maquillarse (to put on make-up), peinarse (to comb one's hair), irse (to leave).

  • ¿Cómo te llamas? — What's your name?
  • Me pongo el abrigo. — I put on my coat.

One pronoun per verb

Me voy a levantarme → Me voy a levantar or Voy a levantarme. Not both.

Tricky Verbs

Some verbs change their meaning when they become reflexive.

Plain Meaning Reflexive Meaning
ir to go irse to leave, go away
dormir to sleep dormirse to fall asleep
llamar to call llamarse to be called
poner to put ponerse to put on (clothes); to become
quedar to arrange to meet; to be left quedarse to stay
parecer to seem parecerse a to look like, resemble
llevar to carry, wear llevarse to take away; to get along
negar to deny negarse a to refuse to
acordar to agree acordarse de to remember
despedir to fire despedirse to say goodbye
  • Me voy. — I'm leaving.
  • Me dormí en el sofá. — I fell asleep on the sofa.
  • Te pareces a tu madre. — You look like your mother.
  • Me llevo bien con mi jefe. — I get on well with my boss.
  • ¿Te acuerdas de mí? — Do you remember me?

Some verbs are always reflexive

quejarse (to complain), atreverse (to dare), arrepentirse (to regret), suicidarse, jactarse (to boast).

Comer vs. comerse

The reflexive can also add emphasis or completeness: Comí pizza (I ate pizza) vs. Me comí toda la pizza (I ate up the whole pizza).

Emotions

Many verbs that express emotions are reflexive. They describe the subject getting or becoming a certain way.

Verb Meaning
alegrarse (de) to be glad, cheer up
enfadarse / enojarse to get angry
preocuparse (por) to worry
aburrirse to get bored
asustarse to get scared
sorprenderse to be surprised
enamorarse (de) to fall in love (with)
emocionarse to get excited / moved
relajarse to relax
ofenderse to take offence
calmarse to calm down
  • Me alegro de verte. — I'm glad to see you.
  • Mi padre se enfada si llego tarde. — My father gets angry if I'm late.
  • No te preocupes. — Don't worry.
  • Se enamoraron en París. — They fell in love in Paris.

Without the pronoun, someone else causes the feeling

  • Me preocupo. — I worry. / Me preocupa el examen. — The exam worries me (a gustar-style verb; see Verbs Like Gustar).
  • Te aburres. — You get bored. / La clase te aburre. — The class bores you.

Preposition after the verb

Many emotion verbs need a preposition: alegrarse de, enamorarse de, preocuparse por, quejarse de.

Talking About Changes

English uses "to become" or "to get" for many changes. Spanish has several reflexive verbs, each with a nuance.

Verb Used for Example
ponerse + adjective sudden, temporary change in mood or appearance Se puso rojo. — He went red.
volverse + adjective sudden, lasting change in character Se volvió loco. — He went mad.
hacerse + noun / adjective gradual change through effort or choice Se hizo médica. — She became a doctor.
convertirse en + noun transformation into something else El agua se convirtió en hielo. — The water turned into ice.
quedarse + adjective result of an event, often a loss Se quedó ciego. — He went blind.
llegar a ser + noun change after a long process Llegó a ser presidente. — He went on to become president.
  • Me puse nervioso antes del examen. — I got nervous before the exam.
  • Con los años, se volvió muy desconfiado. — Over the years he became very distrustful.
  • Se hicieron ricos. — They became rich.
  • Nos quedamos sin palabras. — We were left speechless.

Effort or event?

Hacerse = you did something to get there. Quedarse = something happened to you. Ponerse = it'll pass.

Comprehensive Review

Topic Key point
pronouns me, te, se, nos, os, se match the subject
placement before conjugated verbs; attached to infinitives, gerunds, affirmative commands
meaning changes ir / irse, dormir / dormirse, parecer / parecerse
emotions alegrarse, enfadarse, preocuparse
changes ponerse, volverse, hacerse, convertirse en, quedarse

Fill in:

  • ¿A qué hora ___ (levantarse, tú)? → te levantas
  • Ayer los niños ___ (dormirse) temprano. → se durmieron
  • Estamos ___ (prepararse). → preparándonos
  • Mi tía ___ (ponerse) muy triste. → se puso
  • ¡No ___ (irse, tú)! → te vayas

Extra Practice 1

Focus: daily routine.

  • Yo ___ (ducharse) por la mañana. → me ducho
  • Ellas ___ (maquillarse). → se maquillan
  • Nosotros ___ (vestirse) rápido. → nos vestimos
  • ¿Tú ___ (afeitarse) todos los días? → te afeitas
  • Usted ___ (sentarse) aquí. → se sienta

Extra Practice 2

Focus: reflexive or not?

  • (Llamar / llamarse) a mi madre cada día. → Llamo
  • Mi gato (llamar / llamarse) Simba. → se llama
  • (Ir / irse), que es tarde. → Me voy
  • (Poner / ponerse) la mesa. → Pongo
  • (Poner / ponerse) los zapatos. → Me pongo

Extra Practice 3

Focus: emotions and changes.

  • ___ (alegrarse, yo) de tu éxito. → Me alegro
  • Cuando vio el ratón, ___ (asustarse, ella). → se asustó
  • El príncipe ___ (convertirse) en rana. → se convirtió
  • Después del accidente ___ (quedarse) paralítico. → se quedó
  • Con mucho trabajo, ___ (hacerse) famosa. → se hizo

Convertirse en

Se convirtió a rana → Se convirtió en rana. Convertirse always takes en.

Direct Object Pronouns

The direct object is the person or thing that receives the action directly: I see the car, I call my mother. A direct object pronoun replaces it: "I see it", "I call her".

Singular Plural
1st me — me nos — us
2nd te — you os — you (Spain)
3rd masc. / usted (m.) lo — him, it, you los — them, you
3rd fem. / usted (f.) la — her, it, you las — them, you

Third-person pronouns agree with the noun they replace:

  • ¿Tienes el libro? — Sí, lo tengo. — Do you have the book? — Yes, I have it.
  • ¿Ves a María? — No, no la veo. — Do you see María? — No, I don't see her.
  • Compré las entradas. Las compré ayer. — I bought the tickets. I bought them yesterday.
  • Te quiero. — I love you.
  • ¿Nos llamas mañana? — Will you call us tomorrow?

To find the direct object, ask "what?" or "whom?" after the verb: ¿Qué tengo? → el libro → lo.

Leísmo

In much of Spain, le is used instead of lo for a male person: Le vi ayer (I saw him yesterday). This is accepted for people, but for things always use lo: Lo vi (I saw it).

Lo for ideas

Neuter lo replaces a whole idea or sentence: ¿Sabes que se casa? — Sí, lo sé. — Yes, I know (it).

Direct Object Pronoun Placement

1. Before a conjugated verb

  • Lo leo. — I read it.
  • No la conozco. — I don't know her.
  • Los hemos visto. — We've seen them.

2. With an infinitive or gerund: two options

Either before the conjugated verb or attached to the end of the infinitive or gerund:

  • La voy a llamar. = Voy a llamarla. — I'm going to call her.
  • Lo estoy haciendo. = Estoy haciéndolo. — I'm doing it.
  • Te quiero ver. = Quiero verte. — I want to see you.

Attaching to a gerund requires an accent: haciéndolo, comiéndola.

3. With commands

Attached to affirmative commands, before negative ones:

  • Cómpralo. — Buy it. / No lo compres. — Don't buy it.
  • Llámame. — Call me. / No me llames. — Don't call me.

Never in the middle

Voy lo a comprar → Lo voy a comprar or Voy a comprarlo.

Personal a doesn't disappear with nouns

Veo a Juan → Lo veo. The a belongs to the noun; when the pronoun replaces the noun, it goes.

Indirect Object Pronouns

The indirect object is the person who receives the direct object or benefits from the action: I give my sister a present. Ask "to whom?" or "for whom?".

Singular Plural
1st me — to me nos — to us
2nd te — to you os — to you (Spain)
3rd / usted(es) le — to him, her, you les — to them, you

Only the third person differs from the direct object pronouns, and le / les doesn't show gender.

  • Le doy un regalo a mi hermana. — I give my sister a present.
  • ¿Me dices la verdad? — Will you tell me the truth?
  • Les escribí una carta. — I wrote them a letter.
  • Nos enseñan español. — They teach us Spanish.

Doubling with a + person

Spanish usually keeps the indirect object pronoun even when the person is named, and you can add a + pronoun for clarity or emphasis:

  • Le compré flores a mi madre. — I bought my mother flowers.
  • Le dije a él, no a ella. — I told him, not her.
  • A mí me encanta. — I love it.

Don't drop le

Di el libro a Pedro sounds unnatural; say Le di el libro a Pedro.

Indirect Object Pronoun Placement

Indirect object pronouns follow exactly the same placement rules as direct ones.

Before conjugated verbs

  • Le mando un mensaje. — I send him a message.
  • No nos dijeron nada. — They didn't tell us anything.

With infinitives and gerunds

  • Te voy a contar un secreto. = Voy a contarte un secreto. — I'm going to tell you a secret.
  • Les estoy explicando el problema. = Estoy explicándoles el problema. — I'm explaining the problem to them.

With commands

  • Dime la verdad. — Tell me the truth.
  • No le digas nada. — Don't tell her anything.
  • Tráiganme la cuenta, por favor. — Bring me the bill, please.

Verbs that usually take an indirect object

dar (give), decir (tell), contar (tell, recount), enviar / mandar (send), escribir (write), explicar (explain), mostrar / enseñar (show), pedir (ask for), preguntar (ask), prestar (lend), regalar (give as a gift), traer (bring), servir (serve).

  • ¿Me prestas tu coche? — Will you lend me your car?
  • El camarero nos sirvió la comida. — The waiter served us the food.

Gustar-type verbs

Verbs like gustar, encantar, doler always use indirect object pronouns: Me duele la cabeza. See Verbs Like Gustar.

Using Pronouns Together

When a sentence has both an indirect and a direct object pronoun, the order is fixed: indirect before direct. Remember it as I.D. (Indirect, Direct), or "people before things".

  • Me lo das. — You give it to me.
  • Te la compro. — I'll buy it for you.
  • Nos los enseñaron. — They showed them to us.

Le and les become se

When le or les comes right before lo, la, los, las, it changes to se:

  • Le lo doy → Se lo doy. — I give it to him / her / you.
  • Les las mando → Se las mando. — I send them to them.

Since se can mean many people, add a + person to clarify: Se lo doy a ella.

Placement with two pronouns

They stay together, in the same order, before the verb or attached to the end:

  • Te lo voy a explicar. = Voy a explicártelo. — I'm going to explain it to you.
  • Dámelo. — Give it to me.
  • No me lo digas. — Don't tell me (it).
  • Se la estoy preparando. = Estoy preparándosela. — I'm preparing it for her.

Note the accents when two pronouns are attached: explicártelo, dámelo, preparándosela.

With reflexive se

A reflexive se also comes first: Se lo puso (He put it on), Me la lavé (I washed it, e.g. my face).

Negatives

No goes before the whole group of pronouns: No se lo digas, never Se no lo digas.

Comprehensive Review 1

Direct Indirect
me / us me, nos me, nos
you te, os te, os
him, her, it, them, you (formal) lo, la, los, las le, les (→ se before lo/la/los/las)

Replace the direct object with a pronoun:

  • Leo el periódico. → Lo leo.
  • Busco a mis hijas. → Las busco.
  • Voy a ver la película. → La voy a ver / Voy a verla.
  • Estamos comiendo los tacos. → Los estamos comiendo / Estamos comiéndolos.
  • ¡Abre la ventana! → ¡Ábrela!

Comprehensive Review 2

Add the indirect object pronoun:

  • ___ escribo a mi abuela. → Le
  • ¿___ traes un café? (to me) → Me
  • El profesor ___ explica la lección (to us). → nos
  • ___ di las llaves a los vecinos. → Les
  • Voy a pedir___ ayuda (to you, tú). → pedirte

Comprehensive Review 3

Use two pronouns:

  • Te doy el dinero. → Te lo doy.
  • Le mando las fotos. → Se las mando.
  • Nos sirvieron la cena. → Nos la sirvieron.
  • Les voy a contar la historia. → Se la voy a contar / Voy a contársela.
  • Dame el libro. → Dámelo.
  • No le digas el secreto. → No se lo digas.

Extra Practice 1

Focus: lo, la, los, las.

  • ¿Conoces a Pablo? — Sí, ___ conozco. → lo
  • ¿Tienes las llaves? — No, no ___ tengo. → las
  • ¿Dónde compraste esa chaqueta? — ___ compré en Madrid. → La
  • ¿Llamaste a tus padres? — Sí, ___ llamé. → los

Extra Practice 2

Focus: le, les and placement.

  • Quiero regalar___ un libro (to him). → regalarle
  • ¿___ explicaste el problema a ellos? → Les
  • No ___ preguntes nada (to her). → le
  • Estoy escribiéndo___ (to them). → escribiéndoles

Extra Practice 3

Focus: combining pronouns.

  • ¿Me prestas el lápiz? — Sí, te ___ presto. → lo
  • ¿Le diste la carta a Ana? — Sí, ___ ___ di. → se la
  • ¿Nos traes los platos? — Ahora ___ ___ traigo. → os los (Spain) / se los (to ustedes)
  • Cómprale el regalo. → Cómpraselo.

Never le lo

Le lo doy → Se lo doy. Two l-pronouns never stand side by side. The first always becomes se.

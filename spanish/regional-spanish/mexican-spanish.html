Mexican Spanish

The sound of Mexican Spanish, its typical grammar and the everyday words and expressions you will hear in Mexico.

Pronunciation and accent

Mexican Spanish, especially the variety of central Mexico, is the most widely heard Spanish in the world thanks to films, TV and music. Its main features:

  • seseo: c (before e, i) and z sound like /s/: cerveza /serˈbesa/
  • clear consonants: the s at the end of a syllable is fully pronounced: ¿Cómo estás?
  • reduced unstressed vowels in fast speech, especially next to s: pues can sound like ps, entonces like ntons
  • a typical sing-song intonation, with a rise at the end of many statements
  • x in many place names is pronounced like a Spanish j: México /ˈmexiko/, Oaxaca; but like /s/ in Xochimilco

Grammar features

  • ustedes is the only plural you; vosotros is not used: ¿Ustedes quieren algo? — Do you (all) want something?
  • the preterite is often used where Spain uses the present perfect: Hoy comí tacos. — I ate tacos today.
  • ir a + infinitive is very common for the future: Voy a llamarte. — I'm going to call you.
  • diminutives in -ito / -ita everywhere, even with adverbs: ahorita (right now / in a while), cerquita (very near), un cafecito
  • mande (literally "command") is the polite way to say "Pardon?" or answer when someone calls you

Ahorita

Ahorita can mean "right now" or "in a little while, maybe later". Context and tone decide. If you need something immediately, ask ¿ahorita ahorita?

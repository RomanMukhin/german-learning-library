Everybody Clean Up!

Words for chores and cleaning supplies, present-tense verbs for who does what, rooms of the house, and simple commands to get others to help.

I Clean, You Clean

Household chores are los quehaceres, las tareas domésticas or las faenas. These words are usually plural.

Spanish English
limpiar to clean
barrer el piso / el suelo to sweep the floor
pasar la aspiradora to vacuum
fregar (Spain) / trapear (LatAm) el piso to mop the floor
lavar los platos / fregar los platos to wash the dishes
lavar la ropa to do the laundry
planchar to iron
hacer la cama to make the bed
poner la mesa to set the table
quitar el polvo to dust
sacar la basura to take out the rubbish
regar las plantas to water the plants
cortar el césped to mow the lawn
ordenar to tidy

Regular -ar verbs follow one pattern in the present:

limpiar
yo limpio
tú limpias
usted, él, ella limpia
nosotros limpiamos
ustedes, ellos limpian
  • Yo lavo los platos y tú sacas la basura. — I wash the dishes and you take out the rubbish.
  • Mi padre cocina y mi madre plancha. — My dad cooks and my mum irons.
  • ¡Odio los quehaceres! — I hate chores!

Cleaning supplies

la escoba (broom), la aspiradora (vacuum cleaner), la fregona / el trapeador (mop), el trapo (cloth), el jabón (soap), la lavadora (washing machine), la secadora (dryer), el lavaplatos (dishwasher), la plancha (iron).

Two irregular yo forms

Hacer and poner have irregular yo forms: hago la cama (I make the bed), pongo la mesa (I set the table).

Rooms

Use en for "in" a room, and hay to say what's there:

  • Paso la aspiradora en el salón. — I vacuum in the living room.
  • Hay platos sucios en la cocina. — There are dirty dishes in the kitchen.
  • Mi cuarto está muy desordenado. — My room is very messy.

Useful adjectives (with estar, since they're states): limpio/a (clean), sucio/a (dirty), ordenado/a (tidy), desordenado/a (messy).

el piso has three meanings

In Spain el piso is a flat. It can also mean "floor" (the surface you sweep) and "storey" (el tercer piso, the third floor).

Please Clean Your Room!

To tell someone you call tú to do something, use the tú command. For regular verbs, it's the same as the él / ella form of the present tense:

Verb he / she… Command
limpiar limpia ¡Limpia tu cuarto! — Clean your room!
lavar lava ¡Lava los platos! — Wash the dishes!
barrer barre ¡Barre la cocina! — Sweep the kitchen!
sacar saca ¡Saca la basura! — Take out the rubbish!

A few common verbs have short, irregular commands:

Verb Command
hacer haz — ¡Haz la cama!
poner pon — ¡Pon la mesa!
ir ve — ¡Ve a tu cuarto!
venir ven — ¡Ven aquí!

Telling a group

  • Latin America (and formal in Spain): use the ustedes command, which swaps the vowel: ¡Limpien la cocina!, ¡Saquen la basura!, ¡Hagan las camas!
  • Spain, informal: replace the final -r of the infinitive with -d: ¡Limpiad la cocina!, ¡Poned la mesa!
  • Pablo, pon la mesa, por favor. Los invitados llegan pronto. — Pablo, set the table, please. The guests are arriving soon.
  • Niños, recojan sus juguetes. — Kids, pick up your toys.

Soften it

Commands can sound bossy. Add por favor, or ask a question instead: ¿Puedes sacar la basura? — "Can you take out the rubbish?"

Don't use the command for "don't"

Negative commands use a different form: ¡No toques eso! (Don't touch that!), not No toca. You'll learn these later — for now, use No…, por favor with a full sentence.

## And

*And* joins words, phrases and clauses of the same type: *bread **and** butter*, *She sang **and** danced.*

» ***go and*** / ***come and*** + verb (informal): *Go **and** get your coat.* *Come **and** see us.*
» fixed pairs, in fixed order: *fish **and** chips*, *black **and** white*, *ladies **and** gentlemen*, *salt **and** pepper*, *rock **and** roll*
» *and* + adjective / adverb for emphasis: *nice **and** warm*, *good **and** ready*, *faster **and** faster*
» in numbers (British): *two hundred **and** fifty*

With three or more items, put *and* before the last: *red, white **and** blue*.

## As if and as though

*As if* and *as though* mean the same: they compare with an imagined or likely situation.

» *It looks **as if** it's going to rain.* (likely)
» *He talks **as though** he **knew** everything.* (unreal — past form)
» *She looked at me **as if** I **were / was** mad.*

After *look, sound, seem, feel*, informal English also uses *like*: *It looks **like** it's going to rain.*

## As long as and so long as

*As long as* means "only if, on condition that": *You can borrow my car **as long as** you're careful.* *So long as* means the same and is a little more formal. Use a present tense for the future: ~~as long as you will be careful~~. *As long as* can also mean "for the whole time that": *I'll love you **as long as** I live.*

## Because, because of and cos, cos of

### Because

*Because* + clause gives a reason. It often comes after the main clause: *I stayed at home **because** I was ill.* At the start, use a comma: ***Because** I was ill, I stayed at home.* In writing, *because*-clauses shouldn't stand alone as sentences (*~~Because I was ill.~~*), though this is normal in speech as an answer.

### Because of

*Because of* + noun phrase: ***Because of** the rain, the match was cancelled.* Not: ~~because the rain~~.

### Cos

*Cos* / *'cause* is a very informal spoken form of *because*.

### Just because, simply because

***Just because** you're older, it doesn't mean you're right.* *I did it **simply because** I wanted to.*

## Before

### As a preposition and conjunction

» preposition: ***before** lunch*, ***before** 6 p.m.*, ***before** leaving*
» conjunction: *Finish your homework **before** you go out.* (present for the future) / *I had a shower **before** I went to bed.*

### Before, by, till, until

*Before* = earlier than; *by* = not later than (deadline); *until* = up to a point and then stop.

### Before as an adverb

*I've seen this film **before**.* (= at an earlier time) *the day **before*** (= the previous day). ***Beforehand*** = in advance: *Let me know **beforehand**.*

### Just before, immediately before

*He left **just before** the end.* *Before* + *-ing* needs the same subject: ***Before leaving**, she turned off the lights.*

## But

### As a linking word

*But* joins contrasting ideas: *It's cheap **but** good.* *I tried, **but** I failed.* In informal writing it can start a sentence.

### But meaning except

*Everyone **but** Tom came.* *nothing **but** trouble*, *the last **but** one* (= the second to last).

### But for and all but

***But for*** + noun = if not for: ***But for** your help, I'd have failed.* ***All but*** = almost completely: *The match was **all but** over.*

## Conjunctions

### Coordinating conjunctions

*and, but, or, nor, so, yet* (and two-part *either … or, neither … nor, both … and, not only … but also*) join elements of **equal** grammatical status: words, phrases, clauses, sentences, even prefixes (*pre- **and** post-war*).

### Subordinating conjunctions

They introduce a **subordinate clause** that depends on a main clause:

» time: *when, while, as, before, after, until, since, once, as soon as*
» reason: *because, as, since*
» contrast: *although, though, even though, whereas, while*
» condition: *if, unless, as long as, provided (that), in case*
» purpose and result: *so that, in order that, so … that, such … that*
» other: *that, whether, as if, than*

## Conjunctions: adding

### And, too, as well

» words: *tea **and** coffee*; phrases: *in the morning **and** in the evening*; clauses: *I cooked **and** she washed up.*
» ***and … too / as well***: *She plays piano **and** she sings **too**.*

### Other ways of adding

» ***as well as***, ***in addition to*** + noun / *-ing*: ***As well as** English, she speaks Arabic.* ***In addition to** teaching, he writes books.*
» sentence linkers: *also, besides, moreover, furthermore, in addition, what's more*: *The flat is cheap. **Moreover**, it's near the station.*

## Conjunctions: causes, reasons, results and purpose

### Causes, reasons and results

» reason: *because, as, since, because of, due to, owing to*: *The flight was delayed **due to** fog.*
» result: *so, so … that, such … that, therefore, as a result, consequently*: *It was late, **so** we left.* *She worked hard. **As a result**, she passed.*

### Purpose

» ***to / in order to / so as to*** + infinitive: *I went out **to** buy milk.* *He left early **so as not to** miss the train.*
» ***so that / in order that*** + clause (often with *can, could, will, would*): *I'll write it down **so that** I **don't** forget.*
» ***for*** + noun: *We went out **for** a walk.*

## Conjunctions: contrasting

### But and although / though

*But* joins two main clauses: *It was cold, **but** we went out.* *Although / though* introduce a subordinate clause, which can come first: ***Although** it was cold, we went out.* Don't use both: ~~Although it was cold, but we went out.~~

### Even though, even if

***Even though*** = although (stronger, about a fact): ***Even though** I was tired, I finished it.* ***Even if*** = whether or not (about a possibility): *I'll go **even if** it rains.*

### Other contrast words

*however, nevertheless, whereas, while, despite, in spite of, on the other hand*.

### Typical errors

» ~~Despite it rained~~ → ***Although** it rained* / ***Despite the rain***
» ~~Even it was late~~ → ***Even though** it was late*

## Conjunctions: time

» ***when, once, as soon as***: *Call me **when** you arrive.* ***Once** you've finished, you can go.* ***As soon as** I saw her, I knew.*
» ***before, after, until / till***: *Wait **until** I come back.* ***After** we'd eaten, we left.*
» ***while / as***: *The phone rang **while** I was cooking.* ***As** I left, it started to rain.*

Use present tenses (not *will*) after time conjunctions for the future.

## Either … or…

*Either … or* presents two alternatives: *You can **either** come with us **or** stay here.* ***Either** Tom **or** Anna will help.* The verb agrees with the nearer subject: *Either my parents or **my brother is** coming.* The negative is *neither … nor*.

## If

### Conditions

*If* introduces conditions: ***If** it rains, we'll stay in.* (see [Conditionals](verbs/conditionals-and-wishes))

### If possible, if necessary, if so, if not

Short forms: *Call me, **if possible**, before 6.* *Do you have a ticket? **If not**, you can buy one here.* *Is it ready? **If so**, let's go.*

### Even if, reporting questions, politeness

***Even if*** = whether or not. Reported yes/no questions: *She asked **if** I was free.* Polite requests: ***If** you'd like to follow me…*

## In order to

*In order to* + infinitive expresses purpose and is a little more formal than *to* alone: *She moved to London **in order to** find work.* Negative: ***in order not to*** / ***so as not to***: *We left early **in order not to** miss the train.* (Not ~~to not miss~~ in formal style.)

## Or

*Or* gives alternatives: *tea **or** coffee?* *Hurry, **or** you'll be late.* (= otherwise) ***Either … or*** emphasises the choice.

### Typical errors

» In negatives, use *or*, not *and*: *I don't eat meat **or** fish.* (= neither)
» ~~Do you want tea or coffee or?~~ → *Do you want tea or coffee?*

## Since

### Since: time

*Since* + a point in time or a clause, with the **present perfect** (or past perfect): *I've lived here **since** 2015.* *We've been friends **since** we were children.* ***Since then**, everything has changed.* *Since* + *-ing*: ***Since moving** here, I've felt happier.*

### Since: reason

*Since* = because (the reason is already known): ***Since** you're here, you can help.*

### Typical errors

» ~~I live here since five years.~~ → *I've lived here **for** five years.*

## Unless

*Unless* means "if … not" or "except if": *I'll come **unless** it rains* (= if it doesn't rain). ***Unless** you hurry, you'll miss it.*

### Typical errors

» Don't add another negative: ~~unless you don't hurry~~
» *Unless* is not used for unreal past situations: ~~Unless you had helped~~ → ***If** you **hadn't** helped*

## Whereas

*Whereas* (formal) contrasts two facts: *Tom is tall, **whereas** his brother is short.* *Some people love it, **whereas** others hate it.* *While* is used in the same way.

## Whether

### In indirect questions

*Whether* introduces reported yes/no questions, like *if*: *I don't know **whether** he's coming.*

### Whether … or, whether … or not

***Whether** you like it **or not**, you have to go.* *I can't decide **whether** to stay **or** go.*

### Typical errors

» Use *whether* (not *if*) after prepositions and before *to*: *It depends **on whether**…*, *I'm not sure **whether to** go.*
» Don't confuse with *weather*.

## While and whilst

### While or whilst?

They mean the same; *whilst* is more formal and mainly British.

### While or when?

*While* is used for longer actions in progress (often with continuous tenses): ***While** I was cooking, the phone rang.* *When* is used for points in time: *The phone rang **when** I was cooking.* *While* can also show contrast: ***While** I like him, I don't trust him.*

### While as a noun

*a **while*** = a period of time: *I waited for **a while**.* *It's been **a while**.*

### Typical error

» ~~While my holiday~~ → ***During** my holiday* (while + clause; during + noun)

## Yet

### Yet as an adverb

» negatives: *I haven't finished **yet**.*
» questions: *Has she arrived **yet**?*
» affirmatives (formal): *There's **yet** another problem.* *It's **yet** to be decided.*
» with superlatives: *his best film **yet***
» emphasis: ***yet again***, ***yet more***
» ***as yet*** (formal) = so far; ***have / be yet to*** = haven't done it so far: *She **has yet to** reply.*

### Yet as a conjunction

*Yet* = but, with surprise: *It was cheap, **yet** very good.*

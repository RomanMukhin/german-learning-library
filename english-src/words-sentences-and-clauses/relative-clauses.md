## Relative clauses

### Types

Relative clauses give information about a noun. They begin with a **relative pronoun** (*who, whom, whose, which, that*) or adverb (*where, when, why*), or sometimes with no pronoun at all.

### Defining and non-defining

» **defining**: tells us *which* one; essential; no commas: *The woman **who lives next door** is a doctor.*
» **non-defining**: adds extra information; commas: *My mother, **who lives in Leeds**, is a doctor.*

### Referring to a whole sentence

*Which* can refer to the whole previous clause: *He passed his test, **which surprised everyone**.*

## Relative clauses referring to a whole sentence

When a relative clause comments on the whole idea of the main clause, use ***which*** (never *that* or *what*) after a comma:

» *She didn't call, **which** was strange.*
» *They cancelled the match, **which** meant we went home early.*
» Not: ~~She didn't call, what was strange.~~

## Relative clauses: defining and non-defining

### Defining relative clauses

They identify the noun and can't be removed without changing the meaning: *The book **that I lent you** is mine.*

### Subject or object

» subject: *the man **who called*** (*who* is the subject of *called*)
» object: *the man **(who / that) I called*** (the pronoun can be left out)

### No relative pronoun

In defining clauses, an **object** relative pronoun is often omitted, especially in speech: *the film **we saw***, *the person **I spoke to***.

### Punctuation

No commas in defining clauses.

### Nouns and pronouns

Don't repeat the noun or pronoun in the relative clause: ~~the book that I read it~~.

### Non-defining relative clauses

They add extra information about something already identified. They:

» always have commas (or are spoken with pauses)
» use *who, whom, whose, which*, never *that*
» never leave out the pronoun

» *Paris, **which is the capital of France**, is beautiful.*
» *My boss, **whom I've known for years**, is retiring.* (formal)

### Defining or non-defining?

» *My brother **who lives in Rome** is a chef.* (I have several brothers — this one)
» *My brother, **who lives in Rome**, is a chef.* (I have one brother; extra information)

## Relative clauses: typical errors

» *which* for people: ~~the man which~~ → *the man **who / that***
» *that* in non-defining clauses: ~~My car, that is red,~~ → *My car, **which** is red,*
» repeated pronoun: ~~the girl who I met her~~ → *the girl who I met*
» *what* as a relative: ~~the thing what I said~~ → *the thing **that** I said* / ***what** I said*
» *whose* / *who's*: *the man **whose** car was stolen*
» missing commas around non-defining clauses

## Above

### Higher than

*Above* means "at a higher level than", usually without touching: *The plane flew **above** the clouds.* *Our flat is **above** a shop.*

### Measuring higher

It is used with measurements on a scale: ***above** average*, *10 degrees **above** zero*, ***above** sea level*. For ages and amounts, *over* is more common: *children **over** 12*.

### As mentioned above

In writing, *above* refers back to something earlier in the text: ***the above** address*, ***as mentioned above***. (*Below* refers forward.)

## After, afterwards

### After as a preposition and conjunction

» preposition: ***After** lunch, we went for a walk.* / ***After** leaving school, she travelled.*
» conjunction: ***After** I'd finished, I went home.* (use present for the future: *after you **finish***)

### After or afterwards as an adverb

As an adverb, use ***afterwards*** (or *after that*, *then*, *later*), not *after* alone: *We had dinner, and **afterwards** we watched a film.* *Soon after* and *the day after* are fine.

### Typical error

» ~~After, we went home.~~ → ***Afterwards / After that**, we went home.*

## Against

### Reactions and opposition

*Against* means "opposed to": *Are you for or **against** the plan?* *It's **against** the law.* With verbs: *protest / fight / vote / decide **against***. With nouns: *a campaign **against** racism*, *protection **against** the cold*.

### Physical contact and competition

» *She leaned **against** the wall.* *The rain beat **against** the window.*
» *England are playing **against** Spain.*

### When we don't use against

» *a medicine **for** a cold* (not ~~against~~ in normal English), *the cure **for** cancer*

## Among and amongst

*Among* means "in the middle of" or "part of" a group of three or more: *a house **among** the trees*, *She was **among** the first to arrive.* *Discuss it **among** yourselves.* *Amongst* means the same but is more formal and mainly British. For two people or things, use *between*.

## As

### As as a preposition

*As* + noun means "in the role of" or "being": *She works **as** a nurse.* ***As** a child, I lived in Wales.* *Use this box **as** a table.*

### As as a conjunction

It introduces clauses of time (*as I was leaving*), reason (***As** it was late, we left*) and manner (*Do **as** I say*).

### The same as, as … as

*the same **as***, *as tall **as***, *such **as***.

### Simultaneous changes

*As* shows two things changing together: ***As** you get older, you get wiser.*

## At

### Time and place

» clock times and points: ***at** 7.30*, ***at** midnight*, ***at** the weekend* (Br), ***at** Easter*
» a point or a place for an activity: ***at** the station*, ***at** the cinema*, ***at** home*, ***at** work*, ***at** school*

### Group activities

***at** a party*, ***at** a concert*, ***at** a meeting*, ***at** a wedding*

### Good at, bad at

***good / bad / brilliant / hopeless at*** + noun or *-ing*: *She's **good at** maths / at drawing.*

### Numbers and direction

» speed, price, age, temperature: ***at** 100 km/h*, ***at** £5 each*, ***at** the age of 20*, ***at** 50 °C*
» direction or target: *look **at***, *point **at***, *throw a ball **at** someone* (aggressively; *to* for a friendly throw), *shout **at***, *smile **at***

### At or at the?

***at school / work / home / university*** (the activity) vs ***at the school*** (the building): *My mum's **at the school** for a meeting.*

### Typical errors

» ~~arrive to~~ → *arrive **at / in***; ~~in the weekend~~ → ***at** the weekend* (Br) / ***on** the weekend* (US)

## Below

### Lower than

*Below* = at a lower level than, not necessarily directly under: *The sun sank **below** the horizon.* *the flat **below** ours*.

### Numbers and statistics

***below** average*, ***below** zero*, *temperatures **below** 5 degrees*, *children **below** the age of 5*.

### Referring forward in writing

*Please complete the form **below**.* *See **below** for details.*

### Typical error

» Use *under* (not *below*) for something directly covered: *The cat is **under** the table.*

## Beneath

### Meaning and use

*Beneath* means "directly under" and is formal or literary: *the ground **beneath** our feet*. It can also mean "not good enough for": *He thinks the job is **beneath** him.*

### Preposition and adverb

As an adverb: *She looked down from the bridge to the river **beneath**.*

### Beneath, under or below?

*Under* is the most common, everyday word. *Below* is used for levels and scales. *Beneath* is formal and often figurative.

## Beyond

### Place

*Beyond* means "on the far side of": *The village is **beyond** the hills.*

### Outside the limits

It also means "past a limit" in time or degree: *The bridge is closed **beyond** 10 p.m.* *The car was damaged **beyond** repair.* *It's **beyond** me* (= I can't understand it).

## By

### Time

*By* means "not later than": *Please finish **by** Friday.* *By the time we arrived, the film had started.* (*By* is a deadline; *until* is a continuing situation.)

### The passive

*By* introduces the agent: *The book was written **by** Orwell.* Works of art: *a painting **by** Picasso*.

### How to do something

» transport: ***by** car / bus / train / plane* (no article), but *on foot*
» method: ***by** email*, ***by** credit card*, ***by** hand*, ***by** mistake*, ***by** chance*
» *by* + *-ing*: *You can improve **by practising** every day.*

### By myself

***by** myself / yourself* = alone, without help.

### Place

*By* = next to: *a house **by** the sea*, *Sit **by** me.*

### Measurements and amounts

*paid **by** the hour*, *prices rose **by** 10%*, *a room 5 metres **by** 4*, *one **by** one*, *little **by** little*

### During

***by** day / **by** night* = during the day / night (formal or literary)

### Typical errors

» ~~by the car~~ → ***by** car*; ~~by foot~~ → ***on** foot*

## During

*During* + noun says **when** something happens within a period: *I fell asleep **during** the film.* ***During** the summer, we went to Italy.* It can mean the whole period or a point in it. Use *for* to say **how long**: ~~during three hours~~ → ***for** three hours*. *During* is not a conjunction: ~~during I was sleeping~~ → ***while** I was sleeping*.

## For

### Purpose

*For* + noun shows purpose: *We went out **for** a meal.* *What's this **for**?* With verbs, use a *to*-infinitive for purpose: *I came here **to** study*, not ~~for study~~ / ~~for to study~~.

### For someone

*For* shows who benefits or receives: *This present is **for** you.* *I did it **for** my mother.*

### Duration

*For* + a period of time: *I lived there **for** five years.* *We waited **for** ages.*

### Exchange

*I bought it **for** £20.* *Thanks **for** your help.* *What's the French **for** "dog"?*

### For meaning because

In literary or formal style, *for* can mean "because": *She wept, **for** she was alone.*

### In multi-word verbs

*look **for***, *wait **for***, *ask **for***, *apply **for***, *care **for***, *stand **for***

## For + -ing

### Function

*For* + *-ing* explains the function or use of a thing: *A knife is **for cutting**.* *This cloth is **for cleaning** windows.*

### Reason

It can also give the reason for praise or blame: *They thanked me **for helping**.* *He was punished **for being** late.*

### For + -ing or to + infinitive?

For a person's purpose, use the *to*-infinitive: *I went to the shop **to buy** milk*, not ~~for buying milk~~. Use *for + -ing* for the general purpose of an object.

## From

### Starting point

*From* shows where movement or time starts: *I walked **from** the station.* *The shop is open **from** 9 a.m.*

### Origin

*I'm **from** Ukraine.* *a letter **from** my aunt*, *made **from** wood* (material changed), *translated **from** Spanish*.

### From … to / until

***from** Monday **to** Friday*, ***from** 9 **to** 5*, ***from** London **to** Paris*. For time, *from … until / till* is also possible. American English often uses *through*: *Monday through Friday*.

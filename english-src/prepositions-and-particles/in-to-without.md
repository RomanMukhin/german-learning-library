## In front of

*In front of* means "facing the front of, close to it": *There's a tree **in front of** the house.* *Don't stand **in front of** the TV.* Its opposite is *behind*. Compare *opposite* (on the other side, facing): *The bank is **opposite** the school.* In a car, *in the front* means the front seats.

## In spite of and despite

Both show contrast and are followed by a **noun**, a **pronoun** or an **-ing** form. *Despite* has no *of*.

» **In spite of / Despite** the rain, we went out.
» **Despite** feeling ill, she went to work.
» Not: ~~despite of~~, ~~despite it was raining~~ → ***despite the fact that** it was raining* / ***although** it was raining*

## In, into

### Position and direction

*In* is for position (where something is); *into* is for movement (entering):

» The cat is **in** the box.
» The cat jumped **into** the box.

With verbs like *put, throw, fall, jump*, both are often possible: *put it **in / into** the drawer*.

### Into: interest and enthusiasm

Informally, *be into* means "be very interested in": *She's really **into** jazz.*

### Change into, turn into

*Into* shows a change of state: *The frog **turned into** a prince.* *Translate it **into** English.* *cut it **into** pieces*.

### In: phrasal verbs

*In* is a common particle: *come **in***, *give **in*** (stop arguing), *fill **in*** a form, *drop **in*** (visit), *move **in***.

## Near and near to

### As prepositions

*Near* means "not far from" and usually takes no *to*: *We live **near** the park.* *Near to* is possible, especially in comparatives and with *very*: *nearer (to) the sea*, *very **near to** the station*.

### As an adjective

*Near* as an adjective is used in *the nearest* and some fixed phrases: *Where's **the nearest** bank?* *in **the near** future*. We don't usually say ~~a near shop~~; use *a nearby shop* or *a shop nearby*.

## Of

*Of* is one of the most frequent words in English. It shows:

» **belonging and parts**: *the end **of** the film*, *the roof **of** the house*, *a member **of** the team*
» **quantities and containers**: *a cup **of** tea*, *a lot **of** people*, *some **of** them*, *two **of** us*
» **content and material**: *a book **of** poems*, *a ring **of** gold* (more often *a gold ring*)
» **description**: *a woman **of** great courage*, *a boy **of** ten*
» **after adjectives and verbs**: *afraid **of***, *proud **of***, *tired **of***, *think **of***, *consist **of***
» **dates**: *the 5th **of** May*
» **"of + possessive"**: *a friend **of** mine*

## On, onto

*On* is for position on a surface; *onto* is for movement towards a surface:

» The book is **on** the shelf.
» The cat jumped **onto** the table.

With many verbs (*put, throw, fall*), *on* is enough: *Put it **on** the table.* *Onto* (one word) is usual in British and American English; *on to* (two words) is used when *on* is part of the verb: *Let's move **on to** the next topic.*

Other uses of *on*: days and dates (*on Monday*), transport (*on the bus*), topics (*a book **on** history*), devices (*on TV, on the phone, on the internet*), and *on* = working (*The light is **on***).

## Over

### As a preposition: movement and position

*Over* means "above and covering" or "across to the other side": *a bridge **over** the river*, *She put a blanket **over** him*, *jump **over** the wall*, *the house **over** the road* (opposite).

### Over and all over

***all over*** = everywhere in a place: *There was water **all over** the floor.* *She's travelled **all over** the world.*

### Periods of time and numbers

» *Over the last few years, prices have risen.* (during)
» ***over** 50 people*, *children **over** 12* (more than)

### As an adverb, adjective and prefix

» adverb: *Come **over** here.* *Turn the page **over**.* *Think it **over**.*
» ***over and over (again)*** = many times
» adjective: *The film **is over*** (= finished)
» prefix (too much): ***over**cooked, **over**worked, **over**sleep*

### Typical error

» ~~I've lived here since over two years~~ → *I've lived here **for over** two years.*

## To

### Destination and direction

*To* shows where movement ends: *go **to** Paris*, *walk **to** work*, *the road **to** the airport*. Not with *home*: *go home*.

### Receiver of an action

*give it **to** her*, *send a letter **to** the bank*, *explain something **to** someone* (not ~~explain someone~~), *talk **to***, *listen **to***.

### Time and approximate numbers

» *It's ten **to** six.* (British clock times) / *from Monday **to** Friday*
» *20 **to** 30 people* (range)

### After nouns, verbs and adjectives

*the answer **to***, *the key **to***, *a visit **to***; *belong **to***, *happen **to***, *object **to***; *kind **to***, *similar **to***, *married **to***, *used **to***.

> [!trap] To + -ing
> When *to* is a preposition, it is followed by a noun or *-ing*, not an infinitive: *I'm looking forward **to seeing** you.* *I'm not used **to getting up** early.* *I object **to paying** more.*

### The to-infinitive

*To* is also the infinitive marker: *I want **to go**.*

## Under

*Under* means "directly below" or "covered by": *The dog is **under** the table.* *Write your name **under** the line.* It also means "less than":

» **age**: *children **under** 16*
» **measurements and money**: ***under** £10*, ***under** two hours*

Other meanings: *under pressure, under control, under construction, under the law* (in a condition or controlled by something).

## Until

### As a preposition

*Until* (informal *till*) means "up to a point in time": *The shop is open **until** 8 p.m.* *I'll wait **until** Friday.* *Not until* = not before: *The results won't be ready **until** Monday.*

### As a conjunction

*Wait here **until** I come back.* (present tense for the future, not ~~until I will come~~)

### Typical errors

» *Until* is for time, not place: ~~walk until the station~~ → *walk **as far as / to** the station*.
» *Until* vs *by*: *Finish it **by** Friday* (deadline) / *Stay **until** Friday* (continuing).

## With

### In the same place as

*I went **with** my sister.* *He lives **with** his parents.*

### Reactions and feelings

*angry **with** someone*, *pleased / happy **with** something*, *in love **with***, *bored **with***

### Using

*Cut it **with** a knife.* *Write **with** a pen.* (*By* is used for methods, *with* for tools.)

### Having

*a girl **with** blue eyes*, *a room **with** a view*, *coffee **with** milk*

### Because of

*She was shaking **with** fear.* *He went red **with** embarrassment.*

### Other uses

*agree **with***, *deal **with***, *fill **with***, *What's wrong **with** you?*, ***with** the exception of*

## Within

### Space

*Within* means "inside the limits of" (formal): *Visitors must stay **within** the area.* ***within** walking distance*.

### Time

*Within* means "before the end of a period": *Please reply **within** seven days.* *She was back **within** an hour.*

## Without

*Without* means "not having" or "not doing". It is followed by a noun, a pronoun or an *-ing* form:

» I can't live **without** you.
» He left **without saying** goodbye.
» coffee **without** sugar

### Typical errors

» ~~without to say~~ → *without **saying***
» ~~without no money~~ → *without **any** money* (no double negative)

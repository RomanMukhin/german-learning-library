## Geographical places

| Use **the** | No article |
|---|---|
| rivers: *the Thames, the Nile* | lakes: *Lake Geneva* |
| seas and oceans: *the Atlantic, the Mediterranean* | single mountains: *Everest, Ben Nevis* |
| deserts: *the Sahara* | single islands: *Sicily, Crete* |
| mountain ranges: *the Alps, the Andes* | continents: *Asia, Europe* |
| island groups: *the Canaries, the Philippines* | most countries and cities: *France, Tokyo* |
| countries with plural or "union/republic/kingdom": *the USA, the UK, the Netherlands* | |

## Names and titles: addressing people

### Family and friends

*Mum / Dad* (Br), *Mom / Dad* (US), *Grandma / Grandpa / Granny / Nan*. Terms of endearment: *darling, love, sweetheart, honey* (US). Friends often use first names or nicknames.

### Groups

*Ladies and gentlemen* (formal), *everyone*, *guys* (informal, any gender), *folks* (informal).

### Polite and formal address

» ***Mr*** (men), ***Mrs*** (married women), ***Miss*** (unmarried, young), ***Ms*** /mɪz/ (any woman, neutral) + **surname**: *Mr Smith*, not ~~Mr John~~
» ***Sir / Madam*** in shops and formal letters: *Dear Sir or Madam*
» ***Dr / Professor*** + surname

### Strangers and job titles

To attract a stranger's attention: ***Excuse me…*** Job titles alone are rarely used to address people, except *Doctor*, *Nurse*, *Officer* and *Waiter* (old-fashioned).

## Nationalities, languages, countries and regions

| Country | Adjective / language | Person | People in general |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spain | Spanish | a Spaniard | the Spanish |
| France | French | a Frenchman / woman, a French person | the French |
| Britain | British | a Briton, a British person | the British |
| China | Chinese | a Chinese person | the Chinese |
| Germany | German | a German | (the) Germans |
| Poland | Polish | a Pole | the Poles |
| Ukraine | Ukrainian | a Ukrainian | (the) Ukrainians |

Nationality words always have a **capital letter**: *English, Japanese*. To talk about all the people of a country, use *the* + adjective ending in *-sh, -ch, -ese* (*the French, the Japanese*) or the plural noun (*Germans, Americans*).

### The + country name

Use *the* with plural names and names with common nouns: *the Netherlands*, *the United States*, *the Czech Republic*, *the UK*. Not with most others: *Ukraine* (*the Ukraine* is old-fashioned).

## Place names

### Buildings and monuments

Many famous buildings take *the*: *the White House, the Eiffel Tower, the British Museum, the Taj Mahal*. Names with a person's or place's name + building often have no article: *Buckingham Palace, St Paul's Cathedral, Heathrow Airport, Victoria Station*.

### Roads and streets

No article: *Oxford Street, Park Avenue, Abbey Road*. But: *the High Street, the M25, the A1*. British English: *in / on Oxford Street*.

### Facilities in a town

We say *the* for facilities that people share: *go to **the** bank / **the** cinema / **the** doctor's / **the** post office*.

### The sea and the coast

*go to the seaside*, *live by the sea*, *on the coast*, *in the mountains*, *in the country(side)*.

### Fixed expressions

*at home*, *go home*, *at school*, *in hospital* (Br), *in town*, *in bed*, *at sea*, *at university*.

## Reported speech

### Reporting and reported clauses

Reported speech has two parts: the **reporting clause** (who spoke) and the **reported clause** (what they said): ***She said** that she was tired.*

### Direct and indirect speech

» **direct speech** repeats the exact words in quotation marks: *"I'm tired," she said.*
» **indirect speech** reports the meaning, usually with changes of pronoun, tense and time words: *She said (that) she was tired.*

### Punctuation of direct speech

British English often uses single quotation marks ('…'), American double ("…"). A comma separates the reporting clause; the punctuation of the quote goes inside the marks: *"Come here," he said.* / *He asked, "Where are you?"*

### Reporting verbs: say and tell

***Say*** has no person object (or uses *to*); ***tell*** needs a person:

» *She **said** (to me) that…* / *She **told me** that…*
» Not: ~~She said me~~, ~~She told that~~

### Other reporting verbs

They show the speaker's purpose: *ask, explain, suggest, promise, warn, advise, admit, deny, agree, refuse, offer, complain, insist, claim, reply*. Patterns differ:

| Pattern | Verbs | Example |
|---|---|---|
| + *that*-clause | admit, explain, claim, reply | *He **admitted that** he was wrong.* |
| + *to*-infinitive | agree, refuse, offer, promise | *She **refused to** help.* |
| + object + *to*-inf | tell, ask, warn, advise, remind | *I **told him to** wait.* |
| + *-ing* | deny, suggest, admit | *He **denied taking** it.* |

## Reported speech: direct speech

### Inversion of subject and reporting verb

After a quote, the reporting verb can come before a noun subject, especially in stories: *"Help!" **shouted the boy**.* Not with pronouns: *"Help!" **he shouted**.* (not ~~shouted he~~)

### Present tense reporting verbs

In conversation and informal stories, people often use the present: *So he **says** to me, "Where have you been?"* In speech, ***go*** and ***be like*** are very informal quotation verbs: *And she**'s like**, "No way!"*

### Newspaper headlines

Headlines often quote with a colon: *PM: "We will win."*

## Reported speech: indirect speech

### Reporting statements and backshift

When the reporting verb is in the **past**, the tense of the reported clause usually moves one step back:

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| "I **work** here." | She said she **worked** there. |
| "I'**m working**." | She said she **was working**. |
| "I **worked / have worked**." | She said she **had worked**. |
| "I **will** call." | She said she **would** call. |
| "I **can** swim." | She said she **could** swim. |

Backshift is not needed when the situation is still true: *She said she **lives** in Paris.* *Would, could, should, might* and *ought to* don't change.

### Pronouns, time and place words

*now → then*, *today → that day*, *yesterday → the day before*, *tomorrow → the next day*, *here → there*, *this → that*, *ago → before*.

### Reporting questions

Indirect questions use **statement word order** and no question mark:

» yes/no questions: *"Are you ready?" → He asked **if / whether I was** ready.*
» alternative questions: *She asked **whether** I wanted tea **or** coffee.*
» *wh*-questions: *"Where do you live?" → She asked **where I lived**.* Not: ~~where did I live~~
» *who/what* as subject: *"Who called?" → He asked **who had called**.*

### Reporting commands and requests

Use **tell / ask / order + object + (not) to-infinitive**: *"Sit down." → She **told me to sit** down.* *"Please don't shout." → He **asked us not to shout**.*

### Present simple reporting verb

With *says, tells, asks* in the present, there is no backshift: *She **says** she'**s** coming.* This is common when passing on messages or reporting written texts: *The letter **says** we have to pay.*

### Past continuous reporting verbs

*was saying / was telling* is used in conversation to introduce news: *John **was telling** me you're moving.*

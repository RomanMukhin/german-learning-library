## Dates

### Writing the date

British English: **day – month – year**: *14 March 2025*, *14/03/2025*, *14th March 2025*. American English: **month – day – year**: *March 14, 2025*, *3/14/2025*. So *04/05/2025* is 4 May in Britain and April 5 in the US.

### Speaking the date

» British: *the fourteenth of March* or *March the fourteenth*
» American: *March fourteenth*

### Years

» before 2000: *1995* = *nineteen ninety-five*; *1906* = *nineteen oh six*
» after 2000: *2005* = *two thousand and five*; *2025* = *twenty twenty-five* or *two thousand and twenty-five*
» decades: *the 1990s* = *the nineteen nineties*

### Typical error

» ~~in 14 March~~ → ***on** 14 March*; ~~the 14 March~~ in speech → *the four**teenth** of March*

## Measurements

### Length, width, depth and height

Use number + unit + adjective, or the noun: *The room is **5 metres long** and **4 metres wide**.* *The pool is **2 metres deep**.* *What's **the height** of the tower?* *She's 1.70 m **tall***. (people: *tall*; mountains, buildings from the ground: *high*)

### Weight and volume

*It **weighs** 3 kilos.* *How heavy is it?* *a 2-litre bottle* (singular unit in compound adjectives). Britain uses metric officially, but miles, pints and stones (body weight) are still common.

### Frequency, speed, time

» frequency: *once / twice / three times **a** week*
» speed: *80 kilometres **an** hour*, *60 miles **per** hour (mph)*
» time: *It takes two hours.* *a three-hour journey*

## Number

### Ordinal numbers: first, second, third

*first (1st), second (2nd), third (3rd), fourth (4th), fifth, eighth, ninth, twelfth, twentieth, twenty-first*. Used for order, dates, floors, centuries and fractions: *the **third** floor*, *the **21st** century*, *a **fifth***.

### Cardinal numbers: one, two, three

Hyphenate *21–99*: *twenty-one*, *forty-five*. British English uses ***and*** before tens and units: *three hundred **and** sixty-five*. Zero can be *zero*, *nought* (Br, maths), *oh* (phone numbers, years), *nil* (football scores), *love* (tennis).

### Large numbers

*a / one hundred*, *two **hundred*** (no *-s* after a number), *a thousand*, *a million*, *a billion* (= 1,000,000,000). But: ***hundreds of** people*, ***thousands of** years*. Write commas for thousands: *1,000,000*; decimal points are dots: *3.5* = *three point five*.

## Time

### Seconds, minutes, hours, years

*Time* is uncountable for the general idea: *I don't have **time**.* *Do you have **a minute**?* Countable *a time* = an occasion: *I've been there **three times**.* *We had **a great time**.*

### Clock and calendar time

» ***on time*** = at the planned time, not late: *The train left **on time**.*
» ***in time*** = early enough: *We arrived **in time** for dinner.*

### Past events

*At that time*, *by that time*, *in those days*, *at the time*: *I was living in Rome **at the time**.*

### Telling the time

| Time | British | Also / American |
|---|---|---|
| 7.00 | seven o'clock | |
| 7.15 | (a) quarter past seven | seven fifteen |
| 7.30 | half past seven / half seven (informal) | seven thirty |
| 7.45 | (a) quarter to eight | seven forty-five, a quarter of eight (US) |
| 7.10 | ten past seven | ten after seven (US) |
| 7.50 | ten to eight | ten of eight (US) |

Ask: *What time is it?* / *What's the time?* (Br) / *Have you got the time?* Use ***a.m.*** (morning) and ***p.m.*** (afternoon and evening). Timetables use the 24-hour clock: *19.30* = *nineteen thirty*.

> [!trap] Half seven
> In British English *half seven* means 7.30. In German and some other languages the same words mean 6.30.

A **verb** refers to an action (*run, write*), an event (*happen, arrive*) or a state (*know, belong*). Verbs are the centre of the clause: they decide which tense, how many objects and what pattern follows.

| Form | Example | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| base | *work, go* | present, imperative, after modals |
| *-s* | *works, goes* | present simple, *he / she / it* |
| past | *worked, went* | past simple |
| *-ing* | *working, going* | continuous forms, gerunds |
| past participle | *worked, gone* | perfect forms, passive |

> [!hack] Tense = time + aspect
> English "tenses" combine a time (past, present, future) with an aspect: **simple** (the whole event), **continuous** (in progress) and **perfect** (connected to a later point). Once you see the two parts, the twelve forms stop looking like twelve separate rules.

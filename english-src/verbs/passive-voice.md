## Get passive

In informal English, ***get* + past participle** can replace *be* in the passive, especially for unexpected or unwanted events, or when something changes: *He **got arrested**.* *My phone **got stolen**.* *She **got promoted**.* *Get married / dressed / lost / hurt* are fixed expressions. It is not used for states or in formal writing.

## Have something done

### What someone does for us

***have* + object + past participle** means someone else does a job for us (often for payment): *I'm **having my hair cut** tomorrow.* *We **had the kitchen painted**.* *Get something done* is similar, more informal: *I need to **get my car repaired**.*

### Asking or instructing

*Have someone do something* (active): *I'll **have my assistant call** you.* (US mostly)

### An experience, often negative

*She **had her bag stolen** on the bus.* (something unpleasant happened to her)

## Passive: forms

### Be + -ed

The passive is formed with ***be* + past participle**. The tense is shown by *be*:

| Tense | Passive |
|---|---|
| present simple | The room **is cleaned** every day. |
| present continuous | The room **is being cleaned**. |
| past simple | The room **was cleaned** yesterday. |
| past continuous | The room **was being cleaned**. |
| present perfect | The room **has been cleaned**. |
| past perfect | The room **had been cleaned**. |
| future | The room **will be cleaned**. |
| modal | The room **must / should / can be cleaned**. |
| infinitive / -ing | It needs **to be cleaned**. / I hate **being told** what to do. |

### Which verbs?

Only **transitive** verbs can be passive: *The letter was **sent**.* Not: ~~The accident was happened~~ (*happen* has no object). Some state verbs are rarely passive: *have* (possess), *fit, suit, lack, resemble*.

### Verbs with two objects

Either object can become the subject; the person is more common: *I **was given** a present.* / *A present **was given** to me.*

### Linking verbs

Linking verbs (*be, seem, become*) can't be passive.

## Passives with and without an agent

### With an agent

The doer (agent) is introduced by ***by*** when it is important or new: *The Mona Lisa was painted **by Leonardo da Vinci**.*

### Without an agent

Usually we leave the agent out because it is unknown, obvious, unimportant, or we don't want to say: *My car **was stolen**.* (unknown) *He **was arrested**.* (obviously by the police) *Mistakes **were made**.* (avoiding blame)

### It and there

With reporting verbs (*say, think, believe, expect, report*) we use ***it* + passive + *that*** or **subject + passive + *to*-infinitive**: ***It is said that** he is rich.* / *He **is said to be** rich.* ***There is thought to be** a problem.*

## Passive: uses

We use the passive:

» when the action or the receiver is more important than the doer: *The bridge **was built** in 1890.*
» in formal and scientific writing, to sound objective: *The samples **were heated** to 100 °C.*
» in news reports: *Three people **were injured** in the accident.*
» to keep the topic of the text as the subject: *Our company was founded in 1990. It **was bought** by a US group in 2010.*
» in rules and notices: *Smoking **is not permitted**.*

## Passive: other forms

» passive infinitive: *I want **to be told** the truth.* *It has **to be done** today.*
» passive *-ing*: *She hates **being interrupted**.* *After **being questioned**, he was released.*
» perfect passive infinitive: *The work should **have been finished** by now.*
» passive with prepositional verbs (the preposition stays): *The children **were looked after** by their grandmother.* *She hates being **laughed at**.*
» *need* + *-ing* (passive meaning): *The car **needs washing*** (= needs to be washed).

## Passive: typical errors

» missing *be*: ~~The house built in 1900.~~ → *The house **was** built…*
» wrong participle: ~~The letter was wrote.~~ → *was **written***
» intransitive verbs: ~~It was happened.~~ → *It **happened**.*
» passive with *seem*: ~~It is seemed that~~ → *It **seems** that*
» *by* vs *with*: *killed **by** a falling tree* (agent) / *killed **with** a knife* (instrument)

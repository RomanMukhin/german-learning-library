## Future: will and shall

### Form and short forms

*will / won't + base form*: *I **will** help.* *She **won't** come.* ***Will** you be there?* Short form *'ll*: *I'll, you'll, it'll*. *Shall* is used mainly with *I* and *we* in questions.

### Uses

» predictions based on opinion or experience: *I think it **will** rain.* *You'**ll** love this film.*
» decisions made at the moment of speaking: *"The phone's ringing." "I'**ll** get it."*
» offers, promises, threats, requests: *I'**ll** carry that.* *I **won't** tell anyone.* ***Will** you help me?*
» future facts: *The sun **will** set at 6.15.*

### Shall with I and we

***Shall I** open the window?* (offer) ***Shall we** go?* (suggestion) *What **shall** we do?* (asking for advice)

## Future: be going to (I am going to work)

### Form

*am / is / are going to + base form*: *I'**m going to** study medicine.* *It'**s not going to** work.*

### Uses

» **intentions** (plans already decided): *We'**re going to** buy a house.*
» **predictions based on present evidence**: *Look at those clouds — it'**s going to** rain.* *Watch out! You'**re going to** fall!*
» strong commands (informal): *You'**re going to** finish your homework before you go out.*

### Gonna

In informal speech *going to* is often pronounced and sometimes written *gonna*. Don't use it in formal writing.

### Be going to or will?

» decided before speaking → *going to*: *I'**m going to** visit my aunt this weekend.*
» decided now → *will*: *OK, I'**ll** visit her.*
» evidence now → *going to*: *She'**s going to** have a baby.*
» opinion / belief → *will*: *I'm sure she'**ll** be a great mother.*

## Future: other expressions to talk about the future

» ***be about to*** = very soon: *The film **is about to** start.*
» ***be on the point of** + -ing* = extremely soon: *I **was on the point of** leaving when she called.*
» ***be due to*** = expected according to a timetable: *The plane **is due to** land at 10.*
» ***be to*** (formal) = official plans, obligations, instructions, headlines: *The President **is to** visit Japan.* *You **are to** report to reception.* *PM TO MEET UNIONS*

## Future continuous (I will be working)

### Form

*will be + -ing*: *This time tomorrow I'**ll be flying** to Rome.*

### Uses

» an action in progress at a future time: *At 9 p.m. we'**ll be having** dinner.*
» something that will happen as part of the normal course of events: *I'**ll be seeing** Tom tomorrow, so I can give him the message.*
» polite questions about plans: ***Will** you **be using** the car tonight?*

## Future in the past

To talk about the future from a point in the past, use ***would***, ***was / were going to***, the past continuous, or ***was about to***:

» *She said she **would** call.* (reported)
» *I **was going to** phone you, but I forgot.* (plan that didn't happen)
» *They **were leaving** the next day.* (arrangement)
» *I **was about to** go out when it started to rain.*

## Future perfect continuous (I will have been working here ten years)

### Form

*will have been + -ing*: *By June, I'**ll have been working** here for ten years.*

### Use

It emphasises the length of an activity up to a point in the future: *When she retires, she **will have been teaching** for 40 years.*

## Future perfect simple (I will have worked eight hours)

### Form

*will have + past participle*: *I'**ll have finished** by 6 p.m.*

### Use

It describes something that will be completed before a time in the future, often with ***by*** or ***by the time***: ***By the time** you get here, we **will have eaten**.* *By 2030, prices **will have doubled**.*

## Future: present continuous to talk about the future (I'm working tomorrow)

The present continuous is used for **personal arrangements** — plans fixed with other people, often with a time or place:

» *I'**m meeting** the doctor at 4.*
» *What **are** you **doing** this weekend?*
» *We'**re flying** to Lisbon on Friday.*

It's very common in speech for social plans. Not for predictions: ~~It's raining tomorrow~~ → *It'**s going to** rain / It'**ll** rain tomorrow.*

## Future: present simple to talk about the future (I work tomorrow)

The present simple is used for:

» **timetables and schedules**: *The train **leaves** at 7.45.* *The course **starts** on Monday.*
» future meaning after time and condition words (*when, if, as soon as, before, after, until, unless, in case*): *I'll call you **when** I **arrive**.* Not: ~~when I will arrive~~

## Future: typical errors

» *will* after *when / if*: ~~If it will rain, we'll stay in.~~ → *If it **rains**, …*
» present simple for decisions now: ~~I help you!~~ → *I'**ll** help you!*
» *will* for arrangements: ~~I will meet John tonight (we've arranged it)~~ → *I'**m meeting** John tonight.*
» ~~I'm going to go to the cinema~~ is correct, but *I'm going to the cinema* is more natural.

## Going to

### Going to: present continuous

*Go* in the present continuous shows movement or plans: *I'**m going to** the bank.* (= I'm on my way / I plan to go)

### Going to: future

*Going to* + base form expresses intentions and predictions (see [be going to](#future-be-going-to-i-am-going-to-work)).

### Was going to

*Was / were going to* describes a past plan that didn't happen: *I **was going to** call you, but my phone died.* It is also used to introduce a topic politely: *I **was going to** ask you about the meeting.*

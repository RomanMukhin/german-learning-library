## Verbs: types

### Main verbs and linking verbs

**Main (lexical) verbs** carry the meaning: *run, eat, think*. **Linking verbs** connect the subject to a description: *be, seem, become, look, feel, sound, taste, get, appear, remain*: *She **seems** tired.*

### Auxiliary verbs

» **be**: continuous forms and the passive: *I **am** working.* *It **was** built.*
» **do**: questions, negatives, emphasis: ***Do** you know?* *I **don't** know.* *I **do** like it!*
» **have**: perfect forms: *She **has** left.*

Auxiliaries can stand alone in short answers and tags: *"Do you like it?" "Yes, I **do**."* *Be, do* and *have* are also main verbs: *I **have** a car.* *I **did** my homework.*

### Modal verbs

*can, could, may, might, must, shall, should, will, would* (see [Modal verbs](verbs/modal-verbs)).

### State and action verbs

» **action** verbs describe things that happen and can be continuous: *eat, run, write*
» **event** verbs describe things that happen at a point: *arrive, find, break*
» **state** verbs describe states and are rarely continuous: *know, like, own, believe, seem*

## Verb phrases

### Simple and complex

A **simple** verb phrase has one verb: *She **works**.* A **complex** verb phrase has auxiliaries or modals + a main verb: *She **has been working**.* *It **might have been stolen**.*

### Order and the first verb

The order is: **modal → perfect *have* → continuous *be* → passive *be* → main verb**: *The house **might have been being painted**.* (rare but possible) Only the **first** verb shows tense and agrees with the subject, and it is the one used to make questions and negatives: ***Has** she **been** working?* *She **hasn't** been working.*

## Verbs and verb phrases: typical errors

» ~~She don't like it.~~ → *She **doesn't** like it.*
» ~~I am agree.~~ → *I **agree**.* (*agree* is a verb, not an adjective)
» ~~It's depend.~~ → *It **depends**.*
» ~~I am knowing.~~ → *I **know**.*
» ~~Did you saw?~~ → *Did you **see**?*

## Appear

### How something seems

*Appear* (formal) = seem: *She **appears** (**to be**) happy.* ***It appears that** we were wrong.*

### Come into sight

*A face **appeared** at the window.* *She **appears** in a new film.*

### Appear or seem?

They are similar, but *seem* is more common and can describe feelings and impressions (*It **seems** a pity*); *appear* is more formal and tends to describe how things look from the outside.

## Ask and ask for

### Ask

*Ask* (someone) a question / about something: *Can I **ask** (you) a question?* *She **asked** me **about** my job.* *Ask* + *if / wh*-: *He **asked** where I lived.*

### Ask + to-infinitive

*Ask* (someone) *to* do something: *She **asked** me **to** help.* *I **asked to** see the manager.*

### Ask for

*Ask for* + the thing you want: *He **asked for** a glass of water.* *Ask* someone *for* something: *I **asked** her **for** advice.*

### Typical error

» ~~She asked me a pen.~~ → *She asked me **for** a pen.*

## Be

### Forms

*am, is, are; was, were; been; being*. Contracted: *I'm, you're, he's; isn't, aren't, wasn't, weren't*.

### Be as a main verb

» qualities: *She **is** clever.*
» nationality and group: *They **are** Polish.*
» jobs: *He **is** a nurse.* (with *a/an*)
» age: *I'**m** 30.* (not ~~I have 30~~)
» relationships: *She'**s** my cousin.*
» place: *The keys **are** on the table.*
» time: *It'**s** six o'clock.*
» temporary behaviour: *You'**re being** silly.*
» feeling hot / cold / hungry / afraid: *I'**m** cold.* (not ~~I have cold~~)

### Modal expressions and auxiliary

*be able to, be going to, be to, be about to*; auxiliary in continuous forms and passives.

### Typical errors

» ~~I have 25 years.~~ → *I **am** 25.* / ~~I have hunger.~~ → *I'**m** hungry.*

## Be expressions (be able to, be due to)

» ***be about to*** = very soon: *The show **is about to** begin.*
» ***be able to*** = ability, used where *can* has no form: *I'**ll be able to** come.* *I've **never been able to** swim.* For a **single past achievement**, use *was able to / managed to*, not *could*: *After hours, we **were able to** fix it.*
» ***be due to*** = scheduled: *The train **is due to** arrive at 6.* / because of: *The delay was **due to** fog.*
» ***be likely to*** = probably will: *Prices **are likely to** rise.*
» ***be meant to*** = intended / supposed to: *This **is meant to** be a joke.*
» ***be supposed to*** = expected because of a rule or plan (often not done): *You'**re supposed to** wear a helmet.* *The film **is supposed to** be good.* (people say so)

## Come

### Movement

*Come* is movement **towards** the speaker or listener: ***Come** here!* *I'll **come** to your party.* (towards the listener's place) Movement away is *go*.

### Come meaning arrive

*The bus **came** late.* *Winter **is coming**.*

### Come or come to?

*Come to* + place: *Come **to** my office.* No *to* with *home, here, there*: *Come **home**.*

### Informal expressions

***Come on!*** (hurry / encouragement / disbelief), ***How come?*** (why), ***Coming!*** (I'm on my way), ***come and*** + verb: *Come and see.*

## Do

### Forms

*do, does, did, done, doing*; *don't, doesn't, didn't*

### Do as a main verb

» actions in general: *What are you **doing**?* *I have nothing to **do**.*
» work, tasks and study: ***do** homework, **do** the housework, **do** a course, **do** maths*
» activities: ***do** yoga, **do** exercise, **do** the shopping*
» achieve or deal with: ***do** your best, **do** business*
» clean or tidy: ***do** the dishes, **do** your hair*
» be enough: *Ten euros will **do**.* *That'll **do**!*

### Do as an auxiliary

» questions and negatives in present and past simple: ***Do** you smoke? I **didn't** see it.*
» emphasis: *I **do** love you.* ***Do** sit down.*
» short answers, tags and substitution: *"Did you call?" "Yes, I **did**." She likes jazz, **doesn't** she? So **do** I.*

## Enable

*Enable* = make it possible for someone to do something. Pattern: ***enable* + object + *to*-infinitive**: *The money **enabled us to** buy a house.* Not: ~~enabled us buying~~ / ~~enable to buy~~ without an object. It is more formal than *allow* or *let*.

## Enjoy

*Enjoy* needs an **object** or a reflexive pronoun, and is followed by ***-ing***, not the infinitive:

» *I **enjoyed** the film.* *Did you **enjoy yourselves**?* ***Enjoy** your meal!*
» *I **enjoy swimming**.* Not: ~~I enjoy to swim~~, ~~I enjoyed very much~~ (needs object: *I enjoyed **it** very much*)

## Explain

*Explain* something **to** someone. The person cannot be the direct object:

» *Can you **explain** this word **to me**?* Not: ~~explain me this word~~
» *She **explained (to us) that** the flight was cancelled.*
» *Explain* + *wh*-: *He **explained how** it worked.*

## Get

*Get* is very common and has many meanings:

» **fetch, receive, obtain, buy**: *I'll **get** some milk.* *I **got** a letter.* *She **got** a new job.*
» **become** (+ adjective or comparative): *It's **getting** dark.* *I'm **getting** better.* ***get** married / dressed / lost*
» **arrive**: *What time did you **get** home / **get to** work?*
» **negative events** (get passive): *He **got** hurt.* *My bike **got** stolen.*
» **cause something to happen**: ***get** + object + past participle*: *I **got** my hair cut.* / ***get** + object + to-infinitive*: *I **got** him **to** help me.*
» **understand** (informal): *I don't **get** it.*

## Go

### Go + -ing

For activities, especially sports and hobbies: ***go** swimming / shopping / skiing / dancing*.

### Go + complement (become)

For changes, usually negative: ***go** bad / grey / mad / wrong / blind / quiet*.

### Go and, come and

*Go and* / *come and* + verb (informal): *I'll **go and** get it.* *Come **and** see us.* In American English *and* is often left out: *Go get it.*

## Happen

*Happen* is intransitive (no object, no passive): *What **happened**?* *The accident **happened** at night.* Not: ~~was happened~~. *Happen to* someone: *What **happened to** you?* *Happen* + *to*-infinitive = by chance: *I **happened to** see her.* *Do you **happen to** know…?* (polite question)

## Have

### Forms

*have / has / had*; contracted *'ve, 's, 'd*

### Have as a main verb

» **possession**: *I **have** two brothers.*
» **meals and drinks**: ***have** breakfast / a coffee*
» **events and experiences**: ***have** a party / a meeting / a good time / a problem*
» **actions**: ***have** a shower / a rest / a look / a try / a chat / a dream / an accident*

In these action meanings, *have* can be continuous and uses *do* in questions: *I'**m having** a shower.* ***Did** you **have** a good time?*

### Have as an auxiliary and in have to

Auxiliary in perfect forms: *I **have** finished.* Obligation: *I **have to** go.*

### Typical error

» ~~Have you a car?~~ (old-fashioned) → ***Do** you **have** a car?* / ***Have** you **got** a car?*

## Have got and have

*Have got* means *have* for possession, relationships, illnesses and features. It is common in British English, especially in speech:

» *I'**ve got** a new phone.* = *I **have** a new phone.*
» negative / question: *I **haven't got**…* ***Have** you **got**…?* (or *I don't have…*, *Do you have…?*)

*Have got* is only used in the present; for the past use *had*: *I **had** a cold* (not ~~had got~~ for possession). Don't use *have got* for actions: ~~I've got a shower every day~~ → *I **have** a shower…*

## Hope

### Hope as a verb

» *hope + that*-clause (often present for the future): *I **hope** you **feel** better soon.*
» *hope + to*-infinitive: *We **hope to** see you.*
» *I hope so / I hope not*: *"Will it be sunny?" "I **hope so**."*

Don't confuse with *wish*: *hope* is for possible things, *wish* for unreal ones: *I **hope** I pass.* / *I **wish** I were taller.*

### Hope as a noun

*There's still **hope**.* *in the **hope** of / that…* *My **hope** is that…*

## Know

### Know how + infinitive

*Do you **know how to** swim?* Not: ~~know to swim~~.

### Know + object + infinitive

Formal or passive: *He **is known to be** dishonest.* *I've **known** her **(to) cry** at films* (past experience).

### Know without an object

*"Where's Tom?" "I don't **know**."* *Let me **know**.* *I **know**!*

### You know

As a discourse marker: *It's, **you know**, complicated.*

Note: *know* is a state verb (~~I'm knowing~~). *Get to know* = become familiar with: *I'd like to **get to know** you better.*

## Let, let's

### Let: permission

*Let* + object + **bare infinitive**: *My parents **let** me **go** to the party.* No passive: use *be allowed to*.

### Let's: suggestions, offers, imperatives

***Let's** go!* ***Let's not** argue.* ***Let me** help you.* ***Let me** think.* *Let's* is only for suggestions including the speaker.

### Let meaning rent (Br)

*We **let** our flat to students.* *Room to **let***.

## Like

### Like as a verb (enjoy)

» + noun: *I **like** chocolate.*
» + *-ing*: *I **like** reading.* (enjoyment)
» + *to*-infinitive: *I **like to** get up early.* (habit, choice)
» + *wh*-clause: *Do **what** you **like**.*
» ***would like*** (offer / request): *Would you like a drink?* *I'd like to go.*

### Like as a preposition and conjunction

» preposition: *She looks **like** her mother.* *It's **like** a dream.* *What's it **like**?*
» conjunction (informal, = as / as if): *Do it **like** I showed you.* *It looks **like** it's going to rain.*

### Like as a suffix and in spoken English

» suffix: *child**like**, business**like***
» filler and focusing (very informal): *It was, **like**, really expensive.*
» quoting (very informal): *And I was **like**, "No way!"*

## Look

### Look as a linking verb

*Look* + adjective = seem: *You **look** tired.* Not: ~~You look tiredly.~~

### Look like, look as if

» ***look like*** + noun: *She **looks like** her father.* *It **looks like** rain.*
» ***look as if / as though*** + clause: *It **looks as if** it's going to rain.*

### Look as a discourse marker

***Look**, I'm sorry, but I can't help.* (getting attention, sometimes impatient)

## Made from, made of, made out of, made with

» ***made from*** — the material has been changed and is not easy to recognise: *Paper is **made from** wood.* *Wine is **made from** grapes.*
» ***made of*** — the material is still recognisable: *The table is **made of** wood.* *a ring **made of** gold*
» ***made out of*** — a material or object turned into something unexpected: *a lamp **made out of** a bottle*
» ***made with*** — food, listing ingredients: *This cake is **made with** butter and honey.*

## Make

*Make* means create, produce or cause. Main patterns:

» + object: ***make** a cake, a mistake, a decision, a noise, money, friends, progress, an effort*
» + object + adjective: *The news **made** me **happy**.*
» + object + noun: *They **made** him **captain**.*
» + two objects / *for*: *I'll **make** you a coffee.* / *I'll **make** a coffee **for** you.*
» + object + **bare infinitive** (force or cause): *She **made** me **laugh**.* *They **made** us **wait**.* Passive with *to*: *We **were made to** wait.*
» *make it* = succeed / arrive in time: *We **made it**!*

## Marry and divorce

*Marry* takes a direct object, without *with*: *She **married** an engineer.* Not: ~~married with~~. More common is ***get married (to)***: *They **got married** in June.* *She **got married to** Tom.* State: ***be married (to)***: *I'**ve been married** for five years.* Same with *divorce*: *She **divorced** him.* / *They **got divorced**.*

## Matter

### Matter as a verb

*Matter* = be important. It is used mainly in negatives and questions, with *it* or *what* as subject: *It **doesn't matter**.* ***Does** it **matter** if I'm late?* *What **matters** is that you're safe.* Not continuous, not passive.

### Matter as a noun

» ***What's the matter (with you)?*** = What's wrong?
» countable: *a private **matter*** (subject, situation)
» uncountable: *organic **matter*** (substance)
» ***as a matter of fact*** = actually; ***in a matter of** minutes* = in only a few minutes; ***no matter** what / how / who…* = it doesn't matter: ***No matter what** happens, I'll be there.*

## Mean

» **understanding**: *What does this word **mean**?* *What do you **mean (by that)**?*
» **intention**: *I **meant to** call you.* (+ *to*-infinitive) *I didn't **mean** it.* *She **means** well.*
» **significance**: *Your friendship **means** a lot to me.*
» **result / involve**: *A new job **means moving** house.* (+ *-ing*)
» **be meant to** = be supposed to
» **means** (noun) = method: *a **means** of transport*
» ***I mean*** (explaining or correcting), ***You know what I mean?***

### Typical errors

» ~~What means this word?~~ → *What **does** this word **mean**?*
» ~~I mean that it's good~~ for an opinion → *I **think** it's good.*

## Miss

*Miss* has several meanings:

» **fail to catch, hit or do**: *I **missed** the bus.* *He **missed** the goal.* *Don't **miss** the chance.*
» **feel sad because someone or something is absent**: *I **miss** you.* *I **miss** living by the sea.*
» **notice something is not there / lose**: *When did you **miss** your wallet?* (usually *lose* = not have any more: *I **lost** my wallet*)
» **lack** (as *missing*): *There's a page **missing**.*

## Prefer

### Expressing preference

» *prefer* + noun / *-ing*: *I **prefer** tea **to** coffee.* *I **prefer walking to driving**.* (**to**, not *than*)
» *prefer* + *to*-infinitive: *I **prefer to** walk.* *I prefer to walk **rather than** drive.*

### Would prefer

For a particular situation: *I'**d prefer to** stay in tonight.* *I'd prefer you **not to** smoke.* / *I'd prefer it **if** you didn't smoke.*

### Typical errors

» ~~I prefer tea than coffee.~~ → *I prefer tea **to** coffee.*
» ~~I'd prefer staying~~ → *I'd prefer **to stay***

## Put

*Put* (*put, put, put*) means move something to a place. It always needs an object and a place: ***Put** your bag **on the table**.* Not: ~~Put it.~~ (alone) Common phrasal verbs: *put on* (clothes), *put off* (postpone), *put up with* (tolerate), *put out* (a fire), *put away* (tidy).

## See

*See* is about perception (the ability to see), understanding and meeting: *I can't **see** anything.* *I **see** what you mean.* ***See** you tomorrow.* *I'm **seeing** the doctor at 3* (meeting — continuous possible). *See* + object + bare infinitive / *-ing* (see [Verb patterns](verbs/verb-patterns)). With *can*: *I **can see** the sea* (more natural than *I see* for a continuing perception).

## Seem

### Seem as a linking verb

*Seem* + adjective or noun: *You **seem** tired.* *It **seems** a good idea.* *She **seems** (**to be**) nice.*

### Seem + to-infinitive

*He **seems to know** a lot.* *They **seem to have left**.* (perfect infinitive) ***It seems (that)** she's right.* ***There seems to be** a problem.* *Seem* is not used in the continuous.

## Suggest

### Patterns

» + noun: *Can you **suggest** a good hotel?*
» + *that*-clause: *I **suggest (that)** we **leave** now.* / *…that we **should** leave* (Br)
» + *-ing*: *She **suggested going** to the beach.*
» + *wh*-word: *Can you **suggest where** to eat?*
» someone something **to** someone: *She **suggested** a restaurant **to me**.*

### Typical errors

» ~~I suggest you to go.~~ → *I suggest **(that) you go***.
» ~~She suggested me a book.~~ → *She suggested a book **to me***.

## Take

*Take* means move something away with you, or accept / use / need:

» *Don't forget to **take** your keys.* (*take* away from here / *bring* to here)
» *It **takes** two hours to get there.* (time) / *It **took** me an hour to finish.*
» ***take** a bus / taxi* (transport), ***take** medicine*, ***take** a size 38*
» fixed phrases: ***take** a photo, a shower (Br also *have*), a break, a seat, an exam, part (in), place, care (of), notes, a decision (Br), a look*

### Typical errors

» ~~make a photo~~ → ***take** a photo*; ~~take a coffee~~ (Br usually) → ***have** a coffee*

## Think

» **opinion**: *I **think** it's a good idea.* (state verb, no continuous) *I **don't think** he's coming.* (not ~~I think he isn't~~ — less natural)
» **plans and decisions** (continuous possible): *I'**m thinking of / about** moving to Spain.*
» **remember / imagine**: *I can't **think of** her name.* ***Think of** a number.*
» **consider**: *Let me **think about** it.*
» **softening**: *I **think** we should go now.*
» **uncertainty**: *"Is she coming?" "I **think so** / I **don't think so**."*

## Want

### Wish or desire

*Want* + noun / *to*-infinitive / object + *to*-infinitive: *I **want** a coffee.* *I **want to** go.* *I **want you to** stay.* Not: ~~I want that you stay~~.

### With wh-words and if

*Do **whatever** you **want**.* *You can come with us **if** you **want**.* (no *to* needed)

### In the continuous

Rare, but possible for a temporary or polite desire: *I'**ve been wanting** to tell you…*

### Need, advice and warnings

Informal British: *The car **wants** cleaning* (= needs). Advice: *You **want to** be careful there* (= should). *Would like* is more polite than *want* in requests.

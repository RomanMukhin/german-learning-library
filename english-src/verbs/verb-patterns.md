## Hate, like, love and prefer

These verbs can take ***-ing*** or a ***to*-infinitive** with little difference in British English: *I **like swimming / to swim**.* *-ing* often stresses enjoyment of the activity; *to* + infinitive can suggest a habit or choice: *I **like to go** to the dentist twice a year* (it's a good idea, not fun).

### Would + hate, like, love, prefer

With *would*, use the ***to*-infinitive**, for a specific occasion: *I **would love to** come.* *I'**d prefer to** stay home.* *I'**d hate to** be late.*

## Hear, see, etc. + object + infinitive or -ing

With verbs of perception (*see, hear, watch, notice, feel, smell*):

» object + **bare infinitive** = the whole action, from start to finish: *I **saw** her **cross** the road.*
» object + **-ing** = part of an action in progress: *I **saw** her **crossing** the road.* (when I looked, she was in the middle)

### Typical errors

» ~~I heard him to sing.~~ → *I heard him **sing / singing**.* (passive: *He was heard **to** sing*)

## Help somebody (to) do

*Help* can be followed by an object + infinitive **with or without *to***: *She **helped** me **(to) move** house.* The form without *to* is more common, especially in American English. *Help* can also be followed directly by an infinitive: *This will **help (to) reduce** costs.* *Can't help + -ing* means "can't stop yourself": *I **can't help laughing**.*

## Look forward to

In *look forward to*, *to* is a **preposition**, so it is followed by a noun or ***-ing***: *I'**m looking forward to the holiday**.* *I **look forward to hearing** from you.* (formal letters) Not: ~~look forward to hear~~.

## Stop + -ing form or to-infinitive

The meaning changes:

» ***stop + -ing*** = finish doing an activity: *I **stopped smoking** last year.*
» ***stop + to*-infinitive** = stop one activity in order to do another: *We **stopped to have** lunch.* (we stopped walking, to eat)

## Verb patterns: verb + infinitive or verb + -ing?

### Verbs followed by a to-infinitive

*afford, agree, arrange, ask, choose, decide, expect, fail, hope, learn, manage, need, offer, plan, pretend, promise, refuse, seem, threaten, want, would like*

» *She **agreed to** help.* *I can't **afford to** buy it.*

### Verbs followed by -ing

*admit, avoid, consider, deny, dislike, enjoy, finish, imagine, involve, keep, mind, miss, practise, recommend, risk, suggest, can't stand, give up, put off*

» *I **enjoy reading**.* *Would you **mind waiting**?* Not: ~~enjoy to read~~

### New subject before -ing

*I don't mind **him / his** coming.* (object pronoun is more common in speech; possessive is formal)

### Verbs followed by either, with a change in meaning

| Verb | + -ing | + to-infinitive |
|---|---|---|
| remember | memory of the past: *I **remember locking** the door.* | not forgetting a task: ***Remember to lock** the door.* |
| forget | (negative) memory: *I'll never **forget seeing** her.* | not do a task: *I **forgot to call** her.* |
| try | experiment: ***Try restarting** the computer.* | make an effort: *I **tried to open** it, but it was stuck.* |
| regret | sorry about the past: *I **regret saying** that.* | (formal) sorry to announce: *We **regret to inform** you…* |
| go on | continue: *She **went on talking**.* | change to a new activity: *She **went on to become** a doctor.* |
| mean | involve: *It **means getting** up early.* | intend: *I **meant to** call.* |

### Verbs followed by either, with little change

*begin, start, continue, like, love, hate, prefer*: *It **started raining / to rain**.*

### To-infinitive after an object

*advise, allow, ask, encourage, expect, forbid, force, invite, order, persuade, remind, teach, tell, want, warn*: *She **told me to** wait.* *I **want you to** come.*

## Verb patterns: verb + that-clause

### Reporting verbs + that-clause

*say, think, believe, know, hope, explain, agree, decide, suggest, promise, realise, admit*: *She **said (that)** she was busy.* *That* is often left out in speech.

### Indirect object + that-clause

*tell, inform, remind, promise, assure, convince, persuade, warn* + person + *that*: *He **told me that** he was leaving.* *She **assured us that** it was safe.*

### Prepositional phrase + that-clause

*explain, admit, mention, suggest, complain, say* + *to* someone + *that*: *She **explained to me that** the shop was closed.* Not: ~~She explained me that~~.

## Verb patterns: with and without objects

### Transitive and intransitive

» **transitive** verbs need an object: *She **bought** a car.* ~~She bought.~~ (*buy, enjoy, make, need, want, describe, discuss*)
» **intransitive** verbs have no object: *He **arrived**.* *The baby **is sleeping**.* (*arrive, come, go, happen, sleep, die, fall, wait*)
» many verbs are both: *She **opened** the door* / *The door **opened**.* *I'm **reading** (a book)*.

### Direct and indirect objects

Verbs like *give, send, show, tell, offer, lend, buy, bring, teach* can take two objects:

» person + thing: *She **gave me the book**.*
» thing + *to / for* + person: *She **gave the book to me**.* *He **bought flowers for her**.*

With *explain, describe, suggest, say, announce*, only the second pattern is possible: *Explain **the rule to me**.* Not: ~~Explain me the rule~~.

### Object + complement

*They **elected** her **president**.* *The news **made** him **angry**.* *We **painted** the room **white***.

## Would like

*Would like* is a polite way of saying *want*, for offers, requests and wishes: *I'**d like** a coffee, please.* ***Would** you **like** to come?* It is followed by a noun or a ***to*-infinitive**: *I'd like **to** go*, not ~~I'd like going~~. *Would you like* (offer / invitation) ≠ *Do you like* (general preference).

### Would like to have + past participle

This refers to a past wish that didn't happen: *I **would like to have seen** the show* (= I didn't see it). Also: *I would have liked to see it.*

### I'd like to

As a reply: *"Would you like to join us?" "**I'd like to**, but I'm busy."* (no verb repeated)

## Would rather, would sooner

### Same subject

*Would rather* (*'d rather*) + **bare infinitive** expresses preference: *I'**d rather stay** at home tonight.* *I'd rather walk **than** take the bus.* Negative: *I'**d rather not** go.*

### Different subjects

*Would rather* + subject + **past simple** (for present/future) or **past perfect** (for past): *I'**d rather you didn't** smoke here.* *I'd rather you **had told** me.*

### Much rather and short responses

*I'd **much rather** go by train.* *"Shall we go?" "**I'd rather not**."*

### Would sooner, would just as soon

*Would sooner* means the same (more informal, British). *I'**d just as soon** stay here* = I don't mind, I'd be equally happy to.

### Typical errors

» ~~I'd rather to stay.~~ → *I'd rather **stay**.*
» ~~I'd rather you don't go.~~ → *I'd rather you **didn't** go.*

## Phrasal verbs

A phrasal verb is a verb + an adverb particle (*up, down, off, out, in, on, away, back*): *give up*, *turn off*, *look out*, *break down*.

### Meaning

Sometimes the meaning is clear from the parts (*sit down, go out, come back*); often it is idiomatic and must be learnt as a new word: *give up* (stop trying), *put up with* (tolerate), *turn down* (refuse), *make up* (invent; become friends again).

### Formality

Phrasal verbs are very common in everyday spoken English and are often more informal than one-word equivalents:

| Phrasal verb | More formal |
|---|---|
| find out | discover |
| put off | postpone |
| look into | investigate |
| go up | increase |
| come across | encounter |

## Phrasal verbs and objects

Many phrasal verbs can be transitive. With a **noun** object, the particle can go before or after the object:

» *Turn **off** the light.* / *Turn the light **off**.*

With a **pronoun** object, the pronoun must go **between** the verb and particle:

» *Turn **it off**.* Not: ~~Turn off it.~~

Long objects usually go after the particle: *She **picked up** all the books from the floor.*

## Prepositional verbs

Prepositional verbs are a verb + a preposition, always followed by an object: *look **at**, listen **to**, depend **on**, believe **in**, care **for**, look **after**, deal **with***.

### Objects

The object always comes **after** the preposition, even when it is a pronoun:

» *Look **after** the children.* / *Look **after them**.* Not: ~~Look them after.~~

## Prepositional verbs or phrasal verbs?

| | Phrasal verb | Prepositional verb |
|---|---|---|
| particle | adverb | preposition |
| object position | *turn the TV off / turn it off* | *look at the TV / look at it* (never between) |
| stress | particle often stressed: *turn OFF* | preposition usually unstressed |
| without object? | sometimes: *The plane took off.* | no |

## Phrasal-prepositional verbs

These have a verb + adverb particle + preposition: *look forward **to***, *get on **with***, *put up **with***, *run out **of***, *catch up **with***, *come up **with***, *look up **to***.

### Objects

The object always comes after the whole verb: *I can't **put up with** this noise / it any longer.* Not: ~~put up it with~~.

» *We've **run out of** milk.*
» *She **came up with** a great idea.*
» *Do you **get on with** your boss?*

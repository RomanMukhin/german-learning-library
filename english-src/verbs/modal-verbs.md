## Modality: forms

### Modal verbs

The core modal verbs are *can, could, may, might, must, shall, should, will, would*. They:

» have **one form** for all persons (no *-s*): *She **can** swim.* Not: ~~She cans~~
» are followed by the **bare infinitive**: *You **must** go.* Not: ~~must to go~~
» make negatives with *not* and questions by inversion, without *do*: *I **can't**.* ***Can** you?*
» have contracted forms: *can't, couldn't, won't, wouldn't, shouldn't, mustn't, 'll, 'd*

Adverbs go after the modal: *You **can always** call me.*

### Semi-modal verbs

*Dare, need, ought to* and *used to* behave partly like modals: *You **needn't** come.* / *You **don't need to** come.*

## Modality: meanings and uses

Modal verbs show the speaker's attitude to how **likely**, **necessary**, **possible** or **desirable** something is.

| Meaning | Modals |
|---|---|
| ability | can, could, be able to |
| permission | can, could, may |
| possibility, probability | may, might, could, will, should, must, can't |
| obligation, necessity | must, have to, need to, should, ought to |
| advice | should, ought to, had better |
| offers, requests | can, could, will, would, shall |
| prediction, willingness | will, would |

## Modality: tense

### Past, present and future time

Modals don't have normal past forms. For the past we use ***modal + have + past participle*** or other verbs: *She **must have** forgotten.* *I **could have** helped.* *I **had to** work.* (past of *must* for obligation) *I **was able to** finish.*

### Politeness

Past forms are more polite: ***Could** you…?* ***Would** you…?* ***Might** I suggest…?*

### Reported clauses

*Will → would*, *can → could*, *may → might*, *shall → should* in reported speech: *She said she **would** come.*

### Continuous

*Modal + be + -ing*: *He **might be sleeping**.* *She **must have been working**.*

## Modality: other verbs

### Possibility

*appear, seem, look, sound, feel*: *It **seems** that he's left.* *It **looks as if** it's going to rain.* *She **appears to be** ill.*

### Obligation

*have (got) to, need to, be required to, be supposed to*: *I **have to** work on Saturday.* *You'**re supposed to** wear a tie.*

## Modality: other modal words and expressions

### Degrees of certainty

*certainly, definitely, probably, possibly, perhaps, maybe; certain, sure, likely, unlikely, possible*: *It's **likely** to rain.* *She'll **probably** be late.* ***There's a chance** that…*

### Degrees of obligation

*necessary, essential, compulsory, optional, forbidden, obliged*: *It's **essential** to book in advance.*

### Certainly and surely

*Certainly* = without doubt. *Surely* expresses strong belief and surprise at the possibility of being wrong: ***Surely** you know that!*

### Expressions with be

*be able to, be allowed to, be bound to, be certain / sure to, be likely to, be supposed to, be due to*: *It's **bound to** rain.* (= certain)

## Can

### Forms

*can / cannot (can't)*: *I **can** come.* *She **can't** swim.* ***Can** you help?*

### Uses

» **ability**: *She **can** speak four languages.*
» **permission** (informal): ***Can** I go now?* *You **can** use my car.*
» **general truths and possibility**: *It **can** be very cold here in winter.*
» **deduction** (negative): *That **can't** be Tom — he's in Paris.* (opposite of *must*)
» **requests and offers**: ***Can** you pass the salt?* ***Can** I help?*
» **reproaches**: *You **can** be so annoying!*

### Past and other forms

Past ability: ***could*** or ***was able to***. Future / infinitive / perfect: ***be able to***: *I'**ll be able to** come.* *I'd like **to be able to** swim.*

### Typical errors

» ~~I can to swim~~ → *I can **swim***; ~~I will can~~ → *I'**ll be able to***

## Could

### Forms

*could / could not (couldn't)*

### Uses

» **possibility**: *It **could** rain later.* *That **could** be the answer.*
» **suggestions**: *We **could** go to the cinema.*
» **polite permission and requests**: ***Could** I sit here?* ***Could** you help me?*
» **past ability** (general): *I **could** swim when I was five.* For a single past achievement, use ***was able to / managed to***: *I **was able to** fix it* (not ~~I could fix it~~ for one success); but *couldn't* is fine for single events: *I **couldn't** open it.*
» **deduction** (negative): *He **couldn't** have done it — he was with me.*
» **with senses and thinking**: *I **could** smell gas.* *I **couldn't** believe it.*
» **unreal situations, conditionals**: *I **could** buy a house if I had money.*
» **past possibility / missed opportunity**: *You **could have** told me!*

## Could, may and might

All three express **possibility**. *May* is slightly more formal and a little more certain; *might* and *could* are a little less certain:

» *It **may / might / could** rain.*

In negatives, the meaning differs: *It **may not / might not** rain* (possibly not) but *It **couldn't** rain* (= impossible). In questions about possibility, use *might* or *could*, not *may*: ***Could** it be true?*

## Dare

*Dare* means "be brave enough". It can be a modal (no *to*, no *do*) or a main verb:

» affirmative: *He **dared to** ask.* (main verb)
» negative: *I **daren't** tell her.* (modal, Br) / *I **don't dare (to)** tell her.*
» question: ***Dare** you ask?* (formal) / ***Do** you **dare** (to) ask?*
» fixed expressions: ***How dare you**!* ***Don't you dare**!* ***I dare say*** (= probably)

### Typical error

» ~~I daren't to tell~~ → *I daren't **tell***

## Had better

### Form and meaning

*Had better* (*'d better*) + bare infinitive gives strong advice or a warning about the present or future: *You'**d better** take an umbrella.* (or you'll get wet)

### Negative and questions

Negative: *You'**d better not** be late.* Questions are rare: ***Hadn't** we **better** go?*

### Had better or be better / would rather?

*It's better / best to…* is more general and less urgent: ***It's best to** book early.* *Would rather* expresses preference, not advice.

### Typical errors

» ~~You had better to go~~ → *had better **go***; ~~You'd better went~~ → *'d better **go*** (past form, present meaning)

## May

### Forms

*may / may not* (no common contraction)

### Uses

» **formal permission**: ***May** I come in?* *Visitors **may** park here.*
» **possibility**: *She **may** be at home.* *It **may** be expensive.*
» **general truths** (formal): *Side effects **may** include headaches.*
» **accepting a different view**: *He **may** be rich, but he's not happy.*
» wishes (formal): ***May** you both be happy.*

## Might

### Forms

*might / might not (mightn't, rare)*

### Uses

» **possibility** (often less certain than *may*): *I **might** go to the party.*
» **very polite permission**: ***Might** I make a suggestion?*
» **suggestions**: *You **might** want to check the time.*
» **criticism**: *You **might** have warned me!* (you should have)
» reported *may*: *She said she **might** come.*

### Typical error

» ~~I might to go~~ → *I might **go***

## Must

### Forms

*must / must not (mustn't)*

### Uses

» **deduction / conclusion** (sure it is true): *You **must** be tired after the journey.* *She **must have** left already.*
» **obligation from the speaker**: *I **must** call my mother.* *You **must** wear a seat belt.*
» **rules and laws**: *Passengers **must** not smoke.*
» **strong invitations and encouragement**: *You **must** come and visit us!*
» **prohibition**: *You **mustn't** touch that.* (not allowed)

### Must or have (got) to?

» *must* = obligation from the speaker; *have to* = external obligation (rules, other people): *I **must** stop smoking* (I've decided) / *I **have to** wear a uniform* (company rule)
» past and future: *had to*, *will have to* (must has no past)
» **don't have to** = not necessary ≠ **mustn't** = not allowed: *You **don't have to** come* (it's optional) / *You **mustn't** come* (it's forbidden)

### Typical errors

» ~~I must to go~~ → *I must **go***; ~~I musted~~ → *I **had to***

## Need

### Forms

*Need* can be a main verb (*need to*, with *do*) or, in negatives and questions, a modal (*needn't*):

» *You **need to** finish it.* *You **don't need to** come.* ***Do** I **need to** pay?*
» modal (formal, Br): *You **needn't** come.* ***Need** I say more?*

### Uses

» **no obligation**: *You **needn't** / **don't need to** worry.*
» past: ***didn't need to*** (it wasn't necessary, so maybe I didn't do it) vs ***needn't have*** + participle (I did it, but it was unnecessary): *I **needn't have** cooked so much.*
» *need* + *-ing* (passive meaning): *The walls **need painting**.*

### Typical error

» ~~You needn't to come~~ → *You needn't **come*** / *You **don't need to** come*

## Ought to

### Form

*ought to / ought not to (oughtn't to)* + base form. Questions (formal): ***Ought** we **to** leave?* More often: ***Should** we leave?*

### Uses

» what is right or ideal: *You **ought to** apologise.*
» what is likely or expected: *She **ought to** be home by now.*

### Ought to or should?

They mean nearly the same. *Should* is much more common, especially in questions and negatives. *Ought to* can sound a little stronger or more objective.

## Shall

### Forms

*shall / shall not (shan't, Br)*

### Uses

» **offers and suggestions** with *I* and *we* in questions: ***Shall I** help you?* ***Shall we** dance?* *What **shall** we do?*
» **predictions and intentions** with *I* and *we* (formal, Br): *We **shall** see.* *I **shall** be away next week.*
» **commands and rules** (formal, legal): *The tenant **shall** pay the rent monthly.*

### Typical error

» Not for normal future with *you / they* in modern English: ~~They shall arrive at six~~ → *They'**ll** arrive…*

## Should

### Forms

*should / should not (shouldn't)*

### Uses

» **what is right or ideal**: *We **should** recycle more.*
» **advice and suggestions**: *You **should** see a doctor.* *What **should** I do?*
» **what is likely**: *The parcel **should** arrive tomorrow.*
» **conditional** (formal, = if): ***Should** you need help, call me.*
» **thanking and surprise**: *You **shouldn't** have!* (when given a present) *It's strange that he **should** say that.*
» **past regret or criticism**: *You **should have** told me.* *I **shouldn't have** eaten so much.*
» after *suggest, insist, recommend* (British): *She suggested that we **should** leave.*

### Should and would; should and ought to

In formal British English, *should* can replace *would* with *I/we*: *I **should** be grateful if…* *Should* and *ought to* are similar; *should* is more common.

## Will

### Forms

*will / will not (won't)*; short form *'ll*. *'ll* is normal in speech; *will* in full adds emphasis.

### Uses

» **certainty and prediction**: *She'**ll** be 30 next year.* *I think it'**ll** rain.*
» **conditional sentences**: *If you heat ice, it **will** melt.*
» **decisions, willingness, offers, promises**: *I'**ll** help.* *I **won't** tell anyone.*
» **requests and invitations**: ***Will** you marry me?* ***Will** you have some tea?*
» **commands**: *You **will** do as I say!*
» **general truths and habits**: *Oil **will** float on water.* *She'**ll** sit there for hours.*
» **disapproval of habits** (stressed): *He **will** leave the door open!*
» **assumptions**: *That'**ll** be the postman.*

## Would

### Forms

*would / would not (wouldn't)*; short form *'d*

### Uses

» **polite requests and offers**: ***Would** you open the window?* ***Would** you like a drink?*
» **conditional sentences**: *If I had time, I **would** help.* *I **would have** come if you had asked.*
» **past habits** (not states): *When I was a child, we **would** go fishing.*
» **future in the past**: *She said she **would** call.*
» **past willingness / refusal**: *The car **wouldn't** start.*
» **being less direct**: *I **would** say it's about ten metres.* *I'**d** think so.*

### Would or will?

*Would* is more polite and tentative, and refers to imagined situations: *That **would** be nice* (if it happened) / *That **will** be nice* (it is going to happen).

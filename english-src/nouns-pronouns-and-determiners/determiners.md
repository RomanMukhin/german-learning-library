## A/an and the

### Meaning

**A/an** (indefinite article) introduces something new or not specific: *I saw **a** dog.* **The** (definite article) refers to something both speaker and listener know about: ***The** dog was huge.*

### A or an?

Choose by **sound**, not spelling: **a** before a consonant sound, **an** before a vowel sound.

» **a** book, **a** university /juː/, **a** one-way street /w/
» **an** apple, **an** hour /aʊ/, **an** MP /em/

### Pronouncing the

*The* is /ðə/ before consonant sounds (*the cat*) and /ðiː/ before vowel sounds (*the apple*), or when stressed for emphasis.

### Articles with types of nouns

| Noun | Not specific | Specific |
|---|---|---|
| singular countable | **a** car (never bare) | **the** car |
| plural countable | cars / **some** cars | **the** cars |
| uncountable | water / **some** water | **the** water |

**General statements** use no article with plurals and uncountables: ***Cats** like milk.* ***Life** is short.* Not: ~~The life is short.~~

### Common uses of the

» unique things: ***the** sun, **the** internet, **the** sea*
» everyday shared things: ***the** kitchen, **the** post office, **the** doctor*
» inventions and instruments: *play **the** piano*, *invent **the** telephone*
» groups in society: ***the** rich, **the** elderly, **the** British*
» dates: ***the** 5th of May*, ***the** 1990s*
» radio, newspapers and the internet: *on **the** radio*, *read **the** paper*; but usually ***on TV***

### No article

» before other determiners: ~~the my car~~ → *my car*
» jobs need *a/an*: *She's **a** doctor.* Not: ~~She's doctor.~~
» meals, most countries, cities, streets: *have lunch*, *in France*, *on Oxford Street* (but ***the** USA, **the** UK, **the** Netherlands*)
» institutions used for their purpose: *go to **school**, be in **hospital**, go to **prison**, go to **bed**, at **university*** (British); compare *I went to **the** school to meet the teacher.*

### Typical errors

» ~~I like the music.~~ (in general) → *I like **music**.*
» ~~He is engineer.~~ → *He is **an** engineer.*
» ~~an university~~ → ***a** university*

## Determiners (the, my, some, this)

Determiners come before nouns and before any adjectives. They do two main jobs:

» **referring** — telling us which thing: *the, a/an, this, that, these, those, my, your, his, her, its, our, their, whose*
» **quantifying** — telling us how many or how much: *some, any, no, all, both, each, every, many, much, few, little, several, enough, a lot of*

## Determiners and types of noun

| Determiner | Goes with |
|---|---|
| *a/an, each, every, either, neither, another, one* | singular countable |
| *much, a little, little, less* | uncountable |
| *many, a few, few, fewer, several, both, two, these, those* | plural countable |
| *some, any, no, all, enough, more, most, a lot of, plenty of* | plural countable **or** uncountable |
| *the, my, your, this/that, which, whose* | any noun |

» **each** day / **much** time / **several** books / **some** water, **some** people

## Determiners: position and order

Determiners come first in a noun phrase: *my new car*, not ~~new my car~~. Some can combine:

» **all / both / half** + (*the / my / these*) + noun: ***all the** students*, ***both my** parents*, ***half the** time*
» determiner + number: *the **first three** days*, *my **two** brothers*
» *many / few / little* after another determiner: *the **few** people who came*, *my **many** friends*

**One of, most of, some of, each of** etc. need a determiner before the noun: ***most of the** people*, not ~~most of people~~ (but *most people* is correct).

Determiners can be modified: ***almost** every*, ***nearly** all*, ***very** few*, ***quite a** lot*.

## Determiners: typical errors

» Two central determiners together: ~~the my book~~ → *my book* / *the book*
» ~~a my friend~~ → *a friend **of mine***
» ~~every students~~ → *every **student*** / *all **students***
» ~~much people~~ → ***many** people*
» ~~most of students~~ → ***most** students* / ***most of the** students*

## Determiners used as pronouns

Many determiners can stand alone as pronouns when the noun is understood:

» "Do you want some cake?" "Yes, I'd love **some**."
» **Both** were late. **Neither** knew the answer.
» I bought **this**. **Many** agreed.

*Every*, *no* and the possessives *my, your* cannot: use *every one*, *none*, *mine, yours* instead.

## Every

### Every: each member of a group

*Every* + singular countable noun + singular verb refers to all members of a group: ***Every** child **needs** love.*

### Regular situations

***every** day, **every** two weeks, **every** few minutes*

### Every day or everyday?

*Every day* (two words) is an adverb phrase: *I walk **every day**.* *Everyday* (one word) is an adjective: ***everyday** life*.

### Every single, every one, every other

» ***every single** time* (emphasis)
» ***every one** of them* (each individual) vs ***everyone*** (all people)
» ***every other** day* (one day yes, one day no)

### Typical errors

» ~~every of the students~~ → ***every one of** the students* / ***each of** the students*
» ~~Every people~~ → ***Everyone*** / ***All the** people*

## Possession (John's car, a friend of mine)

### Possessive 's

Add **'s** to singular nouns and to irregular plurals; add only **'** to plurals ending in *-s*:

» *my **sister's** room* (one sister) / *my **sisters'** room* (two or more)
» ***children's** books*, ***women's** rights*
» ***James's** / **James'** car* (both possible with names ending in *-s*)

With two owners together, put *'s* on the last: ***Tom and Anna's** house*.

### Possession with of

We usually use **of** for things and abstract ideas: *the end **of** the film*, *the roof **of** the house*. Use *'s* for people, animals, organisations and times: *the **company's** profits*, ***yesterday's** news*, *a **week's** holiday*.

### A friend of mine, a friend of John's

To say "one of my friends", use **a/an/this + noun + of + possessive pronoun / 's**: *a friend **of mine***, *a colleague **of John's***.

### When not to use 's

» not normally with things: ~~the table's leg~~ → *the leg **of** the table* (or *the table leg*)
» not after a long noun phrase: *the name **of the man who lives next door***

## Such

### Such as a determiner

*Such (a/an)* + (adjective +) noun gives emphasis: *She's **such a** kind person.* *They have **such** nice children.* Not: ~~a such~~.

### Such meaning "of this kind"

*I've never heard **such** stories before.* ***Such** behaviour is unacceptable.* *Such as* introduces examples: *fruit **such as** apples and pears*.

### Such … that

*Such … that* shows a result: *It was **such a** hot day **that** we stayed inside.*

## This, that, these, those

### As determiners

**This** (singular) and **these** (plural) point to things near the speaker in space or time; **that** and **those** point to things further away.

» **This** bag is mine. **That** one over there is yours.
» Look at **these** photos. / Do you remember **those** days?
» **this** morning, **this** week (now) / **that** day (in the past)

### As pronouns

They can stand alone: ***This** is delicious.* *Who's **that**?* With people, use them mainly when identifying: ***This** is my brother.* (introduction) *Hello, **this** is Sam.* (phone)

### Closeness, distance and shared knowledge

*This* can show emotional closeness or new information; *that* can show distance or shared knowledge: *I met **this** guy at a party…* (new) / ***That** restaurant you told me about was great.* (shared)

*That* and *those* also replace a noun to avoid repeating it: *The climate here is warmer than **that** of Scotland.*

## Whole

*Whole* is an adjective meaning "complete, all of". It goes after the determiner and before a singular noun: *the **whole** day*, *my **whole** life*, *a **whole** cake*. With plural nouns *whole* means "complete, not divided": ***Whole** towns were destroyed.*

*The whole of* + determiner + noun is also possible: ***the whole of** the country*. Use *all*, not *whole*, with uncountable nouns: ***all** the money*.

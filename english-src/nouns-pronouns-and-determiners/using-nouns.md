## Piece words and group words

### Piece words

They make uncountable nouns countable: ***a piece of** advice / furniture / news*, ***a slice of** bread*, ***a loaf of** bread*, ***a bar of** chocolate / soap*, ***a cup of** tea*, ***a drop of** water*, ***a sheet of** paper*, ***an item of** clothing*.

### Group words

They name a group of people, animals or things: *a **crowd** of people*, *a **team** of experts*, *a **herd** of cows*, *a **flock** of sheep / birds*, *a **bunch** of flowers / keys*, *a **set** of tools*, *a **pack** of cards* (British) / *wolves*.

## Comparison: nouns (more money, the most points)

### More, less and fewer

» **more** + plural or uncountable noun: ***more** cars*, ***more** money*
» **less** + uncountable: ***less** time*; **fewer** + plural: ***fewer** mistakes*
» *She has **more** friends **than** I do.*

### More and more, fewer and fewer

*There are **more and more** cars on the roads.* *We have **less and less** free time.*

### Most, least and fewest

*Who scored **the most** points?* *the **least** money*, *the **fewest** errors*.

## Nouns and gender

English nouns usually have no special female form. Many traditional *-ess* and *-man* words are now replaced by neutral words:

| Older form | Neutral form |
|---|---|
| actress, waitress, air hostess | actor, server, flight attendant |
| chairman, fireman, policeman | chair(person), firefighter, police officer |
| mankind | humankind, humanity |

Animal names sometimes have male and female forms: *lion / lioness*, *bull / cow*, *cock / hen*, *dog / bitch*. The general word is usually used for both.

## Reported speech: reporting nouns

Nouns such as *claim, suggestion, belief, promise, warning, advice, idea, fact, news, rumour* can introduce reported clauses:

» **a claim that** the product is safe
» **the suggestion that** we leave early
» **a promise to** help / **a warning not to** go
» **the question of whether** to continue

Adjectives can describe these nouns: *a **reasonable** suggestion*, *a **wild** rumour*.

## Age

» *She's 25* / *She's **25 years old*** / *She's **25 years of age*** (formal). Not: ~~She has 25 years.~~
» ***at the age of** 18* / ***at** 18*
» ***a 25-year-old** woman* (singular *year* in the hyphenated adjective) / *a **25-year-old*** (noun)
» *What **age** is she?* / *How **old** is she?*

## Half

### In noun phrases

***half (of)*** + determiner + noun: ***half (of) the** time*, ***half of** them* (*of* needed with pronouns). Without a determiner: ***half** an hour*, ***half** a kilo*. Not: ~~a half hour~~ (American), British uses *half an hour*.

### As an adverb, noun and adjective

» *I **half** expected it.* *It's **half** finished.*
» *two **halves***, *the second **half** of the match*
» *a **half** day*, *a **half** price* ticket

The verb agrees with the noun after *of*: ***Half of** the cake **is**…* / ***Half of** the students **are**…*

## Holiday and holidays

In British English, a **holiday** is time off for travel or rest: *We're going on **holiday** to Spain.* (no article in *on holiday*) **The holidays** often means school holidays. A **public holiday / bank holiday** is a national day off. American English uses **vacation** for time away, and *holiday* for special days like Thanksgiving.

## Mind

### Mind as a verb

*Mind* means "be annoyed by" and is used mainly in negatives and questions, followed by a noun or *-ing*:

» *I **don't mind** waiting.* (= it's OK)
» ***Would you mind** opening the window?* (polite request) — answer *No, not at all* if you agree
» ***Do you mind if** I sit here?* (asking permission)

### Never mind, mind you

***Never mind*** = it doesn't matter, don't worry. ***Mind you*** adds a contrasting point: *He's lazy. **Mind you**, he's very clever.* *Mind (out)!* / ***Mind** the gap* = be careful (British).

### Mind as a noun

*Make up your **mind**.* *I've changed my **mind**.* *What's on your **mind**?*

### Typical errors

» ~~Would you mind to open~~ → *Would you mind **opening***

## Opinion

*Opinion* is countable: *in **my opinion*** (not ~~according to my opinion~~), *What's your **opinion** of / on / about it?* *Have / hold / give / express an **opinion***. *Public **opinion*** is uncountable. Avoid ~~In my opinion, I think~~ (repetition).

## Promise

*Promise* is a verb and a noun. Patterns: ***promise to** do*, ***promise (someone) that** …*, ***promise** someone something*.

» I **promise to** call you. / She **promised** (me) **that** she'd come.
» **make / keep / break a promise**

## Reason

### Reason for and reason why

***the reason for*** + noun: *the **reason for** the delay*. ***the reason why / that*** + clause: *That's **the reason (why)** I left.*

### Reason + to-infinitive

*There's no **reason to** worry.* *We have every **reason to** believe her.*

Avoid ~~The reason is because…~~ in formal writing: *The reason is **that**…*

## Sort, type and kind

All mean "a group with similar qualities". Patterns: ***this sort / type / kind of*** + singular or uncountable noun; ***these sorts of*** + plural noun:

» ***What kind of** music do you like?*
» ***this type of** car* / ***these kinds of** problems*

In informal speech, *sort of* / *kind of* also mean "a little, in a way": *I'm **sort of** tired.*

## Thing and stuff

### Thing

*Thing* is a countable noun for an object, idea, event or action you can't or don't want to name: *What's that **thing**?* *The **thing** is, I don't have time.* *I have a lot of **things** to do.*

### Stuff

*Stuff* is uncountable and informal, used for things in general or material: *Leave your **stuff** here.* *What's this **stuff**?* Not: ~~stuffs~~.

## View

### As a noun

*View* means what you can see from a place (*a room with a **view***), or an opinion: *What are your **views** on this?* ***In my view***, … (formal) ***With a view to*** + *-ing* = with the aim of.

### As a verb

*View* (formal) = consider, or look at: *The plan was **viewed** as a success.* *Viewing* of a house.

## Way

### Way as a noun

*Way* means route, method or manner:

» *Is this the **way** to the station?* (route)
» *What's the best **way to** learn / **of** learning English?* (method)
» *I like **the way** she talks.* (manner; not ~~the way how~~)

### Way as an adverb

Informally, *way* means "very much": *It's **way** too expensive.* *way better*.

### In the way, on the way

*You're **in the way*** (blocking) / *I'm **on my way*** (travelling).

## Work (noun)

### Work as an uncountable noun

*Work* (activity or job) is uncountable: *I have a lot of **work**.* *She's looking for **work**.* Not: ~~a work~~, ~~works~~ for a job → *a **job***.

### At work

*He's **at work*** (at the workplace). *go to **work***, *start / finish **work*** (no article).

### Work as a countable noun

*A work* is a piece of art, music or literature: *a **work** of art*, *the complete **works** of Shakespeare*. *Roadworks* (British) are repairs on a road.

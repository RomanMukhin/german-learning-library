## Noun phrases: dependent words

A noun phrase is a noun (the **head**) with the words that depend on it. It can have four kinds of dependent words:

| Part | Examples |
|---|---|
| determiners | *a, the, my, some, this, every* |
| premodifiers (before the noun) | adjectives: *a **big** house*; nouns: *a **tennis** ball* |
| complements (complete the noun) | *the fact **that she left***, *a teacher **of English*** |
| postmodifiers (after the noun, add detail) | *the man **in the car***, *the book **I bought***, *a place **to stay*** |

### Nouns as premodifiers

A noun before another noun works like an adjective and is usually singular: *a **shoe** shop*, *a **ten-minute** walk* (not ~~a shoes shop~~, ~~ten-minutes walk~~).

### Complements or postmodifiers?

A complement is closely linked to the meaning of the noun (*his **fear of flying***); a postmodifier simply adds information (*the woman **with red hair***). Complements usually come first if both are present.

## Noun phrases: order

The normal order is: **determiner → number → adjectives → noun modifier → head noun → postmodifier**.

» **the** **first** **two** **long** **summer** **holidays** **in Spain**
» **those** **beautiful** **old** **wooden** **chairs** **that my grandfather made**

## Noun phrases: uses

Noun phrases can be:

» **subject**: ***The new manager** starts tomorrow.*
» **object**: *I've read **all her books**.*
» **complement**: *She's **a brilliant scientist**.*
» **part of a prepositional phrase**: *on **the table***
» **adjunct** (adverbial, especially time): *I saw him **last week**.* *We walked **all the way**.*

## Noun phrases and verbs

### Agreement

The verb agrees with the **head** noun, not with the nearest noun:

» **The box** of chocolates **is** on the table. Not: ~~are~~
» **The results** of the survey **were** surprising.

With *a number of* + plural noun, use a plural verb: *A number of people **have** complained.* With *the number of*, use singular: *The number of cars **has** increased.*

## Noun phrases: two noun phrases together

Two noun phrases can sit side by side to refer to the same thing (apposition): *My brother, **a doctor**, lives in Leeds.* *Our neighbour **Mrs Patel** has a new dog.*

### Punctuation

Use commas when the second phrase adds extra information: *Paris, **the capital of France**, is…* Don't use commas when the second phrase identifies which one: *the singer **Adele***, *my friend **Tom*** (if you have more than one friend).

Grammar tells you how to build a sentence; this section is about using it with real people. Who do you call du and who Sie? How do you start an email to a landlord? What time is halb drei, and why do Germans arrive at exactly that time? These are the things that make you sound natural (and polite) in Germany, Austria and Switzerland.

Which one when

  • You are talking to someone and wonder which pronoun to use: formal and informal.
  • You are talking about when something happens: clock and time, plus prepositions of time for am, um, im, seit, vor.
  • You want the small words that make speech sound native (doch, mal, halt, eben) and set phrases: particles and expressions.
  • You want the list of false friends and classic learner errors (bekommen is not "become", Gift is poison): gotchas.

When in doubt, Sie

If you do not know whether to say du or Sie to an adult, start with Sie. Nobody is offended by Sie, and the other person can offer du. The opposite (an unwanted du) can sound rude, and switching back is awkward.

Punctuality is a real thing

The German saying Pünktlichkeit ist die Höflichkeit der Könige ("punctuality is the politeness of kings") is a translation of a line attributed to the French king Louis XVIII. In Germany, five minutes late for a business meeting already needs a short message, while for a private party arriving 10 to 15 minutes late is normal. Deutsche Bahn trains, famously, are another story.

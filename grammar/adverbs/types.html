Types of adverbs

Place and direction (hier, dorthin, hin and her), time and frequency (schon, noch, morgens), manner and degree, comment words like leider, deshalb and trotzdem, and the da- and wo-words.

Adverbs are easiest to learn in families: words that answer the same question. This page goes through the families one by one, with the words you need most at A2 and B1 and the traps in each group.

A1–A2 core

da and dort both mean "there"; dort is a bit further away or more formal, da is the everyday word and can also mean "here, present": Ist Frau Klein da? ("Is Ms Klein in?").

Direction: wohin? woher? with hin and her

German marks direction on the adverb itself. hin means movement away from the speaker, her movement towards the speaker. They attach to place adverbs and question words:

  • Komm bitte hierher! — Please come here!
  • Geh nach oben und hol deine Jacke. — Go upstairs and get your jacket.
  • Herein! — Come in! (said by the person inside)
  • Wo kommst du her? / Wo gehst du hin? — Where are you coming from? / Where are you going? (woher, wohin split in speech)

hin = hence, her = here

Point with your hand: hin goes away from you, her comes towards you. In speech both shrink to r-: rein, raus, rauf, runter, rüber, so you do not need to choose there. In writing, the person outside says Ich gehe hinein, the person inside says Komm herein.

Combine a day adverb with a part of the day: heute Morgen, gestern Abend, morgen Nachmittag. The part of the day is a noun and keeps its capital letter. "Tomorrow morning" is morgen früh (not morgen Morgen).

Frequency: wie oft?

From most to least: immer (always) – meistens (mostly) – oft (often) – manchmal (sometimes) – selten (rarely) – fast nie (hardly ever) – nie (never). Also täglich, wöchentlich, jährlich (daily, weekly, yearly) and einmal, zweimal, dreimal (once, twice, three times).

Days and parts of the day with -s become adverbs for repeated events, written in lower case: montags (on Mondays), morgens (in the mornings), abends, nachts, sonntags. Compare: am Montag (this one Monday) vs montags (every Monday).

Manner and degree: wie? wie sehr?

  • Manner: most adjectives (schnell, langsam, gut, laut) plus so (like this), anders (differently), zusammen (together), allein (alone), gern (gladly), umsonst (for free / in vain).
  • Degree, before an adjective or adverb: sehr (very), ziemlich (quite), ganz (quite / completely), besonders (especially), etwas (a bit), zu (too), so (so), fast (almost), kaum (hardly), genug (enough, after the adjective: alt genug).

gern is not an adjective

gern (or gerne) + verb is how German says "like doing": Ich koche gern ("I like cooking"). It cannot describe a noun and has no ending. For liking a thing or person, use mögen or gefallen: Ich mag Pizza, Der Film gefällt mir. And Gern! alone means "With pleasure!" or "You're welcome."

B1

schon, noch, erst

These three small time adverbs are everywhere and often confuse learners:

  • schon = already, as early as: Bist du schon fertig? ("Are you finished already?"), schon um sechs.
  • noch = still; noch nicht = not yet; nicht mehr = no longer: Er schläft noch. Ich bin noch nicht fertig. Sie wohnt nicht mehr hier.
  • erst = only, not until (later or less than expected): Er ist erst fünf ("He's only five"), Der Zug kommt erst um neun ("not until nine"). For "only" in the sense of quantity, use nur: Ich habe nur zehn Euro.

Comment adverbs: what the speaker thinks

Some adverbs comment on the whole sentence rather than on the verb. They tell how sure the speaker is or how they feel about it:

  • certainty: sicher, bestimmt (certainly), wahrscheinlich (probably), vielleicht (maybe), möglicherweise (possibly)
  • feeling: leider (unfortunately), hoffentlich (hopefully), zum Glück / glücklicherweise (luckily), natürlich (of course)

Many adverbs of this kind end in -weise, made from an adjective + -er-: glücklicherweise, normalerweise, überraschenderweise. From a noun, -weise means "by the ...": stundenweise (by the hour), teilweise (partly).

Reason and consequence: deshalb, trotzdem

These adverbs connect two main clauses. They count as a normal sentence element, so when they come first the verb follows immediately:

Adverb Meaning Example
deshalb, deswegen, darum, daherthat's why, thereforeIch bin krank, deshalb bleibe ich zu Hause.
alsoso, therefore (not "also"!)Es ist spät, also gehen wir.
trotzdemnevertheless, anywayEs regnet, trotzdem gehen wir joggen.
sonstotherwiseBeeil dich, sonst verpasst du den Bus.
dannthenErst essen wir, dann gehen wir ins Kino.
nämlichyou see, because (never first)Ich bin müde. Ich habe nämlich schlecht geschlafen.

also ≠ also

German also means "so, therefore". English "also" is auch: Ich komme auch ("I'm coming too"). Ich komme also means "So I'm coming".

Pronominal adverbs: da- and wo-words

A preposition + "it/that" for a thing becomes one word: da(r)- + preposition. A preposition + "what" becomes wo(r)- + preposition. The -r- appears before a vowel: damit, dafür, davon but darauf, daran, darüber; womit, wofür but worauf, woran, worüber.

  • Das ist mein neues Handy. Ich bin sehr zufrieden damit. — That's my new phone. I'm very happy with it.
  • Worüber sprecht ihr? – Über die Party. – Ach, darüber will ich nichts hören. — What are you talking about? – The party. – Oh, I don't want to hear about that.
  • Ich habe keine Zeit dafür. — I don't have time for it.

For people, keep the preposition with a pronoun: mit ihm, auf wen?. Fixed prepositions after verbs, adjectives and nouns are on Verbs with prepositions and Adjectives and nouns with prepositions. As relative words, the wo-forms also refer to a whole clause: Er hat angerufen, worüber ich mich sehr gefreut habe.

English used to do this too

Old-fashioned English legal language still has pronominal adverbs: thereby, therefore, thereof, whereby, wherefore, herewith. They are built exactly like German dadurch, dafür, davon, wodurch, wofür, hiermit. German simply kept the pattern alive in everyday speech.

Later: adverbs as particles

Some adverbs double as modal particles with a different meaning: Das ist schon gut ("That's fine, really"), Komm doch mit!, Das ist ja toll!. They add tone rather than content; see Particles and expressions.

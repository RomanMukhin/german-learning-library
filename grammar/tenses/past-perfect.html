Plusquamperfekt (past perfect)

The "had done" tense: hatte or war + Partizip II for the earlier of two past events, with nachdem, bevor and schon.

The Plusquamperfekt is the past of the past. When you are already telling a story in the past and need to step back to something that happened even earlier, you use it: Als wir ankamen, hatte der Film schon angefangen (When we arrived, the film had already started). It works almost exactly like the English past perfect had done, and if you know the Perfekt you already know how to build it.

B1

Form: hatte / war + Partizip II

Take the Perfekt and put the helper verb into the Präteritum: habe → hatte, bin → war. The participle stays the same and goes to the end.

The choice between haben and sein is exactly the same as in the Perfekt: movement and change of state take sein, everything else haben. The participle is built the same way too (gemacht, gesehen, verstanden, angekommen, studiert); see past tenses and strong and mixed verbs.

When to use it

You need two past moments. The later one is told in the Präteritum or Perfekt; the earlier one goes into the Plusquamperfekt.

  • Ich war müde, weil ich schlecht geschlafen hatte. — I was tired because I had slept badly.
  • Als die Polizei kam, war der Dieb schon verschwunden. — When the police came, the thief had already disappeared.
  • Sie erzählte mir, dass sie früher in Rom gewohnt hatte. — She told me that she had lived in Rome before.
  • Wir hatten den Weg vergessen und mussten jemanden fragen. — We had forgotten the way and had to ask someone.

Typical signal words: schon, bereits (already), vorher, zuvor (before that), noch nie (never before), and the conjunctions nachdem and, from the other side, bevor.

Draw the two dots

If you can mark two points on a timeline and the action you are describing sits on the earlier dot, use the Plusquamperfekt. One past event on its own never needs it: Gestern habe ich gearbeitet, not Gestern hatte ich gearbeitet.

nachdem: the tense rule

nachdem (after) always marks the earlier action, so the tense in the nachdem-clause is one step further back than in the main clause:

nachdem-clausemain clauseExample
PlusquamperfektPräteritum or PerfektNachdem er gegessen hatte, ging er ins Bett. — After he had eaten, he went to bed.
PerfektPräsens or FuturNachdem er gegessen hat, geht er ins Bett. — After he has eaten, he goes to bed.

With bevor the order is reversed: the main clause holds the earlier action. Bevor ich nach Deutschland zog, hatte ich schon zwei Jahre Deutsch gelernt. — Before I moved to Germany, I had already learnt German for two years. Since bevor already makes the order clear, the Präteritum is also fine there.

Word order

Main clause: hatte/war in position 2, participle at the end. Subordinate clause: participle, then hatte/war at the very end.

  • Ich hatte das Buch schon gelesen. — main clause
  • …, weil ich das Buch schon gelesen hatte. — subordinate clause
  • Hattest du ihn vorher schon einmal gesehen? — Had you seen him before?

Classic mistakes

  • Using it as a "more past" past tense for a single event: Letztes Jahr war ich in Spanien gewesen → Letztes Jahr war ich in Spanien.
  • Nachdem ich aß, … → Nachdem ich gegessen hatte, … (the nachdem-clause must be a step further back).
  • Mixing helpers: Der Zug hatte abgefahren → Der Zug war abgefahren.
  • Translating every English had as Plusquamperfekt: I had a cold is just Ich hatte eine Erkältung (Präteritum of haben).
  • nachdem is a subordinating conjunction: the verb goes to the end, and the main clause starts with its verb: Nachdem wir angekommen waren, riefen wir an.

The double Perfekt

In casual southern speech, where the Präteritum has died out, people build a "Perfekt of the Perfekt" to say had done: Ich habe das schon vergessen gehabt instead of Ich hatte das schon vergessen. Linguists call it doppeltes Perfekt. You will hear it, but it is not standard: do not write it in an exam.

Later: modals and the passive

With modal verbs, the Plusquamperfekt uses hatte and a double infinitive: Er hatte früher gehen müssen. (He had had to leave earlier.) In a subordinate clause, hatte then stands before the two infinitives: …, weil er früher hatte gehen müssen. The passive Plusquamperfekt is war … worden: Das Haus war 1900 gebaut worden. (The house had been built in 1900.)

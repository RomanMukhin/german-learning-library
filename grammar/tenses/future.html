Future tenses

Futur I with werden, why Germans usually just use the present, the three jobs of werden, and Futur I for guesses.

German has a future tense, Futur I (ich werde arbeiten), but in everyday speech it is used less than you might expect. Most plans are said with the present plus a time word. Futur I is for promises, predictions, emphasis and, surprisingly often, for guesses about the present.

A2 core

Futur I = werden + infinitive

werden is conjugated in position 2; the infinitive goes to the very end.

werden + infinitive
ich werde ... kommen
du wirst ... kommen
er/sie/es wird ... kommen
wir werden ... kommen
ihr werdet ... kommen
sie/Sie werden ... kommen
  • Ich werde dich anrufen. — I will call you.
  • Es wird morgen regnen. — It will rain tomorrow.
  • Wirst du mir helfen? — Will you help me?
  • Wir werden das schaffen. — We will manage it.
  • Sie werden es nicht glauben. — You won't believe it.

The present for the future

When a time word makes the future clear, Germans normally use the plain present. This is neutral and very common.

  • Morgen fahre ich nach München. — Tomorrow I'm going to Munich.
  • Nächste Woche habe ich Urlaub. — I'm on holiday next week.
  • Ich komme gleich. — I'll be right there.

Time word? Then just use the present

If your sentence already has morgen, später, bald, nächstes Jahr, am Montag, gleich, use the present and stop thinking. Save werden for when you want to sound sure, solemn, or for a prediction without a time word.

B1

The three jobs of werden

werden is one of the most useful verbs in German, and it does three different things depending on what comes with it.

Job Structure Example Meaning
1. to become werden + noun/adjective Er wird Arzt. Es wird kalt. He is becoming a doctor. It's getting cold.
2. future werden + infinitive Er wird kommen. He will come.
3. passive werden + Partizip II Das Haus wird gebaut. The house is being built.

So you identify the job by looking at the end of the sentence: an infinitive (-en) means future, a participle (ge-...-t/-en) means passive, nothing verbal means "become". For the passive, see the passive page; for werden itself, see sein, haben, werden.

Look at the last word

Ich werde müde (adjective: become), ich werde schlafen (infinitive: future), ich werde gefragt (participle: passive). The last word tells you which werden you are reading.

Futur I for guesses

Very often Futur I is not about the future at all. With wohl, sicher, wahrscheinlich or just by itself, it expresses an assumption about the present.

  • Er ist nicht da. Er wird krank sein. — He's not here. He's probably ill.
  • Das wird wohl stimmen. — That's probably right.
  • Sie wird schon zu Hause sein. — She's probably home by now.
  • Wo ist mein Handy? — Es wird im Auto liegen. — It's probably in the car.

This is close to English That'll be the postman when the doorbell rings.

Futur I for promises and firm intentions

Using werden where the present would do adds weight: Ich werde es nie vergessen. (I will never forget it.) Das werde ich nicht machen! (I'm not going to do that!)

In subordinate clauses

In a subordinate clause, werden goes to the very end, after the infinitive: Ich glaube, dass es morgen regnen wird. — I think it will rain tomorrow.

Classic future mistakes

  • Ich will morgen kommen to mean "I will come": wollen means want. Say Ich werde kommen or Ich komme morgen.
  • Er werdet kommen → er wird kommen; du werdest → du wirst.
  • Ich werde gekommen → ich werde kommen (infinitive, not participle; the participle makes it passive or Futur II).
  • würde is not the future: ich würde kommen = I would come (Konjunktiv II).
  • Overusing Futur I for every plan sounds stiff. Morgen gehe ich ins Kino is what people say.

English "will" is German "wollen"

English will and German wollen come from the same Germanic root, which meant to want. English turned it into a future marker; German kept the old meaning and built its future with werden instead, which comes from a root meaning to turn, to become (related to Latin vertere). So I will and ich will are cousins that went different ways.

Later: Futur II

Futur II = werden + Partizip II + haben/sein. It says something will be finished by a point in the future: Bis Freitag werde ich das Buch gelesen haben. (By Friday I will have read the book.) More often it is a guess about the past: Er wird den Zug verpasst haben. (He has probably missed the train.) In speech people usually replace it with the Perfekt: Bis Freitag habe ich das Buch gelesen.

Strong and mixed verbs

Weak, strong and mixed verbs: how to recognise each type, the vowel patterns that make the strong verbs learnable, and the 60 forms you need most.

Every German verb belongs to one of three families. Weak verbs are the regular ones (machen, machte, gemacht). Strong verbs change their stem vowel in the past (fahren, fuhr, gefahren). Mixed verbs do a bit of both (bringen, brachte, gebracht). There are only about 170 strong and mixed verbs in everyday use, but they are the most frequent verbs in the language, so you meet them in almost every sentence.

A2 core

The three families side by side

So the participle ending is your first clue: -en (gesehen, geschrieben) means strong; -t with a changed vowel (gedacht, gekannt) means mixed; -t with the same vowel (gekauft) means weak.

Learn three forms at once

You cannot tell from the infinitive whether a verb is strong. trinken is strong (trank, getrunken) but winken (to wave) is weak (winkte, gewinkt). So learn every new strong or mixed verb as a little chant of its principal parts (Stammformen), with the helper verb and, if it changes, the er-form of the present:

  • fahren – er fährt – fuhr – ist gefahren — to drive, go
  • sprechen – er spricht – sprach – hat gesprochen — to speak
  • schreiben – schrieb – hat geschrieben — to write
  • denken – dachte – hat gedacht — to think

New verbs are always weak

Verbs that entered German recently are weak: googeln – googelte – gegoogelt, chatten – chattete – gechattet, joggen, mailen, and every verb in -ieren (telefonieren – telefonierte – telefoniert). If you have never seen a verb before and must guess, guess weak: you will usually be right.

Präteritum endings of strong verbs

Strong verbs take the changed stem and the same endings as the present, except that ich and er/sie/es have no ending at all. Compare with a weak verb:

Stems ending in -s, -ß, -z need an -e- in the du form (du lasest, du aßest, du saßest), though the short forms are also seen; stems ending in -t, -d add -e- for du and ihr: du batest, ihr fandet. Stems ending in -ie do not double the e: wir schrien (not schrieen).

The present tells you something too

If a verb changes e → i/ie or a → ä in the present (er spricht, er fährt), it is always strong. The reverse is not true: trinken and schreiben are strong with no change in the present.

Prefix verbs copy their base verb

A verb with a prefix conjugates exactly like the verb without it. Learn stehen – stand – gestanden and you also have:

  • verstehen – verstand – verstanden (inseparable prefix: no ge-)
  • aufstehen – stand auf – ist aufgestanden (separable: ge after the prefix)
  • entstehen – entstand – ist entstanden — to arise

The same goes for bekommen, ankommen, mitkommen (from kommen), anfangen (from fangen: fing an, angefangen), einladen (from laden: lud ein, eingeladen) and verkaufen, which is weak because kaufen is weak. See separable verbs.

  • Der Film fing um acht an, aber wir kamen zu spät. — The film started at eight, but we arrived late.
  • Ich habe nicht verstanden, was er gemeint hat. — I didn't understand what he meant.
  • Sie hat mir eine Karte geschrieben und Blumen gebracht. — She wrote me a card and brought flowers.
  • Wir sind den ganzen Tag gelaufen und haben kaum etwas gegessen. — We walked all day and hardly ate anything.

In southern Germany, Austria and Switzerland, liegen, sitzen and stehen take sein in the Perfekt: ich bin gesessen. Both are correct standard German in their regions.

Classic mistakes

  • Weak endings on strong verbs: er gehte, gesitzt, getrinkt → er ging, gesessen, getrunken.
  • Strong endings on mixed verbs: gedenkt, gebringt, gekennt → gedacht, gebracht, gekannt.
  • gewisst → gewusst; er wisste → er wusste.
  • Adding -e to ich/er in the strong Präteritum: ich kame, er sahe → ich kam, er sah.
  • Carrying the present-tense vowel change into the past: er nimmte → er nahm.
  • Giving prefix verbs ge-: geverstanden, gebekommen → verstanden, bekommen.

English has the same families

The vowel patterns are older than both languages, which is why English strong verbs often match German ones: sing – sang – sung / singen – sang – gesungen, drink – drank – drunk / trinken – trank – getrunken, give – gave – given / geben – gab – gegeben. Even the mixed verbs match: bring – brought and think – thought change the vowel and add a -t, just like bringen – brachte and denken – dachte.

Later: one verb, two conjugations

A few verbs are strong in one meaning and weak in another. Usually the strong form has no object and the weak form has one: Das Bild hing an der Wand (was hanging) vs Ich hängte das Bild an die Wand (I hung it up); Ich erschrak (I got a fright) vs Du erschrecktest mich (you frightened me). Others split by meaning: schaffen – schuf – geschaffen (to create) vs schaffen – schaffte – geschafft (to manage); bewegen – bewog – bewogen (to persuade) vs bewegen – bewegte – bewegt (to move).

Participles: Partizip I and II

How to form the Partizip II of regular, irregular, mixed and prefixed verbs, how to form the Partizip I, and what both are used for.

German verbs have two participles. The Partizip II (gemacht, gegangen) is the one you need every day: it builds the Perfekt, the Plusquamperfekt and the passive. The Partizip I (lachend, schlafend) is rarer and mostly written; it works like English -ing in front of a noun. Both can also act as adjectives, adverbs and nouns. This page shows how to form them and everything they are used for.

A2–B1 core: forming the Partizip II

The three basic patterns

Verb type Pattern Examples
regular (weak) ge- + stem + -t machen → gemacht, kaufen → gekauft, spielen → gespielt
irregular (strong) ge- + stem (often a new vowel) + -en sehen → gesehen, fahren → gefahren, trinken → getrunken, schreiben → geschrieben
mixed ge- + new vowel + -t bringen → gebracht, denken → gedacht, kennen → gekannt, wissen → gewusst

The vowel of a strong verb cannot be predicted from the infinitive alone, so learn the three forms together (trinken – trank – getrunken). The irregular verbs table groups them by vowel pattern.

Spelling and prefix rules

Rule Examples
Stem ends in -t, -d or a consonant + m/n: regular verbs add -et arbeiten → gearbeitet, warten → gewartet, baden → gebadet, öffnen → geöffnet, atmen → geatmet
Verbs in -ieren: no ge- studieren → studiert, telefonieren → telefoniert, reparieren → repariert
Inseparable prefixes be-, emp-, ent-, er-, ge-, miss-, ver-, zer-: no ge- besuchen → besucht, verstehen → verstanden, erklären → erklärt, gehören → gehört
Separable prefixes: ge- goes between prefix and stem einkaufen → eingekauft, anrufen → angerufen, aufstehen → aufgestanden
Separable + inseparable prefix: no ge- vorbereiten → vorbereitet, anerkennen → anerkannt

Where is the stress?

ge- only appears when the verb is stressed on its first syllable: MAchen → geMACHT, EINkaufen → EINgekauft. If the stress is further back, as in beSUchen, studIEren, verSTEhen, there is no ge-. That one rule explains both the -ieren verbs and the inseparable prefixes.

Same form, two verbs

Some participles look alike: gefallen is the Partizip II of both fallen (ist gefallen, fell) and gefallen (hat gefallen, pleased). gehört belongs to hören and gehören. The helper verb and the context tell you which one is meant.

B1: what the Partizip II is used for

Tenses and the passive

  • Perfekt: Ich habe den Film schon gesehen. – I have already seen the film.
  • Plusquamperfekt: Als ich ankam, war der Zug schon abgefahren. – When I arrived, the train had already left.
  • Passive: Die Fenster werden jedes Jahr geputzt. – The windows are cleaned every year.
  • State passive: Die Bank ist heute geschlossen. – The bank is closed today.

As an adjective, adverb or noun

In front of a noun the Partizip II takes normal adjective endings and has a finished or passive meaning: das reparierte Auto (the car that has been repaired), ein gekochtes Ei. More examples are on participles as adjectives.

  • adverb: Sie hörte gespannt zu. – She listened intently.
  • noun: der Verletzte (the injured man), die Angestellten (the employees), das Gesagte (what was said). As nouns they keep adjective endings: ein Verletzter, die Verletzten.
  • short commands, often shouted: Aufgepasst! (Watch out!), Hiergeblieben! (Stay here!), Stillgestanden! (Attention!)

B1: the Partizip I

Form

Infinitive + -d. It is completely regular; only sein and tun add an -e-.

Infinitive Partizip I Example
schlafen schlafend das schlafende Baby – the sleeping baby
wachsen wachsend eine wachsende Stadt – a growing city
lächeln lächelnd Er grüßte lächelnd. – He said hello with a smile.
sein seiend rare, mostly in philosophy texts
tun tuend rare: wohltuend (soothing)

Uses

The Partizip I always describes an action that is going on at the same time and is done by the noun itself (active meaning):

  • adjective: die steigenden Preise – the rising prices; ein spannendes Buch – an exciting book.
  • adverb (how?): Die Kinder kamen singend nach Hause. – The children came home singing.
  • noun for people doing something: die Studierenden (the students), die Reisenden (the travellers), ein Auszubildender (a trainee). Such forms are now common as gender-neutral words: die Studierenden instead of die Studentinnen und Studenten.

Not a progressive tense

English "-ing" forms tenses: I am reading. German Partizip I never does: Ich bin lesend is wrong. Say Ich lese (gerade). In the same way "I saw him leaving" is Ich sah ihn weggehen (infinitive), and "Swimming is healthy" is Schwimmen ist gesund (a noun made from the infinitive).

Active or passive?

The noun does it → Partizip I: die schreiende Frau (the woman who is shouting). It is done to the noun → Partizip II: der geschriebene Text (the text that has been written). Turn the phrase into a relative clause and you see which one you need.

B2: participles in compact written style

Extended participle phrases

Written German puts whole phrases between the article and the noun, where English uses a relative clause:

  • der seit Stunden am Gleis wartende Zug = der Zug, der seit Stunden am Gleis wartet – the train that has been waiting at the platform for hours
  • die im letzten Jahr renovierte Schule = die Schule, die im letzten Jahr renoviert wurde – the school that was renovated last year

Participle clauses

A participle can also open a short clause without a subject of its own. Partizip I shows something happening at the same time, Partizip II something that is finished or passive:

  • Laut lachend verließ sie den Raum. – Laughing loudly, she left the room.
  • Gut vorbereitet ging er in die Prüfung. – Well prepared, he went into the exam.
  • In Wien angekommen, rief ich sofort meine Eltern an. – Having arrived in Vienna, I called my parents at once.

zu + Partizip I

The Partizip I with zu in front of a noun means "that must / can be done": die zu lösende Aufgabe = die Aufgabe, die gelöst werden muss; die zu erwartenden Kosten = the costs to be expected. With separable verbs zu goes inside: die auszufüllenden Formulare.

ge- used to be optional

In older Germanic the ge- prefix was not part of every participle. Modern English lost it completely (Old English still had gecumen, "come"), Dutch kept it like German (gemaakt), and German verbs like werden still show the old prefix-free form in the passive: Er ist gefragt worden, not geworden.

Later: the double infinitive

Modal verbs and lassen have a normal participle (gekonnt, gemusst, gelassen) only when they stand alone: Das habe ich nicht gewollt. With another verb they use the infinitive instead: Ich habe nicht kommen können. Ich habe das Auto reparieren lassen. See modal verbs.

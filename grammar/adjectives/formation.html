Forming adjectives

How German builds adjectives from nouns, verbs and other adjectives: -ig, -lich, -isch, -bar, -los, -voll, -sam, un- and compound adjectives.

Many German adjectives are built from other words with a small set of endings (suffixes) and prefixes: die Sonne → sonnig, der Freund → freundlich, essen → essbar, glücklich → unglücklich. Once you know what each suffix means, you can understand thousands of adjectives you have never seen and often build the right one yourself. All of these adjectives take the normal adjective endings in front of a noun.

A2–B1 core

From nouns

Suffix Meaning Examples
-ig having, full of, like die Sonne → sonnig (sunny), der Schmutz → schmutzig (dirty), die Lust → lustig (funny), der Durst → durstig
-lich typical of, relating to der Freund → freundlich (friendly), der Tag → täglich (daily), der Beruf → beruflich (professional), der Herbst → herbstlich
-isch belonging to a place, a person or a field Europa → europäisch, Italien → italienisch, der Typ → typisch, die Technik → technisch
-los without die Arbeit → arbeitslos (unemployed), die Kosten → kostenlos (free), der Erfolg → erfolglos
-voll full of die Liebe → liebevoll (loving), der Wert → wertvoll (valuable), die Hoffnung → hoffnungsvoll
-reich / -arm with a lot / little of die Hilfe → hilfreich (helpful), das Fett → fettarm (low-fat), die Kalorie → kalorienarm
-frei free of der Zucker → zuckerfrei, der Alkohol → alkoholfrei, die Barriere → barrierefrei (step-free)
-haft like, in the manner of der Traum → traumhaft (dreamlike, wonderful), das Kind → kindhaft, der Fehler → fehlerhaft (faulty)
-(e)n / -ern made of (material) das Gold → golden, die Seide → seiden, das Holz → hölzern, das Silber → silbern

Notice the frequent umlaut (Tag → täglich, Jahr → jährlich, Holz → hölzern) and the linking -s- or -n- after some nouns (hoffnungsvoll, kalorienarm).

From verbs

Take the verb stem (infinitive minus -en) and add the suffix:

Suffix Meaning Examples
-bar can be done (English -able) essen → essbar (edible), machen → machbar (doable), lesen → lesbar (readable), erreichen → erreichbar (reachable)
-lich often also "can be done" verstehen → verständlich (understandable), erklären → erklärlich, bewegen → beweglich (movable, flexible)
-sam tending to sparen → sparsam (thrifty), schweigen → schweigsam (quiet), wirken → wirksam (effective)
-ig in the state of abhängen → abhängig (dependent), gelten → gültig (valid)
Partizip I / II doing / done lachen → lachend, kochen → gekocht: see Participles as adjectives

-bar = "-able", un- ... -bar = "in- ... -ible"

-bar works almost exactly like English -able, and with un- you get the negative: trinkbar → untrinkbar (undrinkable), vermeidbar → unvermeidbar (unavoidable), glaublich → unglaublich (unbelievable). If you can say "it can be ...ed", try -bar.

From other adjectives

-lich after a colour or quality softens it, like English -ish: rot → rötlich (reddish), blau → bläulich, krank → kränklich (sickly), süß → süßlich. Sometimes the meaning shifts completely: klein → kleinlich (petty, fussy), arm → ärmlich (shabby).

Prefixes

Prefix Meaning Examples
un- not, the opposite unglücklich, unmöglich, unfreundlich, unbekannt, unklar
ur- very old, original; very uralt (ancient), urgemütlich (super cosy)
miss- badly, wrongly missverständlich (misleading), missmutig (sullen)
super-, top-, hyper- (spoken) extremely supergut, topmodern, hypernervös

B1

Compound adjectives

German also makes adjectives by joining a noun or adjective in front. The first part often works as an intensifier or a comparison ("as ... as"):

  • eiskalt (ice-cold), steinhart (rock-hard), federleicht (light as a feather), schneeweiß (snow-white), todmüde (dead tired), blitzschnell (lightning-fast)
  • colour shades: hellblau (light blue), dunkelgrün, himmelblau, blauweiß
  • noun + adjective with a real meaning: lebensgefährlich (life-threatening), umweltfreundlich (eco-friendly), kinderleicht (very easy), sehenswert (worth seeing), arbeitsfähig (fit to work)

-lich or -isch? Pairs with different meanings

-lich (neutral or positive) -isch (often negative)
kindlich — childlike kindisch — childish
weiblich — female, feminine weibisch — effeminate (pejorative)
herrlich — wonderful herrisch — bossy, domineering
heimlich — secret heimisch — native, at home (die heimische Küche)

With places, -isch is the rule: französisch, spanisch, ukrainisch, japanisch, often with a change in the stem: Frankreich → französisch. Cities instead take -er, written with a capital and never declined: der Berliner Flughafen, die Wiener Oper, in der Kölner Altstadt.

Time words: -ig versus -lich

The two suffixes make a useful contrast with time nouns: -ig means "lasting that long", -lich means "every". ein zweistündiger Kurs (a two-hour course) versus stündlich (every hour); ein dreitägiger Ausflug (a three-day trip) versus täglich (daily); wöchentlich, monatlich, jährlich (weekly, monthly, yearly). Adverbs of time also turn into adjectives with -ig: heute → heutig (die heutige Zeitung), gestern → gestrig, hier → hiesig (local), dort → dortig.

Classic mistakes

  • merkwürdig does not mean "worth noticing" but strange. "Remarkable" is bemerkenswert.
  • Er ist ein kindischer Mensch when you mean "young at heart": use kindlich or jung geblieben; kindisch is an insult.
  • Der berliner Flughafen — city adjectives in -er are capitalised and take no ending: der Berliner Flughafen.
  • eine zweiwöchentliche Reise for a trip lasting two weeks: eine zweiwöchige Reise; zweiwöchentlich means "every two weeks".

-lich and English "-ly"

-lich and English -ly are the same old Germanic word, meaning "body, shape" (compare German Leiche, "corpse", and English lychgate). freundlich and friendly literally mean "having the shape of a friend". In the same way, -haft is related to haben ("having"), and -sam to English -some (einsam and lonesome are cousins).

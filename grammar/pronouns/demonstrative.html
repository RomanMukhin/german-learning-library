Demonstrative pronouns

der/die/das as pronouns in speech, dieser and jener, and derselbe for "the same".

Demonstratives point at something: "this one", "that one", "the same". In spoken German the most common demonstrative is simply the article der / die / das used on its own: Der ist nett! You will hear it all the time, so it is worth learning to recognise and use it, along with dieser and derselbe.

A2 core

der / die / das as pronouns

In speech Germans often replace er / sie / es with der / die / das. It sounds a bit more pointed, like "that one":

  • Kennst du Thomas? – Ja, der ist nett. – Do you know Thomas? – Yes, he's nice.
  • Wie findest du die Jacke? – Die ist zu teuer. – What do you think of the jacket? – It's too expensive.
  • Das Café da? Das kenne ich nicht. – That café? I don't know it.
  • Die Nachbarn? Die sind im Urlaub. – The neighbours? They're on holiday.

dieser

dieser = "this (one)" and takes the same endings as der/die/das (-er, -e, -es):

Masculine Feminine Neuter Plural
Nominative dieser diese dieses diese
Accusative diesen diese dieses diese
Dative diesem dieser diesem diesen
  • Dieser Film ist super. – This film is great.
  • Ich nehme diese Schuhe. – I'll take these shoes.
  • In diesem Haus wohnt mein Freund. – My friend lives in this house.

dieser = der + "dies-"

Look at the last letters of the article and copy them: der → dieser, den → diesen, dem → diesem, die → diese, das → dieses. The only change is das → dieses (with -es, not -as).

B1

The full forms of der/die/das as pronouns

Used as pronouns, der/die/das are almost the same as the articles. Only the dative plural and the genitive are longer:

Masculine Feminine Neuter Plural
Nominative der die das die
Accusative den die das die
Dative dem der dem denen
Genitive dessen deren dessen deren
  • Mit denen rede ich nicht mehr. – I'm not talking to them any more.
  • Den kenne ich! – I know him! (the pronoun often jumps to position 1 for emphasis)
  • Das glaube ich nicht. – I don't believe that.

These are the same forms as the relative pronouns, see Relative pronouns. The difference: a demonstrative starts a main clause (verb second), a relative pronoun starts a subordinate clause (verb at the end).

denen, not den

Dative plural pronoun is denen: Ich helfe denen (I help them). Ich helfe den is incomplete; den needs a noun after it: Ich helfe den Kindern.

jener

jener ("that one there") has the same endings as dieser, but it is rare in speech and sounds bookish. Today you will mostly meet it in writing and fixed contrasts: dieser oder jener ("this or that one"). In conversation say der da or das da drüben instead.

derselbe, dieselbe, dasselbe

"The same" is written as one word: article + selb-. Both halves are declined: the article part like der, the selb- part like an adjective after der.

Masculine Feminine Neuter Plural
Nominative derselbe dieselbe dasselbe dieselben
Accusative denselben dieselbe dasselbe dieselben
Dative demselben derselben demselben denselben
  • Wir haben dasselbe Handy. – We have the same phone.
  • Sie trägt dieselbe Jacke wie gestern. – She's wearing the same jacket as yesterday.
  • With a contracted preposition the parts separate: im selben Haus, zur selben Zeit.

derselbe vs der gleiche

Strictly, derselbe = the very same one (one object), der gleiche = an identical one (two objects). Wir wohnen im selben Haus (one house) vs Wir haben das gleiche Auto (two identical cars). In everyday speech many Germans mix them, so do not worry too much, but the strict rule is good for writing.

Why "der" can be impolite

Using der/die for a person who is present (Die hat mich angeschaut!) can sound rude or dismissive, a bit like pointing with your finger. Talking about friends with der is normal, but for a stranger or a superior, er/sie is safer.

Later: dessen, deren in writing

In written German dessen/deren can clarify ownership: Maria sprach mit Anna und deren Mutter = with Anna's mother, not Maria's.

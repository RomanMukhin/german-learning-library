Plural of nouns

The five plural patterns, the umlaut, rules of thumb by gender and ending, foreign plurals, and nouns that are only singular or only plural.

English makes almost every plural with -s. German has five plural patterns, and some of them also add an umlaut. The good news: in the plural the article is always die (in the nominative and accusative), whatever the gender, and the patterns are much more predictable than they first look. Learn each new noun with its plural (der Tisch, die Tische), and use the tendencies below when you meet a word for the first time.

A1–A2 core

The five patterns

Ending Umlaut? Typical nouns Examples
-e often (masc.), always for one-syllable fem. many masculine nouns, one-syllable feminine nouns der Tag → die Tage, der Stuhl → die Stühle, die Hand → die Hände
-(e)n never almost all feminine nouns, weak masculine nouns die Lampe → die Lampen, die Frau → die Frauen, der Junge → die Jungen
-er always if possible many one-syllable neuter nouns, a few masculine ones das Kind → die Kinder, das Buch → die Bücher, der Mann → die Männer
-s never words ending in a vowel (not -e), many loanwords, short forms das Auto → die Autos, das Hotel → die Hotels, die Oma → die Omas
– (no ending) sometimes masc./neuter nouns in -er, -el, -en, -chen, -lein der Lehrer → die Lehrer, der Vogel → die Vögel, das Mädchen → die Mädchen

Rules of thumb by gender

  • Feminine: about nine out of ten take -(e)n: die Straße → Straßen, die Zeitung → Zeitungen, die Tür → Türen. Nouns in -in double the n: die Freundin → die Freundinnen. A small group of one-syllable feminines take umlaut + -e: die Hand → Hände, die Nacht → Nächte, die Stadt → Städte, die Wand → Wände, die Wurst → Würste. Only two feminines are umlaut-only: die Mutter → Mütter, die Tochter → Töchter.
  • Masculine: most take -e, often with umlaut: der Freund → Freunde, der Zug → Züge, der Arzt → Ärzte. Those in -er, -el, -en add nothing: der Koffer → Koffer, der Schlüssel → Schlüssel, der Wagen → Wagen, sometimes with umlaut: der Vater → Väter, der Garten → Gärten.
  • Neuter: one-syllable ones often take -er + umlaut: das Haus → Häuser, das Land → Länder, das Glas → Gläser; others take -e: das Jahr → Jahre, das Tier → Tiere, das Problem → Probleme. Those in -chen, -lein, -er, -el, -en add nothing: das Zimmer → Zimmer.

Feminine? Add -n. Ends in a vowel? Add -s.

Two shortcuts cover a huge number of nouns. If it is feminine, the plural is almost always -(e)n. If it ends in -a, -i, -o, -u, -y (an Oma, a Taxi, a Kino, a Hobby), the plural is -s. For the rest, say the plural out loud each time you learn the noun.

The plural article: always die (or no article)

In the plural there are no genders. The definite article is die, and ein has no plural form at all: you just leave it out. The negative kein does have a plural: keine.

  • Ich habe ein Kind. → Ich habe zwei Kinder. / Ich habe keine Kinder. — I have a child. → I have two children. / I have no children.
  • Das ist ein Buch. → Das sind Bücher. — That is a book. → Those are books.
  • der Tisch, die Lampe, das Bett → die Tische, die Lampen, die Betten

Dative plural: add -n

In the dative plural the noun takes an extra -n unless the plural already ends in -n or -s: mit den Kindern, in den Häusern, von meinen Freunden, but mit den Frauen, in den Autos. See Dative.

Foreign words in -um, -us, -ma, -a

Many Latin and Greek loanwords replace their ending with -en:

Singular Plural
das Museum, das Zentrum, das Datum die Museen, die Zentren, die Daten
der Rhythmus, der Virus, der Organismus die Rhythmen, die Viren, die Organismen
das Thema, das Drama die Themen, die Dramen
die Firma, die Pizza die Firmen, die Pizzen (or Pizzas)
das Konto, das Visum, das Album die Konten, die Visa, die Alben

Only singular, only plural

Some nouns normally appear only in the singular: das Geld, die Milch, das Obst, das Gemüse, der Hunger, das Glück, die Polizei, das Wetter. To count them, use another noun: zwei Stück Obst, drei Gemüsesorten. Others exist only in the plural: die Eltern, die Leute, die Geschwister, die Ferien, die Kosten, die Lebensmittel. For one person you need a different word: ein Elternteil, eine Person.

Units of measurement stay singular after numbers when they are masculine or neuter: zwei Kilo Äpfel, drei Glas Wasser, fünf Euro, hundert Meter. Feminine units do take the plural: zwei Tassen Kaffee, drei Flaschen Wein.

Two plurals, two meanings

Singular Plural 1 Plural 2
die Bank die Bänke — benches die Banken — banks (money)
das Wort die Wörter — separate words die Worte — words spoken, a statement
die Mutter die Mütter — mothers die Muttern — nuts (for bolts)
der Strauß die Sträuße — bouquets die Strauße — ostriches

Classic mistakes

  • die Autoen, die Hotele — nouns that end in a vowel and many loanwords take -s: die Autos, die Hotels.
  • die Kinds — the English -s is not the default: die Kinder.
  • Ich habe eine Kinder — ein has no plural; just drop it: Ich habe Kinder.
  • mit den Freunde — dative plural needs the -n: mit den Freunden.
  • zwei Kilos — masculine and neuter units stay singular: zwei Kilo, drei Euro.
  • die Leuten in the nominative — the plural is die Leute; Leuten is only dative.

Why German has so many plurals

The plural endings were once tied to separate noun classes, much as Latin or Ukrainian have several declensions. The umlaut is a leftover sound effect: an old ending with an i (Old High German gast, plural gesti) pulled the vowel forward before it disappeared, giving modern Gast → Gäste. English has the same fossil in foot → feet, mouse → mice.

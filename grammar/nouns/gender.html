Noun gender: der, die, das

Why every noun has a gender, the meaning and ending patterns that predict it, compounds, female forms and words with two genders.

Every German noun belongs to one of three genders: masculine (der), feminine (die) or neuter (das). The gender is a property of the word, not of the thing: a spoon is der Löffel, a fork die Gabel and a knife das Messer. You need the gender for almost everything else in German (the article, pronouns, adjective endings), so it pays to learn it from day one.

Two more things make German nouns look different from English ones: they are always written with a capital letter (der Tisch, die Liebe, das Essen), and the dictionary marks their gender with m, f and n (or simply with the article).

A1–A2 core

Learn every noun with its article

There is no rule that predicts every gender, so the safest habit is to treat the article as part of the word: not Tisch but der Tisch, not Lampe but die Lampe. The patterns below help you guess well, and they get you right most of the time, but the article you learnt with the word always wins.

Colour-code your vocabulary

Write masculine nouns in blue, feminine in red and neuter in green (or put them in three columns). Your visual memory then stores the gender together with the word, and when you hesitate you often "see" the colour before you remember the article.

Gender by meaning

Usually masculine (der) Usually feminine (die) Usually neuter (das)
male people and animals: der Vater, der Onkel, der Hahn female people and animals: die Mutter, die Tante, die Kuh young people and animals: das Kind, das Baby, das Kalb, das Ferkel
days, months, seasons: der Montag, der Mai, der Winter (but das Frühjahr) numbers used as nouns: die Eins, die Zehn (a mark at school, a bus line) letters and colours as nouns: das A, das Rot, das Blau
weather and points of the compass: der Regen, der Schnee, der Wind, der Norden many trees and flowers: die Eiche, die Tanne, die Rose, die Tulpe infinitives used as nouns: das Essen, das Schwimmen, das Leben
car makes and alcoholic drinks: der BMW, der Wein, der Schnaps (but das Bier) ships and planes by name: die Titanic, die Boeing metals and most chemical elements: das Gold, das Eisen, das Silber (but der Stahl)
verb stems without -en: der Lauf, der Sprung, der Schlaf, der Anfang most countries and cities: (das schöne) Wien, das heutige Deutschland

Gender by ending

The ending of a noun is the most reliable clue, because suffixes decide the gender of the whole word.

Gender Endings Examples
die (very reliable) -ung, -heit, -keit, -schaft, -ion, -tät, -ik, -ei, -ie, -ur, -enz, -anz, -in die Zeitung, die Freiheit, die Möglichkeit, die Freundschaft, die Nation, die Universität, die Musik, die Bäckerei, die Familie, die Kultur, die Konferenz, die Toleranz, die Lehrerin
die (strong tendency) -e (about nine out of ten nouns) die Lampe, die Straße, die Woche, die Reise (but der Junge, der Name, der Käse, das Auge, das Ende)
der -ling, -ig, -ich, -ismus, -or, -eur, -ant, -ent, -ist, -ner der Frühling, der Honig, der Teppich, der Tourismus, der Motor, der Friseur, der Praktikant, der Student, der Polizist, der Rentner
der (tendency) -er for people and tools that do something der Lehrer, der Fahrer, der Computer, der Wecker (but die Butter, das Fenster, das Zimmer)
das (very reliable) -chen, -lein das Mädchen, das Brötchen, das Fräulein, das Büchlein
das -um, -ment, -ma, -o, -tum, -nis, Ge- at the start das Museum, das Dokument, das Thema, das Kino, das Eigentum, das Ergebnis, das Gebäude, das Gemüse (but der Irrtum, die Erlaubnis, der Geschmack)

-chen and -lein beat everything

The small-word endings -chen and -lein make any noun neuter, whatever it was before: der Hund → das Hündchen, die Frau → das Fräulein, die Magd → das Mädchen. Notice the umlaut that usually comes with them: der Baum → das Bäumchen, das Haus → das Häuschen.

Compound nouns: the last part decides

German builds long nouns by joining shorter ones. The gender (and the plural) always comes from the last part; the first parts only describe it.

  • das Haus + die Tür → die Haustür — the front door
  • die Hand + der Schuh → der Handschuh — the glove
  • der Kaffee + die Maschine → die Kaffeemaschine — the coffee machine
  • die Arbeit + der Platz → der Arbeitsplatz — the workplace (with a linking -s-)

Grammatical gender versus real gender

For people, grammar and biology usually agree (der Mann, die Frau), but not always. das Mädchen and das Fräulein are neuter because of their endings, and die Person, die Geisel (hostage) and das Mitglied (member) keep their gender for anyone. In writing the pronoun follows the grammar (Das Mädchen sagt, es ist müde); in speech most people say sie.

B1

Female forms with -in

Most nouns for people have a feminine form with -in (plural -innen), often with an umlaut:

Masculine Feminine Plural (fem.)
der Lehrer die Lehrerin die Lehrerinnen
der Arzt die Ärztin die Ärztinnen
der Koch die Köchin die Köchinnen
der Kollege die Kollegin (the -e drops) die Kolleginnen
der Franzose die Französin die Französinnen
der Beamte die Beamtin (but die Angestellte) die Beamtinnen

A mixed group has traditionally been named with the masculine plural (die Lehrer), the so-called generisches Maskulinum. Many institutions now prefer forms that name everyone: both forms (Lehrerinnen und Lehrer), a neutral word (die Lehrkräfte, die Studierenden) or a written marker (Lehrer*innen, Lehrer:innen, read with a short pause). You will meet all of these; in exams any consistent choice is fine.

Same word, different gender

A few nouns change their meaning with their gender. Learn them as pairs:

Masculine / feminine Neuter / other
der See — the lake die See — the sea
der Leiter — the manager die Leiter — the ladder
der Teil — the part (of a whole) das Teil — the piece, the item
die Steuer — the tax das Steuer — the steering wheel
der Band — the volume (book) das Band — the ribbon; die Band — the (music) band
der Kiefer — the jaw die Kiefer — the pine tree

Some words allow two genders with the same meaning: der/das Joghurt, der/das Laptop, der/das Virus (das Virus in science, der Virus in everyday speech).

Loanwords

English words usually take the gender of a German word with a similar meaning: der Computer (der Rechner), die E-Mail (die Nachricht), die App (die Anwendung), der Job (der Beruf). Nouns in -ing are usually neuter: das Meeting, das Training, das Shopping. Words that are verbs in English often become masculine: der Download, der Check.

Classic mistakes

  • die Mädchen for one girl — -chen is always neuter: das Mädchen (die Mädchen is the plural).
  • das Haustür — compounds take the gender of the last part: die Haustür.
  • der Zeitung — every noun in -ung is feminine: die Zeitung.
  • die Name, die Käse — the -e rule has famous exceptions: der Name, der Käse, das Ende, das Auge.
  • Writing nouns in lower case: ich habe hunger → Ich habe Hunger. Infinitives used as nouns are capitalised too: beim Essen.

Three genders, older than German

Proto-Indo-European, the ancestor of English, German, Ukrainian and Hindi, already had masculine, feminine and neuter. German kept all three. Dutch and the Scandinavian languages merged masculine and feminine into one "common" gender (Dutch de versus neuter het), and English lost grammatical gender completely after the Norman conquest, keeping only he, she, it.

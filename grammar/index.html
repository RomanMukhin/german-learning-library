Where to start (A2 to B1)

  1. Verbs and tenses first. Make the present and the Perfekt automatic, plus modal verbs. That alone covers most everyday conversation.
  2. Cases next. Accusative and dative decide your articles, pronouns and adjective endings, so everything later builds on them.
  3. Word order. Subordinate clauses with weil, dass, wenn are the single biggest step from A2 to B1.
  4. Prepositions and adjective endings. Two-way prepositions and adjective endings make your German sound correct, not just understandable.
  5. Then the B1 extras: Konjunktiv II for polite requests and wishes, the passive, verbs with prepositions.

Learn sentences, not tables

For every new rule, memorise one short sentence that shows it: Ich bin nach Hause gegangen. Ich helfe dir. Ich weiß, dass er kommt. When you are unsure later, say your model sentence and copy its pattern.

One grammar, many accents

Standard German grammar was fixed largely through writing: Luther's Bible translation in the 16th century and Konrad Duden's spelling dictionary (1880) spread one written norm across dozens of dialects. That is why Austrians, Swiss and Germans speak very differently but write almost the same grammar.

Moods

Indicative, imperative and the two Konjunktivs: how German marks facts, commands, wishes and reported words.

In this section

The imperativeHow to tell, ask and invite people to do things: du, ihr, Sie and wir forms, plus the little words that make an order sound friendly.A2–B1Konjunktiv IIThe "would" mood: polite requests, wishes, advice and unreal if-clauses with würde, hätte, wäre and könnte.A2–B1Konjunktiv IThe reported-speech mood of newspapers and news broadcasts: recognise er sei, er habe, er komme, but you do not need to produce it yet.B2+

A verb's mood tells you how the speaker relates to what they say: is it a fact, an order, a wish or a dream, or something someone else said? German has four moods you will meet. Two you use every day from A2, one you need for polite and hypothetical German at B1, and one you mostly read in the news.

The four moods at a glance

Mood What it does Example Level
Indikativ facts, real situations, questions Ich habe Zeit. — I have time. A1
Imperativ orders, requests, instructions Komm her! — Come here! A2
Konjunktiv II unreal, hypothetical, polite, wishes Ich hätte gern Zeit. — I'd like to have time. A2–B1
Konjunktiv I reported speech, mostly news and formal writing Er sagt, er habe Zeit. — He says he has time. B2+

Which one when

  • You state something you believe is real: Indikativ. Wenn ich Zeit habe, komme ich. (a real possibility)
  • You tell someone to do something: Imperativ. Gib mir bitte das Salz!
  • You talk about something that is not real, not yet real, or you want to sound polite: Konjunktiv II. Wenn ich Zeit hätte, käme ich. / Könnten Sie mir helfen?
  • You report what someone else said and want to keep your distance from it: Konjunktiv I. Der Minister sagte, er sei nicht informiert gewesen. In speech, people usually just use the indicative or Konjunktiv II instead.

The quickest test between Indikativ and Konjunktiv II: is the "if" realistic? Wenn es morgen regnet, bleibe ich zu Hause (it might rain) vs Wenn ich Millionär wäre, würde ich nicht arbeiten (I am not).

One word unlocks most of Konjunktiv II

For almost any verb you can say würde + infinitive: ich würde gehen, ich würde kaufen. Learn only wäre, hätte, könnte, müsste, sollte by heart and use würde for everything else. That covers 95% of spoken German.

Ukrainian has one particle for what German does with a whole mood

Ukrainian builds the hypothetical with past tense + би: я пішов би. German changes the verb itself (ich ginge) or adds würde (ich würde gehen). Wherever you would say би in Ukrainian, German wants Konjunktiv II.

Why "Konjunktiv"

The name comes from the Latin modus coniunctivus, the "joining" mood, because Latin used it mainly in clauses joined to a main clause. German grammar books still use the Latin names: Indikativ, Imperativ, Konjunktiv.

Konjunktiv I

The reported-speech mood of newspapers and news broadcasts: recognise er sei, er habe, er komme, but you do not need to produce it yet.

Konjunktiv I is the mood of reported speech in formal German. A journalist uses it to say "this is what X said, not what I claim". You will hear it every evening on the Tagesschau and read it in every newspaper, but almost nobody uses it in everyday conversation. At A2–B1 the goal is only to recognise it.

Later: the whole page

Everything below is B2+ material. Read it so the forms do not confuse you in the news; do not try to use them yet.

How it is built: infinitive stem + Konjunktiv endings. The form you meet most is the er/sie/es form, which ends in -e with no -t:

Infinitive Indikativ Konjunktiv I
sein er ist er sei
haben er hat er habe
kommen er kommt er komme
können er kann er könne
werden er wird er werde
wissen er weiß er wisse

sein is irregular in all persons: ich sei, du seist, er sei, wir seien, ihr seiet, sie seien.

Where you see it:

  • Der Minister sagte, er sei nicht informiert gewesen. — The minister said he had not been informed.
  • Die Polizei teilte mit, der Fahrer habe keinen Führerschein. — The police said the driver had no licence.
  • Sie erklärte, sie komme morgen zurück. — She said she would come back tomorrow.
  • Fixed phrases: Es lebe die Freiheit! (Long live freedom!), Gott sei Dank! (Thank God!)

When it is replaced: if the Konjunktiv I looks the same as the Indikativ (sie haben = sie haben), writers switch to Konjunktiv II: Sie sagten, sie hätten keine Zeit.

In speech people simply say Er hat gesagt, dass er keine Zeit hat or ..., er hätte keine Zeit. Both are fine.

Spot it by the missing -t

er sei, er habe, er komme: if a third-person verb ends in -e instead of -t, and someone is being quoted, it is Konjunktiv I. Translate it as a normal "he says that ...".

Gott sei Dank

You already use Konjunktiv I without knowing it: Gott sei Dank literally means "thanks be to God". The same old wish-form survives in Es lebe...! and in recipes from older cookbooks: Man nehme drei Eier ("take three eggs").

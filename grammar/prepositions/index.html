Prepositions

Every German preposition takes a fixed case, and the four groups fit on one table.

In this section

A German preposition does two jobs: it gives meaning (in, on, with) and it picks the case of the noun after it. Most prepositions always take the same case, so the best thing you can do is learn them in groups. Only nine, the two-way prepositions, change their case, and their rule is simple: location takes the dative, direction takes the accusative.

The four groups at a glance

Group Prepositions Example
Always accusative durch, für, gegen, ohne, um (+ bis, entlang after the noun) für den Chef, um den See
Always dative aus, bei, mit, nach, seit, von, zu (+ ab, gegenüber, außer) mit dem Bus, zu der = zur Arbeit
Two-way (Wechselpräpositionen) an, auf, hinter, in, neben, über, unter, vor, zwischen Ich gehe in den Park (wohin) / Ich bin in dem = im Park (wo)
Genitive (B1, formal) wegen, trotz, während, statt, innerhalb, außerhalb wegen des Regens, während der Ferien

The full list with every rare member is in the old reference: Prepositions reference.

Which page when

  • Two-way prepositions (two-way): in der Stadt or in die Stadt? legen or liegen? Start here, it is the biggest source of mistakes.
  • Time (time): am Montag, um acht, im Mai, seit drei Jahren, vor zwei Tagen.
  • Place and direction (place and direction): nach Berlin, in die Schweiz, zum Arzt, beim Arzt, zu Hause or nach Hause.

For verbs that need a fixed preposition (warten auf, denken an), see verbs with prepositions.

Sing the dative list

Learn the dative group as one chanted word: aus-bei-mit-nach-seit-von-zu. Say it ten times today and it stays for life. For the accusative group, remember DOGFU: durch, ohne, gegen, für, um.

The dative is killing the genitive

In everyday speech most Germans say wegen dem Wetter instead of wegen des Wetters. Journalist Bastian Sick made this the title of his 2004 bestseller about German usage: Der Dativ ist dem Genitiv sein Tod ("the dative is the death of the genitive"), a title that is itself written in the "wrong" colloquial style. Write the genitive, but do not be surprised when you hear the dative.

Ukrainian does the same thing

Ukrainian prepositions also choose a case: з другом (instrumental), до школи (genitive). And Ukrainian has its own two-way pair: в школу (accusative, where to) vs в школі (locative, where). German in die Schule / in der Schule works the same way.

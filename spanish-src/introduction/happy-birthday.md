## Happy Birthday!

The word for birthday is *el cumpleaños* (literally "it completes years"). It's the same in singular and plural.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| *¡Feliz cumpleaños!* | Happy birthday! |
| *¡Felicidades!* / *¡Felicitaciones!* | Congratulations! / Best wishes! |
| *¡Que cumplas muchos más!* | Many happy returns! |
| *¡Que la pases bien!* (LatAm) / *¡Que lo pases bien!* (Spain) | Have a great time! |
| *Hoy es mi cumpleaños.* | Today is my birthday. |
| *¿Cuándo es tu cumpleaños?* | When is your birthday? |
| *Mi cumpleaños es el 3 de mayo.* | My birthday is on 3 May. |

Dates follow the pattern *el* + number + *de* + month. Months are written in lower case: *enero, febrero, marzo, abril, mayo, junio, julio, agosto, septiembre, octubre, noviembre, diciembre*. The first of the month is *el primero* or *el uno*.

» *¿Cuándo es el cumpleaños de tu madre? —Es el veinte de julio.* — When is your mum's birthday? — It's on 20 July.

> [!fact] Birthday songs
> In Spain people sing *Cumpleaños feliz* to the familiar tune. In Mexico the traditional song is *Las mañanitas*, sometimes sung early in the morning to wake the birthday person.

> [!trap] Celebrate with cumplir
> "To turn" an age is *cumplir*: *Mañana cumplo treinta años.* — I turn thirty tomorrow.

## How Old Are You?

Spanish uses ***tener*** (to have) + number + *años* to give age — you "have" years.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| *¿Cuántos años tienes?* | How old are you? (informal) |
| *¿Cuántos años tiene usted?* | How old are you? (formal) |
| *Tengo veinticinco años.* | I'm twenty-five. |
| *Mi hijo tiene dos años.* | My son is two. |
| *¿Cuántos años cumples?* | How old are you turning? |

~~Soy veinte años~~ → *Tengo veinte años*

### Numbers for ages

| | | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 *diez* | 15 *quince* | 20 *veinte* | 21 *veintiuno* |
| 30 *treinta* | 40 *cuarenta* | 50 *cincuenta* | 60 *sesenta* |
| 70 *setenta* | 80 *ochenta* | 90 *noventa* | 100 *cien* |

From 31 upwards, tens and units are joined with *y*: *treinta y dos* (32), *cuarenta y cinco* (45). From 16 to 29 they are written as one word: *dieciséis, veintitrés*.

Before *años*, *uno* shortens: *veintiún años*, *treinta y un años*.

> [!hack] Asking an adult's age
> As in English, asking an adult their age can be sensitive. With children it's a friendly, everyday question: *¿Cuántos años tienes, guapa?*

## Quinceañera

A ***quinceañera*** or ***quince*** is the celebration of a girl's 15th birthday, marking the step from childhood to young womanhood. *Quinceañera* also means the girl herself. It is especially important in Mexico, but is celebrated across Latin America, the Caribbean and Hispanic communities in the US.

### Where it comes from

The tradition blends two roots: coming-of-age ceremonies of Indigenous peoples in Mesoamerica, and Catholic and European customs brought by the Spanish in the 16th century — the church mass, the formal ball, the long gown.

### What usually happens

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| *la misa* | the mass held before the party |
| *la entrada* | the girl's grand entrance |
| *las damas y los chambelanes* | her court of young ladies and escorts |
| *el brindis* | the toast |
| *el vals* | the waltz, often danced with her father |
| *el vestido* | the ball gown |
| *el pastel* | the (often tiered) cake |

Some gifts are symbolic. In the **ceremony of the last doll** (*la última muñeca*), the girl receives a final doll, representing the end of childhood. In the **changing of the shoes** (*el cambio de zapatos*), flat shoes are swapped for high heels.

» *El sábado es la fiesta de quince de mi prima.* — On Saturday it's my cousin's fifteenth birthday party.
» *La quinceañera baila el vals con su papá.* — The birthday girl dances the waltz with her dad.

> [!fact] Not every quince is the same
> Some families hold a huge ball rather like a wedding; others keep it small. In some countries there is a candle-lighting ceremony honouring important people in the girl's life.

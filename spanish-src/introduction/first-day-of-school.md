## The Basics!

On the first day you need words for the people and things around you.

### People and places

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| *el profesor / la profesora* | teacher |
| *el compañero / la compañera (de clase)* | classmate |
| *el amigo / la amiga* | friend |
| *el / la estudiante* | student |
| *el salón de clase*, *el aula* | classroom |
| *el despacho* | office (of a teacher) |
| *las horas de oficina* | office hours |
| *el edificio* | building |
| *el gimnasio* | gym |
| *la biblioteca* | library |
| *la cafetería* | cafeteria |
| *la residencia* | dormitory |

### School supplies

| Spanish | English | Spanish | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| *la mochila* | backpack | *el cuaderno* | notebook |
| *el libro* | book | *el lápiz* | pencil |
| *el bolígrafo* | pen | *la goma* | eraser |
| *el papel* | paper | *el estuche* | pencil case |
| *la calculadora* | calculator | *la agenda* | planner |
| *la pizarra* | board | *la tiza* | chalk |
| *la mesa* | table | *la silla* | chair |
| *la computadora* (LatAm), *el ordenador* (Spain) | computer | *los rotuladores* | markers |

» *¿Tienes un lápiz? —Sí, aquí tienes.* — Do you have a pencil? — Yes, here you go.
» *Necesito un cuaderno nuevo.* — I need a new notebook.

> [!trap] lápiz → lápices
> Nouns ending in *-z* change it to *-c* in the plural: *un lápiz, dos lápices*.

## It's My Favorite Class!

### School subjects

| Spanish | English | Spanish | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Matemáticas* | Maths | *Historia* | History |
| *Ciencias* | Science | *Geografía* | Geography |
| *Biología* | Biology | *Literatura* | Literature |
| *Química* | Chemistry | *Física* | Physics |
| *Informática* | Computer Science | *Estadística* | Statistics |
| *Español* | Spanish | *Inglés* | English |
| *Francés* | French | *Alemán* | German |
| *Arte* | Art | *Música* | Music |
| *Educación Física* | PE | *Religión* | Religious Studies |

As in English, subjects are capitalised when they are the **name of a class**, but not when they are general words:

» *Mi clase favorita es Música.* — My favourite class is Music.
» *Me encanta escuchar música.* — I love listening to music.

### Possessive words

To say *my class*, *your teacher*, use short **possessive adjectives** before the noun. They agree with the **thing owned**, not with the owner.

| English | singular noun | plural noun |
|---|---|---|
| my | *mi* | *mis* |
| your (*tú*) | *tu* | *tus* |
| his, her, its, your (*usted*), their | *su* | *sus* |
| our | *nuestro / nuestra* | *nuestros / nuestras* |
| your (*vosotros*, Spain) | *vuestro / vuestra* | *vuestros / vuestras* |

Only *nuestro* and *vuestro* change for masculine and feminine.

» *Mi profesora es muy simpática.* — My teacher is very nice.
» *Mis clases favoritas son Arte e Historia.* — My favourite classes are Art and History.
» *Nuestra escuela es grande.* — Our school is big.

> [!trap] Agree with the thing, not the person
> A boy talking about his backpack still says *mi mochila*; a girl talking about her notebooks says *mis cuadernos*. And *nuestra clase* (feminine) whoever "we" are.

## What's Your Favorite Class?

To describe your teachers, classes and classmates, use ***ser*** (to be), the verb for identity and lasting qualities.

| | *ser* |
|---|---|
| *yo* | *soy* |
| *tú* | *eres* |
| *usted, él, ella* | *es* |
| *nosotros, nosotras* | *somos* |
| *vosotros, vosotras* | *sois* |
| *ustedes, ellos, ellas* | *son* |

» *¿Cuál es tu clase favorita?* — What's your favourite class?
» *Mi clase favorita es Química. Es muy interesante.* — My favourite class is Chemistry. It's really interesting.
» *Mis profesores son Paula y Andrés.* — My teachers are Paula and Andrés.
» *Mi mochila es azul.* — My backpack is blue.
» *Somos compañeros de clase.* — We're classmates.

Useful adjectives: *interesante, aburrido/a* (boring), *fácil* (easy), *difícil* (difficult), *divertido/a* (fun), *simpático/a* (nice), *estricto/a* (strict).

> [!trap] Cuál, not qué, before ser
> To ask "what is your…?" Spanish usually uses *cuál*: ~~¿Qué es tu clase favorita?~~ → *¿Cuál es tu clase favorita?* (*¿Qué es…?* asks for a definition.)

> [!hack] First-day dialogue
> *—Hola, soy Leo. ¿Eres nueva? —Sí, me llamo Irene. ¿Quién es tu profesor de Historia? —Es el señor Ortiz. Es muy divertido. —¡Qué bien! Mi clase favorita es Historia.*

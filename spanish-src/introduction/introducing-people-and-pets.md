## Introduction

### Words for people and animals

| Spanish | English | Spanish | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| *un hombre* | a man | *una mujer* | a woman |
| *un perro* | a (male) dog | *una perra* | a (female) dog |
| *un gato* | a (male) cat | *una gata* | a (female) cat |
| *hola* | hello | *adiós* | goodbye |

### Every noun has a gender

All Spanish nouns are **masculine** or **feminine** — not just people and animals, but also things and ideas. For animals and people, the ending often shows the sex: *gato* (tom cat), *gata* (female cat).

### Un and una

The word for *a* / *an* must match the noun's gender:

| masculine | feminine |
|---|---|
| *un hombre* | *una mujer* |
| *un gato* | *una gata* |
| *un perro* | *una perra* |

~~un mujer~~ → *una mujer*

### The silent h

The letter ***h*** is almost always silent in Spanish: ***hola*** sounds like "OH-la", ***hombre*** like "OM-breh". It is only pronounced in *ch* (*chocolate*) and in a few foreign words like *Hawái*.

> [!fact] Punctuation at both ends
> Exclamations, like questions, open and close: *¡Hola!* *¡Adiós!*

## Introducing Yourself

### Subject pronouns

Subject pronouns say **who** does the action.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| *yo* | I |
| *tú* | you (informal) |
| *él* | he |
| *ella* | she |

*Yo* isn't capitalised unless it begins a sentence. *Tú* and *él* need their accents (*tu* = your, *el* = the).

### The verb ser

| | *ser* |
|---|---|
| *(yo) soy* | I am |
| *(tú) eres* | you are |
| *(él / ella) es* | he / she is |

» *Yo soy Daniel.* — I'm Daniel.
» *Soy Raquel.* — I'm Raquel.
» *Soy un hombre.* — I'm a man.
» *Soy una mujer.* — I'm a woman.

Both *Yo soy Raquel* and *Soy Raquel* are correct. Because *soy* can only mean "I am", the pronoun is often dropped; use it for emphasis: *Yo soy Raquel, y ella es Marta.*

> [!hack] Start every introduction the same way
> *¡Hola! Soy…* + your name is all you need. Add *Mucho gusto* and you're done.

## Introducing Other People

Use ***es*** (he / she is) and ***eres*** (you are):

» *Él es Mario.* — He's Mario.
» *Ella es Sandra.* — She's Sandra.
» *Él es un hombre.* — He's a man.
» *Ella es una mujer.* — She's a woman.
» *Tú eres Alex.* — You're Alex.

### Introducing pets

Spanish speakers use *él* and *ella* for animals too — there's no special word for "it" as a subject.

» *Él es un perro. Es Toby.* — He's a dog. He's Toby.
» *Ella es una gata. Es Luna.* — She's a cat. She's Luna.
» *¿Él es tu perro?* — Is he your dog?

> [!trap] Don't translate "it is" with a pronoun
> For "It's a dog", Spanish just says *Es un perro*. There's no ~~Ello es~~.

## Review

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| *Soy* + name | *Soy Pablo.* |
| *Soy un* / *una* + noun | *Soy una mujer.* |
| *Eres* + name | *Eres Clara.* |
| *Él es* / *Ella es* + name | *Ella es Ana.* |
| *Es un* / *una* + animal | *Es un gato.* |

Check yourself:

» *___ soy Daniel.* → *Yo* soy Daniel.
» *Tú ___ una mujer.* → Tú *eres* una mujer.
» *Él es ___ perro.* → Él es *un* perro.
» *Ella es ___ gata.* → Ella es *una* gata.

> [!trap] Matching the verb to the person
> ~~Yo es~~ → *Yo soy*; ~~Tú es~~ → *Tú eres*; ~~Ella soy~~ → *Ella es*.

## Let's Have a Conversation!

Put everything together:

» *—¡Hola! Yo soy Daniel. ¿Y tú?* — Hi! I'm Daniel. And you?
» *—Hola, Daniel. Soy Alex.* — Hi, Daniel. I'm Alex.
» *—Mucho gusto, Alex. Ella es Sandra.* — Nice to meet you, Alex. This is Sandra.
» *—¡Hola, Sandra!* — Hi, Sandra!
» *—Y él es Mario, mi perro.* — And this is Mario, my dog.
» *—¡Qué lindo! Yo tengo una gata. Ella es Luna.* — How cute! I have a cat. She's Luna.
» *—¡Adiós, Alex!* — Bye, Alex!
» *—¡Adiós!* — Bye!

> [!hack] "Él es…" also means "This is…"
> When introducing someone standing next to you, *Él es Mario* or *Ella es Sandra* works just like English "This is Mario / Sandra".

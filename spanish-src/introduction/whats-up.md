## Hello!

Every chat begins with a hello and a "how are you?". The question uses ***estar***, the verb for temporary states and feelings:

| | Informal | Formal |
|---|---|---|
| one person | *¿Cómo estás?* | *¿Cómo está (usted)?* |
| several people | *¿Cómo están?* (Spain: *¿Cómo estáis?*) | *¿Cómo están (ustedes)?* |

Combine it with a greeting to sound natural:

» *¡Hola! ¿Cómo estás?* — Hi! How are you?
» *Buenos días, señor García. ¿Cómo está?* — Good morning, Mr García. How are you?
» *Hola, chicos, ¿cómo están?* — Hi, guys, how are you all?

Your answer also uses *estar*:

| *estar* | |
|---|---|
| *yo estoy* | I am |
| *tú estás* | you are |
| *usted, él, ella está* | you are, he/she is |
| *nosotros estamos* | we are |
| *ustedes, ellos están* | you (all), they are |

» *Estoy bien, gracias.* — I'm fine, thanks.
» *Estamos muy cansados.* — We're very tired.

> [!trap] ser vs. estar for feelings
> How you feel right now takes *estar*: ~~Soy bien~~ → *Estoy bien*. ~~Soy cansado~~ → *estoy cansado*.

## What's Up?

Besides *¿Cómo estás?* there are lots of other ways to ask how someone is doing. Some are neutral, some are for friends only.

| Spanish | English | Register |
|---|---|---|
| *¿Qué tal?* | How are things? | neutral, very common |
| *¿Cómo te va?* | How's it going? | informal |
| *¿Cómo le va?* | How's it going? | formal |
| *¿Qué hay?* | What's up? | informal |
| *¿Qué pasa?* | What's up? | informal (Spain especially) |
| *¿Qué cuentas?* | What's new? (lit. what do you tell?) | informal |
| *¿Cómo vas?* | How are you getting on? | informal |
| *¿Cómo andas?* | How are you doing? | informal |
| *¿Qué onda?* | What's up? | slang, Mexico / Central America |

*¿Qué tal?* can stand alone or take a noun: *¿Qué tal el trabajo?* — How's work? *¿Qué tal tu madre?* — How's your mother?

> [!trap] ¿Qué pasa? can also mean "what's wrong?"
> Said with a worried tone, *¿Qué pasa?* asks what the problem is. Context and intonation tell you which meaning is meant.

## Not Much!

There's a range of answers, from great to terrible. On their own, short answers can sound abrupt, so add *gracias* and ask back with *¿Y tú?* or *¿Y usted?*.

| Feeling | Spanish | English |
|---|---|---|
| 😀 | *¡Excelente!* / *¡Genial!* | Excellent! / Great! |
| 🙂 | *Muy bien.* | Very well. |
| 🙂 | *Bien.* / *Todo bien.* | Fine. / All good. |
| 😐 | *Regular.* / *Normal.* | OK. / Not bad. |
| 😐 | *Más o menos.* / *Así así.* | So-so. |
| 🙁 | *No muy bien.* | Not so good. |
| 😞 | *Mal.* / *Muy mal.* | Bad. / Very bad. |
| 😫 | *¡Fatal!* / *¡Terrible!* | Awful! / Terrible! |

» *Muy bien, gracias. ¿Y tú?* — Very well, thanks. And you?
» *Bien, gracias. ¿Y usted?* — Fine, thank you. And you?
» *Más o menos. ¿Y tú?* — So-so. And you?

To answer *¿Qué hay?* or *¿Qué pasa?*, use *nada* ("nothing"):

» *—¿Qué hay de nuevo? —Nada, lo de siempre.* — What's new? — Nothing, same old thing.
» *—¿Qué cuentas? —No mucho.* — What's new? — Not much.

### Answers with personality

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| *No me quejo.* | Can't complain. |
| *Aquí, tranquilo / tranquila.* | Just taking it easy. |
| *Aquí andamos.* | Hanging in there. |
| *Dos tres.* | So-so. (Mexico) |
| *Tirando.* | Getting by. (Spain) |

> [!hack] Answer "bad" and get asked why
> If you say *mal* or *regular*, expect a follow-up: *¿Y eso?* or *¿Qué te pasa?* — "Oh, why's that?" Have a reason ready: *Estoy muy cansado* or *Tengo mucho trabajo*.

## Parents

The core of the *familia* and the generations above it:

| Spanish | English | Spanish | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| *la madre* | mother | *el padre* | father |
| *la mamá* | mum, mom | *el papá* | dad |
| *los padres* | parents | *la abuela* | grandmother |
| *el abuelo* | grandfather | *los abuelos* | grandparents |
| *la abuelita* | grandma | *el abuelito* | grandpa |
| *la bisabuela* | great-grandmother | *el bisabuelo* | great-grandfather |
| *la tatarabuela* | great-great-grandmother | *el tatarabuelo* | great-great-grandfather |
| *la tía* | aunt | *el tío* | uncle |

Step-parents and in-laws:

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| *la madrastra* / *el padrastro* | stepmother / stepfather |
| *la suegra* / *el suegro* | mother-in-law / father-in-law |
| *la madre política* / *el padre político* | mother- / father-in-law (also step-) |

» *Mis padres viven en Quito.* — My parents live in Quito.
» *Mi abuela tiene noventa años.* — My grandmother is ninety.

> [!trap] padres means parents
> *Los padres* is "the parents" (mother and father), not only "fathers". "Relatives" in general is *los parientes* or *los familiares* — ~~los relativos~~.

> [!fact] Little words of love
> The ending *-ito / -ita* adds affection: *abuelita*, *mamita*, *hermanito*. You'll hear it constantly in family talk.

## Siblings

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| *el hermano* / *la hermana* | brother / sister |
| *los hermanos* | siblings, brothers and sisters |
| *el hermano mayor* / *la hermana mayor* | older brother / older sister |
| *el hermano menor* / *la hermana menor* | younger brother / younger sister |
| *el hermanito* / *la hermanita* | little brother / little sister |
| *el medio hermano* / *la media hermana* | half-brother / half-sister |
| *el hermanastro* / *la hermanastra* | stepbrother / stepsister |
| *el gemelo* / *la gemela* | twin |
| *el primo* / *la prima* | cousin |
| *el cuñado* / *la cuñada* | brother-in-law / sister-in-law |

» *¿Tienes hermanos? —Sí, tengo dos hermanas y un hermano.* — Do you have any brothers or sisters? — Yes, I have two sisters and a brother.
» *Soy hijo único.* / *Soy hija única.* — I'm an only child.
» *Mi prima es mi mejor amiga.* — My cousin is my best friend.

### The masculine plural covers everyone

When a group has at least one male member, the masculine plural is used: *mis hermanos* can mean "my brothers" or "my brothers and sisters". *Mis tíos* is "my uncles" or "my aunt and uncle".

> [!hack] Ask the right question
> *¿Tienes hermanos?* is the natural way to ask "Do you have siblings?" — even if the answer is "only sisters".

## Children

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| *el hijo* / *la hija* | son / daughter |
| *los hijos* | children (sons and daughters) |
| *el nieto* / *la nieta* | grandson / granddaughter |
| *el bisnieto* / *la bisnieta* | great-grandson / great-granddaughter |
| *el sobrino* / *la sobrina* | nephew / niece |
| *el yerno* | son-in-law |
| *la nuera* | daughter-in-law |
| *el esposo*, *el marido* / *la esposa*, *la mujer* | husband / wife |
| *el bebé* | baby |

» *Tenemos tres hijos: dos niños y una niña.* — We have three children: two boys and a girl.
» *Mi sobrino tiene cinco años.* — My nephew is five.
» *Los nietos visitan a sus abuelos los domingos.* — The grandchildren visit their grandparents on Sundays.

### Describing a family tree

» *Ana es la madre de Luis.* — Ana is Luis's mother.
» *Pedro es el abuelo de mis primos.* — Pedro is my cousins' grandfather.

> [!trap] No apostrophe-s in Spanish
> Possession uses *de*: ~~Luis's madre~~ → *la madre de Luis*. With *el*, *de* + *el* contracts to *del*: *el hijo del profesor*.

> [!fact] Two surnames
> Most Spanish speakers have two surnames: first the father's, then the mother's. So the daughter of *Juan García López* and *Ana Ruiz Pérez* is *María García Ruiz*.

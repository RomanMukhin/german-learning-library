## Hot and Cold

Spanish describes most weather with the verb ***hacer*** (to do, to make), always in the *it* form ***hace***. Think of it as "it makes heat".

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| *Hace calor.* | It's hot. |
| *Hace frío.* | It's cold. |
| *Hace fresco.* | It's cool / chilly. |
| *Hace sol.* | It's sunny. |
| *Hace viento.* | It's windy. |
| *Hace buen tiempo.* | The weather's nice. |
| *Hace mal tiempo.* | The weather's bad. |

To make it stronger, use ***mucho*** (not *muy*), because *calor* and *frío* are nouns here:

» *Hace mucho calor en Sevilla en agosto.* — It's very hot in Seville in August.
» *Hoy hace mucho frío.* — It's really cold today.

~~Hace muy calor~~ → *Hace mucho calor*

### The temperature

» *Estamos a treinta grados.* — It's thirty degrees.
» *Hace cinco grados bajo cero.* — It's five degrees below zero.

> [!trap] hace frío vs. tengo frío
> *Hace frío* describes the weather. To say *you* feel cold, use *tener*: *Tengo frío* — I'm cold. ~~Estoy frío~~ means your body is cold to the touch.

## Rain or Shine

Some weather words are verbs of their own, used only in the *it* form:

| Infinitive | Now | English |
|---|---|---|
| *llover* | *llueve* / *está lloviendo* | it rains / it's raining |
| *nevar* | *nieva* / *está nevando* | it snows / it's snowing |
| *tronar* | *truena* | it thunders |
| *lloviznar* | *llovizna* | it drizzles |

Others use ***está*** + adjective or ***hay*** + noun:

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| *Está nublado.* | It's cloudy. |
| *Está despejado.* | It's clear. |
| *Está soleado.* | It's sunny. |
| *Hay niebla.* | It's foggy. |
| *Hay tormenta.* | There's a storm. |
| *Hay nubes.* | There are clouds. |

» *Llueve mucho en Bogotá.* — It rains a lot in Bogotá.
» *Está nevando en la sierra.* — It's snowing in the mountains.
» *Lleva el paraguas: está nublado.* — Take the umbrella: it's cloudy.

### The seasons

*la primavera* (spring), *el verano* (summer), *el otoño* (autumn), *el invierno* (winter).

> [!fact] Upside-down seasons
> South of the equator, in Argentina, Chile or Uruguay, Christmas falls in *verano* and July is the middle of *invierno*. Near the equator, people often speak of the *temporada de lluvias* (rainy season) and *temporada seca* (dry season) instead.

## What's the Weather Like?

To ask about the weather:

» *¿Qué tiempo hace?* — What's the weather like?
» *¿Qué tiempo hace en Lima en enero?* — What's the weather like in Lima in January?
» *¿Hace calor allí?* — Is it hot there?

> [!trap] tiempo has two meanings
> *El tiempo* means both "weather" and "time": *¿Qué tiempo hace?* (weather) but *No tengo tiempo* (time).

### More expressions with hacer

*Hacer* appears in many everyday phrases:

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| *hacer la maleta* | to pack a suitcase |
| *hacer la tarea* / *los deberes* | to do homework |
| *hacer la compra* | to do the grocery shopping |
| *hacer ejercicio* | to exercise |
| *hacer cola* | to queue, stand in line |
| *hacer un viaje* | to take a trip |
| *hacer caso a* | to pay attention to |
| *hacer falta* | to be needed |
| *hacerse* + job | to become |

» *Hace falta un paraguas.* — An umbrella is needed.
» *Mi hermana se hizo médica.* — My sister became a doctor.

### Hace for time

*Hace* + period of time = "ago":

» *Llegué hace dos días.* — I arrived two days ago.
» *Hace tres años que vivo aquí.* / *Vivo aquí desde hace tres años.* — I've lived here for three years.

> [!hack] Small talk starter
> Weather is the perfect icebreaker: *¡Qué calor hace hoy!* — "It's so hot today!" or *¡Qué frío!* Anyone will answer.

## Introduction

Two of Mexico's best-known foods make a tasty first vocabulary set.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| *el taco* | taco |
| *el tamal* | tamale |
| *un* | a, an (before masculine nouns) |
| *quiero* | I want |
| *quieres* | you want |
| *sí* / *no* | yes / no |

> [!trap] Tamal, not tamale
> In Spanish the singular is ***tamal*** and the plural ***tamales***. English made "tamale" out of the plural. ~~un tamale~~ → *un tamal*.

### What are they?

- A ***taco*** is a soft corn (or flour) *tortilla* folded around a filling such as meat, beans or vegetables, topped with onion, coriander (*cilantro*), lime and *salsa*.
- A ***tamal*** is a dough of corn (*masa*) with a filling, wrapped in a corn husk or banana leaf and steamed. You unwrap it before eating!

Popular fillings: *al pastor* (marinated pork), *de pollo* (chicken), *de carne asada* (grilled beef), *de pescado* (fish), *de frijoles* (beans), *de queso* (cheese), *de rajas* (pepper strips).

> [!fact] Not the hard-shell kind
> Tacos in Mexico are almost always on small, soft tortillas and sold at a *taquería* or street stand. The crunchy, U-shaped shell is mostly a US invention.

## What's for Lunch?

Use *quiero* (I want) and *quieres* (you want) with *un*:

» *Quiero un taco.* — I want a taco.
» *Quieres un tamal.* — You want a tamale.
» *¿Quieres un tamal?* — Do you want a tamale?
» *Sí, quiero un tamal.* — Yes, I want a tamale.
» *No, quiero un taco.* — No, I want a taco.

A statement becomes a question just by adding *¿ ?* (and a rising voice): *Quieres un taco.* → *¿Quieres un taco?*

### More than one

Add *-s* for the plural after a vowel and *-es* after a consonant:

| One | More |
|---|---|
| *un taco* | *dos tacos* |
| *un tamal* | *tres tamales* |

» *Quiero tres tacos al pastor.* — I want three pork tacos.
» *¿Quieres dos tamales de pollo?* — Do you want two chicken tamales?

> [!hack] "De" tells you the filling
> *Un taco **de** pollo* is a chicken taco; *un tamal **de** queso* is a cheese tamale. The filling comes after *de*.

## Mind Your Manners

Three little expressions make every order polite:

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| *por favor* | please |
| *gracias* / *muchas gracias* | thank you / thank you very much |
| *de nada* | you're welcome |

» *Quiero un taco, por favor.* — I'd like a taco, please.
» *—Aquí tiene. —Gracias. —De nada.* — Here you are. — Thanks. — You're welcome.

Other polite replies to *gracias*:

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| *No hay de qué.* | Don't mention it. |
| *Con gusto.* | With pleasure. |
| *A la orden.* | At your service. (Colombia, Venezuela) |
| *A ti.* / *A usted.* | Thank *you*. |

### At the taco stand

» *—¡Buenas! ¿Qué quieres?* — Hi! What would you like?
» *—Quiero dos tacos de carne asada, por favor.* — Two grilled beef tacos, please.
» *—¿Quieres salsa?* — Do you want salsa?
» *—Sí, gracias.* — Yes, thanks.
» *—Aquí tienes.* — Here you go.
» *—Muchas gracias.* — Thank you very much.
» *—De nada.* — You're welcome.

> [!trap] Careful with the salsa
> *¿Pica?* means "Is it spicy?". If the answer is *Sí, pica mucho*, you have been warned!

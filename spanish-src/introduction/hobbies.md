## What Do You Like to Do?

Talking about *pasatiempos* (hobbies) is one of the easiest ways to get to know people. Start with the activities themselves.

### Activities (verbs)

| Spanish | English | Spanish | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| *bailar* | to dance | *cantar* | to sing |
| *leer* | to read | *escribir* | to write |
| *nadar* | to swim | *correr* | to run |
| *caminar* | to walk | *viajar* | to travel |
| *dibujar* | to draw | *pintar* | to paint |
| *cocinar* | to cook | *hornear* | to bake |
| *escuchar música* | to listen to music | *ver películas* | to watch films |
| *ir al cine* | to go to the cinema | *sacar fotos* | to take photos |
| *jugar al fútbol* | to play football | *jugar videojuegos* | to play video games |
| *montar en bicicleta* | to ride a bike | *hacer senderismo* | to go hiking |
| *hacer ejercicio* | to exercise | *hacer yoga* | to do yoga |

### Sports and hobbies (nouns)

| Spanish | English | Spanish | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| *el fútbol* | football / soccer | *el baloncesto*, *el básquetbol* | basketball |
| *el tenis* | tennis | *la natación* | swimming |
| *el ciclismo* | cycling | *el esquí* | skiing |
| *la lectura* | reading | *la cocina* | cooking |
| *la fotografía* | photography | *la jardinería* | gardening |
| *el ajedrez* | chess | *los juegos de mesa* | board games |

To ask about someone's hobbies:

» *¿Qué te gusta hacer?* — What do you like to do?
» *¿Qué haces en tu tiempo libre?* — What do you do in your free time?

> [!trap] jugar a + sport
> With *jugar* (to play a game or sport), Spain and many countries add *a* + article: *jugar al tenis*, *jugar a las cartas*. In parts of Latin America you'll also hear *jugar fútbol*. You never *jugar* an instrument: that's *tocar la guitarra*.

## I Like It Too!

"I like" in Spanish is ***me gusta***, which literally means "it pleases me". The thing you like is the grammatical subject, so the verb agrees with **it**, not with you.

| Form | Use with | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *me gusta* | a verb or a singular noun | *Me gusta nadar.* / *Me gusta el fútbol.* |
| *me gustan* | a plural noun | *Me gustan los deportes.* |
| *te gusta(n)* | you (informal) like | *¿Te gusta bailar?* / *¿Te gustan los perros?* |
| *le gusta(n)* | he / she likes; you (formal) like | *A Marta le gusta leer.* |
| *nos gusta(n)* | we like | *Nos gusta viajar.* |

» *Me gusta cocinar y hornear.* — I like cooking and baking.
» *Me gustan las películas de terror.* — I like horror films.
» *¿Te gusta la natación?* — Do you like swimming?

Agree with *también* (also) or, after a negative, *tampoco* (neither):

| They say | You agree |
|---|---|
| *Me gusta el tenis.* | *¡A mí también!* — Me too! |
| *No me gusta correr.* | *A mí tampoco.* — Me neither. |

~~Me gusto el fútbol.~~ → *Me gusta el fútbol.* ~~Me gusta los videojuegos.~~ → *Me gustan los videojuegos.*

> [!hack] Use the article with nouns
> When you like something in general, Spanish uses *el / la / los / las*: *Me gusta **la** música*, not ~~Me gusta música~~.

## Let's Agree to Disagree!

To say you **don't** like something, put *no* in front: *no me gusta, no me gustan*.

» *No me gusta el golf.* — I don't like golf.
» *No me gustan los juegos de mesa.* — I don't like board games.

Disagreeing politely is just as easy:

| They say | You disagree |
|---|---|
| *Me gusta bailar.* | *A mí no.* — I don't. |
| *No me gusta el yoga.* | *A mí sí.* — I do. |

» *—Me gustan mucho los videojuegos. —¿Sí? A mí no. Prefiero leer.* — I really like video games. — Really? I don't. I prefer reading.
» *—No me gusta correr. —A mí sí, ¡me encanta!* — I don't like running. — I do, I love it!

### Stronger and weaker

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| *Me encanta(n)…* | I love… |
| *Me gusta(n) mucho…* | I really like… |
| *Me gusta(n)…* | I like… |
| *No me gusta(n) mucho…* | I don't really like… |
| *No me gusta(n) nada…* | I don't like… at all |
| *Prefiero…* | I prefer… |
| *Odio…* | I hate… |

> [!trap] me encanta is never "muy"
> *Encantar* is already strong. ~~Me encanta mucho~~ sounds odd; just say *Me encanta*.

> [!fact] The extra "a mí"
> *A mí*, *a ti*, *a él* add emphasis or contrast: *A mí me gusta el té, pero a él le gusta el café.* It's optional in normal sentences but required in short replies like *A mí también*.

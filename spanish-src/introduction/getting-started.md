## What Is Your Name?

A conversation starts with a greeting, and the right greeting depends on **how formal the situation is**. With a stranger, an older person or a client you choose polite forms; with friends and classmates you can relax.

| Situation | Greeting | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| any situation | *Hola.* | Hello. / Hi. |
| morning, until about lunch | *Buenos días.* | Good morning. |
| afternoon, until it gets dark | *Buenas tardes.* | Good afternoon. |
| evening and night | *Buenas noches.* | Good evening. / Good night. |

To ask someone's name, Spanish uses the verb *llamarse* (literally "to call oneself"):

| | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| informal (*tú*) | *¿Cómo te llamas?* | *Me llamo Lucía.* |
| formal (*usted*) | *¿Cómo se llama usted?* | *Me llamo Pedro Ruiz.* |
| either | *¿Cuál es tu / su nombre?* | *Mi nombre es Ana.* |

» *Hola, me llamo Marta. ¿Cómo te llamas?* — Hi, my name is Marta. What's your name?
» *Buenas tardes. ¿Cómo se llama usted?* — Good afternoon. What is your name?
» *Soy Diego. Mucho gusto.* — I'm Diego. Nice to meet you.

After learning the name, say *Mucho gusto* or *Encantado* (a man speaking) / *Encantada* (a woman speaking) — "pleased to meet you".

> [!trap] Cómo, not qué
> English says "*What* is your name?", but Spanish asks "*How* do you call yourself?". ~~¿Qué te llamas?~~ → *¿Cómo te llamas?*

## What Do You Do?

Once you know someone's name, the next question is usually about work or study. There are several common ways to ask:

| Question | Literal sense | Register |
|---|---|---|
| *¿A qué te dedicas?* | What do you devote yourself to? | informal |
| *¿A qué se dedica usted?* | | formal |
| *¿En qué trabajas?* | What do you work in? | informal |
| *¿Qué haces?* | What do you do? (can also mean "what are you doing right now?") | informal |
| *¿Estudias o trabajas?* | Do you study or work? | informal |

Answers use *trabajar* (to work) or *estudiar* (to study) in the *yo* form, which ends in **-o**:

» *Trabajo en un banco.* — I work in a bank.
» *Trabajo en casa.* — I work from home.
» *Estudio medicina en la universidad.* — I study medicine at university.
» *Estudio y trabajo.* — I study and work.

| Verb | I… | you (informal)… | you (formal), he, she… |
|---|---|---|---|
| *trabajar* | *trabajo* | *trabajas* | *trabaja* |
| *estudiar* | *estudio* | *estudias* | *estudia* |

> [!hack] Bounce the question back
> Add *¿Y tú?* (informal) or *¿Y usted?* (formal) — "And you?" — after your answer and the conversation keeps going on its own.

## I'm a Teacher

To say what your job **is**, use *soy* ("I am", from *ser*) followed directly by the profession. Spanish leaves out the article *un / una* here, unlike English "a teacher".

» *Soy profesor.* — I'm a teacher. (man)
» *Soy profesora.* — I'm a teacher. (woman)
» *Soy estudiante.* — I'm a student.
» *Mi hermano es médico.* — My brother is a doctor.

~~Soy un profesor.~~ → *Soy profesor.*

Most job words change their ending for a woman: *-o* becomes *-a*, and a final consonant takes an extra *-a*. Words ending in *-e* or *-ista* stay the same.

| Man | Woman | English |
|---|---|---|
| *camarero* | *camarera* | waiter / waitress |
| *abogado* | *abogada* | lawyer |
| *profesor* | *profesora* | teacher |
| *estudiante* | *estudiante* | student |
| *dentista* | *dentista* | dentist |

The article comes back as soon as you add a description: *Es **un** médico excelente.* — He's an excellent doctor.

The full rules are in [Introduce Your Friends](introduction/introduce-your-friends).

## Tell Me About Yourself

A short self-introduction usually covers name, origin, home town, age and job. Each piece has a fixed pattern:

| To say… | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| your name | *Me llamo* / *Soy* + name | *Me llamo Sara.* |
| where you're from | *Soy de* + place | *Soy de Chile.* |
| where you live | *Vivo en* + place | *Vivo en Madrid.* |
| your age | *Tengo* + number + *años* | *Tengo veinte años.* |
| your job | *Soy* + job | *Soy enfermera.* |
| what you like | *Me gusta* + activity | *Me gusta leer.* |

» *Hola, soy Tomás. Soy de México, pero vivo en Texas.* — Hi, I'm Tomás. I'm from Mexico, but I live in Texas.
» *Tengo treinta años y soy ingeniero.* — I'm thirty and I'm an engineer.
» *Me gusta mucho el fútbol.* — I really like football.

> [!trap] Age is something you have
> Spanish uses *tener* (to have) for age: ~~Soy veinte años.~~ → *Tengo veinte años.* To ask, say *¿Cuántos años tienes?* — "How many years do you have?"

Notice that Spanish usually drops *yo* (I): the verb ending already shows who is speaking, so *Vivo en Madrid* is more natural than *Yo vivo en Madrid*.

## Let's Chat

Between people who already know each other, greetings get shorter and more relaxed. Use these with friends, classmates and coworkers you're on good terms with:

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| *¿Cómo estás?* | How are you? |
| *¿Qué tal?* | How's it going? |
| *¿Cómo te va?* | How's it going for you? |
| *¿Qué hay de nuevo?* | What's new? |
| *¿Qué pasa?* | What's up? |
| *¿Cómo van las cosas?* | How are things going? |

Among close friends you will also hear very casual, often regional greetings: *¿Qué onda?* (Mexico and Central America), *¿Quiubo?* (Colombia, Chile), *¿Qué más?* (Colombia), *¿Qué es la que hay?* (Puerto Rico), or *Oye* ("Hey!") to catch someone's attention.

» *—¡Hola, Ana! ¿Qué tal?* — Hi, Ana! How's it going?
» *—Bien, ¿y tú? ¿Qué hay de nuevo?* — Fine, and you? What's new?
» *—Nada, todo igual.* — Nothing, same as always.

> [!trap] Don't be too casual too soon
> *¿Qué onda?* to your new boss or a grandmother you've just met can sound cheeky. With strangers and older people stick to *Buenos días* and *¿Cómo está?*.

For more ways to answer these questions, see [What's Up?](introduction/whats-up).

## Goodbye!

The everyday word for goodbye is *adiós*, but Spanish speakers often prefer a "see you" phrase built with *hasta* (until):

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| *Adiós.* | Goodbye. |
| *Chao.* / *Chau.* | Bye. (very common, informal) |
| *Hasta luego.* | See you later. |
| *Hasta pronto.* | See you soon. |
| *Hasta mañana.* | See you tomorrow. |
| *Hasta el lunes.* | See you on Monday. |
| *Nos vemos.* | See you. |
| *Buenas noches.* | Good night. (also as a goodbye) |
| *Que tengas un buen día.* | Have a nice day. (informal) |
| *Cuídate.* / *Cuídese.* | Take care. (informal / formal) |

» *Bueno, me voy. ¡Hasta luego!* — Well, I'm off. See you later!
» *Gracias por todo. Nos vemos mañana.* — Thanks for everything. See you tomorrow.

> [!fact] Kisses and handshakes
> In Spain people often greet and say goodbye with two light kisses on the cheeks; in most of Latin America one kiss is usual between women and between men and women. A handshake is the safe choice in formal settings.

> [!hack] Hasta luego is the all-rounder
> *Hasta luego* works in shops, offices and with friends, even if you'll never see the person again. Shop assistants in Spain say it all day long.

## Conjugation and Uses

The **imperfect subjunctive** (*el pretérito imperfecto de subjuntivo*) is the past form of the subjunctive. It's used in the same situations as the present subjunctive, but when the trigger is in the **past** or the situation is **hypothetical**.

### Finding the stem

1. Take the **ellos** form of the **preterite**.
2. Drop **-ron**.
3. Add the endings.

| Infinitive | Preterite (ellos) | Stem |
|---|---|---|
| hablar | habla**ron** | habla- |
| comer | comie**ron** | comie- |
| vivir | vivie**ron** | vivie- |
| tener | tuvie**ron** | tuvie- |
| ser / ir | fue**ron** | fue- |
| decir | dije**ron** | dije- |
| pedir | pidie**ron** | pidie- |
| leer | leye**ron** | leye- |

Because it's built on the preterite, every preterite irregularity carries over automatically.

### Endings

There are two equivalent sets. The **-ra** forms are more common in speech, especially in Latin America; the **-se** forms are frequent in Spain and in writing.

| Person | -ra | -se | Example (tener) |
|---|---|---|---|
| yo | -ra | -se | tuviera / tuviese |
| tú | -ras | -ses | tuvieras / tuvieses |
| él / usted | -ra | -se | tuviera / tuviese |
| nosotros | **-ramos** | **-semos** | tuviéramos / tuviésemos |
| vosotros | -rais | -seis | tuvierais / tuvieseis |
| ellos / ustedes | -ran | -sen | tuvieran / tuviesen |

> [!trap] Watch the accent
> The *nosotros* form always takes an accent on the vowel before the ending: *habláramos, comiéramos, fuéramos, tuviésemos*.

### Main uses

- after a trigger in the **past** or **conditional**: *Quería que vinieras.*
- in **unreal *si* clauses**: *Si tuviera dinero, viajaría.*
- in **polite requests**: *Quisiera un café.*

» *Mis padres querían que estudiara derecho.* — My parents wanted me to study law.
» *Me sorprendió que no dijeras nada.* — It surprised me that you didn't say anything.

## Subjunctive vs. Indicative

The rules for choosing the mood don't change in the past: facts and beliefs take the **indicative** (preterite or imperfect); wishes, emotions, doubt and so on take the **imperfect subjunctive**.

| Indicative (past) | Imperfect subjunctive |
|---|---|
| *Sabía que estabas enfermo.* — I knew you were ill. | *Temía que estuvieras enfermo.* — I was afraid you were ill. |
| *Creía que era verdad.* — I thought it was true. | *No creía que fuera verdad.* — I didn't think it was true. |
| *Estaba claro que tenía razón.* | *Era posible que tuviera razón.* |
| *Dijo que venía.* — He said he was coming. | *Dijo que viniera.* — He told me to come. |

### Sequence of tenses

| Main clause | Subjunctive clause |
|---|---|
| present, future, present perfect, command | present (or present perfect) subjunctive |
| preterite, imperfect, past perfect, conditional | imperfect (or past perfect) subjunctive |

» *Te pido que me ayudes.* → *Te pedí que me ayudaras.*
» *Es necesario que lo sepan.* → *Era necesario que lo supieran.*

> [!fact] Present opinion of a past event
> A present trigger can also take the imperfect subjunctive when the action is in the past: *Me alegra que vinieras ayer.* — I'm glad you came yesterday.

## WEIRDO

The same trigger categories you learned for the present subjunctive work in the past. Remember **WEIRDO**:

| Letter | Category | Past example |
|---|---|---|
| **W** | Wishes | *Quería que me llamaras.* — I wanted you to call me. |
| **E** | Emotions | *Le molestó que llegáramos tarde.* — It annoyed her that we arrived late. |
| **I** | Impersonal expressions | *Era importante que todos votaran.* — It was important for everyone to vote. |
| **R** | Recommendations | *El médico me recomendó que descansara.* — The doctor recommended I rest. |
| **D** | Doubt / denial | *Dudaba que fuera posible.* — I doubted it was possible. |
| **O** | Ojalá | *Ojalá tuviera tu energía.* — I wish I had your energy. |

> [!hack] Ojalá + imperfect = I wish
> With the imperfect subjunctive, *ojalá* expresses a wish that is contrary to reality or unlikely now: *Ojalá estuvieras aquí* (I wish you were here).

## If Clauses

The imperfect subjunctive is essential for **hypothetical "if" sentences** about the present or future — things that aren't true or are unlikely.

**Formula:** *si* + imperfect subjunctive, + conditional

» *Si tuviera tiempo, aprendería japonés.* — If I had time, I'd learn Japanese.
» *Si fuera tú, no lo haría.* — If I were you, I wouldn't do it.
» *¿Qué harías si ganaras la lotería?* — What would you do if you won the lottery?
» *Si viviéramos en la costa, iríamos a la playa cada día.* — If we lived on the coast, we'd go to the beach every day.

The clauses can swap order: *Aprendería japonés si tuviera tiempo.*

### Three types of si sentence

| Type | Si clause | Result | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| real / likely | present indicative | present, future, command | *Si llueve, me quedo.* |
| unreal present / unlikely future | imperfect subjunctive | conditional | *Si lloviera, me quedaría.* |
| unreal past | past perfect subjunctive | conditional perfect | *Si hubiera llovido, me habría quedado.* |

> [!trap] Never conditional after si
> ~~Si tendría dinero~~ → *Si tuviera dinero*. The conditional goes in the result clause, not after *si*.

### Como si

*Como si* (as if) always takes the imperfect (or past perfect) subjunctive:

» *Habla como si fuera el jefe.* — He talks as if he were the boss.

## Relative Clauses

In the past, relative clauses about **unknown or non-existent** antecedents take the imperfect subjunctive.

» *Buscábamos un hotel que tuviera piscina.* — We were looking for a hotel that had a pool.
» *No había nadie que pudiera ayudarnos.* — There was nobody who could help us.
» *Necesitaban a alguien que hablara chino.* — They needed someone who spoke Chinese.
» *Me dijo que hiciera lo que quisiera.* — She told me to do whatever I wanted.

Known antecedents keep the indicative:

» *Encontramos un hotel que tenía piscina.* — We found a hotel that had a pool.

## Conditional Outcomes

Conjunctions that require the subjunctive (*para que, sin que, antes de que, a menos que, con tal de que, en caso de que*) take the imperfect subjunctive when the main clause is in the past or conditional.

» *Lo expliqué despacio para que todos lo entendieran.* — I explained it slowly so that everyone would understand.
» *Se fueron sin que nadie se diera cuenta.* — They left without anyone noticing.
» *Llegamos antes de que empezara la película.* — We arrived before the film started.
» *Te prestaría el coche con tal de que lo cuidaras.* — I'd lend you the car provided you looked after it.

Time conjunctions in the past refer to completed facts, so they usually take the **indicative**: *Cuando llegó, cenamos.* But "future in the past" takes the subjunctive: *Dijo que llamaría cuando llegara.* — He said he would call when he arrived.

## Polite Requests and Suggestions

The *-ra* forms of *querer*, *poder* and *deber* soften requests and advice, much like English *would*, *could* and *should*.

| Form | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *quisiera* | I would like | *Quisiera reservar una mesa.* — I'd like to book a table. |
| *pudiera* | could | *¿Pudiera ayudarme?* — Could you help me? (more common: *¿Podría…?*) |
| *debiera* | should | *Debieras descansar más.* — You ought to rest more. |

» *Quisiera hablar con la directora.* — I would like to speak to the director.
» *Quisiéramos dos cafés, por favor.* — We'd like two coffees, please.

> [!fact] Only the -ra form
> In this polite use, only the *-ra* form works: *quisiera*, not ~~quisiese~~.

Suggestions in the past also take it: *Sería mejor que te quedaras.* — It would be better if you stayed.

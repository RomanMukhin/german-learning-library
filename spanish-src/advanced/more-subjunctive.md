## Emotions

A **trigger** is a word or phrase in the main clause that makes the verb after *que* go into the subjunctive. Emotion triggers express how someone **feels about** another person's action or situation.

| Trigger | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *alegrarse de que* | to be glad that |
| *me gusta / me encanta que* | I like / love that |
| *me molesta / me fastidia que* | it bothers / annoys me that |
| *me preocupa que* | it worries me that |
| *sentir que* | to be sorry that |
| *temer que / tener miedo de que* | to fear that |
| *me sorprende que* | it surprises me that |
| *estar harto de que* | to be fed up that |
| *me da pena / rabia que* | it makes me sad / angry that |

» *Me encanta que cocines para mí.* — I love that you cook for me.
» *Estoy harta de que siempre llegues tarde.* — I'm fed up with you always being late.
» *Temo que no haya suficiente comida.* — I'm afraid there won't be enough food.
» *Nos preocupa que el niño no coma.* — It worries us that the child isn't eating.

### One subject, no que

When the person who feels and the person who acts are the same, use the infinitive:

» *Me alegro de verte.* — I'm glad to see you. (I feel, I see)
» *Me alegro de que me veas.* — I'm glad you see me. (I feel, you see)

> [!trap] Sentir: feel vs. be sorry
> *Siento que hace frío* (I feel that it's cold — perception, indicative) vs. *Siento que te vayas* (I'm sorry you're leaving — emotion, subjunctive).

## Wishes and Desires

Verbs of wanting and hoping trigger the subjunctive when the wish is about someone or something else.

| Trigger | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *querer que* | to want |
| *desear que* | to wish, desire |
| *esperar que* | to hope |
| *preferir que* | to prefer |
| *necesitar que* | to need (someone to) |
| *tener ganas de que* | to look forward to |

» *Espero que te guste el regalo.* — I hope you like the present.
» *Necesito que me ayudes con esto.* — I need you to help me with this.
» *Tengo ganas de que llegue el verano.* — I can't wait for summer to come.
» *¿Prefieres que vayamos en coche?* — Would you rather we went by car?

### Wishes as greetings

A bare *que* + subjunctive expresses good (or bad) wishes:

» *¡Que aproveche!* — Enjoy your meal!
» *¡Que duermas bien!* — Sleep well!
» *¡Que te diviertas!* — Have fun!

> [!hack] Esperar has two meanings
> *Esperar* = to hope (subjunctive) or to wait (*Espero a que llegue* — I'm waiting for him to arrive, also subjunctive). Either way, a different subject means subjunctive.

## Doubt and Denial

Doubting or denying puts the following verb into the subjunctive, because the speaker doesn't present it as fact.

| Subjunctive (doubt/denial) | Indicative (belief/certainty) |
|---|---|
| *dudar que* | *no dudar que* |
| *no creer que* | *creer que* |
| *no pensar que* | *pensar que* |
| *negar que* | *no negar que* (often) |
| *no estar seguro de que* | *estar seguro de que* |
| *no es verdad / cierto que* | *es verdad / cierto que* |

» *Dudo que el tren salga a tiempo.* — I doubt the train will leave on time.
» *No pienso que sea tan difícil.* — I don't think it's that hard.
» *El ministro niega que haya corrupción.* — The minister denies there is corruption.
» *No estoy segura de que lo sepan.* — I'm not sure they know.

But:

» *Estoy seguro de que lo saben.* — I'm sure they know.
» *Creo que va a llover.* — I think it's going to rain.

> [!trap] No dudo
> *No dudo que* removes the doubt, so it normally takes the **indicative**: *No dudo que es buena persona.*

## Uncertainty

Some words express that something is **possible but not certain**. Several of them are adverbs rather than verbs, so there may be no *que* at all.

| Word | Mood |
|---|---|
| *quizá(s), tal vez* | subjunctive (or indicative for more certainty) |
| *posiblemente, probablemente* | subjunctive (or indicative) |
| *es posible / probable que* | always subjunctive |
| *puede (ser) que* | always subjunctive |
| *a lo mejor* | always **indicative** |

» *Quizás vengan mañana.* — Perhaps they'll come tomorrow. (open)
» *Tal vez tengas razón.* — Maybe you're right.
» *Puede que no funcione.* — It might not work.
» *A lo mejor llueve.* — Maybe it'll rain. (*a lo mejor* + indicative)

> [!fact] Shades of certainty
> With *quizás* and *tal vez*, the subjunctive sounds less sure and the indicative more sure: *Quizás viene* (he's probably coming) vs. *Quizás venga* (he might come).

## Recommendations and Requests

When you try to influence someone else's behaviour — advise, ask, order, allow, forbid — the subjunctive follows.

| Trigger | Example |
|---|---|
| *aconsejar* | *Te aconsejo que duermas más.* |
| *recomendar* | *Os recomiendo que veáis esa serie.* |
| *sugerir* | *Sugiero que lo dejemos para mañana.* |
| *pedir* | *Me pide que le preste dinero.* — He asks me to lend him money. |
| *rogar* | *Les rogamos que apaguen el móvil.* |
| *exigir* | *Exigen que se respete el horario.* |
| *mandar / ordenar* | *Me mandó que me callara.* |
| *permitir / dejar* | *No me dejan que salga.* |
| *prohibir* | *Prohíben que se fume dentro.* |
| *insistir en* | *Insiste en que lo acompañe.* |

### Infinitive alternative

Verbs like *permitir, prohibir, mandar, dejar, aconsejar* can also take an indirect object + **infinitive**:

» *Te prohíbo que salgas.* = *Te prohíbo salir.* — I forbid you to go out.
» *Me aconsejó que descansara.* = *Me aconsejó descansar.*

> [!trap] Pedir vs. preguntar
> *Pedir* = to ask **for** / ask someone **to do** (subjunctive). *Preguntar* = to ask a **question** (indicative or *si*): *Me preguntó si venía.*

## Ojalá

*Ojalá* always takes the subjunctive, but the **tense** of the subjunctive changes the meaning.

| Ojalá + | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| present subjunctive | a hope that is possible | *Ojalá gane mi equipo.* — I hope my team wins. |
| present perfect subjunctive | hope about something that may already have happened | *Ojalá haya ganado.* — I hope it has won. |
| imperfect subjunctive | an unlikely or impossible wish now | *Ojalá tuviera más tiempo.* — I wish I had more time. |
| past perfect subjunctive | regret about the past | *Ojalá hubiera estudiado más.* — I wish I had studied more. |

» *Ojalá que no haya mucho tráfico.* — Hopefully there won't be much traffic.
» *Ojalá supiera tocar la guitarra.* — I wish I could play the guitar.

> [!hack] Hope vs. wish
> English separates *I hope* (possible) and *I wish* (unreal). Spanish uses *ojalá* for both and lets the subjunctive tense do the work.

## Impersonal Expressions

Impersonal expressions are phrases with no specific subject, usually *ser* + adjective/noun + *que*. Those expressing **judgement, necessity, possibility or emotion** take the subjunctive; those expressing **certainty** take the indicative.

| Subjunctive | Indicative |
|---|---|
| *es importante / necesario / esencial que* | *es verdad / cierto que* |
| *es bueno / malo / mejor que* | *es obvio / evidente que* |
| *es posible / imposible / probable que* | *está claro que* |
| *es raro / extraño / increíble que* | *es seguro que* |
| *es una lástima / pena que* | *no cabe duda de que* |
| *es lógico / normal / natural que* | *es indudable que* |

» *Es normal que estés nervioso.* — It's normal for you to be nervous.
» *Es increíble que no haya llamado.* — It's incredible that he hasn't called.
» *Es evidente que está cansada.* — It's obvious she's tired.

Without a specific subject, use the infinitive: *Es importante dormir bien.* — It's important to sleep well.

> [!trap] Negated certainty
> *No es verdad que*, *no es obvio que*, *no está claro que* deny certainty, so they switch to the **subjunctive**: *No está claro que sea culpable.*

## Comprehensive Review 1

Identify the trigger type and complete with the present subjunctive.

1. Me sorprende que (ellos, no saber) ___ nada.
2. Quiero que (tú, ser) ___ feliz.
3. Dudo que (haber) ___ entradas.
4. Es probable que (nosotros, llegar) ___ tarde.
5. Te sugiero que (ir) ___ al dentista.
6. Ojalá (salir) ___ el sol.

> [!fact] Answers
> 1. emotion — *no sepan* 2. wish — *seas* 3. doubt — *haya* 4. uncertainty — *lleguemos* 5. recommendation — *vayas* 6. *ojalá* — *salga*

### Triggers in other tenses

The trigger's tense decides the subjunctive tense. A present or future trigger takes the **present** subjunctive; a past or conditional trigger takes the **imperfect** subjunctive.

» *Quiero que vengas.* → *Quería que vinieras.* — I wanted you to come.
» *Le pido que se quede.* → *Le pedí que se quedara.* — I asked her to stay.

Indicative or subjunctive?

1. Es obvio que (necesitas / necesites) ayuda.
2. No es obvio que (necesitas / necesites) ayuda.
3. A lo mejor (vamos / vayamos) al cine.

> [!fact] Answers

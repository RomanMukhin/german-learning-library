## Conjugation and Uses

The **past perfect** (*el pretérito pluscuamperfecto*, also called the pluperfect) describes an action that was already finished **before another moment in the past**. English uses *had + past participle*; Spanish does exactly the same thing with the imperfect of *haber*.

**Formula:** imperfect of *haber* + past participle

| Person | haber (imperfect) | + participle |
|---|---|---|
| yo | había | terminado |
| tú | habías | comido |
| él / ella / usted | había | salido |
| nosotros / nosotras | habíamos | llegado |
| vosotros / vosotras | habíais | vivido |
| ellos / ellas / ustedes | habían | dormido |

Regular participles end in *-ado* (for *-ar* verbs) or *-ido* (for *-er* and *-ir* verbs). In the past perfect the participle **never changes** for gender or number: it always ends in *-o*.

» *Cuando llegamos al cine, la película ya había empezado.* — When we got to the cinema, the film had already started.
» *Marta nunca había probado el pulpo antes de ese viaje.* — Marta had never tried octopus before that trip.
» *¿Habías estudiado alemán antes de mudarte a Berlín?* — Had you studied German before moving to Berlin?
» *Los niños se habían dormido cuando volvimos.* — The children had fallen asleep when we came back.

### When to use it

- To show that one past event came **before** another past event: *ya había cenado cuando me llamaste*.
- With *ya* (already), *nunca* (never) and *todavía no* (not yet) to talk about experience up to a past point.
- In reported speech, to shift a preterite back one step: *Dijo que había perdido las llaves.* — He said he had lost his keys.

### Word order

*Haber* and the participle are a single unit. Nothing goes between them: object and reflexive pronouns go **before** *haber*, and so does *no*.

» *Ya **lo** había leído.* — I had already read it.
» *No **se lo** habíamos contado a nadie.* — We hadn't told anyone (about it).

~~había lo visto~~ → *lo había visto*

> [!hack] Same as English
> If you can say "had done" in English, you can almost always say *había hecho* in Spanish. The two tenses line up very closely.

## Irregular Past Participles

A handful of very common verbs have participles that don't end in *-ado/-ido*. You need them constantly in the past perfect.

| Infinitive | Participle | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| abrir | abierto | opened |
| cubrir | cubierto | covered |
| decir | dicho | said |
| escribir | escrito | written |
| hacer | hecho | done, made |
| morir | muerto | died |
| poner | puesto | put |
| resolver | resuelto | solved |
| romper | roto | broken |
| ver | visto | seen |
| volver | vuelto | returned |

Verbs built on these keep the same irregularity: *describir → descrito*, *deshacer → deshecho*, *devolver → devuelto*, *suponer → supuesto*, *descubrir → descubierto*, *prever → previsto*.

» *Alguien había roto la ventana durante la noche.* — Someone had broken the window during the night.
» *Todavía no habían vuelto de la playa.* — They still hadn't come back from the beach.
» *Le pregunté si había hecho los deberes.* — I asked him if he had done his homework.
» *Nunca habíamos visto tanta nieve.* — We had never seen so much snow.

### Accent on -ído

When the stem ends in *a*, *e* or *o*, the *-ido* ending takes an accent so the *i* is pronounced separately: *leer → leído*, *traer → traído*, *oír → oído*, *caer → caído*, *creer → creído*. Verbs in *-uir* do **not** take it: *construir → construido*.

> [!trap] Don't regularise
> ~~había ponido~~ → *había puesto*, ~~habían rompido~~ → *habían roto*, ~~había volvido~~ → *había vuelto*. These are among the most common learner errors at this level.

## Comprehensive Review 1

Time to put the form and the timeline together. The past perfect answers the question "what had already happened by then?"

### Recap

| Step | Example |
|---|---|
| Pick the imperfect of *haber* | *habíamos* |
| Add the participle (-ado / -ido or irregular) | *habíamos salido* |
| Pronouns and *no* go in front | *no nos habíamos ido* |

### Practice: choose the right form

Complete each sentence with the past perfect of the verb in brackets.

1. Cuando llegó la policía, el ladrón ya ___ (escapar).
2. Yo nunca ___ (ver) un eclipse antes de 2024.
3. ¿Vosotros ya ___ (comer) cuando os llamé?
4. Ella me dijo que ___ (escribir) tres cartas.
5. Nosotros no ___ (abrir) los regalos todavía.

> [!fact] Answers
> 1. *había escapado* 2. *había visto* 3. *habíais comido* 4. *había escrito* 5. *habíamos abierto*

### Past perfect or preterite?

The preterite simply narrates events in order. The past perfect jumps **backwards** from a past reference point.

» *Salí de casa y empezó a llover.* — I left home and it started to rain. (first one, then the other)
» *Cuando salí de casa, ya había empezado a llover.* — When I left home, it had already started raining. (the rain came first)

> [!hack] Look for the anchor
> Words like *cuando*, *antes de que*, *ya* and *para entonces* (by then) usually signal a past anchor point. Whatever happened before that anchor goes in the past perfect.

## Comprehensive Review 2

This review focuses on irregular participles, pronoun placement and reported speech.

### Reported speech

When you report what someone said in the past, a preterite or present perfect in the original words usually becomes past perfect.

» *"Perdí el autobús."* → *Dijo que **había perdido** el autobús.* — He said he had missed the bus.
» *"Hemos terminado."* → *Explicaron que **habían terminado**.* — They explained that they had finished.

### Practice: rewrite with pronouns

Replace the noun in italics with a pronoun and keep the past perfect.

1. Habíamos visto *la película*. → ___
2. Ella había puesto *los platos* en la mesa. → ___
3. Le habías dicho *la verdad* a tu madre. → ___
4. No me había lavado *las manos*. → ___

> [!fact] Answers
> 1. *La habíamos visto.* 2. *Ella los había puesto en la mesa.* 3. *Se la habías dicho.* 4. *No me las había lavado.*

### Common mistakes

- ~~Había ya terminado~~ → *Ya había terminado* (keep *ya* outside the verb group)
- ~~Las cartas que había escritas~~ → *Las cartas que había escrito* (no agreement after *haber*)
- ~~Habíamos devolvido el libro~~ → *Habíamos devuelto el libro*

> [!trap] Había is not "there was" here
> On its own, *había* can mean "there was/were" (*había mucha gente*). Followed by a participle, it is the auxiliary: *había llegado* = "he had arrived".

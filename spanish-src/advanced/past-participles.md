## Regular Participles

The **past participle** (*el participio*) is the verb form English writes with *-ed* or *-en*: *worked*, *eaten*, *written*. In Spanish it does two main jobs: it builds the **perfect tenses** with *haber*, and it works as an **adjective**.

### How to form it

| Verb type | Ending | Example |
|---|---|---|
| -ar | **-ado** | *cerrar → cerrado* |
| -er | **-ido** | *conocer → conocido* |
| -ir | **-ido** | *salir → salido* |

When the stem ends in *a*, *e* or *o*, the *i* of *-ido* carries an accent: *leer → leído*, *caer → caído*, *oír → oído*, *reír → reído*, *traer → traído*. Verbs in *-uir* do not: *incluir → incluido*.

### Job 1: perfect tenses

With *haber*, the participle forms the compound tenses. Here it is **invariable** — always *-o*.

| Tense | Formula | Example |
|---|---|---|
| present perfect | he, has, ha… + participle | *He cerrado la puerta.* — I have closed the door. |
| past perfect | había, habías… + participle | *Había cerrado la puerta.* — I had closed the door. |
| future perfect | habré, habrás… + participle | *Habré cerrado la puerta.* — I will have closed the door. |
| conditional perfect | habría, habrías… + participle | *Habría cerrado la puerta.* — I would have closed the door. |
| present perfect subjunctive | haya, hayas… + participle | *…que haya cerrado la puerta.* |
| past perfect subjunctive | hubiera, hubieras… + participle | *…que hubiera cerrado la puerta.* |

### Job 2: adjective

Used as an adjective, the participle **agrees** with the noun in gender and number, like any adjective ending in *-o*.

» *una ventana cerrada* — a closed window
» *los platos lavados* — the washed dishes
» *La tienda está cerrada.* — The shop is closed. (with *estar*: a resulting state)
» *Las cartas fueron enviadas ayer.* — The letters were sent yesterday. (with *ser*: passive voice)

> [!trap] Agreement depends on the helper
> After *haber*: no agreement (*Ella ha cerrado las ventanas*). After *estar* or *ser*, or next to a noun: agreement (*Las ventanas están cerradas*).

## Irregular Participles

Some of the most common verbs have irregular participles. Most end in **-to** or **-cho**.

| Infinitive | Participle | Infinitive | Participle |
|---|---|---|---|
| abrir | abierto | poner | puesto |
| cubrir | cubierto | resolver | resuelto |
| decir | dicho | romper | roto |
| escribir | escrito | ver | visto |
| hacer | hecho | volver | vuelto |
| morir | muerto | satisfacer | satisfecho |
| freír | frito / freído | imprimir | impreso / imprimido |

### Families

Prefixed verbs inherit the irregular form of their base verb:

» *descubrir → descubierto* (discovered), *encubrir → encubierto*
» *componer → compuesto*, *proponer → propuesto*, *suponer → supuesto*
» *deshacer → deshecho*, *rehacer → rehecho*
» *devolver → devuelto*, *envolver → envuelto*, *revolver → revuelto*
» *describir → descrito*, *inscribir → inscrito*
» *prever → previsto*, *predecir → predicho*

> [!trap] Not all lookalikes are irregular
> *Prevenir* is regular (*prevenido*), and *bendecir / maldecir* use *bendecido / maldecido* in the perfect tenses, even though they come from *decir*.

### Double forms

*Freír*, *imprimir* and *proveer* have two accepted participles. The irregular one is most common as an adjective (*patatas fritas*, *un libro impreso*); both work after *haber*.

» *He frito / He freído los huevos.* — I've fried the eggs.
» *Me encantan los huevos fritos.* — I love fried eggs.

## Comprehensive Review 1

Focus: forming participles.

### Practice

Write the past participle of each verb.

1. comprar 2. vender 3. pedir 4. creer 5. escribir 6. poner 7. construir 8. morir

> [!fact] Answers
> 1. *comprado* 2. *vendido* 3. *pedido* 4. *creído* 5. *escrito* 6. *puesto* 7. *construido* 8. *muerto*

### Accents at a glance

| Needs an accent | No accent |
|---|---|
| *leído, creído, caído, traído, oído, reído* | *construido, huido, incluido, destruido* |

> [!hack] Hear the gap
> If you pronounce the *i* as a separate syllable after a vowel (*le-í-do*), it needs a written accent. In *-uir* verbs the *ui* glides together, so no accent is written.

## Comprehensive Review 2

Focus: perfect tense versus adjective.

### Practice

Choose the right form in each pair.

1. Las puertas están (abierto / abiertas).
2. Hemos (abierto / abiertos) las puertas.
3. Encontré dos libros (escrito / escritos) en latín.
4. Ella ha (escrito / escrita) una novela.
5. La comida ya está (hecho / hecha).

> [!fact] Answers
> 1. *abiertas* 2. *abierto* 3. *escritos* 4. *escrito* 5. *hecha*

### State versus action

*Estar* + participle describes the **result** of an action; *haber* + participle reports the **action** itself.

» *He arreglado la bici.* — I have fixed the bike. (the action)
» *La bici está arreglada.* — The bike is fixed. (the resulting state)

## Comprehensive Review 3

Focus: participles as adjectives and nouns.

### Participles as nouns

Many participles turn into nouns, often with *el / la / lo*:

» *el herido* — the injured person
» *los invitados* — the guests
» *la entrada* — the entrance / ticket
» *lo dicho* — what has been said
» *Lo hecho, hecho está.* — What's done is done.

### Fixed adjectives from participles

Some participles have become ordinary adjectives with their own shade of meaning:

| Participle | Adjective meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *aburrido* | bored / boring | *una clase aburrida* |
| *cansado* | tired / tiring | *Estoy cansado.* |
| *divertido* | fun | *una fiesta divertida* |
| *preocupado* | worried | *Están preocupados.* |
| *sentado* | sitting, seated | *Está sentada junto a la ventana.* |

> [!trap] Sitting and lying are states
> English uses *-ing* for body positions, but Spanish uses the participle: *Está sentado* (he's sitting), *Están acostados* (they're lying down), ~~está sentando~~.

## Extra Practice 1

Translate using *haber* + participle (present perfect).

1. I have seen that film.
2. They have returned home.
3. We have said nothing.
4. Have you (tú) done the shopping?
5. She has broken her arm.

> [!fact] Answers
> 1. *He visto esa película.* 2. *Han vuelto a casa.* 3. *No hemos dicho nada.* 4. *¿Has hecho la compra?* 5. *Se ha roto el brazo.*

Notice sentence 5: Spanish uses a reflexive and the definite article with body parts, not a possessive: ~~ha roto su brazo~~ in the accidental sense.

## Extra Practice 2

Complete with *estar* + participle, making it agree.

1. Los niños ___ (dormir).
2. La mesa ___ (poner).
3. Las tiendas ___ (cerrar) los domingos.
4. El problema ya ___ (resolver).
5. Mis abuelas ___ (sentar) en el sofá.

> [!fact] Answers
> 1. *están dormidos* 2. *está puesta* 3. *están cerradas* 4. *está resuelto* 5. *están sentadas*

> [!hack] Make it match the noun
> With *estar*, check the subject: feminine plural subject → *-as*, masculine plural → *-os*.

## Extra Practice 3

Mixed practice: decide whether the participle agrees.

1. Ellas han (llegado / llegadas) tarde.
2. Las paredes fueron (pintado / pintadas) de azul.
3. Tengo los deberes (hecho / hechos).
4. Habíamos (perdido / perdidos) las llaves.
5. Es una ciudad muy (conocido / conocida).

> [!fact] Answers
> 1. *llegado* (after *haber*) 2. *pintadas* (passive with *ser*) 3. *hechos* (*tener* + object + participle agrees) 4. *perdido* 5. *conocida*

> [!fact] Tener + participle
> *Tengo escritas tres páginas* stresses the completed result you now have. The participle agrees with the object, unlike the *haber* perfect.

## Regular Present Participles

The Spanish **present participle**, called the *gerundio*, is the form ending in **-ndo**. It is the equivalent of the English *-ing* form in *I am reading* or *he left running*.

> [!fact] A note on the word "gerund"
> In English grammar, a *gerund* is an *-ing* form used as a noun (*Swimming is fun*). Spanish does **not** use the *gerundio* that way; it uses the infinitive instead: *Nadar es divertido.* So although Spanish grammars call the -ndo form *gerundio*, it works like the English present participle.

### How to form it

Remove the infinitive ending and add:

| Verb type | Ending | Example |
|---|---|---|
| -ar | **-ando** | *bailar → bail- → bailando* |
| -er | **-iendo** | *beber → beb- → bebiendo* |
| -ir | **-iendo** | *abrir → abr- → abriendo* |

The *stem* is what remains when you take off *-ar*, *-er* or *-ir*.

» *Estoy cocinando.* — I'm cooking.
» *¿Qué estás haciendo?* — What are you doing?
» *Seguimos aprendiendo.* — We keep on learning.
» *Pasé la tarde escribiendo correos.* — I spent the afternoon writing emails.

The *gerundio* never changes for gender or number: *ella está cantando*, *ellos están cantando*.

> [!hack] Only two endings
> Every regular *-ar* verb gives *-ando* and every regular *-er/-ir* verb gives *-iendo*. Learn the handful of exceptions in the next lesson and you know them all.

## Irregular Present Participles

A few groups of verbs change their spelling or their stem in the *gerundio*.

### 1. -yendo after a vowel

If the stem of an *-er* or *-ir* verb ends in a vowel, *-iendo* becomes **-yendo**. An unstressed *i* between two vowels is always written *y*.

| Infinitive | Gerundio |
|---|---|
| leer | leyendo |
| creer | creyendo |
| traer | trayendo |
| caer | cayendo |
| oír | oyendo |
| construir | construyendo |
| huir | huyendo |

### 2. Ir

*Ir* has no real stem, so its *gerundio* is simply **yendo**: *Estamos yendo al centro.* — We're heading to the centre.

### 3. -endo after ll and ñ

If the stem ends in *ll* or *ñ*, the *i* disappears: *bullir → bullendo*, *gruñir → gruñendo*, *reñir → riñendo*, *teñir → tiñendo*.

### 4. Stem changes

*-ir* verbs that change their stem in the third-person preterite (*pidió*, *durmió*) keep that change in the *gerundio*. *Poder* does the same.

| Change | Infinitive | Preterite (él) | Gerundio |
|---|---|---|---|
| e → i | pedir | pidió | pidiendo |
| e → i | decir | dijo | diciendo |
| e → i | sentir | sintió | sintiendo |
| e → i | venir | vino | viniendo |
| o → u | dormir | durmió | durmiendo |
| o → u | morir | murió | muriendo |
| o → u | poder | pudo | pudiendo |

» *El bebé sigue durmiendo.* — The baby is still sleeping.
» *Me estás pidiendo demasiado.* — You're asking too much of me.

> [!trap] Not every stem-changer
> *-ar* and *-er* stem-changers keep a regular *gerundio*: *pensar → pensando*, *volver → volviendo*, *querer → queriendo*.

## Uses

The *gerundio* has two main jobs in Spanish.

### 1. Progressive (continuous) tenses

With **estar** it forms the progressive tenses, which stress that an action is in progress at a given moment.

» *Estoy hablando por teléfono.* — I'm talking on the phone.
» *Estaban jugando cuando empezó a llover.* — They were playing when it started to rain.

Other verbs combine with it too, each adding a nuance: *seguir / continuar* (keep on), *ir* (gradually), *andar* (going around doing), *llevar* + time (have been doing).

» *Sigue nevando.* — It's still snowing.
» *Poco a poco va mejorando.* — Little by little, he's getting better.
» *Llevo dos horas esperando.* — I've been waiting for two hours.

### 2. Adverbial use: how or by what means

On its own, the *gerundio* can describe **how** something is done or what is happening at the same time.

» *Salió corriendo.* — She ran out. (literally: she left running)
» *Aprendí inglés viendo series.* — I learned English by watching series.
» *Llegaron cantando.* — They arrived singing.

### What it does NOT do

> [!trap] No gerundio as a noun or adjective
> As a subject or after a preposition, use the infinitive: ~~Fumando es malo~~ → *Fumar es malo*; ~~antes de saliendo~~ → *antes de salir*. To describe a noun, use a relative clause: ~~una caja conteniendo libros~~ → *una caja que contiene libros*.

Spanish also avoids the progressive for future plans, where English uses it freely: *Salgo mañana* (I'm leaving tomorrow), not ~~estoy saliendo mañana~~.

## Pronouns and Present Participles

Object and reflexive pronouns can go in **two** places with a *gerundio*:

1. **Attached** to the end of the *gerundio* (written as one word).
2. **Before** the conjugated verb (usually *estar*).

Both are equally correct.

» *Estoy leyéndolo.* = *Lo estoy leyendo.* — I'm reading it.
» *Están duchándose.* = *Se están duchando.* — They're having a shower.
» *Sigue explicándonoslo.* = *Nos lo sigue explicando.* — She keeps explaining it to us.

### The written accent

When you attach a pronoun, the stress stays on the same syllable, so you must add an accent to keep it there: *comiendo → comiéndolo*, *mirando → mirándote*, *diciendo → diciéndoselo*.

~~estoy haciendolo~~ → *estoy haciéndolo*

### When there is no estar

If the *gerundio* stands alone (adverbial use), the pronoun **must** be attached:

» *Aprendí mucho escuchándola.* — I learned a lot by listening to her.
» *Se fue despidiéndose de todos.* — He left saying goodbye to everyone.

> [!hack] Never split them up
> The pronoun can go at the very front or the very end, but never in the middle: ~~estoy lo leyendo~~.

## Comprehensive Review

### Form check

| Infinitive | Rule | Gerundio |
|---|---|---|
| trabajar | -ar → -ando | trabajando |
| aprender | -er → -iendo | aprendiendo |
| oír | vowel stem → -yendo | oyendo |
| ir | special | yendo |
| repetir | e → i | repitiendo |
| morir | o → u | muriendo |

### Practice

Give the *gerundio*.

1. escuchar 2. traer 3. servir 4. poder 5. destruir 6. reír

> [!fact] Answers
> 1. *escuchando* 2. *trayendo* 3. *sirviendo* 4. *pudiendo* 5. *destruyendo* 6. *riendo* (e → i stem change, and the two *i*s merge into one)

### Use check

Choose between the infinitive and the *gerundio*.

» *Me encanta ___ (bailar).* → *bailar* (noun-like: "dancing" as a thing you love)
» *Está ___ (bailar) en la cocina.* → *bailando* (action in progress)
» *Después de ___ (cenar), salimos.* → *cenar* (after a preposition)
» *Se ganó la vida ___ (pintar) retratos.* → *pintando* (by what means)

> [!trap] English -ing ≠ Spanish -ndo
> Ask yourself: is the English *-ing* a noun? Then Spanish needs the infinitive. Is it an action in progress or a manner? Then use the *gerundio*.

## Extra Practice 1

Focus: forming the *gerundio* of everyday verbs.

Turn each sentence into the present progressive (*estar* + *gerundio*).

1. Mis padres viajan por Perú.
2. Leo un libro muy largo.
3. ¿Duermes?
4. El profesor repite la pregunta.
5. Construimos una casa en el campo.
6. Vais al supermercado.

> [!fact] Answers
> 1. *Mis padres están viajando por Perú.* 2. *Estoy leyendo un libro muy largo.* 3. *¿Estás durmiendo?* 4. *El profesor está repitiendo la pregunta.* 5. *Estamos construyendo una casa en el campo.* 6. *Estáis yendo al supermercado.*

### Quick tips

- Check whether the stem ends in a vowel: that triggers *-yendo*.
- For *-ir* verbs, think of the *él* preterite: *repitió → repitiendo*.
- *Ir* is always *yendo*, but in practice Spanish speakers often prefer the simple present for "going": *Voy al súper* is more natural than *Estoy yendo al súper*.

## Extra Practice 2

Focus: the adverbial *gerundio* and verbs other than *estar*.

Translate into Spanish using a *gerundio*.

1. He keeps calling me.
2. We spent the weekend painting the flat.
3. She came in crying.
4. I've been living here for five years.
5. You learn by making mistakes.

> [!fact] Answers
> 1. *Sigue llamándome.* / *Me sigue llamando.* 2. *Pasamos el fin de semana pintando el piso.* 3. *Entró llorando.* 4. *Llevo cinco años viviendo aquí.* 5. *Se aprende cometiendo errores.*

### Nuance of auxiliary verbs

| Verb + gerundio | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| estar | in progress | *Está lloviendo.* |
| seguir / continuar | still, keep on | *Seguimos esperando.* |
| ir | gradual change | *Va oscureciendo.* — It's getting dark. |
| andar | going around | *Anda diciendo tonterías.* — He goes around talking nonsense. |
| llevar + time | duration up to now | *Llevan un mes ensayando.* |

## Extra Practice 3

Focus: pronouns and accents.

Rewrite each sentence with the pronoun attached to the *gerundio*, adding the accent.

1. Lo estoy pensando.
2. Te estaba buscando.
3. Nos lo están preparando.
4. Se está vistiendo.
5. Se los estoy enviando.

> [!fact] Answers
> 1. *Estoy pensándolo.* 2. *Estaba buscándote.* 3. *Están preparándonoslo.* 4. *Está vistiéndose.* 5. *Estoy enviándoselos.*

### Spot the error

- ~~Estamos nos divirtiendo~~ → *Nos estamos divirtiendo* or *Estamos divirtiéndonos*
- ~~Está comiendola~~ → *Está comiéndola*
- ~~Me gusta corriendo~~ → *Me gusta correr*

> [!hack] Count the syllables
> Adding even one pronoun pushes the stressed syllable to third-from-last, so the *a* of *-ando* or the *e* of *-iendo* always needs a written accent.

## Informal Future

The easiest way to talk about the future is the **informal future**, built just like English "going to":

**ir** (present) + **a** + infinitive

| Subject | *ir* | + *a* + infinitive |
|---|---|---|
| *yo* | *voy* | *a comer* |
| *tú* | *vas* | *a comer* |
| *él / ella / usted* | *va* | *a comer* |
| *nosotros* | *vamos* | *a comer* |
| *vosotros* | *vais* | *a comer* |
| *ellos / ustedes* | *van* | *a comer* |

» *Voy a llamar a mi madre esta noche.* — I'm going to call my mother tonight.
» *¿Vas a venir a la fiesta?* — Are you going to come to the party?
» *Va a llover.* — It's going to rain.

It is used for plans, intentions and things that are clearly about to happen. In everyday speech, especially in Latin America, it is far more common than the simple future.

### Placing pronouns

Object and reflexive pronouns go either before *ir* or attached to the infinitive:

» *Lo voy a comprar.* = *Voy a comprarlo.* — I'm going to buy it.
» *Me voy a duchar.* = *Voy a ducharme.* — I'm going to have a shower.

> [!trap] Don't forget the a
> ~~Voy comer~~ → *Voy a comer*. Without *a*, the sentence is ungrammatical.

> [!fact] Vamos a...
> *Vamos a* + infinitive can also mean "let's": *¡Vamos a bailar!* — Let's dance!

## Regular Simple Future

The **simple future** is a single word: *hablaré* — I will speak. Unlike most tenses, you build it on the **whole infinitive**, and all three verb groups (*-ar, -er, -ir*) share the same endings.

| Subject | Ending | *viajar* | *comer* | *escribir* |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *yo* | *-é* | *viajaré* | *comeré* | *escribiré* |
| *tú* | *-ás* | *viajarás* | *comerás* | *escribirás* |
| *él / ella / usted* | *-á* | *viajará* | *comerá* | *escribirá* |
| *nosotros* | *-emos* | *viajaremos* | *comeremos* | *escribiremos* |
| *vosotros* | *-éis* | *viajaréis* | *comeréis* | *escribiréis* |
| *ellos / ustedes* | *-án* | *viajarán* | *comerán* | *escribirán* |

Every form except *nosotros* carries a written accent.

### What it is used for

1. **Predictions** about the future: *Mañana hará sol.* — Tomorrow it'll be sunny.
2. **Guesses about the present** (the "future of probability"): *¿Dónde estará Ana? Estará en casa.* — I wonder where Ana is. She's probably at home.
3. **Solemn or firm commands**, as in laws and rules: *No matarás.* — Thou shalt not kill.

» *Algún día viviremos en la playa.* — Some day we'll live by the beach.
» *¿Qué hora será?* — I wonder what time it is.

> [!fact] Where the endings come from
> The endings are the present of *haber* (*he, has, ha, hemos...*) glued onto the infinitive. *Cantar he* ("I have to sing") slowly became *cantaré*.

## Irregular Simple Future

Twelve common verbs (plus their compounds) change their stem. The endings stay exactly the same.

| Pattern | Infinitive | Stem | *yo* form |
|---|---|---|---|
| vowel → *d* | *tener* | *tendr-* | *tendré* |
| | *poner* | *pondr-* | *pondré* |
| | *venir* | *vendr-* | *vendré* |
| | *salir* | *saldr-* | *saldré* |
| | *valer* | *valdr-* | *valdré* |
| vowel dropped | *poder* | *podr-* | *podré* |
| | *saber* | *sabr-* | *sabré* |
| | *haber* | *habr-* | *habrá* (there will be) |
| | *caber* | *cabr-* | *cabré* |
| | *querer* | *querr-* | *querré* |
| fully irregular | *decir* | *dir-* | *diré* |
| | *hacer* | *har-* | *haré* |

» *Te lo diré mañana.* — I'll tell you tomorrow.
» *No podremos ir.* — We won't be able to go.
» *Habrá mucha gente.* — There will be lots of people.
» *¿Qué harás este verano?* — What will you do this summer?

Compounds follow their base verb: *mantener → mantendré*, *proponer → propondré*, *deshacer → desharé*.

> [!hack] Same stems, two tenses
> The conditional uses exactly the same twelve irregular stems, so learning them once covers both tenses.

> [!trap] Querer has two r's
> ~~queré~~ → *querré*. The double *r* is what distinguishes future *querré* from other forms.

## Comprehensive Review 1

Two ways to talk about the future:

| | Informal future | Simple future |
|---|---|---|
| Form | *ir a* + infinitive | infinitive + *-é, -ás, -á, -emos, -éis, -án* |
| Feel | plans, near future, everyday speech | predictions, promises, formal writing |
| Extra use | "let's" with *vamos a* | guesses about the present |

Rewrite with the simple future:

» *Voy a estudiar.* → *Estudiaré.*
» *Van a salir tarde.* → *Saldrán tarde.*
» *¿Vas a tener tiempo?* → *¿Tendrás tiempo?*
» *Vamos a hacer la cena.* → *Haremos la cena.*
» *Va a haber un examen.* → *Habrá un examen.*

## Comprehensive Review 2

Choose the meaning: future event or guess about now?

» *Serán las diez.* → guess: It's probably ten o'clock.
» *Llegaré a las diez.* → future: I'll arrive at ten.
» *Juan tendrá unos cuarenta años.* → guess: Juan must be about forty.
» *El tren saldrá a las ocho.* → future: The train will leave at eight.

Conjugate in the simple future:

» *nosotros / poner* → *pondremos*
» *tú / saber* → *sabrás*
» *ellos / decir* → *dirán*
» *yo / venir* → *vendré*
» *usted / querer* → *querrá*

> [!hack] Present for timetables
> For fixed schedules, Spanish often uses the plain present: *El concierto empieza a las nueve.*

## Extra Practice 1

Focus: *ir a* + infinitive. Say what each person is going to do.

» *Marta / viajar a Chile* → *Marta va a viajar a Chile.*
» *nosotros / cocinar* → *Vamos a cocinar.*
» *tú / acostarse temprano* → *Te vas a acostar temprano* / *Vas a acostarte temprano.*
» *ellos / no comprar nada* → *No van a comprar nada.*
» *yo / decírselo* → *Voy a decírselo* / *Se lo voy a decir.*

## Extra Practice 2

Focus: regular simple future. Watch the accents.

» *yo / trabajar* → *trabajaré*
» *vosotros / beber* → *beberéis*
» *ella / vivir* → *vivirá*
» *nosotros / aprender* → *aprenderemos*
» *ustedes / ir* → *irán*
» *tú / ser* → *serás*

*Ir* and *ser* are regular in the future, even though they are wildly irregular elsewhere.

## Extra Practice 3

Focus: irregular stems in context.

» *Si tienes tiempo, ¿me (hacer) un favor?* → *harás*
» *Mañana no (caber) todos en el coche.* → *cabremos*
» *¿Cuánto (valer) esta casa en diez años?* → *valdrá*
» *Te prometo que (salir) a tiempo.* → *saldré*
» *¿Dónde (estar) mis llaves?* → *estarán* (I wonder where my keys are)

> [!trap] Dirá, not decirá
> ~~deciré~~ → *diré*; ~~haceré~~ → *haré*. These two lose most of their infinitive.

## Pronouns and Family Terms

Every Spanish noun is either **masculine** or **feminine**, and that gender shows up in the words around it. For people, grammatical gender usually follows the person's sex, and you can see it clearly in pronouns and family vocabulary.

| | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| he / she | *él* | *ella* |
| we | *nosotros* | *nosotras* |
| you all (Spain) | *vosotros* | *vosotras* |
| they | *ellos* | *ellas* |

The masculine plural is used for a mixed group, even if there is only one man among many women: *ellos* can mean "they" for Marta, Lucía, Ana and Pablo together.

Family words follow the same pattern. Most swap *-o* for *-a*:

| Masculine | Feminine | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *hermano* | *hermana* | brother / sister |
| *hijo* | *hija* | son / daughter |
| *tío* | *tía* | uncle / aunt |
| *abuelo* | *abuela* | grandfather / grandmother |
| *primo* | *prima* | male / female cousin |
| *padre* | *madre* | father / mother |
| *yerno* | *nuera* | son-in-law / daughter-in-law |

» *Mis tíos viven en Sevilla.* — My aunt and uncle (or my uncles) live in Seville.
» *Ella es mi prima favorita.* — She's my favourite cousin.
» *Nosotras vamos al gimnasio.* — We (all women) go to the gym.

> [!fact] Plural masculine = mixed group
> *Los padres* means "the parents", *los hermanos* can mean "the siblings", and *los abuelos* "the grandparents". Context tells you whether only males are meant.

A few family pairs are completely different words: *padre / madre*, *yerno / nuera*, *padrino / madrina* (godfather / godmother) is regular again. Learn the odd ones as pairs.

## Articles

Articles are the most reliable signal of a noun's gender. Spanish has four forms of each article, one for every combination of gender and number.

| | Masc. singular | Fem. singular | Masc. plural | Fem. plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| the | *el* | *la* | *los* | *las* |
| a / some | *un* | *una* | *unos* | *unas* |

» *el libro, los libros* — the book, the books
» *una mesa, unas mesas* — a table, some tables
» *un amigo y una amiga* — a (male) friend and a (female) friend

### Feminine nouns with *el*

A feminine singular noun that begins with a **stressed** *a-* or *ha-* takes *el* and *un* instead of *la* and *una*, purely to avoid two "a" sounds colliding. The noun stays feminine: adjectives and the plural article show it.

» *el agua fría* — the cold water
» *un águila negra* — a black eagle
» *las aguas frías* — the cold waters
» *el hambre* — hunger

> [!trap] Only when the first syllable is stressed
> *la amiga* and *la hamaca* keep *la*, because the stress falls on *-mi-* and *-ma-*, not on the first *a*.

When you learn a new noun, learn it with its article: say *la mano*, not just *mano*. That habit saves you from most gender mistakes later.

## Masculine and Feminine Nouns 1

You can usually guess a noun's gender from its ending.

### Masculine endings

Most nouns ending in **-o** are masculine: *el vaso, el perro, el cuaderno*. So are most nouns ending in:

| Ending | Examples |
|---|---|
| *-or* | *el color, el amor, el motor* |
| *-aje* | *el viaje, el paisaje, el garaje* |
| *-ma* (of Greek origin) | *el problema, el tema, el sistema, el idioma* |
| stressed vowel | *el sofá, el menú, el café* |

Days, months, languages, colours used as nouns, rivers, seas and numbers are masculine too: *el lunes, el español, el rojo, el Amazonas, el Mediterráneo, el siete*.

> [!fact] Borrowed from Greek
> Words such as *el programa, el clima, el planeta, el mapa* and *el día* end in *-a* but are masculine. Many of them are international words that look similar in English.

### Masculine exceptions

A few common nouns ending in *-o* are feminine, usually because they are short forms of longer feminine words:

» *la mano* — the hand
» *la foto* (from *la fotografía*) — the photo
» *la moto* (from *la motocicleta*) — the motorbike
» *la radio* — the radio

## Masculine and Feminine Nouns 2

### Feminine endings

Most nouns ending in **-a** are feminine: *la casa, la silla, la ventana*. These endings are feminine almost without exception:

| Ending | Examples |
|---|---|
| *-ción, -sión* | *la canción, la estación, la televisión* |
| *-dad, -tad* | *la ciudad, la verdad, la libertad* |
| *-tud* | *la juventud, la actitud* |
| *-umbre* | *la costumbre, la cumbre* |
| *-ie* | *la serie, la especie* |
| *-sis, -itis* | *la crisis, la tesis, la bronquitis* |

Letters of the alphabet are feminine as well: *la a, la eme*.

### Feminine exceptions

Apart from the Greek *-ma* words, watch out for *el día* (the day) and *el mapa* (the map), and for *el análisis* and *el énfasis*, which break the *-sis* rule.

### Endings that tell you nothing

Nouns ending in *-e* or in most consonants can go either way, so you simply have to learn them:

» *el coche, la noche* — the car, the night
» *el árbol, la sal* — the tree, the salt
» *el lápiz, la luz* — the pencil, the light

### Same word, different meaning

Some nouns change meaning with gender: *el capital* (money) / *la capital* (capital city), *el cura* (priest) / *la cura* (cure), *el orden* (order, tidiness) / *la orden* (command), *el corte* (cut) / *la corte* (court).

> [!hack] Use the article as a label
> Write new vocabulary on cards with *el* or *la* in front, and colour-code them. Your memory links the colour to the word.

## Professions

Job titles generally change their ending to show whether a man or a woman is meant.

| Pattern | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| *-o → -a* | *el abogado* | *la abogada* (lawyer) |
| consonant *+ -a* | *el profesor* | *la profesora* (teacher) |
| consonant *+ -a* | *el doctor* | *la doctora* |
| *-e* (only the article changes) | *el estudiante* | *la estudiante* |
| *-ista* (only the article changes) | *el periodista* | *la periodista* (journalist) |
| special form | *el actor* | *la actriz* |

» *Mi madre es ingeniera.* — My mother is an engineer.
» *La dentista es muy simpática.* — The dentist (a woman) is very nice.
» *Él es taxista.* — He's a taxi driver.

Notice that after *ser*, a bare job title takes **no article**: *Soy médica*, not ~~soy una médica~~ → *soy médica*. The article comes back as soon as you add an adjective: *Es una médica excelente.*

> [!trap] Changing usage
> Forms like *la presidenta, la jueza* and *la médica* are now standard, but you will still hear *la presidente, la juez* or *la médico* in some regions. Both are understood; the *-a* forms are increasingly common.

Some words for people have one fixed gender no matter who is meant: *la persona, la víctima, el personaje*. *Pedro es una persona amable* is correct.

## Comprehensive Review

Gender is decided by the noun and then copied by its article (and, as you will see in the next unit, by its adjectives).

| Usually masculine | Usually feminine |
|---|---|
| *-o, -or, -aje*, Greek *-ma* | *-a, -ción, -sión, -dad, -tad, -tud, -umbre* |
| days, months, languages, numbers | letters of the alphabet |
| exceptions: *la mano, la foto, la moto* | exceptions: *el día, el mapa, el problema* |

Choose the article, then check yourself:

» *___ canción* → *la canción* (feminine *-ción*)
» *___ tema* → *el tema* (Greek *-ma*)
» *___ ciudad* → *la ciudad* (*-dad*)
» *___ agua* → *el agua* (stressed *a-*, still feminine)
» *___ garaje* → *el garaje* (*-aje*)
» *___ mano* → *la mano* (exception)

For people, swap the ending or only the article: *el cantante / la cantante*, *el camarero / la camarera*, *el escritor / la escritora*. A group of men and women takes masculine plural forms: *los profesores, ellos, nosotros*.

> [!hack] Three-step check
> 1) Is it a person? Use their sex. 2) Do you know the ending? Apply the rule. 3) Ending is *-e* or a consonant? Look it up and learn it with its article.

## Extra Practice 1

Focus: pronouns and family words. Replace the names with the right pronoun and decide the article.

» *Carmen y Rosa* → *ellas*
» *Carmen y Luis* → *ellos* (mixed group)
» *tú (a woman) y yo* → *nosotras*
» *mi tío y mi tía* → *mis tíos*
» *el hijo y la hija* → *los hijos* (the children)

Now make each word feminine:

» *el abuelo* → *la abuela*
» *el padrino* → *la madrina*
» *el yerno* → *la nuera*
» *el sobrino* → *la sobrina* (niece)

> [!trap] Los padres
> *Los padres* almost always means "the parents", not "the fathers". Likewise *los reyes* is "the king and queen".

## Extra Practice 3

Focus: professions and mixed review. Give the feminine form:

» *el enfermero* → *la enfermera* (nurse)
» *el pintor* → *la pintora* (painter)
» *el artista* → *la artista*
» *el actor* → *la actriz*
» *el gerente* → *la gerente* (manager)

Translate into Spanish:

» My sister is a pilot. → *Mi hermana es piloto* (or *pilota*).
» The (female) students are here. → *Las estudiantes están aquí.*

» The capital of Peru is Lima. → *La capital de Perú es Lima.*

> [!fact] Meaning can hang on the article
> *Necesito el capital* means "I need the money"; *necesito la capital* would mean "I need the capital city". Gender is not just decoration.

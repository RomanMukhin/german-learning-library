## First to Tenth

**Ordinal numbers** show position in a series: first, second, third. The first ten are the ones you'll use most.

| Number | Ordinal | Abbreviation |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | *primero* | *1.º / 1.ª* |
| 2nd | *segundo* | *2.º* |
| 3rd | *tercero* | *3.º* |
| 4th | *cuarto* | *4.º* |
| 5th | *quinto* | *5.º* |
| 6th | *sexto* | *6.º* |
| 7th | *séptimo* | *7.º* |
| 8th | *octavo* | *8.º* |
| 9th | *noveno* | *9.º* |
| 10th | *décimo* | *10.º* |

Ordinals are adjectives, so they agree with the noun: *el segundo piso, la segunda calle, los primeros días, las primeras semanas*.

### Shortening

*Primero* and *tercero* drop the final *-o* before a **masculine singular** noun:

» *el primer día* — the first day
» *el tercer capítulo* — the third chapter
» but: *la primera vez*, *la tercera planta*, *los primeros años*

» *Vivo en el cuarto piso.* — I live on the fourth floor.
» *Es mi primer viaje a Asia.* — It's my first trip to Asia.
» *Llegó en quinto lugar.* — She came fifth.

### Abbreviations

Write the number, a full stop and a raised *o* or *a*: *1.º, 2.ª, 3.er* (for the short form *tercer*), *1.er* (for *primer*).

> [!trap] Dates use cardinal numbers
> Spanish says *el dos de mayo*, not ~~el segundo de mayo~~. Only the first day of the month may use the ordinal: *el primero de julio* (common in Latin America) or *el uno de julio*.

## Eleventh to Nineteenth

From 11th to 19th, Spanish combines *décimo* with the first nine ordinals. Two forms are accepted.

| Number | Traditional | Modern single word |
|---|---|---|
| 11th | *undécimo* | *decimoprimero* |
| 12th | *duodécimo* | *decimosegundo* |
| 13th | *decimotercero* | *decimotercero* |
| 14th | *decimocuarto* | *decimocuarto* |
| 15th | *decimoquinto* | *decimoquinto* |
| 16th | *decimosexto* | *decimosexto* |
| 17th | *decimoséptimo* | *decimoséptimo* |
| 18th | *decimoctavo* | *decimoctavo* |
| 19th | *decimonoveno* (or *decimonono*) | *decimonoveno* |

### Spelling variations

You may also see them as two words, *décimo primero, décimo tercero*. In that case **both parts** agree: *la décima tercera edición*. In the one-word form, only the end changes: *la decimotercera edición*.

*Decimoctavo* loses one *o*, because two in a row would be awkward.

» *el siglo decimonoveno* — the nineteenth century
» *la decimoquinta planta* — the fifteenth floor
» *Es su undécimo título.* — It's his eleventh title.

### Shortening again

*Decimoprimero* and *decimotercero* shorten before masculine singular nouns, like *primero* and *tercero*: *el decimotercer piso*.

> [!hack] In speech, use cardinals
> Above ten, people often switch to ordinary numbers after the noun: *el piso quince, el capítulo doce*. It's perfectly natural.

## Numbers Divisible by Ten

The tens have their own ordinals:

| Number | Ordinal |
|---|---|
| 10th | *décimo* |
| 20th | *vigésimo* |
| 30th | *trigésimo* |
| 40th | *cuadragésimo* |
| 50th | *quincuagésimo* |
| 60th | *sexagésimo* |
| 70th | *septuagésimo* |
| 80th | *octogésimo* |
| 90th | *nonagésimo* |
| 100th | *centésimo* |
| 1000th | *milésimo* |

For numbers in between, combine the ten with a unit. These are usually written as two words that both agree, though one-word forms like *vigesimoprimero* are also accepted:

» *vigésimo primero* — 21st
» *trigésima quinta* — 35th (feminine)
» *el cuadragésimo aniversario* — the fortieth anniversary

### Placement

Ordinals usually go **before** the noun: *la tercera calle a la derecha*. With kings, queens, popes and centuries, they go **after** the name and are written in Roman numerals: *Felipe II* (*segundo*), *Isabel I* (*primera*). From 11 on, cardinals are used: *Luis XIV* (*catorce*), *el siglo XXI* (*veintiuno*).

> [!fact] Partitive look-alikes
> Some ordinals double as fractions: *un cuarto* (a quarter), *un quinto* (a fifth), *un décimo* (a tenth; also a share of a lottery ticket). *Centésimo* also means hundredth part.

## Comprehensive Review 1

| Rule | Example |
|---|---|
| agree in gender and number | *la segunda vez, los segundos platos* |
| *primero, tercero* shorten before masc. sing. | *el primer premio, el tercer hijo* |
| usually before the noun | *el quinto piso* |
| cardinals for dates (except "first") | *el tres de junio* |

Write the ordinal:

» *the 1st prize* → *el primer premio*
» *the 3rd time* → *la tercera vez*
» *the 7th floor* → *el séptimo piso*
» *the 9th symphony* → *la novena sinfonía*

## Comprehensive Review 2

Write the ordinal in words:

» *12th* → *duodécimo* / *decimosegundo*
» *16th (feminine)* → *decimosexta*
» *18th* → *decimoctavo*
» *20th* → *vigésimo*
» *50th* → *quincuagésimo*
» *100th* → *centésimo*

Read aloud: *Carlos V* → *Carlos quinto*; *Alfonso XIII* → *Alfonso trece*; *el siglo XV* → *el siglo quince*.

## Comprehensive Review 3

Translate:

» It's the first day of the course. → *Es el primer día del curso.*
» Take the second street on the left. → *Toma la segunda calle a la izquierda.*
» We celebrated our 25th anniversary. → *Celebramos nuestro vigésimo quinto aniversario* (or *nuestras bodas de plata*).
» She finished in third place. → *Terminó en tercer lugar.*
» Today is the fourth of July. → *Hoy es el cuatro de julio.*

> [!trap] Primero as an adverb
> *Primero* also means "first(ly)" and then never shortens: *Primero comemos, después salimos.*

## Extra Practice 3

Focus: higher ordinals and placement.

» *the 11th edition* → *la undécima / decimoprimera edición*
» *the 13th floor* → *el decimotercer piso* (or *el piso trece*)

> [!hack] When in doubt
> Outside formal writing, ordinals above *décimo* are rare. *La planta veinte* is what most people say.

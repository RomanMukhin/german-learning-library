At intermediate level you start telling stories and making plans. Most of the work goes into the **two past tenses**, which you need for almost any story about the past, and into the **pronouns** that let you avoid repeating nouns. Around them come the future and conditional, comparisons, commands and a set of verb pairs that English speakers often confuse.

| Area | Units |
|---|---|
| Gender and agreement | nouns and pronouns, adjectives |
| Tenses | future, conditional, imperfect, preterite, preterite vs. imperfect, *hacer* with time |
| Describing and comparing | comparatives and superlatives, possessives, demonstratives, ordinal numbers |
| Small words | prepositions, *por* vs. *para*, prepositional pronouns |
| Pronouns and verb patterns | commands, reflexive, reciprocal, object pronouns, verbs like *gustar* |
| Choosing the right verb | *ser* vs. *estar*, *tener* expressions, *saber* vs. *conocer*, tricky verbs |

> [!hack] Learn tenses as a set
> The future and the conditional share the same twelve irregular stems, and the preterite and imperfect only make sense side by side. Study each pair together.

> Each unit ends with review and practice sections. Cover the answer after each arrow and try to produce it yourself before you look.

## Pronunciation and regional accents

Colombia has several distinct accents:

» **Bogotá (rolo / cachaco)**: often called one of the clearest Spanish accents, with every consonant pronounced and a calm, steady rhythm
» **Paisa** (Medellín, Antioquia): sing-song intonation and a soft *s*, sometimes close to *sh*
» **Costeño** (Caribbean coast: Cartagena, Barranquilla): Caribbean features, with dropped final *s* and *d*: *pescado* → *pescao*

All Colombian accents use *seseo* (*z* and *c* before *e, i* = /s/).

## Grammar: usted, tú and vos

Colombians use ***usted*** much more than other Latin Americans, even with friends, family and partners, especially in Bogotá and the Andes. It shows respect, but also affection:

» *Mamá, ¿**usted** quiere un tinto?* — Mum, do you want a coffee?
» *¿Cómo **está**, mi amor?* — How are you, darling? (to a partner)

***Tú*** is common on the coast and among young people in cities. In Medellín and the Paisa region, ***vos*** is used informally, with forms like Argentina's: *¿Vos qué **querés**?*

Colombians also use many **diminutives** and polite phrases: *un momentico* (just a moment), *¿Me regala…?* (literally "Will you give me…?", meaning "Can I have…?", used when buying something).

## Vocabulary and expressions

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *parce / parcero* | friend, mate |
| *chévere* | great, cool |
| *bacano* | great, cool |
| *tinto* | a small black coffee (not red wine!) |
| *¿Qué más?* | How are you? / What's up? |
| *¡Qué pena!* | Sorry! / How embarrassing! |
| *rumbear* | to party; *la rumba* — the party |
| *plata* | money |
| *guayabo* | hangover |
| *berraco* | brave, impressive (or angry) |
| *pola* | beer (informal) |
| *paila* | bad luck, too bad |
| *dar papaya* | to make yourself an easy target |

» *¿Qué más, **parce**? ¿Todo bien?* — What's up, mate? All good?
» *¿Me regala un **tinto**, por favor?* — Can I have a black coffee, please?
» *¡**Qué pena** con usted!* — I'm so sorry! (apology)

> [!trap] Tinto
> In Spain, *un tinto* is a glass of red wine. In Colombia it is a small cup of black coffee. Order carefully.

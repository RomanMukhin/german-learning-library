## Pronunciation

Panamanian Spanish belongs to the Caribbean family of accents:

» **final s** is weakened to an *h* or dropped: *¿Cómo estás?* → *¿Cómo ehtá?*
» **d between vowels** disappears: *cansado* → *cansao*
» the **ch** sound is often softened to *sh*: *muchacho* → *mushasho*
» syllables are often shortened: *para* → *pa'*, *está* → *'tá*

## Grammar features

» ***tú*** is the normal informal *you*; *usted* is used for respect. *Vosotros* is not used.
» like in other Caribbean varieties, **subject pronouns** are common and can come before the verb in questions: *¿Qué tú dices?* — What are you saying?
» a typical feature is ***¿Qué xopá?*** — "What's up?", which is *¿Qué pasó?* with its syllables reversed. Playing with syllable order like this (*al revés*) is common in Panamanian slang.
» ***ir a* + infinitive** for the future and the preterite for recent past, as in most of Latin America

## Vocabulary and slang

Panama's close contact with the USA, especially during the building and running of the canal, brought many English words into everyday speech.

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *¿Qué xopá?* | What's up? |
| *chantin* | home (from English *shanty*) |
| *chombo* | person of Afro-Caribbean origin (can be offensive) |
| *buay* | boy, guy (from English *boy*) |
| *bien cuero* | very cool |
| *pelao / pelaíto* | kid, young person |
| *chuleta* | Wow! / Damn! (mild exclamation) |
| *vaina* | thing, stuff |
| *rantan* | a lot (*un rantan de gente* — loads of people) |
| *chiva* | small bus |
| *guial* | girl (from English *girl*) |

» *¿**Qué xopá**, **buay**?* — What's up, man?
» *Había un **rantan** de gente en la fiesta.* — There were loads of people at the party.

## Pronunciation: distinción and other sounds

The Spanish of central and northern Spain (*castellano*) has some sounds that Latin American Spanish doesn't:

» **distinción**: *c* before *e, i* and *z* are pronounced /θ/, like English *th* in *think*: *gracias* /ˈgɾaθjas/, *zapato* /θaˈpato/. *S* stays /s/, so *casa* (house) and *caza* (hunt) sound different.
» **j** and **g** before *e, i* are a strong, rasping /x/, made at the back of the throat: *jamón*, *gente*
» the **s** is often pronounced with the tip of the tongue, sounding a little like *sh* to English ears
» in the south (Andalusia) and the Canary Islands, speakers use *seseo* (/s/ for *c, z*) and often drop final *s*: *los amigos* → *lo' amigo'*

> [!fact] The lisping king myth
> A popular story says Spaniards pronounce *z* like *th* because a king had a lisp. It isn't true: the sound developed naturally from older Spanish sibilants in the 16th and 17th centuries.

## Grammar: vosotros and the present perfect

### Vosotros

Spain uses ***vosotros / vosotras*** as the informal plural *you*, with its own verb forms, and *ustedes* only for formal situations:

| | Present | Preterite | Command |
|---|---|---|---|
| *hablar* | *habláis* | *hablasteis* | *¡hablad!* |
| *comer* | *coméis* | *comisteis* | *¡comed!* |
| *vivir* | *vivís* | *vivisteis* | *¡vivid!* |

» *¿Vosotros venís a la fiesta?* — Are you (all) coming to the party?
» Possessive: *vuestro / vuestra*: *¿Es vuestra casa?* — Is it your house?

### Present perfect for today

Spain uses the **present perfect** for events earlier today, this week or this year, where Latin America often uses the preterite:

» *Hoy **he comido** paella.* — I ate paella today. (Mexico: *Hoy comí paella.*)
» *Este año **hemos viajado** mucho.*

### Leísmo

In Madrid and much of Spain, *le* is often used instead of *lo* for a male person as direct object: *A Juan **le** vi ayer* (standard: ***lo** vi*). The Royal Spanish Academy accepts this for men only.

## Vocabulary and expressions

| Spain | Latin America | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *coche* | *carro, auto* | car |
| *ordenador* | *computadora* | computer |
| *móvil* | *celular* | mobile phone |
| *zumo* | *jugo* | juice |
| *patata* | *papa* | potato |
| *conducir* | *manejar* | to drive |
| *coger* (neutral) | *tomar, agarrar* | to take, catch |
| *vale* | *está bien, OK* | OK |
| *guay* | *chévere, padre, bacán* | cool |
| *tío / tía* | *güey, che, parce* | mate, dude |
| *vosotros* | *ustedes* | you (plural) |

» *¿Quedamos a las ocho? — **Vale**.* — Shall we meet at eight? — OK.
» *¡Qué **guay**!* — How cool!

> [!trap] Coger
> In Spain *coger* is an everyday verb (*coger el autobús* — to catch the bus). In much of Latin America it is a vulgar word for sex. Use *tomar* or *agarrar* there.

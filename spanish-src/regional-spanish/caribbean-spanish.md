## Pronunciation: aspiration and dropped consonants

Caribbean Spanish is fast and rhythmic, and many consonants at the end of a syllable are weakened:

» **final s** becomes an *h* sound or disappears: *¿cómo estás?* → *¿cómo ehtá?*, *los niños* → *lo' niño'*
» **d between vowels** is often dropped, especially in *-ado*: *cansado* → *cansao*, *pescado* → *pescao*
» whole syllables shrink in fast speech: *para* → *pa'*, *nada* → *na'*
» **n** at the end of a word is pronounced at the back of the mouth, like *-ng* in *sing*: *pan* /paŋ/

## Grammar: subject pronouns and questions

Because the final *s* of verbs often disappears (*tú habla'* vs *él habla*), Caribbean speakers use **subject pronouns** more than other Spanish speakers, to keep the meaning clear:

» ***Yo** creo que **tú** tienes razón.* — I think you're right.

In **questions**, the subject often comes before the verb, which is unusual elsewhere:

» *¿Qué **tú** quieres?* — What do you want? (standard: *¿Qué quieres tú?*)
» *¿Dónde **tú** vives?* — Where do you live?

Infinitives can also take a subject: *para **yo** entender* (standard: *para que yo entienda*) — so that I understand.

## Cuban Spanish

» the final **r** and **l** often merge or become a double consonant: *verdad* → *veddad*, *puerta* → *puetta*
» ***asere*** — friend, buddy; ***¿Qué bolá?*** — What's up?
» ***guagua*** — bus (also Canary Islands, Puerto Rico, Dominican Republic)
» ***yuma*** — a foreigner, especially from the USA
» ***jamar*** — to eat (slang); ***la pincha*** — work

## Puerto Rican Spanish

» **r** at the end of a syllable often sounds like **l**: *puerta* → *puelta*, *Puerto Rico* → *Puelto Rico*
» the trilled **rr** is often pronounced at the back of the throat, like a French *r*
» many English loanwords because of close ties with the USA: *parquear* (to park), *janguear* (to hang out), *chequear*
» ***boricua*** — Puerto Rican; ***¡Wepa!*** — Hooray!; ***chavos*** — money; ***corillo*** — group of friends

## Dominican Spanish

» very fast speech, with final consonants often dropped
» in the Cibao region, final **r** and **l** can become **i**: *por favor* → *poi favoi*
» ***¿Qué lo que?*** — What's up?; ***vaina*** — thing, stuff (very common: *¿Qué es esa vaina?*)
» ***chin*** — a little bit; ***jevi*** — cool (from English *heavy*); ***tíguere*** — a street-smart guy
» ***motoconcho*** — motorbike taxi

## Vocabulary shared across the Caribbean

| Word | Meaning | Elsewhere |
|---|---|---|
| *guagua* | bus | *autobús, camión* |
| *chévere* | great, cool | *padre, guay* |
| *china* (Puerto Rico) | orange (fruit) | *naranja* |
| *habichuelas* | beans | *frijoles* |
| *zafacón* | rubbish bin (PR, DR) | *basurero, papelera* |
| *ahorita* | soon, later | *ahora mismo* (Spain: right now) |
| *¿Qué tal?* → *¿Qué hay?* | how's it going | |

» *Coge la **guagua** en la esquina.* — Catch the bus at the corner.
» *¡Qué **chévere**!* — How cool!

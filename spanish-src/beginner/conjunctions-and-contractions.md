## Y and E

Conjunctions join words, phrases or sentences. The most basic one is *y* — **and**.

» *pan y mantequilla* — bread and butter
» *Estudio y trabajo.* — I study and work.
» *Tengo un perro y dos gatos.* — I have a dog and two cats.

### Y becomes e

Before a word that starts with the sound *i* — written *i-* or *hi-* — *y* changes to *e*, so that the two *i* sounds don't merge:

» *Pedro e Isabel* — Pedro and Isabel
» *padres e hijos* — parents and children
» *español e inglés* — Spanish and English
» *Es rápido e inteligente.* — He's fast and intelligent.

### When y stays

*y* stays before *hie-* and *hia-*, because they sound like *ye* / *ya*, not *i*: *agua y hielo* — water and ice. It also stays at the beginning of a question: *¿Y Inés?* — And Inés?

> [!hack] Listen, don't look
> The rule depends on sound, not spelling. *Hi-* sounds like *i* (silent *h*), so it triggers *e*; *hie-* sounds like *ye*, so it doesn't.

## O and U

*o* means **or**.

» *¿Té o café?* — Tea or coffee?
» *Podemos ir hoy o mañana.* — We can go today or tomorrow.

### O becomes u

Before a word starting with the sound *o* — written *o-* or *ho-* — *o* changes to *u*:

» *siete u ocho* — seven or eight
» *mujeres u hombres* — women or men
» *¿Es hoy u otro día?* — Is it today or another day?
» *ayer u hoy* — yesterday or today

### Accent between numbers

When *o* stands between numerals written as digits, it used to carry an accent (*3 ó 4*) to avoid confusion with zero. The current rule says no accent is needed: *3 o 4*.

> [!trap] U is still "or"
> The word *u* is simply *o* in disguise, used only before an *o* sound: *diez u once* — ten or eleven. Before any other sound, keep *o*: *once o doce*.

## Ni...Ni

*ni... ni...* means **neither... nor**. It makes both items negative.

» *No tengo ni hermanos ni hermanas.* — I have neither brothers nor sisters.
» *Ni Juan ni Ana vienen hoy.* — Neither Juan nor Ana is coming today.
» *No como ni carne ni pescado.* — I don't eat meat or fish.

### Position and the extra no

If *ni... ni...* comes **after** the verb, you also need *no* before the verb. If it comes **before** the verb, *no* is dropped:

» *No quiero ni té ni café.* — I want neither tea nor coffee.
» *Ni el té ni el café me gustan.* — I like neither tea nor coffee.

### Ni on its own

A single *ni* means **not even** or **nor**:

» *No tengo ni un euro.* — I don't have even one euro.
» *Ni idea.* — No idea.

> [!fact] Verb agreement
> When *ni... ni...* precedes the verb, the verb usually goes plural: *Ni mi madre ni mi padre saben nadar.* — Neither my mother nor my father can swim.

## O...O

*o... o...* means **either... or**. It presents two alternatives and insists on choosing one.

» *O vienes conmigo o te quedas aquí.* — Either you come with me or you stay here.
» *Puedes pagar o con tarjeta o en efectivo.* — You can pay either by card or in cash.
» *O lo haces bien o no lo hagas.* — Either do it right or don't do it.

The second *o* still turns into *u* before an *o-* sound: *o uno u otro* — either one or the other.

» *Elige: o el rojo o el azul.* — Choose: either the red one or the blue one.

> [!hack] Ya sea... o
> In more formal writing, you may also see *ya sea... o...* (whether... or...): *Ya sea en tren o en avión, llegaremos mañana.* — Whether by train or by plane, we'll arrive tomorrow.

## Pero and Sino

Both *pero* and *sino* translate as **but**, but they aren't interchangeable.

### Pero: but, however

*pero* adds a contrast or limitation to what was said. The first part can be positive or negative.

» *Es caro, pero es muy bueno.* — It's expensive, but it's very good.
» *No tengo tiempo, pero te ayudo.* — I don't have time, but I'll help you.

### Sino: but rather, but instead

*sino* corrects a **negative** statement: "not X, but Y". Y replaces X.

» *No es azul, sino verde.* — It's not blue but green.
» *No vivo en Madrid, sino en Toledo.* — I don't live in Madrid but in Toledo.

### Sino que before a verb

When the correction is a whole clause with its own conjugated verb, use *sino que*:

» *No lo compré, sino que me lo regalaron.* — I didn't buy it; rather, they gave it to me.

### No solo... sino también

» *No solo habla inglés, sino también francés.* — She speaks not only English but also French.

> [!trap] The replacement test
> If you can say "instead" or "rather" in English, use *sino*: ~~*No es mi hermano, pero mi primo.*~~ → *No es mi hermano, sino mi primo.* — He's not my brother but my cousin.

### Mas

*mas* (no accent) is a literary synonym of *pero*. Don't confuse it with *más* (more).

## Al

Spanish has only two contractions, and both are compulsory. The first: *a* (to, at) + *el* (the) = **al**.

» ~~*Vamos a el cine.*~~ → *Vamos al cine.* — Let's go to the cinema.
» *Llamé al médico.* — I called the doctor.
» *Doy de comer al perro.* — I feed the dog.

### Only with the article el

The contraction doesn't happen with *la*, *los*, *las*, or with the pronoun *él*:

» *Voy a la playa.* — I'm going to the beach.
» *Escribo a los abuelos.* — I write to my grandparents.
» *Se lo di a él.* — I gave it to him.

### Not with names that include El

When *El* is part of a proper name, the contraction is not written: *Viajamos a El Salvador.* — We're travelling to El Salvador.

### Al + infinitive: "on / when doing"

» *Al llegar, llámame.* — When you arrive, call me.
» *Al salir del cine, vimos a Juan.* — On leaving the cinema, we saw Juan.

## Del

The second contraction: *de* (of, from) + *el* (the) = **del**.

» ~~*la casa de el profesor*~~ → *la casa del profesor* — the teacher's house
» *Vengo del supermercado.* — I'm coming from the supermarket.
» *el final del partido* — the end of the match

### Possession

Spanish has no *'s*; it uses *de*, which becomes *del* before *el*:

» *el coche del vecino* — the neighbour's car
» *la hija de la directora* — the headteacher's daughter (no contraction)

### Same exceptions as al

» *el libro de él* — his book (pronoun: no contraction)
» *Es de El Cairo.* — He's from Cairo (part of a name).

> [!hack] Only two contractions
> Spanish has no contractions like English *don't* or *I'm*. *al* and *del* are the only two, and they're never optional.

## Comprehensive Review 1

Choose the right conjunction.

» *Francia ___ Italia* → *e* (before *I-*)
» *¿Uno ___ otro?* → *u* (before *o-*)
» *No quiero agua, ___ zumo.* → *sino* (correction)
» *Quiero ir, ___ no puedo.* → *pero*
» *No tengo ___ coche ___ moto.* → *ni... ni*
» *___ estudias ___ trabajas.* → *O... o*
» *madre ___ hija* → *e* (before *hi-*)

## Comprehensive Review 2

Contractions and mixed practice.

» *Voy (a + el) ___ banco.* → *al*
» *Es la novia (de + el) ___ hermano de Ana.* → *del*
» *Vengo (de + la) ___ escuela.* → *de la*
» *Pregunta (a + él) ___.* → *a él*
» *(a + el) ___ entrar, saluda.* → *Al entrar* — When you go in, say hello.

### Spot the mistake

» ~~*agua e hielo*~~ → *agua y hielo* — water and ice
» ~~*No es lunes, pero martes.*~~ → *No es lunes, sino martes.* — It's not Monday but Tuesday.
» ~~*Ni Pedro ni Luis no vienen.*~~ → *Ni Pedro ni Luis vienen.* — Neither Pedro nor Luis is coming.
» ~~*Salgo de el trabajo a las seis.*~~ → *Salgo del trabajo a las seis.* — I leave work at six.

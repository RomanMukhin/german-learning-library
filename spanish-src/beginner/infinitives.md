## -Ar Verbs

The **infinitive** (*el infinitivo*) is the basic, unconjugated form of a verb — the one you find in the dictionary. It tells you the action but not who does it or when: *hablar* — to speak.

Every Spanish infinitive ends in *-ar*, *-er* or *-ir*. Remove that ending and you get the **stem**, to which you attach conjugation endings.

*-ar* verbs are the largest family and the most regular. Almost all new verbs that enter the language are *-ar* verbs (*chatear*, *tuitear*).

| Infinitive | Stem | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *hablar* | *habl-* | to speak |
| *trabajar* | *trabaj-* | to work |
| *estudiar* | *estudi-* | to study |
| *cantar* | *cant-* | to sing |
| *llegar* | *lleg-* | to arrive |
| *mirar* | *mir-* | to look at |
| *tomar* | *tom-* | to take, to drink |
| *escuchar* | *escuch-* | to listen to |

» *Me gusta cantar.* — I like singing.
» *Necesito trabajar más.* — I need to work more.

> [!hack] Learn verbs in the infinitive
> Always learn a new verb as its infinitive. The ending immediately tells you which set of endings to use for every tense.

## -Er Verbs

*-er* verbs are the second-largest group. Many of the most common irregular verbs are in this family.

| Infinitive | Stem | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *comer* | *com-* | to eat |
| *beber* | *beb-* | to drink |
| *aprender* | *aprend-* | to learn |
| *leer* | *le-* | to read |
| *correr* | *corr-* | to run |
| *vender* | *vend-* | to sell |
| *comprender* | *comprend-* | to understand |
| *creer* | *cre-* | to believe |

» *Quiero aprender a cocinar.* — I want to learn to cook.
» *Es importante beber agua.* — It's important to drink water.

> [!fact] -er and -ir are close relatives
> In most tenses *-er* and *-ir* verbs use the same endings. Only a few forms (like *comemos* vs *vivimos*) tell them apart.

## -Ir Verbs

*-ir* verbs are the smallest group, but they include many everyday verbs.

| Infinitive | Stem | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *vivir* | *viv-* | to live |
| *escribir* | *escrib-* | to write |
| *abrir* | *abr-* | to open |
| *subir* | *sub-* | to go up |
| *recibir* | *recib-* | to receive |
| *compartir* | *compart-* | to share |
| *decidir* | *decid-* | to decide |
| *salir* | *sal-* | to go out, leave |

» *Prefiero vivir en el campo.* — I prefer living in the countryside.
» *Hay que abrir la ventana.* — We need to open the window.

### Reflexive infinitives

Reflexive verbs carry the pronoun *se* on the end of the infinitive: *levantarse* (to get up), *ducharse* (to shower), *divertirse* (to have fun). When you use them, the pronoun changes to match the subject: *Quiero ducharme.* — I want to shower.

## Irregular Verbs

An irregular verb is one whose stem or endings change unpredictably in some tenses. The **infinitive itself is never irregular** — it always ends in *-ar*, *-er* or *-ir* — but it doesn't tell you whether the verb will behave.

Here are the irregular infinitives you'll meet most often:

| Infinitive | Meaning | Present *yo* |
|---|---|---|
| *ser* | to be | *soy* |
| *estar* | to be | *estoy* |
| *ir* | to go | *voy* |
| *tener* | to have | *tengo* |
| *hacer* | to do, make | *hago* |
| *poder* | to be able to | *puedo* |
| *querer* | to want | *quiero* |
| *decir* | to say | *digo* |
| *venir* | to come | *vengo* |
| *saber* | to know | *sé* |
| *poner* | to put | *pongo* |
| *dar* | to give | *doy* |

*ir* is unusual: its stem is just *i-*, and in the present it doesn't use it at all (*voy, vas, va*). Its infinitive still works normally: *Quiero ir.* — I want to go.

> [!hack] Infinitives need no conjugating
> Whenever a sentence needs an infinitive (after *querer*, *poder*, a preposition...), you can use any verb, regular or irregular, without worrying about its forms.

## Infinitives as Nouns

An infinitive can act like a noun — the subject or object of a sentence. English often uses the *-ing* form here; Spanish uses the infinitive.

» *Fumar es malo para la salud.* — Smoking is bad for your health.
» *Me encanta bailar.* — I love dancing.
» *Leer antes de dormir me relaja.* — Reading before bed relaxes me.

### With el

You can put the masculine article *el* in front, especially at the start of a sentence or in formal style:

» *El comer bien es importante.* — Eating well is important.

### After prepositions

After a preposition, Spanish **always** uses the infinitive, never the *-ando/-iendo* form:

» *antes de salir* — before leaving
» *después de comer* — after eating
» *sin hablar* — without speaking
» *Gracias por venir.* — Thanks for coming.

> [!trap] Never -ando after a preposition
> ~~*Después de comiendo, salimos.*~~ → *Después de comer, salimos.* — After eating, we went out.

## Infinitive Commands

The infinitive can be used as an **impersonal command** on signs, labels, instructions and recipes, where the instruction is aimed at everyone.

» *No fumar.* — No smoking.
» *Empujar.* — Push.
» *No tocar.* — Do not touch.
» *Pelar las patatas y cortarlas en dados.* — Peel the potatoes and dice them.
» *Agitar antes de usar.* — Shake before use.

### A + infinitive

In speech, *a* + infinitive gives a quick, energetic order to a group:

» *¡A comer!* — Time to eat! / Come and eat!
» *¡A dormir!* — Off to bed!
» *¡A trabajar!* — Get to work!

> [!fact] Not for personal commands
> In standard Spanish, the infinitive isn't used to tell a specific person what to do. To a friend you'd say *¡Ven!* (come), not ~~*¡Venir!*~~. In Spain, however, you'll often hear *¡Sentaros!* instead of *¡Sentaos!* (sit down, you all) in casual speech.

## Verbs Used with Infinitives

Many verbs can be followed directly by an infinitive. Only the first verb is conjugated.

### Verb + infinitive (no preposition)

| Verb | Example |
|---|---|
| *querer* — want | *Quiero viajar.* — I want to travel. |
| *poder* — can | *¿Puedes ayudarme?* — Can you help me? |
| *deber* — should, must | *Debes descansar.* — You should rest. |
| *necesitar* — need | *Necesito dormir.* — I need to sleep. |
| *preferir* — prefer | *Prefiero quedarme.* — I'd rather stay. |
| *saber* — know how to | *¿Sabes nadar?* — Can you swim? |
| *pensar* — plan, intend | *Pienso salir.* — I'm planning to go out. |
| *esperar* — hope | *Espero verte pronto.* — I hope to see you soon. |

### Verb + preposition + infinitive

| Verb | Example |
|---|---|
| *ir a* — be going to | *Voy a estudiar.* — I'm going to study. |
| *empezar a* — start | *Empieza a llover.* — It's starting to rain. |
| *aprender a* — learn to | *Aprendo a conducir.* — I'm learning to drive. |
| *tener que* — have to | *Tengo que irme.* — I have to go. |
| *acabar de* — have just | *Acabo de llegar.* — I've just arrived. |
| *dejar de* — stop | *Dejé de fumar.* — I stopped smoking. |
| *tratar de* — try to | *Trato de entender.* — I try to understand. |

> [!hack] Learn the preposition with the verb
> *empezar a*, *dejar de*, *tener que* — the little word is part of the expression. See [Verbal Periphrasis](beginner/verbal-periphrasis) for more.

## Comprehensive Review 1

Identify the family and the stem.

» *bailar* → *-ar*, stem *bail-*
» *romper* → *-er*, stem *romp-*
» *permitir* → *-ir*, stem *permit-*
» *ver* → *-er*, stem *v-*
» *ir* → *-ir*, stem *i-* (irregular)

### Infinitive or conjugated verb?

» *Me gusta (nadar / nado).* → *nadar* — I like swimming.
» *Antes de (salgo / salir), apago la luz.* → *salir* — Before going out, I turn off the light.
» *Ella (puede / poder) venir.* → *puede* — She can come.

Complete with the right preposition (or none).

### Spot the mistake

» ~~*Quiero a viajar.*~~ → *Quiero viajar.* — I want to travel.
» ~~*Gracias por ayudando.*~~ → *Gracias por ayudar.* — Thanks for helping.
» ~~*Tengo de trabajar.*~~ → *Tengo que trabajar.* — I have to work.

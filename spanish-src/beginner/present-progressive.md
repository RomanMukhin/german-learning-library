## Formation

The present progressive (*estoy hablando* — I am speaking) describes an action **in progress at this moment**. It is built from the present of *estar* plus a **present participle** (*el gerundio*).

### Step 1: estar

| Subject | *estar* |
|---|---|
| *yo* | *estoy* |
| *tú* | *estás* |
| *él, ella, usted* | *está* |
| *nosotros/as* | *estamos* |
| *vosotros/as* | *estáis* |
| *ellos, ellas, ustedes* | *están* |

### Step 2: the present participle

| Verb type | Ending | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| *-ar* | *-ando* | *hablar → hablando*, *trabajar → trabajando* |
| *-er* | *-iendo* | *comer → comiendo*, *hacer → haciendo* |
| *-ir* | *-iendo* | *vivir → viviendo*, *escribir → escribiendo* |

The participle never changes for gender or number: only *estar* is conjugated.

» *Estoy cocinando la cena.* — I'm cooking dinner.
» *¿Qué estás haciendo?* — What are you doing?
» *Los niños están jugando en el jardín.* — The children are playing in the garden.

### Present progressive vs simple present

Spanish uses the progressive less than English. It stresses that something is happening **right now** or is temporary. For habits, plans and general facts, use the simple present.

» *Estudio derecho.* — I study law. (my degree)
» *Estoy estudiando para el examen.* — I'm studying for the exam. (now / these days)
» ~~*Estoy yendo a Lima mañana.*~~ → *Voy a Lima mañana.* — I'm going to Lima tomorrow.

> [!trap] No progressive for the future
> English "I'm leaving tomorrow" uses a progressive form for plans. Spanish uses the simple present or *ir a*: *Salgo mañana.* / *Voy a salir mañana.*

## Irregular Present Participles

A few participles change their spelling or their stem.

### -yendo instead of -iendo

When the stem of an *-er* or *-ir* verb ends in a vowel, the *i* of *-iendo* turns into *y*:

» *leer → leyendo* — reading
» *oír → oyendo* — hearing
» *traer → trayendo* — bringing
» *construir → construyendo* — building
» *ir → yendo* — going

### Stem changes (e → i, o → u)

*-ir* verbs that change their stem in the present also change in the participle, but more modestly:

| Infinitive | Participle |
|---|---|
| *pedir* | *pidiendo* |
| *decir* | *diciendo* |
| *seguir* | *siguiendo* |
| *sentir* | *sintiendo* |
| *venir* | *viniendo* |
| *dormir* | *durmiendo* |
| *morir* | *muriendo* |

Other common one-offs: *poder → pudiendo*.

» *¿Me estás oyendo?* — Can you hear me? / Are you listening?
» *El bebé está durmiendo.* — The baby is sleeping.
» *Están construyendo un puente nuevo.* — They're building a new bridge.

> [!hack] -ar participles are never irregular
> Every *-ar* verb, even an irregular one like *estar* or *dar*, forms its participle regularly: *estando*, *dando*, *jugando*, *pensando*.

## Pronoun Placement

Object and reflexive pronouns (*me, te, lo, la, le, se, nos, los, las, les*) have **two possible positions** in the present progressive. Both are correct and mean the same.

### Before estar

» *Lo estoy leyendo.* — I'm reading it.
» *Me estoy duchando.* — I'm having a shower.
» *Le estamos escribiendo.* — We're writing to him.

### Attached to the participle

» *Estoy leyéndolo.* — I'm reading it.
» *Estoy duchándome.* — I'm having a shower.
» *Estamos escribiéndole.* — We're writing to him.

When you attach a pronoun, the word gains a syllable, so you must **add a written accent** to keep the stress in the same place: *leyendo → leyéndolo*, *duchando → duchándome*.

### Two pronouns

With an indirect and a direct pronoun together, the order is indirect + direct, and they stay together in either position:

» *Te lo estoy explicando.* = *Estoy explicándotelo.* — I'm explaining it to you.

> [!trap] Never split the pair
> ~~*Te estoy explicándolo.*~~ — both pronouns go before *estar* or both attach to the participle.

## Comprehensive Review 1

Focus: building the present progressive.

### Practice

» *Yo (trabajar) ___.* → *estoy trabajando* — I'm working.
» *Ana (beber) ___ agua.* → *está bebiendo* — Ana is drinking water.
» *Nosotros (escribir) ___ un correo.* → *estamos escribiendo* — We're writing an email.
» *¿Tú (aprender) ___ japonés?* → *estás aprendiendo* — Are you learning Japanese?
» *Ellos (bailar) ___ salsa.* → *están bailando* — They're dancing salsa.

» *dormir* → *durmiendo* · *Papá está durmiendo.* — Dad is sleeping.

## Comprehensive Review 3

Focus: pronoun placement. Rewrite each sentence with the pronoun in the other position.

» *Lo estoy pensando.* → *Estoy pensándolo.* — I'm thinking about it.
» *Nos estamos preparando.* → *Estamos preparándonos.* — We're getting ready.
» *Estás mirándola.* → *La estás mirando.* — You're looking at her.
» *Se lo están contando.* → *Están contándoselo.* — They're telling it to him.

## Comprehensive Review 4

Focus: progressive or simple present?

» *Ahora mismo (llover) ___.* → *está lloviendo* — It's raining right now.
» *Normalmente (yo, comer) ___ a las dos.* → *como* — I usually eat at two.
» *Mi hermano (vivir) ___ con nosotros este mes.* → *está viviendo* — My brother is living with us this month. (temporary)
» *El tren (salir) ___ a las cinco mañana.* → *sale* — The train leaves at five tomorrow.

> [!hack] "Right now" test
> If you can add *ahora mismo* (right now) or *estos días* (these days) to the sentence naturally, the progressive fits.

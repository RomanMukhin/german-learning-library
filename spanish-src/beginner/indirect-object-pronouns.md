## Me, Te

The **indirect object** is the person *to whom* or *for whom* something is done. In *I give Ana a book*, the book is the direct object and Ana is the indirect object. Indirect object pronouns replace or announce that person.

| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| 1st person | *me* — (to/for) me | *nos* — (to/for) us |
| 2nd person | *te* — (to/for) you (informal) | *os* — (to/for) you all (Spain) |
| 3rd person | *le* — (to/for) him, her, you (formal) | *les* — (to/for) them, you all |

*Me* and *te* look exactly like the direct object forms; the verb tells you which role they play.

» *Mi abuela me cuenta historias.* — My grandmother tells me stories.
» *¿Te doy mi número?* — Shall I give you my number?
» *Te compré un regalo.* — I bought you a present / a present for you.

### Finding the indirect object

Ask "to whom?" or "for whom?". In *Me escribe una carta* (she writes me a letter), *una carta* is what is written; *me* is who receives it.

### Position

Same rules as direct pronouns: before a conjugated verb, or attached to an infinitive, participle or affirmative command.

» *Voy a decirte algo.* = *Te voy a decir algo.* — I'm going to tell you something.
» *Dime la verdad.* — Tell me the truth.

> [!hack] Gustar uses indirect pronouns
> *Me gusta el café* literally means "coffee is pleasing to me". The person is the indirect object: *me gusta*, *te gusta*, *le gusta*, *nos gusta*, *les gusta*.

## Le

*Le* is the indirect pronoun for **him**, **her** and formal **you** (*usted*). Unlike *lo / la*, it does not change for gender.

» *Le escribo a mi hermano.* — I'm writing to my brother.
» *Le doy las llaves a la vecina.* — I give the keys to the neighbour.
» *¿Le traigo la carta, señor?* — Shall I bring you the menu, sir?

### Doubling: le + a + noun

Spanish usually keeps the indirect pronoun even when the person is named. This "doubling" is normal and often required:

» *Le compré flores a mi madre.* — I bought my mother flowers.
» *Le pregunté a Juan.* — I asked Juan.

Because *le* can mean him, her or you, you can add *a él*, *a ella* or *a usted* to make it clear or to add emphasis:

» *Le hablo a ella, no a él.* — I'm talking to her, not to him.

### Common verbs with an indirect object

*dar* (give), *decir* (tell), *enviar / mandar* (send), *escribir* (write), *explicar* (explain), *mostrar / enseñar* (show), *pedir* (ask for), *preguntar* (ask), *prestar* (lend), *regalar* (give as a gift), *traer* (bring).

> [!trap] Gender doesn't matter for le
> ~~*La di el libro a María.*~~ → *Le di el libro a María.* — I gave María the book. (*la* is direct; María is the indirect object.)

## Nos, Os

*Nos* means **to/for us**; *os* means **to/for you all** (informal, Spain only).

» *El guía nos explica la historia del castillo.* — The guide explains the history of the castle to us.
» *Nuestros vecinos nos prestan el coche.* — Our neighbours lend us their car.
» *Os mando las fotos esta noche.* — I'll send you guys the photos tonight. (Spain)
» *¿Os gusta la comida mexicana?* — Do you guys like Mexican food? (Spain)

In Latin America, *les* replaces *os*: *Les mando las fotos esta noche.*

### Doubling with a nosotros

To emphasise, add *a nosotros / a nosotras*:

» *A nosotros nos encanta bailar.* — We love dancing.

> [!hack] Os = vosotros
> If you are using *vosotros* as the subject, the matching object pronoun is always *os*: *Vosotros os levantáis temprano y yo os veo.*

## Les

*Les* is the indirect pronoun for **to/for them** and **to/for you all** (*ustedes*), for any gender.

» *Les escribo una postal a mis amigos.* — I'm writing my friends a postcard.
» *¿Les sirvo más vino, señores?* — Shall I pour you more wine, gentlemen?
» *A los niños les gustan los dibujos animados.* — Children like cartoons.

### Le and les become se

When an indirect pronoun *le* or *les* comes right before a direct pronoun *lo, la, los, las*, it changes to **se**:

» *Le doy el libro.* → ~~*Le lo doy.*~~ → *Se lo doy.* — I give it to him.
» *Les mando las cartas.* → *Se las mando.* — I send them to them.

The order is always indirect + direct: *me lo*, *te la*, *nos los*, *se las*.

> [!fact] Les in Latin America
> Latin American Spanish uses *les* for every plural "you" as an indirect object, both with friends and strangers: *Chicos, les traje pizza.* — Guys, I brought you pizza.

## Comprehensive Review 1

Choose the correct indirect pronoun.

» *(A mí) ___ duele la cabeza.* → *me* — My head hurts.
» *(A ti) ¿___ gusta el té?* → *te* — Do you like tea?
» *(A Juan) ___ regalo un libro.* → *le* — I'm giving Juan a book.
» *(A nosotros) ___ explican el problema.* → *nos* — They explain the problem to us.
» *(A mis padres) ___ escribo cada semana.* → *les* — I write to my parents every week.
» *(A usted) ¿___ traigo un café?* → *le* — Shall I bring you a coffee?

## Comprehensive Review 2

Mix direct and indirect pronouns.

» *Doy la llave a Pedro.* → *Le doy la llave.* → *Se la doy.* — I give it to him.
» *¿Me prestas tu bici?* → *¿Me la prestas?* — Will you lend it to me?
» *Te enseño las fotos.* → *Te las enseño.* — I'll show them to you.
» *Vamos a enviar el paquete a los clientes.* → *Vamos a enviárselo.* / *Se lo vamos a enviar.* — We're going to send it to them.

### Spot the mistake

» ~~*Lo di un beso.*~~ → *Le di un beso.* — I gave him a kiss.
» ~~*Les lo explico.*~~ → *Se lo explico.* — I'll explain it to them.
» ~~*A María gusta el cine.*~~ → *A María le gusta el cine.* — María likes the cinema.

> [!hack] Direct or indirect? Try English "to"
> If you can naturally say "to" or "for" before the person (*I give a book **to** her*), the Spanish pronoun is indirect: *le*, *les*.

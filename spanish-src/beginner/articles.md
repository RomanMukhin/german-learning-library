## Singular Definite Articles

An article is the little word in front of a noun that says whether you mean a particular thing (*the*) or any one of a kind (*a*, *an*). English has a single word *the*; Spanish has several, because every Spanish noun is either **masculine** or **feminine**, and the article has to match.

In the singular there are two forms:

| Article | Used with | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *el* | masculine singular nouns | *el tren* — the train |
| *la* | feminine singular nouns | *la mesa* — the table |

Use a definite article when both you and the listener know exactly which thing you mean, or when there is only one of it.

» *¿Dónde está el mando de la tele?* — Where's the TV remote?
» *La puerta está abierta.* — The door is open.
» *El sol sale a las siete.* — The sun rises at seven.

Spanish also uses the definite article in places where English leaves it out, most importantly with nouns used in a **general sense** and with titles when you talk *about* someone:

» *Me encanta el chocolate.* — I love chocolate (chocolate in general).
» *La señora Ruiz vive aquí.* — Mrs Ruiz lives here.
» *El lunes tengo examen.* — On Monday I have an exam.

> [!trap] El is not él
> *el* without an accent is the article *the*; *él* with an accent is the pronoun *he*. *El chico come.* — The boy eats. *Él come.* — He eats.

### Feminine nouns that start with a stressed a

A feminine singular noun whose first syllable is a stressed *a-* or *ha-* takes *el* instead of *la*, purely to avoid the clash of two *a* sounds. The noun is still feminine, so adjectives stay feminine and the plural goes back to *las*.

» *el agua fría* — the cold water
» *el águila* → *las águilas* — the eagle → the eagles

## Plural Definite Articles

For more than one item, the definite article becomes plural and still agrees in gender:

| Singular | Plural | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *el* | *los* | *los trenes* — the trains |
| *la* | *las* | *las mesas* — the tables |

» *Los niños juegan en el parque.* — The children are playing in the park.
» *Las llaves están en mi bolso.* — The keys are in my bag.

### Mixed groups

When a plural noun refers to a group that includes both masculine and feminine members, Spanish uses the masculine form. *Los hermanos* can mean "the brothers" or "the brothers and sisters"; *los padres* usually means "the parents". Only use *las* when every member of the group is feminine.

» *Los profesores de mi escuela son simpáticos.* — The teachers at my school are nice (men and women).
» *Las profesoras de baile llegan tarde.* — The (female) dance teachers are arriving late.

### General statements

Plural nouns used to talk about a whole category keep the article in Spanish, where English drops it:

» *Los gatos duermen mucho.* — Cats sleep a lot.
» *Me gustan las películas de miedo.* — I like horror films.

> [!hack] Check the noun ending, then the article
> Most nouns ending in *-o* take *el / los*; most ending in *-a* take *la / las*. Learn every new noun together with its article (*la mano*, *el día*) so the exceptions stick from day one.

## Singular Indefinite Articles

The indefinite article corresponds to English *a* or *an*. It introduces something new or something that is one of many, where it does not matter which one.

| Article | Used with | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *un* | masculine singular nouns | *un libro* — a book |
| *una* | feminine singular nouns | *una silla* — a chair |

» *Necesito un bolígrafo.* — I need a pen (any pen).
» *Hay una farmacia en la esquina.* — There's a pharmacy on the corner.
» *Compré un regalo para mi madre.* — I bought a present for my mother.

Compare the two types: *¿Me das el bolígrafo?* asks for a specific pen you can both see; *¿Me das un bolígrafo?* asks for any pen at all.

Like *el*, the article *un* is used before feminine nouns starting with a stressed *a-*: *un hacha* — an axe, *un aula* — a classroom. The longer form *una* is also accepted here, but *un* is more common.

### Where Spanish drops "a"

After *ser* (and verbs like *hacerse*), an unmodified job, religion or nationality takes **no** article:

» *Mi tía es médica.* — My aunt is a doctor.
» ~~*Soy un estudiante.*~~ → *Soy estudiante.* — I'm a student.

Add the article only if you describe the noun: *Es una médica excelente.* — She's an excellent doctor.

Spanish also usually drops the article after *sin*, after *tener* / *buscar* with things people normally have one of, and before *otro*, *cien* and *mil*: *otro café* (~~*un otro café*~~) — another coffee; *cien personas* — a hundred people.

## Plural Indefinite Articles

The plural forms are *unos* (masculine) and *unas* (feminine). They mean **some**, **a few** or **about**, and are often left out when English would just use the bare plural.

| Singular | Plural | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *un* | *unos* | *unos amigos* — some friends |
| *una* | *unas* | *unas fotos* — some photos |

» *Tengo unos libros para ti.* — I have some books for you.
» *Vinieron unas vecinas a saludar.* — A few neighbours came to say hello.

### "About" with numbers

Before a number, *unos* / *unas* means "approximately":

» *El viaje dura unas tres horas.* — The trip takes about three hours.
» *Había unos cincuenta invitados.* — There were about fifty guests.

### When to leave it out

With plural nouns after verbs like *tener*, *comprar*, *hay* or *necesitar*, Spanish usually uses no article at all:

» *¿Hay manzanas?* — Are there any apples?
» *Necesitamos sillas.* — We need chairs.

Adding *unos/unas* makes the idea of "a certain number of them" stronger: *Compré unas manzanas muy buenas.* — I bought some really good apples.

> [!trap] Unos is not "ones"
> *unos* never means "ones" in the sense of *the red ones*. For that you use the article plus an adjective: *los rojos* — the red ones.

## The Neuter Article "Lo"

Besides the gendered articles there is a third, **neuter** article: *lo*. It never goes in front of a noun. Instead it turns an adjective, adverb or a whole clause into an abstract idea.

### Lo + adjective: "the ... thing" / "the ... part"

» *Lo bueno es que no llueve.* — The good thing is that it isn't raining.
» *Lo difícil del español son los verbos.* — The hard part of Spanish is the verbs.
» *Lo mejor de la fiesta fue la comida.* — The best thing about the party was the food.

The adjective stays in its masculine singular form, because it does not describe any particular noun.

### Lo que: "what", "the thing that"

» *Lo que quiero es dormir.* — What I want is to sleep.
» *No entiendo lo que dices.* — I don't understand what you're saying.

### Lo + adjective/adverb + que: "how"

Here the adjective **does** agree with the noun it describes:

» *No sabes lo cansada que estoy.* — You don't know how tired I am.
» *Mira lo rápido que corre.* — Look how fast he runs.

> [!trap] Two different words called lo
> The article *lo* (before adjectives, *que*) is not the same as the pronoun *lo* ("it, him") you will meet in [direct object pronouns](beginner/direct-object-pronouns). *Lo importante* — the important thing; *Lo veo.* — I see it.

## Comprehensive Review 1

Choosing an article is a three-step decision: **definite or indefinite?** → **masculine or feminine?** → **singular or plural?**

| | Masc. sing. | Fem. sing. | Masc. pl. | Fem. pl. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Definite (*the*) | *el* | *la* | *los* | *las* |
| Indefinite (*a, some*) | *un* | *una* | *unos* | *unas* |

Plus the neuter *lo*, which goes before adjectives and *que*, never before nouns.

### Practice: fill in the definite article

» *___ casa* → *la casa* — the house
» *___ problemas* → *los problemas* — the problems (a masculine noun in *-a*)
» *___ agua* → *el agua* — the water (stressed *a-*)
» *___ ciudades* → *las ciudades* — the cities
» *___ mapa* → *el mapa* — the map

### Practice: fill in the indefinite article

» *Quiero ___ café.* → *un café* — I'd like a coffee.
» *Leo ___ revistas.* → *unas revistas* — I'm reading some magazines.
» *Es ___ idea genial.* → *una idea* — It's a great idea.

## Comprehensive Review 2

This review mixes all article types, including the cases where Spanish and English disagree.

### Spanish uses an article, English does not

» *Odio el ruido.* — I hate noise. (general sense)
» *El doctor García llega a las nueve.* — Dr García arrives at nine. (title, talking about him)
» *Los domingos comemos en casa.* — On Sundays we eat at home. (days of the week)

### English uses "a", Spanish does not

» *Es abogado.* — He's a lawyer.
» *Salió sin paraguas.* — She went out without an umbrella.
» *Quiero otra cerveza.* — I want another beer.

### Spot the mistake

» ~~*la problema*~~ → *el problema* — the problem
» ~~*Soy una profesora.*~~ → *Soy profesora.* — I'm a teacher.
» ~~*la agua*~~ → *el agua* — the water
» ~~*Lo importante cosa*~~ → *Lo importante* — the important thing

> [!hack] Say the article with the noun
> When you learn vocabulary, never write down *casa* — write *la casa*. The article is part of the word as far as your memory is concerned.

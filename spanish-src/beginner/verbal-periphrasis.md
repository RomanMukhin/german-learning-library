## Informal Future

A **verbal periphrasis** is a construction of two verbs that works as one: a conjugated verb + an unconjugated form (infinitive, *-ando/-iendo* or participle). Only the first verb changes; the second carries the meaning.

The most useful is ***ir a* + infinitive** — "going to do". It's the everyday way to talk about plans and the near future.

| Subject | *ir* | + *a* + infinitive |
|---|---|---|
| *yo* | *voy* | *a comer* |
| *tú* | *vas* | *a comer* |
| *él, ella, Ud.* | *va* | *a comer* |
| *nosotros/as* | *vamos* | *a comer* |
| *vosotros/as* | *vais* | *a comer* |
| *ellos, ellas, Uds.* | *van* | *a comer* |

» *Voy a llamar a mi madre.* — I'm going to call my mother.
» *¿Qué vas a hacer el sábado?* — What are you going to do on Saturday?
» *Va a llover.* — It's going to rain.
» *Vamos a ver.* — Let's see.

### Vamos a: "let's"

*Vamos a* + infinitive can also mean "let's": *¡Vamos a bailar!* — Let's dance!

> [!trap] Don't forget the a
> ~~*Voy comer.*~~ → *Voy a comer.* — The *a* is obligatory.

## Present Progressive

***estar* + present participle** (*-ando / -iendo*) describes an action happening right now. It's covered in detail in [Present Progressive](beginner/present-progressive).

» *Estoy leyendo un libro buenísimo.* — I'm reading a great book.
» *¿Con quién estás hablando?* — Who are you talking to?

### Other verbs + -ando / -iendo

The *-ando / -iendo* form combines with other verbs too, each adding its own nuance:

| Construction | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *seguir* + -ando | to keep on, still be | *Sigue lloviendo.* — It's still raining. |
| *llevar* + time + -ando | to have been doing | *Llevo dos horas esperando.* — I've been waiting for two hours. |
| *ir* + -ando | to be gradually doing | *Voy entendiendo.* — I'm slowly getting it. |
| *andar* + -ando | to go around doing | *Anda diciendo tonterías.* — He goes around saying nonsense. |

> [!hack] Llevar for "have been doing"
> *Llevo tres años estudiando español.* is a very natural way to say "I've been studying Spanish for three years."

## Present Perfect

***haber* + past participle** forms the present perfect: "I have done". It's a periphrasis because *haber* has lost its own meaning here and just marks the tense.

| Subject | *haber* | + participle |
|---|---|---|
| *yo* | *he* | *hablado* |
| *tú* | *has* | *comido* |
| *él, ella, Ud.* | *ha* | *vivido* |
| *nosotros/as* | *hemos* | *hablado* |
| *vosotros/as* | *habéis* | *comido* |
| *ellos, ellas, Uds.* | *han* | *vivido* |

Regular participles end in *-ado* (*-ar* verbs) or *-ido* (*-er/-ir* verbs). Common irregular ones: *hecho* (*hacer*), *dicho* (*decir*), *visto* (*ver*), *escrito* (*escribir*), *puesto* (*poner*), *vuelto* (*volver*), *abierto* (*abrir*), *roto* (*romper*), *muerto* (*morir*).

» *He terminado los deberes.* — I've finished my homework.
» *¿Has visto mis gafas?* — Have you seen my glasses?
» *Nunca hemos estado en Perú.* — We've never been to Peru.

> [!trap] Never split haber
> Pronouns and *no* go before *haber*, never between it and the participle: ~~*He lo visto.*~~ → *Lo he visto.* — I've seen it.

## Verb + Infinitive 1

Many periphrases combine a verb with a preposition and an infinitive. These express **obligation** and **ability**.

| Construction | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *tener que* + inf. | to have to | *Tengo que trabajar mañana.* — I have to work tomorrow. |
| *hay que* + inf. | one has to (impersonal) | *Hay que reciclar.* — You have to recycle. |
| *deber* + inf. | should, must | *Debes llamar a tu abuela.* — You should call your grandmother. |
| *deber de* + inf. | must (probability) | *Debe de ser tarde.* — It must be late. |
| *poder* + inf. | can, be able to | *No puedo dormir.* — I can't sleep. |
| *saber* + inf. | to know how to | *Sé tocar el piano.* — I can play the piano. |

### Tener que vs hay que

*tener que* has a specific subject; *hay que* is impersonal and never changes:

» *Tenemos que salir ya.* — We have to leave now.
» *Hay que salir ya.* — We need to leave now. (generally, everyone)

> [!hack] Poder vs saber
> *poder* is about permission or circumstance; *saber* is about a learned skill: *Sé nadar, pero hoy no puedo porque estoy enfermo.* — I can swim, but I can't today because I'm ill.

## Verb + Infinitive 2

Another group describes the **phases** of an action: starting, stopping, repeating or having just finished.

| Construction | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *empezar a / comenzar a* + inf. | to start | *Empecé a estudiar a las ocho.* — I started studying at eight. |
| *ponerse a* + inf. | to suddenly start | *Se puso a llorar.* — She burst into tears. |
| *acabar de* + inf. | to have just | *Acabo de comer.* — I've just eaten. |
| *dejar de* + inf. | to stop | *Dejé de fumar hace un año.* — I stopped smoking a year ago. |
| *volver a* + inf. | to do again | *Vuelve a llamar más tarde.* — Call again later. |
| *terminar de* + inf. | to finish | *¿Has terminado de leer?* — Have you finished reading? |
| *estar a punto de* + inf. | to be about to | *Estamos a punto de salir.* — We're about to leave. |

» *El bebé acaba de dormirse.* — The baby has just fallen asleep.
» *No vuelvas a hacerlo.* — Don't do it again.

> [!trap] Acabar de ≠ to finish
> *Acabo de llegar* means "I've just arrived", not "I've finished arriving". For "finish doing", use *terminar de*.

## Comprehensive Review 1

Complete the periphrasis.

» *Mañana (yo) ___ visitar a mis abuelos.* (going to) → *voy a*
» *¿___ terminado el proyecto?* (have you) → *Has*
» *Ellos ___ jugando al fútbol.* (are) → *están*
» *___ estudiar más.* (I have to) → *Tengo que*
» *___ llegar a casa.* (we've just) → *Acabamos de*
» *___ frío.* (it's still cold) → *Sigue haciendo* — *Sigue haciendo frío.*

## Comprehensive Review 2

Choose the best construction for each meaning.

» "I stopped eating meat." → *Dejé de comer carne.*
» "It started to snow." → *Empezó a nevar.*
» "You must wear a helmet." (general rule) → *Hay que llevar casco.*
» "I've been living here for five years." → *Llevo cinco años viviendo aquí.*
» "The film is about to start." → *La película está a punto de empezar.*
» "Don't say that again!" → *¡No vuelvas a decir eso!*

### Spot the mistake

» ~~*Voy cocinar.*~~ → *Voy a cocinar.* — I'm going to cook.
» ~~*Tengo de salir.*~~ → *Tengo que salir.* — I have to go out.
» ~~*He no comido.*~~ → *No he comido.* — I haven't eaten.
» ~~*Acabo de terminar a leer.*~~ → *Acabo de terminar de leer.* — I've just finished reading.

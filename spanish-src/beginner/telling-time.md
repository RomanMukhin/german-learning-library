## Ser

To say what time it is, Spanish uses ***ser*** + the feminine article *la / las* + a number. The article is feminine because it stands for *la hora* (the hour).

### One o'clock: singular

Only one o'clock is singular:

» *¿Qué hora es? —Es la una.* — What time is it? — It's one o'clock.

### Two to twelve: plural

Every other hour uses the plural *son las*:

» *Son las dos.* — It's two o'clock.
» *Son las siete.* — It's seven o'clock.
» *Son las doce.* — It's twelve o'clock.

### Adding minutes

Past the hour, add *y* + minutes. Before the hour, say the **next** hour + *menos* + minutes:

» *Es la una y diez.* — It's ten past one.
» *Son las cuatro y veinte.* — It's twenty past four.
» *Son las nueve menos cinco.* — It's five to nine.
» *Son las seis menos veinte.* — It's twenty to six.

### At what time?

To say when something happens, use *a la / a las*:

» *¿A qué hora empieza? —A las ocho.* — What time does it start? — At eight.
» *Comemos a la una y media.* — We eat at half past one.

> [!trap] Es vs son
> ~~*Son la una.*~~ → *Es la una.* · ~~*Es las tres.*~~ → *Son las tres.* — The verb agrees with the hour.

## Media and Cuarto

### Half past: y media

» *Son las tres y media.* — It's half past three.
» *Es la una y media.* — It's half past one.

### Quarter past and quarter to: cuarto

» *Son las cinco y cuarto.* — It's quarter past five.
» *Son las cinco menos cuarto.* — It's quarter to five.

### Digital style

Especially in Latin America and with timetables, people also read the minutes directly:

» *Son las tres y treinta.* — It's three thirty.
» *Son las ocho y cuarenta y cinco.* — It's eight forty-five.

### Other ways to say "to"

In much of Latin America you'll also hear *faltar* or *para*:

» *Faltan diez para las seis.* — It's ten to six.
» *Son diez para las seis.* — It's ten to six.

> [!hack] Menos after half past
> In Spain, the switch to *menos* comes after *y media*: 7:35 → *las ocho menos veinticinco*. With the digital style you can always say *las siete y treinta y cinco*.

## Useful Time Expressions

### Parts of the day

» *de la mañana* — in the morning (a.m.): *las ocho de la mañana*
» *del mediodía* — around noon: *la una del mediodía*
» *de la tarde* — in the afternoon/early evening: *las cinco de la tarde*
» *de la noche* — at night: *las once de la noche*
» *de la madrugada* — in the small hours: *las tres de la madrugada*

When you're not giving a clock time, use *por*: *por la mañana* — in the morning; *por la noche* — at night.

### Exact and approximate

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| *en punto* | on the dot, sharp |
| *más o menos* | more or less |
| *sobre las / hacia las* | at about |
| *el mediodía* | noon |
| *la medianoche* | midnight |
| *temprano / tarde* | early / late |

» *La clase empieza a las nueve en punto.* — Class starts at nine sharp.
» *Llego sobre las seis.* — I'll get there at about six.
» *Es mediodía.* — It's noon.

### The 24-hour clock

Timetables, TV schedules and official announcements use the 24-hour clock: *El tren sale a las diecisiete cuarenta.* — The train leaves at 17:40.

> [!fact] Tarde means two things
> *la tarde* is the afternoon (roughly from lunch until dark); *tarde* as an adverb means "late": *Llegué tarde por la tarde.* — I arrived late in the afternoon.

## Yes and No Questions

Spanish questions are written with an upside-down question mark at the start (*¿*) and a normal one at the end (*?*). Exclamations work the same way: *¡Qué bien!*

To turn a statement into a yes/no question, you have three options. All are common.

### 1. Just change your intonation

Keep the word order and let your voice rise at the end:

» *Tienes hambre.* — You're hungry.
» *¿Tienes hambre?* — Are you hungry?

### 2. Put the subject after the verb

» *¿Habla usted inglés?* — Do you speak English?
» *¿Viene tu hermana a la fiesta?* — Is your sister coming to the party?

### 3. Add a tag at the end

*¿verdad?*, *¿no?* and *¿vale?* (Spain) work like English *right?*, *isn't it?*, *OK?*:

» *Eres de Chile, ¿verdad?* — You're from Chile, aren't you?
» *Nos vemos mañana, ¿no?* — We'll see each other tomorrow, right?

There is no helper verb like English *do*: you simply ask *¿Hablas inglés?* — Do you speak English?

### Answering

» *—¿Te gusta? —Sí, me gusta mucho.* — Do you like it? — Yes, I like it a lot.
» *—¿Está abierto? —No, no está abierto.* — Is it open? — No, it isn't. (the first *no* answers, the second negates)

> [!trap] The opening mark goes where the question starts
> If only part of a sentence is a question, *¿* goes there: *Si llueve mañana, ¿qué hacemos?* — If it rains tomorrow, what shall we do?

## Qué

*Qué* means **what** (and sometimes **which**). Every question word carries a written accent when it asks a question, including in indirect questions (*No sé qué hacer*).

### Asking for a definition or explanation

With *ser*, *qué* asks what something **is** — its definition:

» *¿Qué es un aguacate?* — What is an avocado?
» *¿Qué es esto?* — What's this?

### Qué + noun

Directly before a noun, *qué* means "what" or "which" and is the usual choice in Latin America:

» *¿Qué color prefieres?* — What colour do you prefer?
» *¿Qué hora es?* — What time is it?
» *¿Qué día llegas?* — What day are you arriving?

### With other verbs

» *¿Qué quieres?* — What do you want?
» *¿Qué haces este fin de semana?* — What are you doing this weekend?

### Prepositions go in front

» *¿De qué hablan?* — What are they talking about?
» *¿En qué trabajas?* — What do you do for a living?
» *¿Para qué sirve esto?* — What is this for?

> [!hack] ¡Qué...! in exclamations
> *Qué* also starts exclamations: *¡Qué bonito!* — How pretty! *¡Qué frío!* — It's so cold!

## Cuál

*Cuál* (plural *cuáles*) means **which** or **what**, and implies a choice from a set of possibilities.

» *¿Cuál quieres, el rojo o el azul?* — Which one do you want, the red or the blue?
» *¿Cuáles son tus libros?* — Which ones are your books?

### Cuál with ser for specific information

English "What is your...?" asking for a piece of information (a name, number, address) becomes *¿Cuál es...?*, not *¿Qué es...?*:

» *¿Cuál es tu dirección?* — What's your address?
» *¿Cuál es la capital de Perú?* — What's the capital of Peru?
» *¿Cuál es tu número de teléfono?* — What's your phone number?

Compare: *¿Qué es la capital?* would ask for a definition of the word "capital".

### Cuál de

» *¿Cuál de los dos prefieres?* — Which of the two do you prefer?

> [!trap] Cuál before a noun
> In standard Spanish, use *qué* directly before a noun: ~~*¿Cuál libro quieres?*~~ → *¿Qué libro quieres?* (*Cuál libro* is heard in parts of Latin America but sounds informal.)

## Dónde

*Dónde* means **where**. It combines with prepositions to ask about movement and origin.

| Question word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *dónde* | where (location) | *¿Dónde está el baño?* — Where's the bathroom? |
| *adónde* / *a dónde* | where to | *¿Adónde vas?* — Where are you going? |
| *de dónde* | where from | *¿De dónde eres?* — Where are you from? |
| *por dónde* | which way, whereabouts | *¿Por dónde se va al centro?* — Which way is the centre? |
| *hasta dónde* | how far | *¿Hasta dónde llega el tren?* — How far does the train go? |

» *¿Dónde vives?* — Where do you live?
» *¿De dónde viene este ruido?* — Where's this noise coming from?

> [!hack] Moving or staying?
> With verbs of motion (*ir*, *venir*, *llegar*), think "where to?" → *adónde*. With *estar* and *vivir*, think "in what place?" → *dónde*.

## Cuándo

*Cuándo* means **when**.

» *¿Cuándo es tu cumpleaños?* — When is your birthday?
» *¿Cuándo empiezan las clases?* — When do classes start?
» *¿Cuándo vuelves?* — When are you coming back?

### Asking for a clock time

For a specific time of day, use *¿A qué hora...?*:

» *¿A qué hora sale el autobús?* — What time does the bus leave?

### With prepositions

» *¿Desde cuándo estudias español?* — How long have you been studying Spanish? (since when?)
» *¿Hasta cuándo te quedas?* — Until when are you staying?
» *¿Para cuándo necesitas el informe?* — When do you need the report by?

> [!trap] Cuándo vs cuando
> With an accent, *cuándo* asks; without one, *cuando* is the conjunction "when": *Te llamo cuando llegue.* — I'll call you when I arrive.

## Quién

*Quién* (singular) and *quiénes* (plural) mean **who**.

» *¿Quién es esa chica?* — Who's that girl?
» *¿Quiénes son tus amigos?* — Who are your friends?
» *¿Quién quiere postre?* — Who wants dessert?

### With prepositions

The preposition comes first. This is how Spanish says *whom* and *whose*:

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| *¿A quién?* | (to) whom? |
| *¿De quién?* | whose? / about whom? |
| *¿Con quién?* | with whom? |
| *¿Para quién?* | for whom? |

» *¿A quién llamas?* — Who are you calling?
» *¿De quién es este móvil?* — Whose phone is this?
» *¿Con quién vives?* — Who do you live with?

> [!trap] Whose
> There's no single word for "whose" in questions: ~~*¿Cuyo es?*~~ → *¿De quién es?*

## Cuánto

*Cuánto* means **how much** or **how many** and agrees with the noun it refers to.

| | Singular (how much) | Plural (how many) |
|---|---|---|
| Masculine | *cuánto* | *cuántos* |
| Feminine | *cuánta* | *cuántas* |

» *¿Cuánto dinero tienes?* — How much money do you have?
» *¿Cuánta leche quieres?* — How much milk do you want?
» *¿Cuántos años tienes?* — How old are you? (how many years do you have?)
» *¿Cuántas personas vienen?* — How many people are coming?

### Cuánto on its own

Before a verb, the masculine singular *cuánto* is used:

» *¿Cuánto cuesta?* — How much does it cost?
» *¿Cuánto es?* — How much is it? (when paying)
» *¿Cuánto tardas en llegar?* — How long does it take you to get there?

> [!hack] Agreement = gender + number
> *¿Cuántas horas?* (horas is feminine plural) · *¿Cuántos días?* (días is masculine plural).

## Cómo

*Cómo* means **how**. It asks about manner, condition and also about what something is like.

» *¿Cómo estás?* — How are you?
» *¿Cómo vas al trabajo?* — How do you get to work?
» *¿Cómo se dice "spoon" en español?* — How do you say "spoon" in Spanish?
» *¿Cómo te llamas?* — What's your name? (literally "how do you call yourself?")

### Cómo es vs cómo está

» *¿Cómo es tu jefe?* — What's your boss like? (personality, description: *ser*)
» *¿Cómo está tu jefe?* — How is your boss? (health, mood: *estar*)

### ¿Cómo? to ask for repetition

On its own, *¿Cómo?* means "Sorry? Pardon?" — more polite than *¿Qué?*

> [!trap] How + adjective
> English "how tall / how far / how old" can't be translated word for word: ~~*¿Cómo alto es?*~~ → *¿Cuánto mide?* — How tall is he? · *¿Qué tan lejos está?* (Latin America) / *¿A qué distancia está?* — How far is it?

## Por Qué

*Por qué* (two words, accent on *qué*) means **why**. The answer usually uses *porque* (one word, no accent), meaning **because**.

» *—¿Por qué estudias español? —Porque quiero viajar.* — Why are you studying Spanish? — Because I want to travel.
» *¿Por qué no vienes con nosotros?* — Why don't you come with us?

### Four similar spellings

| Form | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *por qué* | why (question) | *¿Por qué lloras?* — Why are you crying? |
| *porque* | because | *Lloro porque estoy triste.* |
| *el porqué* | the reason (noun) | *No entiendo el porqué.* — I don't understand the reason. |
| *por que* | for which (rare) | *el motivo por que...* |

### Para qué: what for?

*¿Para qué?* asks about the purpose rather than the cause:

» *¿Para qué necesitas el coche?* — What do you need the car for?

> [!hack] Why not?
> *¿Por qué no...?* is a friendly way to suggest something: *¿Por qué no vamos al cine?* — Why don't we go to the cinema?

## More Question Words

A few more question words and phrases round out the set.

| Spanish | English | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *¿Qué tal?* | how's it going / how was it | *¿Qué tal el viaje?* — How was the trip? |
| *¿A qué hora?* | at what time | *¿A qué hora cierran?* — What time do you close? |
| *¿Cada cuánto?* | how often | *¿Cada cuánto vas al gimnasio?* — How often do you go to the gym? |
| *¿Cuánto tiempo?* | how long | *¿Cuánto tiempo llevas aquí?* — How long have you been here? |
| *¿De qué color?* | what colour | *¿De qué color es tu coche?* — What colour is your car? |
| *¿Qué tan...?* | how (+ adjective, Latin America) | *¿Qué tan grande es?* — How big is it? |

### Indirect questions keep the accent

Question words keep their accent inside a longer sentence, when the sentence reports or implies a question:

» *No sé dónde vive.* — I don't know where she lives.
» *Pregúntale cuánto cuesta.* — Ask him how much it costs.

> [!trap] Relatives have no accent
> When the same word introduces a description rather than a question, no accent: *la ciudad donde nací* — the city where I was born; *el chico que conocí* — the boy (that) I met.

## Comprehensive Review 1

Choose the question word.

» *¿___ te llamas?* → *Cómo* — What's your name?
» *¿___ años tienes?* → *Cuántos* — How old are you?
» *¿___ es tu correo electrónico?* → *Cuál* — What's your email address?
» *¿___ vives?* → *Dónde* — Where do you live?
» *¿___ es tu cumpleaños?* → *Cuándo* — When is your birthday?
» *¿___ es ese señor?* → *Quién* — Who is that man?
» *¿___ estudias?* → *Qué* — What are you studying?

## Comprehensive Review 2

Prepositions and tricky choices.

» *¿___ eres?* (origin) → *De dónde* — Where are you from?
» *¿___ vas ahora?* (destination) → *Adónde* — Where are you going now?
» *¿___ es esta mochila?* (owner) → *De quién* — Whose backpack is this?
» *¿___ sale el tren?* (clock time) → *A qué hora* — What time does the train leave?
» *¿___ no comes?* (cause) → *Por qué* — Why aren't you eating?
» *¿___ sirve este botón?* (purpose) → *Para qué* — What is this button for?

### Spot the mistake

» ~~*¿Qué es tu nombre?*~~ → *¿Cuál es tu nombre?* / *¿Cómo te llamas?* — What's your name?
» ~~*¿Porque estás aquí?*~~ → *¿Por qué estás aquí?* — Why are you here?
» ~~*¿Cuántos leche?*~~ → *¿Cuánta leche?* — How much milk?

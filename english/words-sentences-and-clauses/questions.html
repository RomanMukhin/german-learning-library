Questions: alternative questions (Is it black or grey?)

Form

Alternative questions offer a choice with or. They are formed like yes-no questions, with an auxiliary before the subject: Is it black or grey? Would you like tea or coffee? They can also begin with a wh-word: Which do you prefer, red or white?

Reduced forms

In informal speech: Tea or coffee? Here or to take away?

Responding

Answer with one of the options, not yes or no: "Tea or coffee?" "Coffee, please." Intonation rises on the first option and falls on the last.

Questions: statement questions (you're over 18?)

A statement question has the word order of a statement but rising intonation. It is used to check or confirm something the speaker believes, or to show surprise: You're over 18? She's leaving? You haven't eaten yet? They are informal and common in speech.

Questions: two-step questions

Two-step yes-no questions

In conversation, speakers often ask a question in two steps, making it easier for the listener: What about dinner? Shall we eat out?

Pre-questions

A pre-question checks before the main question: Can I ask you something? Are you free on Saturday? Are you busy? I need some help.

Suggesting the answer

The second part can offer an answer: What do you want to do? Go to the cinema? Where shall we meet? At the station?

Questions: typical errors

  • missing auxiliary: Where you live? → Where do you live?
  • wrong word order: Where you are going? → Where are you going?
  • auxiliary with subject questions: Who did call you? (when who is the subject) → Who called you?
  • indirect questions with question order: Can you tell me where is the station? → …where the station is?
  • -s or past after does/did: Does she works? → Does she work?

Questions: wh-questions

Form

Wh-questions begin with what, who, which, where, when, why, whose, how.

  • with an auxiliary: wh-word + auxiliary + subject + verb: Where do you live? What are you doing? Why did she leave?
  • without an auxiliary (when the wh-word is the subject): Who called? What happened? Which bus goes to the centre?

Prepositions usually go at the end: Who are you talking to? What is it for?

Responding and emphasis

Answers are often short: "Where do you live?" "In Leeds." Emphasis: What on earth are you doing? Why ever did you say that?

Negative wh-questions and wh-words at the end

  • Why don't you come? (suggestion) Who hasn't paid?
  • informal, with surprise: You did what? She went where?

Intonation

Wh-questions usually have falling intonation; a rise shows surprise or asking for repetition.

Questions: yes-no questions (Are you feeling cold?)

Form

Put the auxiliary, modal or be before the subject: Are you feeling cold? Can you swim? Have you finished? Without an auxiliary, use do / does / did: Do you like jazz? Did she call?

Responding

Short answers use the auxiliary: "Are you tired?" "Yes, I am." / "No, I'm not." "Do you smoke?" "No, I don't." A bare yes or no can sound abrupt.

Negative yes-no questions

Isn't it beautiful? (expecting yes) Don't you know? (surprise)

Intonation

Yes-no questions usually have rising intonation.

Questions: follow-up questions

Reduced wh-questions

In conversation, short wh-questions ask for more detail: "I'm going on holiday." "Where to?" / "When?" / "Who with?"

Showing interest or surprise

Short auxiliary questions show interest: "I've just got married." "Have you? Congratulations!" "She's from Peru." "Is she?"

Questions: echo and checking questions

Echo questions repeat part of what someone said, to check or show surprise, often with a wh-word in place of the part we didn't hear or can't believe: "I paid £500 for it." "You paid how much?" "She's moving to Alaska." "Where?" Checking questions confirm information: "It starts at eight." "At eight?"

Questions: short forms

Short questions are common in informal speech and leave out words that are understood:

  • Ready? (= Are you ready?) Coffee? (= Would you like a coffee?)
  • Anything else? Any questions?
  • Why not? How come? (= why?) What for? Since when?
  • short auxiliary questions: Did you? Can't she? Will they?

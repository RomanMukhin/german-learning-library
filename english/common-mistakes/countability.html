Common mistakes in English

Countability mistakes

Uncountable nouns that learners treat as countable, and countable nouns used wrongly: advice, equipment, information, knowledge, staff, year.

Advice

  • an advice, advices → some advice, a piece of advice

Advice is uncountable. Can you give me some advice?

Behaviour

  • behaviours (in general English) → behaviour

His behaviour was terrible. (Behaviours appears only in scientific writing.)

Damage

  • a damage, damages (= harm) → damage

The storm caused a lot of damage. Damages (plural) means money paid by court order.

Equipment

  • equipments, an equipment → equipment, a piece of equipment

We need new sports equipment.

Furniture

  • furnitures, a furniture → furniture, a piece of furniture

The furniture is old.

Information

  • informations, an information → information, a piece of information

Thank you for the information.

Kind or kinds?

  • these kind of problems → these kinds of problems / this kind of problem

Match the determiner and kind: this kind, these kinds.

Knowledge

  • knowledges → knowledge

She has a good knowledge of history (a is possible with of, but no plural).

Less or fewer?

  • less people (informal) → fewer people

Fewer with plural nouns, less with uncountable nouns.

More

  • more better, more cheaper → better, cheaper

More + noun is fine with plurals and uncountables: more students, more time.

One of

  • one of my friend → one of my friends

One of is followed by a plural noun and a singular verb: One of my friends is a pilot.

Progress

  • a progress, progresses → progress

You've made a lot of progress.

Research

  • researches, a research → research, a piece of research, a study

There has been a lot of research into this.

Sale or sales?

  • The sale of the company increased → Sales increased

Sales (plural) = the number of things sold; a sale = one act of selling or a time of reduced prices: in the sales.

Software

  • softwares → software, a piece of software, a program

The company makes software.

Staff

  • a staff, staffs (= people) → a member of staff, the staff

Staff is a group noun: The staff are / is very friendly.

Surroundings

  • surrounding (as a noun) → surroundings

Surroundings is always plural: The hotel has beautiful surroundings.

Training

  • trainings → training, a training course

Training is usually uncountable: You'll get training on the job.

Transport

  • transports, a transport → transport, a means of transport

Public transport is good here. (US transportation)

Year

  • I am 20 years → I am 20 / I'm 20 years old; a 20 years old man → a 20-year-old man

Don't leave out old after years, and keep year singular in compound adjectives.

Common mistakes in English

Hundreds of short entries on the errors learners make most often, grouped by type: collocation, countability, determiners, part of speech, prepositions, spelling, word choice, word order and word patterns.

In this section

9 topics. Read them in order, then take the quiz at the bottom of this page.

Collocation mistakesWords that go together: ask a question, make a mistake, do business, take an exam, give a speech and more.Countability mistakesUncountable nouns that learners treat as countable, and countable nouns used wrongly: advice, equipment, information, knowledge, staff, year.Determiner mistakesMissing and extra articles, much and many, hundred or hundreds, most or most of, this or that, and nouns like life, nature and society.Part of speech mistakesUsing a noun instead of a verb, an adjective instead of an adverb and similar mix-ups: advice/advise, breath/breathe, good/well, life/live, your/yours.Preposition mistakesThe wrong preposition with times, places and nouns: on Monday afternoon, at home, on the beach, increase in, interest in, solution to.Spelling mistakesCommon errors with verb endings and word building, and fifty words that learners (and native speakers) misspell most often.Word choice mistakesPairs of words that learners mix up, from accept/agree and actual/current to win/beat, wish/hope and work/job.Word order mistakesWhere to put adverbs like again, already, also, always, just, often, only, still and very much, and the order in indirect questions with how, what, where and why.Word pattern mistakesThe structures that follow common words: afford to, apologise to, arrive at, avoid -ing, depend on, discuss, enjoy -ing, explain to, look forward to, suggest and many more.

Each entry shows the typical mistake crossed out and the correct form next to it, with a one-line reason. The entries are grouped by the kind of mistake:

Group What goes wrong
Collocation the wrong word partner: make a question
Countability treating an uncountable noun as countable: advices
Determiners missing or extra the, a, much, many: the nature
Part of speech noun instead of verb, adjective instead of adverb: I advice you
Prepositions the wrong preposition: in the afternoon of Monday
Spelling letters doubled, dropped or swapped: accomodation
Word choice two similar words mixed up: I borrowed him my pen
Word order adverbs and question words in the wrong place: I like very much it
Word patterns the wrong structure after a word: I enjoy to swim

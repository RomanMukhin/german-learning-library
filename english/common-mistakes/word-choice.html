Accept or agree?

  • I accept with you. → I agree with you. (same opinion) / I accept your offer. (say yes to)

According to

  • According to me, … → In my opinion, … / According to the report, …

Actual or current?

  • the actual situation (= now) → the current situation

Actual = real; current = happening now.

Advertisement or advertising?

  • an advertising → an advertisement / an ad / an advert (one) / advertising (the industry, uncountable)

All or everything?

  • All is ready. → Everything is ready.

All alone as a subject usually means "the only thing": All I want is sleep.

Alone or lonely?

  • I live lonely. → I live alone. (without others) / I feel lonely. (unhappy because alone)

Amount of or number of?

  • a large amount of people → a large number of people; a large amount of money

Ancient, former, or old?

  • my ancient teacher → my former / old teacher

Ancient = thousands of years old; former = previous; old = aged, or previous (informal).

Announce or advertise?

  • They announced the job in the paper. → They advertised the job. (to sell or offer) / They announced the results. (to tell publicly)

Another or other?

  • another books → other books; other book → another book

As or like?

  • He works like a teacher. (= his job) → He works as a teacher. / He sings like a professional.

At last or finally?

  • At last, I want to thank… → Finally, I want to thank…

At last expresses relief after waiting; finally also introduces the last point.

Avoid or prevent?

  • avoid an accident to happen → prevent an accident (from happening); avoid crowds (keep away from)

Besides or also?

  • I play tennis and besides I swim. → …and I also swim.

Besides = in addition to (+ noun) or "and anyway" (linking): Besides, it's too late.

Bored or boring?

  • I'm boring. (= I feel bored) → I'm bored.

Bring or take?

  • I'll bring you to the airport. → I'll take you to the airport. (away from here)

Camping or campsite?

  • We stayed at a camping. → We stayed at a campsite. / We went camping.

Class or classroom?

  • The class has 20 desks. → The classroom has 20 desks. (the room) / The class has 20 students. (the group)

Closed or close?

  • The shop is close. → The shop is closed. / The shop is close (to here). (= near)

Cloth, clothes, or clothing?

  • I bought new cloths. → I bought new clothes.

Cloth = material; clothes = things you wear (plural); clothing = clothes in general (uncountable, formal).

Come

  • Come to here. → Come here.; I'll come to home late. → I'll come home late.

No to before here, there, home.

Common or ordinary?

  • an common day → an ordinary day (normal) / a common problem (happens often)

Contain or include?

  • The price contains breakfast. → The price includes breakfast. (part of a total) / The box contains books. (physically inside)

Correct or right?

Both mean "not wrong": right / correct answer. Only right is used for moral questions, directions and be right (person): You're right, turn right.

Country, land, nation, or state?

  • my land is Poland → my country is Poland

Country = the normal word; nation = the people; state = the political organisation or part of a federal country; land = ground or literary.

Drive or ride?

  • drive a bike / a horse → ride a bike / a horse / a motorbike; drive a car / a bus

During or for?

  • during two hours → for two hours; during the night

Economical or economic?

  • economical crisis → economic crisis; an economical car (cheap to run)

Education or upbringing?

  • My parents gave me a good education (about manners) → a good upbringing

Education = school learning; upbringing = how parents raise a child.

Elder or elderly?

  • elder people → elderly people (old, polite) / my elder brother

Employer or employee?

  • I'm an employer at the bank. (if you work there) → I'm an employee.

Employer = the person or company that pays; employee = the worker.

Excited or exciting?

  • I'm very exciting about the trip. → I'm very excited. / The trip is exciting.

Far or away?

  • It's 5 km far. → It's 5 km away. / Is it far?

Fault or mistake?

  • It was my mistake that we were late (blame) → It was my fault; I made three faults → three mistakes

Mistake = something wrong; fault = responsibility for something bad.

Few or a few?

  • I have few friends, so I'm happy → I have a few friends (some) / I have few friends (not many)

Find out or find?

  • I need to find the truth. → find out the truth (discover information) / find my keys (locate)

Fit or suit?

  • That colour fits you. → That colour suits you. (looks good) / These shoes fit. (right size)

Forget or leave?

  • I forgot my phone at home. → I left my phone at home.

Funny or fun?

  • The trip was very funny. (enjoyable) → The trip was great fun.

Further or farther?

  • farther information → further information

Go, gone, and been

  • She has been to the shop and she is still there. → She has gone to the shop. / I have been to Paris (and returned).

Go or come?

  • Can I go to your party? → Can I come to your party? (towards the listener)

Go or get?

  • What time did you go home last night? (= arrive) → What time did you get home?; How do I get to the station?

Hardly or hard?

  • She works hardly. → She works hard.

Hardly = almost not.

History or story?

  • He told me a funny history. → a funny story

History = the past, the subject.

Hour or time?

  • What hour is it? → What time is it?; I don't have hour → I don't have time

How or what?

  • How is it called? → What is it called?; How does it look like? → What does it look like?

Improve or increase?

  • improve the number of students → increase the number; improve the quality

Interesting

  • It's very interesting for me. → I find it very interesting / I'm very interested in it.

Interesting or interested?

  • I'm interesting in music. → I'm interested in music.

Its or it's?

  • The company and it's staff → The company and its staff; It's = it is / it has.

Join or attend?

  • attend a club, join a lecture → attend a meeting / a lecture / a school; join a club / a team

Know or find out?

  • I knew yesterday that she was ill. → I found out yesterday… (learned) / I knew it already.

Know or get to know?

  • I knew him at university. (first met) → I got to know him at university. / I met him at university.

Last or latest?

  • his last film (if more will come) → his latest film (most recent); his last film (final, or the one before this)

Lay and lie

  • I'm going to lay down. → I'm going to lie down. / Lay the table. (put)

Learn, teach, or study?

  • She learnt me English. → She taught me English.

Learn = get knowledge; teach = give knowledge; study = spend time learning, often formally: study law.

Leave or live?

  • I leave in Madrid. → I live /lɪv/ in Madrid. / I leave /liːv/ at six.

Lend and borrow

  • Can I lend your pen? → Can I borrow your pen? / Can you lend me your pen?

Let or leave?

  • Let me alone! → Leave me alone!; Leave me go → Let me go

Let = allow; leave = go away from, or not disturb.

Listen, listen to, or hear?

  • I listen music. → I listen to music.; I'm listening a noise → I hear a noise.

Look, look at, see, or watch?

  • Look this! → Look at this!; see a football match on TV → watch a match

Lose or miss?

  • I lost the bus. → I missed the bus. / I lost my wallet.

May or can?

  • May you help me? → Can / Could you help me?

May I…? is for asking permission, not for requests to others.

Meet, get to know, and know

  • I knew her last year at a party → I met her last year (first time) / I got to know her (became familiar) / I know her (state)

Meet or visit?

  • I'll meet the museum. → I'll visit the museum. / I'll meet my friend there.

Miss or lack?

  • The team misses experience. → The team lacks experience. (doesn't have enough) / I miss my family. (feel sad they're absent)

Move or travel?

  • We travelled to a new flat. → We moved to a new flat. (changed home) / We travelled around Asia.

Needed or necessary?

  • It is needed to book. → It is necessary to book. / Help is needed.

Nervous, agitated, or irritable?

  • He gets nervous easily (= angry) → He gets irritable / annoyed

Nervous = worried, anxious; agitated = very worried and unable to stay calm; irritable = easily annoyed.

Not ... either

  • I don't like it too. → I don't like it either.

Notice or news?

  • I have a good notice. → I have some good news.

A notice = a written sign or warning; notice (verb) = see.

Number or figure?

  • the sales numbers for May (formal reports) → the sales figures for May

Figures = written numbers and official statistics; number = an amount or a value: a large number of people, phone number.

Offer or give?

  • She offered me a present on my birthday. (if she handed it over) → She gave me a present.

Offer = ask if someone wants something.

On the contrary or by/in contrast?

  • London is big. On the contrary, Bath is small. → By / In contrast, Bath is small.

On the contrary rejects a statement as untrue.

Parents or relations/relatives?

  • I have many parents in Spain. → I have many relatives / relations in Spain.

Parents = mother and father only.

Parking or car park?

  • a parking → a car park (Br) / a parking lot (US); parking = the activity (uncountable)

Place or room/space?

  • There is no place in the car. → There is no room / space in the car.

Place = a location.

Point of view or opinion?

  • In my point of view → In my opinion / From my point of view

Point or point out?

  • She pointed that the plan was risky. → She pointed out that… (mentioned) / She pointed at the map.

Possibility, occasion, or opportunity?

  • I had the possibility to work abroad. → the opportunity to work abroad; a special occasion (event)

Propose or offer?

  • He proposed to drive me home. → He offered to drive me home.

Propose = suggest a plan, or ask to marry.

Protect or prevent?

  • protect the disease → prevent the disease; protect people from the disease

Reach or achieve?

  • achieve the station → reach the station (arrive) / achieve a goal (succeed); reach also works with goals and agreements.

Relation or relationship?

  • a good relation with my boss → a good relationship with my boss

Relations (plural) = contacts between countries or groups.

Relevant

  • relevant = important? → Relevant = connected with the subject: This is not relevant to the discussion. For "important", use important, significant.

Remind or remember?

  • Remember me to call him. → Remind me to call him.

Require or request?

  • I require you to send (rude) → I request that you send / Please send

Require = need, or demand (formal); request = ask politely.

Rest, stay, or remain?

  • We rested in a hotel for a week. → We stayed in a hotel.

Rest = relax; remain = continue to exist or be (formal).

Right or true?

  • Is it right that she left? (= correct fact) → Is it true that she left?

Rise or raise?

  • raise up, prices raised → prices rose; they raised prices

Say or tell?

  • She said me → She told me / She said to me

Security or safety?

  • for your security, wear a helmet → for your safety

Safety = protection from accidents; security = protection from crime and attack.

Sensitive or sensible?

  • She's very sensible (= easily hurt) → She's very sensitive. / a sensible decision (practical)

Since, from, or for?

  • I've lived here from 2010. → …since 2010.; since three years → for three years

Some or any?

  • I don't have some money. → I don't have any money.

Something or anything?

  • I didn't buy something. → I didn't buy anything.

Sometime or sometimes?

  • Sometime I go swimming. → Sometimes I go swimming. / Let's meet sometime.

Station or stop?

  • the bus station near my house (just a sign by the road) → the bus stop; bus / train station = a building

Staying or stay?

  • I enjoyed my staying in London. → I enjoyed my stay in London.

Strange or foreign?

  • Do you speak strange languages? → foreign languages

Strange = unusual; a stranger = a person you don't know.

Stuff or staff?

  • The stuff are friendly. → The staff are friendly. (employees) / Put your stuff here. (things)

Sympathetic

  • She's very sympathetic (= nice) → She's very nice / friendly / likeable

Sympathetic = understanding someone's problems.

Take part or take place?

  • The concert will take part in the park. → …will take place in the park. / I'll take part in the race.

Tell or say?

  • tell hello → say hello; say a story → tell a story

That or than?

  • She's taller that me. → She's taller than me.

Think about, think of, or think?

  • I think to go → I'm thinking of / about going; What do you think about? (opinion) → What do you think of it?

Tired of or tired from?

  • I'm tired of walking all day (physically) → I'm tired from walking all day / tired of waiting (bored and annoyed)

Travel, trip, or journey?

  • a nice travel → a nice trip / journey

Travel is usually a verb or uncountable noun; trip = a visit and return; journey = going from one place to another.

Trouble or problem?

  • I have a trouble. → I have a problem. / I'm in trouble. / I have trouble sleeping. (uncountable)

Unique or only?

  • She is my unique sister. → She is my only sister.

Unique = very special, the only one of its kind.

Until now or so far?

  • Until now everything is fine. → So far, everything is fine. (until now suggests a change: Until now, I lived alone)

Used to and be used to

  • I'm used to get up early. → I'm used to getting up early. / I used to get up early (past habit).

Various or varied?

  • a various diet → a varied diet (with variety) / various reasons (several different)

View or sight?

  • the sights from my window → the view from my window; see the sights (famous places)

Wait or expect?

  • I'm waiting a baby. → I'm expecting a baby.; I expected for an hour → I waited for an hour.

What or which?

  • What of these do you want? → Which of these do you want?

When or if?

  • When I win the lottery… (unlikely) → If I win the lottery…

Where or when?

  • the day where we met → the day when we met; the place where we met

Which or what?

  • Which is your name? → What is your name?

Which or who?

  • the man which → the man who

Win or beat?

  • We won the other team. → We beat the other team. / We won the match.

Wish or hope?

  • I wish you will pass. → I hope you pass. / I wish you luck.

Work, job, or occupation?

  • I'm looking for a work. → I'm looking for a job / for work

Occupation = a formal word for job, used on forms.

Would or should?

  • You would see a doctor. (advice) → You should see a doctor.

Would = imagined situations, polite requests.

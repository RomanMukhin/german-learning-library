Finite and non-finite verbs

A finite verb shows tense and agrees with a subject: She works. They worked. Every complete clause has a finite verb. Non-finite forms don't show tense: the infinitive ((to) work), the -ing form (working) and the past participle (worked). They are used after other verbs and in non-finite clauses:

  • I want to go. She enjoys swimming. Built in 1900, the house is old.

Imperative clauses (Be quiet!)

The imperative uses the base form, usually with no subject: Be quiet! Open the window.

With subject pronouns and do

  • a subject adds emphasis or contrast: You stay here. Somebody call a doctor!
  • do + imperative is emphatic or polite: Do come in. Do be careful.

Let's and let

  • first person (suggestion): Let's go. Negative: Let's not go. (informal Don't let's)
  • third person: Let them wait.

Negative imperatives

Don't touch! Don't you dare! (with subject, strong) Never do that again.

Tags and softening

Sit down, will you? Help me, would you? Pass the salt, please. Imperatives are also used for instructions, advice, warnings and invitations: Have a seat. Turn left.

Infinitives with and without to

To-infinitive

  • after many verbs: want, decide, hope, plan, agree, refuse, promise, learn, manage: I decided to stay.
  • after adjectives: It's easy to learn. I'm happy to help.
  • for purpose: I came to see you.
  • after question words: I don't know what to do.
  • after too / enough: too cold to swim

Infinitive without to (bare infinitive)

  • after modal verbs: I can swim. You should go.
  • after make and let: She made me laugh. Let me help.
  • after had better, would rather: You'd better go.
  • after see, hear, watch, feel + object (whole action): I saw him leave.
  • help (with or without to): Can you help me (to) carry this?

Typical errors

  • I can to swim. → I can swim.
  • She made me to cry. → She made me cry. (but passive: I was made to cry)
  • I want that you come. → I want you to come.

Infinitive: active or passive?

Focus on the agent or on the receiver

The active infinitive (to do) focuses on who does the action; the passive (to be done) focuses on the receiver: I need to clean the car / The car needs to be cleaned (or needs cleaning). She hopes to be chosen for the team.

After there is / there are

Both are often possible: There's a lot of work to do / to be done. There's nothing to eat.

Perfect infinitive with to (to have worked)

To have + past participle refers to a time before the main verb, or to things that didn't happen:

  • She seems to have left. (= it seems she has left)
  • I'm glad to have met you.
  • I would like to have seen it. (but I didn't)
  • with modals: You should have told me. He must have forgotten. They might have missed the bus.

Verbs: basic forms

The three basic forms

English verbs have three main forms: base form (work, take), past simple (worked, took) and past participle (worked, taken). Dictionaries list them as take, took, taken. Regular verbs add -ed for both past forms.

Spelling rules for regular verbs

  • base ending in -e: add -d: live → lived
  • consonant + y: y → ied: study → studied (but play → played)
  • single vowel + single consonant, stressed: double: stop → stopped, prefer → preferred
  • vowel + l (British): double: travel → travelled (US traveled)

Person and number

Only the present simple 3rd person singular (works) and be (am, is, are, was, were) change for person and number.

Verbs: formation

Identifying verbs

Verbs can be preceded by to or a modal, take tense endings, and have a subject.

Typical prefixes and suffixes

  • prefixes: re- (rewrite), un- (undo), dis- (disagree), mis- (misunderstand), over- (oversleep), out- (outnumber), en- (enable)
  • suffixes making verbs: -ise / -ize (modernise), -en (widen, shorten), -ify (simplify), -ate (activate)

Verbs related to nouns and adjectives

Many nouns and verbs share one form (email, text, google, phone, chair). Adjectives often become verbs: dry → to dry, empty → to empty, calm → to calm down.

Compound verbs

babysit, sightsee, download, overtake, undergo, dry-clean, double-check.

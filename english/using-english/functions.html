Commands and instructions

Giving commands

The imperative (base form of the verb, no subject) gives direct commands: Sit down. Don't touch that! Imperatives can sound rude between adults, so we soften them with please, tags or modals: Sit down, please. Pass the salt, would you? Could you close the door? Do + imperative is emphatic or friendly: Do sit down.

Public notices

Signs often use short forms, nouns or the passive: No smoking. Keep off the grass. Passengers are requested to …

Giving instructions

Instructions (recipes, manuals, directions) use imperatives with sequencing words: First, heat the oil. Then add the onions. Finally, stir in the cream. Turn left at the lights, then take the second right.

Commentaries

Commentaries (sports, cooking shows, demonstrations) describe events as they happen. They use the present simple for quick actions and the present continuous for longer ones, and often leave out words: Smith passes to Jones… Jones shoots… and it's a goal! Now I'm adding the flour.

Invitations

To invite someone: Would you like to come to dinner? Do you fancy a coffee? (informal) How about joining us? Why don't you come along? (informal)

Accepting

Thanks, I'd love to. That sounds great. Yes, why not?

Refusing

Refusals are usually softened with thanks, regret and a reason: I'd love to, but I'm busy on Friday. I'm afraid I can't make it. Thanks for asking, though.

Offers

Food and drink

Would you like some tea? Can I get you anything? Help yourself. Have some more cake. Replies: Yes, please. / No, thanks, I'm fine.

Offering to do something

Shall I carry that for you? Can I help? Let me help you. I'll get it. Would you like me to call a taxi? Replies: That's very kind of you. / No, it's OK, thanks.

In different situations

Shops: Can I help you? Formal: May I take your coat?

Requests

Asking for something

Can / Could I have a glass of water, please? I'd like a single ticket. Could I possibly borrow your car? (very polite) Have you got…?

Asking someone to do something

  • Can you pass the salt? (informal)
  • Could you help me? (polite)
  • Would you mind opening the window? (very polite; + -ing)
  • I was wondering if you could … (very polite, indirect)

Answers: Sure. / Of course. / No problem. / I'm sorry, I can't.

Greetings and farewells: hello, goodbye, Happy New Year

Saying hello

Informal: Hi! / Hello! / Hiya! (Br) / Hey! Formal: Good morning / afternoon / evening. Then: How are you? — Fine, thanks. And you? First meetings: Nice / Pleased to meet you. How do you do? is very formal (the reply is the same phrase).

Saying goodbye

Bye! / See you! / See you later! / Take care! / Cheers! (Br, informal) Formal: Goodbye. At night: Good night (only for leaving, not greeting). Before leaving: I'd better be going. It was nice talking to you.

Congratulating and celebrating

Congratulations! (on a success) Well done! Happy birthday! Happy New Year! Merry / Happy Christmas! Good luck! Cheers! (when drinking) Get well soon.

Suggestions

How about, what about, why not, let's

  • How about / What about + -ing / noun? — How about going to the beach?
  • How about + clause — How about we meet at six? (informal)
  • Why not + base form? / Why don't we / you …? — Why not try again?
  • Let's + base form / Let's not … — Let's go. Let's not argue.
  • Shall we …? — Shall we start?

Could and weaker or stronger suggestions

  • neutral: We could take a taxi. You might want to check that.
  • strong: You should see it. You must try this cake!
  • tentative: I thought we might / could go out. You could always ask him. There's always the bus.
  • annoyed: Can't you turn it down?

Telephoning

Making and answering a call

Answer: Hello? / Hello, Sarah speaking. Business: Good morning, ABC Ltd, how can I help? Identify yourself with It's … or This is …, not I am: Hi, it's Tom.

Asking for someone, waiting, unavailable

  • Can I speak to Ms Jones, please? / Is Anna there?
  • Hold on a moment. / Hang on. / I'll put you through.
  • I'm afraid she's not available / in a meeting. Can I take a message? / Would you like to leave a message?

Ending a call

Thanks for calling. I'll let you go. Speak soon. Bye.

Warnings

Weak and strong warnings

  • weak: Be careful with that knife. Watch out, it's hot. Mind the step. (Br)
  • strong: Don't touch that! You'd better not be late. If you do that again, I'll …

Public warning notices

Danger! Beware of the dog. Caution: wet floor. Keep out. Mind the gap. (London Underground)

Immediate danger

Look out! Watch out! Careful! Stop! Fire!

Politeness

Showing respect

We show respect with titles (Mr, Ms, Dr), please and thank you, and by giving people choices rather than orders.

Making what we say less direct

English uses many ways to soften what we say:

  • hedges and vague language: I just wondered…, It's a bit expensive, sort of
  • modal verbs: Could you…?, It might be better to…, Would you mind…?
  • past tenses and continuous forms: I was wondering if…, I wanted to ask you…
  • if: If you could sign here, please.
  • two-step questions: Can I ask you something? … Would you mind…?
  • using names: Thanks, John.

What sounds impolite?

  • plain imperatives to adults you don't know well: Give me a coffee. → Could I have a coffee, please?
  • titles with first names: Mr John → Mr Smith or John
  • very familiar words (love, mate, dear) with strangers in some situations
  • What? instead of Sorry? / Pardon?

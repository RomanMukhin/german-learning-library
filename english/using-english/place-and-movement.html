Place and movement

Abroad, away, back, inside, nearby, outside and up as adverbs, prepositions, adjectives and particles.

On this page

Abroad

Abroad is an adverb meaning "in or to a foreign country". It takes no preposition and no article:

  • She lives abroad. / We're going abroad next week.
  • Not: go to abroad, in abroad

The only exception is from abroad: students from abroad.

Away and away from

Away

Away means "to or at another place, at a distance": Go away! She's away on holiday. The beach is 2 km away. With time: Christmas is only a week away. It also shows continuous action: She was singing away in the kitchen.

Away from

Away from + noun: Keep away from the fire. We live far away from the city.

Back

As an adverb

Back means "returning to an earlier place or state", or "in return": Come back soon! I'll call you back. Give it back. Back home = at home again.

As a noun, adjective and verb

  • noun: My back hurts. at the back of the room (inside); behind the house (outside)
  • adjective: the back door, the back seat
  • verb: back the car out (reverse), back someone (support), back up files

Inside

Inside as an adjective, noun, adverb and preposition

  • adjective: the inside pocket
  • noun: the inside of the box; inside out (with the inside on the outside)
  • adverb: Let's go inside. It's warm inside.
  • preposition: inside the house (inside of is American/informal)

Nearby

Nearby is an adjective or adverb meaning "not far away":

  • adjective (before the noun): a nearby village
  • adverb (often at the end): Is there a bank nearby? They live nearby.

Don't use it as a preposition: nearby the station → near the station.

Outside

Outside works like inside:

  • preposition: Wait outside the door. (outside of is mostly American)
  • adverb: The children are playing outside.
  • noun: the outside of the building
  • adjective: an outside toilet

It also means "beyond the limits of": outside office hours, outside my experience.

Up

Up as an adverb and preposition

Up shows movement towards a higher position or to the north or a city centre: Stand up. Prices went up. walk up the stairs, climb up a tree, go up to Scotland.

Up as an adjective

Are you up? (= out of bed) Time's up. (= finished) What's up? (= what's wrong / what's happening)

Up to with numbers

Up to = as much as: The car holds up to five people. It's up to you (= your decision).

Up as a particle

Up is very common in phrasal verbs, often meaning "completely" or "increase": eat up, use up, clean up, give up, turn up (arrive / increase volume), grow up, look up a word, make up a story.
